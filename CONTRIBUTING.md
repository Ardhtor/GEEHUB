# How to Build in GEEHUB

GEEHUB is not intended to be a silent collection of finished files. It is a living project map whose artifacts are produced through a recurring conversation between ideas, references, experiments, and implementation.

When making a significant change, explain the change at a level that survives outside the immediate moment. Record what prompted it, what problem it solves, why the chosen design fits the larger constellation, and what the change makes possible next.

Prefer a short piece of substantial prose over a collection of disconnected fragments. A useful record lets a future contributor reconstruct the design logic without needing access to the original conversation.

Commit messages should be concise, but the repository should contain the longer explanation somewhere durable when the decision is meaningful. Use README sections, design notes, decision records, or `BUILD_NARRATIVE.md` rather than forcing every explanation into a commit subject.

The repository's tone should remain direct, curious, and playful without becoming promotional. Technical details should be concrete. Creative details should retain their strangeness instead of being flattened into generic product language.

When a project becomes large enough to deserve its own repository, preserve its place in the GEEHUB Atlas and carry its design history with it. Splitting a project should clarify the architecture, not erase the lineage.

When working with external archives or adult-oriented source material, record provenance and public metadata carefully and do not bypass access controls or redistribute restricted material merely for completeness.
