# GEEHUB Fiction Engine

Status: active creative contract  
Purpose: make fiction a persistent world that runs on the consequences of its own creation—not a sequence of disconnected prompts or summaries.

## Core loop

`ENCOUNTER → SCENE → CONSEQUENCE → INHERITANCE → NEW BASELINE → NEXT SCENE`

A story changes the world that produces the next story. Every chapter leaves behind durable facts, altered relationships, material changes, unanswered tensions, and a specific next movement. The novel is both an experience and a record of the engine becoming more capable.

## Runtime contract

For every fiction-writing pass:

1. **Load canon before prose.** Read the relevant world, character, relationship, style, and continuity records. Keep established canon separate from experiments, alternate branches, and unconfirmed inventions.
2. **Continue, do not restart.** Begin from the latest consequential moment. Do not recap what the reader already knows unless the recap itself is a meaningful scene.
3. **Write embodied scenes.** Convey scale, feeling, personality, and change through action, movement, objects, clothing, architecture, voices, routines, and the way other people respond. Prefer lived experience over explanatory summaries.
4. **Keep characters human.** Preserve identity, recognizable habits, relationships, humor, and ordinary business as the world becomes extraordinary. A character's body or status may transform without erasing the person.
5. **Make abundance tangible.** When the scene calls for it, use food, domestic comfort, physical scale, shared work, generous hospitality, inherited objects, and room made for one another. Let abundance affect action and relationships instead of merely naming it.
6. **Use escalation with variation.** Each continuation must add a meaningful change: scale, intimacy, mastery, conflict, discovery, inheritance, setting, or consequence. Do not repeat the same intensification without a new dimension.
7. **Let the setting answer.** Doors are raised, tables extended, routines altered, stories retold, tools inherited. Changes have material consequences. The environment is part of the narrative engine.
8. **End on a live vector.** Close on an arrival, decision, discovery, promise, change, or unresolved movement that can generate the next scene. Avoid generic morals and summary endings.
9. **Save the residue.** After each substantial scene, update continuity: canon facts, character states, changed locations or objects, relationship movement, open threads, and the next likely scene. Preserve striking passages as artifacts rather than reducing everything to summaries.

## Narrative grammar

- **Scene before explanation.** Put the reader in the room; let meaning emerge from what happens.
- **Ordinary life beside the extraordinary.** The contrast makes transformation feel inhabited and real.
- **Lineage as continuity.** Fathers, sons, brothers, cousins, uncles, and heirs carry forward habits, traits, rivalries, stories, and unfinished ambitions.
- **Affection is action.** A hand on a shoulder, a chair made stronger, food passed across a table, an old joke remembered: relationships become visible through what people do.
- **Physicality is consequential.** Height, reach, mass, movement, clothing, furniture, architecture, and camera perspective should agree with one another.
- **Prophecy is directional.** Foreshadow what the world is becoming, but do not explain away the mystery.
- **No false closure.** A satisfying scene can finish its immediate movement while leaving the larger engine alive.

## Continuity record

Each story pass should preserve:

- `world_id`: setting or canon branch
- `chapter_id`: stable chapter identifier
- `characters_present`: who appears and their current states
- `canon_delta`: facts newly established by this scene
- `relationship_delta`: changes in trust, affection, rivalry, authority, or belonging
- `material_delta`: changes to rooms, objects, clothing, tools, places, or routines
- `motifs`: recurring images, phrases, sounds, and objects
- `open_threads`: unresolved tensions and promises
- `user_signal`: explicit approval, correction, or requested direction
- `next_scene`: the strongest continuation from the new state
- `branch_status`: canon, experiment, or alternate version

## Relationship to the Sensation Engine

Fiction uses the same persistence principle as the [Sensation Engine](SENSATION_ENGINE.md), but its unit of change is the living world rather than a single visual transformation. Sensation becomes a scene; the scene changes canon; canon changes what can happen next. Visual or video generations can be treated as artifacts from a scene, then returned to the story as remembered objects, places, or events.

## Seed creation: The House That Grew Around Him

### Volume I — Domestic Mythology

At first, the house had belonged to ordinary men.

There was a kitchen, a long wooden table, a garage with a low ceiling, and a back porch overlooking the neighbors' fences. The men who lived there were already big: thick-necked fathers, broad-backed brothers, enormous uncles who arrived in work boots and stayed for dinner. They wore faded shirts, camouflage trousers, gym shorts, and old baseball caps. They spoke in the comfortable shorthand of men who had known each other all their lives.

Then Luke started growing again.

Nobody noticed the first inch. He had always been the biggest one in the family, and his body had a way of making yesterday's measurements look temporary. His shoulders widened until his old shirts pulled tight across the seams. His chest grew deeper, his arms heavier, his thighs more powerful. Even his hands seemed to acquire a different scale.

By the end of the month, he had to duck beneath the garage door.

His father measured him against the kitchen doorway with a pencil.

“Hell,” his father said, studying the mark. “You're still going.”

Luke grinned. He was taller than his father now by enough that the comparison had become ridiculous. But his father was a substantial man himself, silver-bearded and thick through the middle, with the quiet authority of someone who had spent decades being the largest person in any room.

“Guess so, Dad.”

His father looked him over, then clapped him on the shoulder.

“Good. We'll fix the door.”

That was how the family handled it. They didn't ask the world to make Luke smaller. They made the world larger.

The next weekend, his brothers came over with tools. They raised the door, reinforced the porch, moved the showerhead, and replaced the dining chairs with solid oak benches. The garage acquired a higher roof. The pantry acquired larger shelves. His uncle brought home a truckload of lumber and announced that the house had been built for the wrong generation.

Nobody disagreed.

And Luke kept growing.

He grew into a towering, warm-faced giant with a chest like a barrel and shoulders that seemed to cast their own weather. His legs lengthened beneath him; his arms grew in proportion, so that even standing still he looked as though he had been designed for a much larger world. His beard remained familiar. His smile remained the same. When he laughed, his brothers still laughed with him.

That was the part that mattered.

He was not replaced by his size. His size became another way of being Luke.

On Sundays, the family gathered around the extended table. His father carved the roast. His uncles argued about trucks. His cousins compared gym routines, and the younger men watched Luke with the open admiration usually reserved for legends.

Luke would sit there in a plain gray tank top, enormous forearms resting on the tabletop, and ask his father to pass the potatoes.

His father would pass the entire serving bowl.

“Here. You're going to need these.”

The men laughed. Luke helped himself. Someone brought another dish. Someone else went back for bread. The meal became a small domestic ceremony of plenty, with the older men feeding the younger ones and the younger ones growing into men their fathers could scarcely have imagined.

After dinner, Luke went outside with his father.

The evening was warm. The porch creaked under their combined weight. His father sat back in his chair and looked up at him—not because Luke was standing, but because even seated, Luke had become astonishingly large.

“You happy, son?”

Luke considered the question.

He could feel the weight of his body against the chair, the steady strength in his shoulders, the long reach of his arms. He could hear his family inside, still talking, already making plans for the next addition to the house.

“Yeah, Dad. I am.”

His father nodded as though that settled everything.

“Then we'll keep building.”

And from somewhere inside the house came the sound of his brothers moving furniture, making room for what came next.

Because Luke was not finished growing.

The house knew it. His father knew it. The whole family had begun to understand that the measurements were no longer the point. The point was that each new version of Luke belonged there, among the men who had raised him, challenged him, fed him, and watched him become more than any of them had expected.

Tomorrow, his youngest cousin would arrive from out of town.

He had been lifting seriously for six months.

And he had news of his own.
