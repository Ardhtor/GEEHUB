## 3. DeviantArt collection mechanics

Official DeviantArt documentation provides important context for interpreting the collection metadata:

- Favourites are saved deviations or posts that a user wants to revisit.
- Collections organize those saved favourites.
- Collections can be created for subject, medium, style, or personal preference.
- Items can be moved or copied between collections; copying allows the same deviation to remain in its existing collection while also appearing in another.
- Collection order and deviation order can be changed.
- Collections can be renamed, removed, and given descriptions.

Consequently, collection membership is not necessarily exclusive. If Image (31) is associated with all three named collections, that may express several simultaneous interests or filing contexts. It does not prove that the work belongs to three separate genres, nor that the creator placed it in those collections. The account whose collection view supplied the metadata must be identified before the relationship is described more precisely.

## 4. NoAI label and handling rule

The source record transcribes the platform label as **Trial NoAI**. DeviantArt's current Help Center describes the **NoAI** label as a signal that artwork is not authorized for inclusion in third-party datasets for training AI models. Its label-options article distinguishes NoAI from “Created using AI tools” and from the Mature Content label.

Until the original deviation page is recovered and the exact label state is confirmed, preserve “Trial NoAI” verbatim as the capture wording. Do not silently normalize it to another label or assume the label's current state.

For GEEHUB handling, use a conservative rule: retain bibliographic metadata and provenance, but do not feed the artwork into image-generation, model-training, image-to-image, embedding, or visual-reference workflows while the NoAI restriction is present or unresolved. Do not create derivative images from it. A future rights review must be based on the original page's current label and the applicable platform terms, not on an assumption that public visibility means permission.

This is separate from whether a person can view a public page. Access to view is not the same as permission to reproduce, redistribute, train on, or transform an artwork.
