# Language Emergence Record

This directory records language that arises from the archive's own material. It is not a preloaded glossary and must not be populated with invented terms merely to make the system look complete.

## Capture rule

When a word, phrase, gesture, label, or distinction appears in a source:

1. Preserve the exact wording where possible.
2. Link it to the originating image, story, conversation, or event.
3. Record who used it, if known, and the immediate context.
4. Keep the speaker's apparent meaning separate from an editor's interpretation.
5. Note repetitions and variations without assuming they mean the same thing.
6. Leave uncertain meanings open. A term may remain unexplained.
7. Record disagreements, shifts, abandoned uses, and local meanings rather than resolving them artificially.

## When a pattern becomes visible

A recurring expression may be marked as recurring only when multiple source instances support that observation. A provisional interpretation can be attached, but it must cite the instances that led to it. Do not promote a pattern into a universal rule simply because it appears more than once.

## Editorial boundary

The archive may organize and cross-reference language, but it does not assign identities, motives, or definitions unsupported by the source. Characters and communities retain the right to name their own experiences, revise their language, disagree, and leave things unnamed.
