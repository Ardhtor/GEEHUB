# GEEHUB — Wider Character and Ensemble Register

**Status:** active navigation and continuity register  
**Purpose:** prevent character development from collapsing into one small central group. This register connects the wider cast while preserving separate worlds, books, branches, and canon boundaries. It is not a declaration that every named character belongs in the same scene or setting.

## The central correction

The six guests in *The Unplanned Convergence*—Luke, Tyler, Chase, Gabe, Andrew, and Joseph—are the opening ensemble, not the whole population of GEEHUB and not the only men whose lives matter to the wider narrative. The repository contains many other adult male characters, established relationships, teams, households, residents, rivals, friends, mentors, and independent communities. Their existence must affect how the story is built.

A character should not be included merely to make a scene feel crowded. Nor should an established man be reduced to a function, a cameo, a production reference, or an extension of one of the six. Each needs his own point of view, ordinary life, motives, relationships, boundaries, and capacity to change events. Where the record is incomplete, the story should develop him through scenes without silently inventing biography and treating it as old canon.

## Ensemble-writing requirements

1. **The six are not the whole cast.** Do not let the original Convergence guests monopolise narrative attention or become the only characters with interiority.
2. **Respect each branch.** A shared repository does not mean a shared in-world location, chronology, relationship network, or book.
3. **Give supporting men full lives.** A man who enters for one chapter should still have a motive, a preference, a voice, and something at stake beyond the task he performs.
4. **Build relationships in both directions.** Do not develop only how the central characters see newcomers. Show what newcomers think, want, refuse, misunderstand, and decide about the central group.
5. **Preserve private relationships.** Especially the Michael Brock–Kirk connection and the distinct Joseph–Kirk continuity, do not disclose or conflate what different characters know.
6. **Keep names distinct.** Joseph is not Joey; Luke is not Luke Mercer; Tyler is not Tyler Boeskin; Marcus is not automatically Teal Titan; Teal Titan is not Joey.
7. **No crowd-as-worldbuilding shortcut.** Populate places through recurring routines, work, food, sport, friendship, domestic life, local institutions, disagreements, and return visits.
8. **Do not fill gaps with false certainty.** Use character records as boundaries and prompts. Mark new inventions as new fictional development, not recovered fact.
9. **Let other men redirect the plot.** New arrivals should not merely confirm the original six's ideas. They may challenge a route, change a plan, establish a boundary, invite someone elsewhere, or refuse participation.
10. **Keep the growth engine reciprocal.** Encounters should change both the newcomer and the existing community, while preserving provenance and continuity.
