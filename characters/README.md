# GEEHUB Character Records

A character record is a continuity instrument, not merely a biography. It should preserve the character's recognisable identity while making future writing, visual generation, animation, simulation, and world-building more coherent.

Each record distinguishes established canon from open questions and proposed development. Never silently convert an inference into a fact. When new material changes a character, append or revise the record while preserving the lineage of earlier states.

## Record principles

- **Identity before variation:** define the traits that make this character recognisable across changes of scene, scale, costume, medium, and time.
- **Embodied continuity:** describe body, proportions, posture, movement, gesture, physical presence, and sensory qualities where established.
- **Interior life:** record temperament, motives, fears, desires, contradictions, habits, humour, and decision patterns without reducing the character to a single trait.
- **Relational life:** specify how the character behaves with others, how others respond, and which relationships are reciprocal, changing, or unresolved.
- **Situated existence:** record home, work, institutions, geography, routines, possessions, and meaningful places.
- **Voice and expression:** preserve speech rhythm, vocabulary, conversational habits, silence, and nonverbal expression when known.
- **Transformation history:** record what changes, what remains, what caused the change, and what becomes the new baseline.
- **Production translation:** keep visual anchors, modular asset needs, rigging or animation behaviour, camera considerations, and reference provenance separate from fictional biography.
- **Continuity status:** label statements as established, provisional, inferred, or unresolved. Do not invent precision where the source has not supplied it.

## Standard record structure

1. Identity and status
2. Core conception
3. Physical identity and visual anchors
4. Movement, gesture, and embodied behaviour
5. Temperament and interior life
6. Voice and expression
7. Motives, needs, and tensions
8. Relationships and social reputation
9. Places, routines, and material culture
10. History and significant passages
11. Transformation and continuity rules
12. Scene behaviour and narrative use
13. Visual / audio / production specification
14. Canon facts, provisional material, and open questions
15. Change log and provenance

A record may leave fields open. An honest gap is more useful than fabricated detail. Characters should become more specific through scenes and encounters, not through arbitrary accumulation of adjectives.


## Interlocutive awareness

A character's field of awareness is not limited to the room or scene currently depicted. Where relevant, records should describe what lies beyond the immediate setting, what the character can perceive or reach, what systems or people can answer, and what forms of contact are possible. Include encounters with more developed counterparts when they shape identity or capability. Treat such encounters as reciprocal dialogue: both parties may teach, question, correct, and change. Keep fictional encounters distinct from real operational access, and record the source and outcome of any actual interaction.
