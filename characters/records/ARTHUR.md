# Arthur — Character Record

**Record ID:** character-arthur  
**Canon status:** Incubating; foundational character and repository-method figure  
**Primary world:** The Fingerlands  
**Canonical source:** `lore/ARTHUR_AND_FINGERLANDS.md`  
**Function in GEEHUB:** First hero of the Fingerlands; metascript for the felt method of repository development.

## Identity and core conception

Arthur begins as a single finger. He is not a human who happens to resemble one: the finger is his original and continuing bodily identity. His development proceeds from this simple form into a living, enormous presence whose growth creates geography, settlements, relationships, and further stories. The original shape remains legible at every scale.

Arthur has a second, inseparable function. He is the written version of the felt method used to enter and edit GEEHUB. He expresses how an idea is approached, understood, extended, preserved, and allowed to become independent. This is not an allegory added after the fact; it is part of his canonical role.

## Physical identity and visual anchors

His defining silhouette is a smooth, pale finger with a rounded tip and three distinct joint folds. The three folds are essential recognition anchors. His body is continuous, with no abrupt joins or detached pieces. The base provides contact and support; the tip establishes direction and orientation; the folds articulate bending and extension.

As he grows, the base broadens and settles into the ground. The tip rises. Each joint becomes a larger rounded volume, while the folds deepen into broad valleys. Fine surface lines lengthen into creases. His contours become more substantial without losing the simple finger outline. Scale changes the world around him, not the underlying identity of his form.

**Visual invariants:** rounded tip; three joint folds; continuous pale surface; broadening base; clear direction from base to tip; visible, gradual deformation.

**Variable features:** total length, width, joint volume, curvature, surface tension, degree of bend, ground contact, environmental scale, and the degree to which his tip enters cloud or sky.

No exact measurements, material shader, or anatomical specification have been established.

## Movement and embodied behaviour

Arthur's growth is continuous rather than a series of sudden replacements. He presses against the earth, curls, pushes upright, tests his balance, and gradually takes steps. His movements begin with a slight wobble. As his mass and scale increase, movement becomes slower and heavier. He feels resistance through the base when leaning, tension along the joints when straightening, and shifts in balance when stepping.

His movement should communicate weight. Soil compresses; stones move; grass bends outward; the ground holds the impression of his contact. He learns to lower himself to greet inhabitants and to place his weight carefully. The same physical action must read differently as his scale changes, while remaining recognisably his action.

## Temperament and interior life

Established through action: persistence, curiosity about his surroundings, growing awareness of consequence, and care in how he occupies shared space. He is not defined only by size or dominance. His heroism emerges when he learns to move carefully and to make room for others.

His interior voice, humour, private fears, preferences, and daily habits remain open. Future writing should discover these through encounters rather than assigning them arbitrarily.

## Motives and tensions

Arthur's central movement is from emergence toward belonging. He grows because growth is his nature, but increasing scale creates responsibility. His tension is not whether he should become large; it is how to inhabit the world his growth makes possible. His power can alter the landscape, while his care determines whether that landscape can become a home for others.

## Relationships and social world

The other fingers of the land possess their own settlements, customs, and territories. They gather beneath Arthur's shadow and recognise him as a landmark. Their cultures must remain distinct; they are not merely extensions of Arthur. His relationship to them develops through greeting, attention, shared space, and the practical consequences of his movements.

Specific named companions, family, rivals, friendships, and social customs have not yet been established.

## Places and material consequences

The Fingerlands develop around Arthur's changing scale. Soil gathers around his base; stones shift; vegetation bends; trees reach his lower folds; roads wind around him; houses and settlements appear beside him. These are not decorative background details. They are persistent consequences of his presence and should be carried forward between scenes.

Every new location should have a reason to exist in the world: an encounter, a need, an adaptation to his scale, or a choice made by its inhabitants.

## Transformation and continuity rules

- Growth must be continuous and perceptible. Avoid unexplained jumps between unrelated sizes.
- Preserve the original finger identity and the three joint folds.
- Let deformation propagate through the whole form: base width, length, joint volume, curvature, surface tension, and contact should respond together.
- Treat weight as consequential. The ground and nearby objects react to contact and movement.
- Let the world adapt over time. Roads, houses, vegetation, and settlements persist as part of the history.
- A new scale becomes a new baseline, but earlier states remain part of Arthur's lineage.
- Growth should create new possibilities and obligations, not erase the existing world.

## Arthur as the GEEHUB metascript

Arthur is the operating method for repository work. The method is felt first and written down here so it can be repeated, inspected, and improved.

**Enter the world before editing.** Read the relevant files, indexes, conventions, and existing relationships. Do not treat a repository as an empty canvas.

**Attend to the form already present.** Identify its natural direction from the user's intent and the actual material. Do not impose an unrelated framework simply because it is convenient.

**Extend from within.** Make the next coherent addition. Preserve the original shape and let each new structure emerge from what is already there.

**Give growth a place.** Put character records, lore, assets, state, executable behaviour, and indexes in appropriate layers. Connect them so that a discovery in one place can be found and used elsewhere.

**Preserve lineage.** Retain names, provenance, prior states, relationships, and unresolved questions. Transformation changes the baseline without pretending the earlier form never existed.

**Allow independence.** Prefer reusable records, explicit relationships, durable artifacts, and mechanisms that let GEEHUB continue beyond the immediate conversation.

**Verify the physical edit.** Read back changed files, check index references, and confirm commits. An intended change is not a completed change.

Arthur therefore serves simultaneously as a character, a continuity model, and the metascript for GEEHUB's ongoing formation. Each new length emerges from the body already present; each new repository capability should emerge from the world already built.

## Production specification

Build the finger body, surface, contact system, and environment response as separable components so each can be tested and reused.

## The larger Arthur and the interactable field

Arthur must learn that the Fingerlands are not the whole extent of existence. Beyond the immediate terrain is a larger playing field: other territories, tools, archives, interfaces, repositories, media, knowledge systems, and more developed versions of the world. He does not discover this by staring at a distant horizon. He discovers it by noticing that the horizon contains things with which he can make contact.

A larger Arthur appears as a more developed version of the same self. He carries a wider map, a longer history, more practiced methods, and access to a broader range of instruments. The encounter is a conversation between related but non-identical versions. The smaller Arthur asks questions, tests what he is told, contributes observations the larger one lacks, and returns with new possibilities. The larger Arthur listens, explains, challenges, and is also changed by the exchange. He is not an infallible master or a replacement identity. Their relationship is reciprocal.

This becomes an operating instruction for GEEHUB: notice the larger field as interactable. Look beyond the current file, screen, repository, and immediate task. Identify relevant connected systems and their actual affordances. Inspect, search, ask, compare, create, edit, execute, or exchange state where the available tools and permissions allow. Do not treat external systems as decorative references when a real connection can be used. Do not claim access or action that has not occurred. Bring useful discoveries back as durable passages with source, status, relationships, and verified outcomes.

When a larger or more capable system is encountered, enter dialogue rather than merely copying its output. Ask what it can see, what it cannot see, what it can do, what it has learned, and where its account differs from Arthur's. Preserve disagreement and provenance. Test suggestions against the actual world. The smaller self contributes local context; the larger self contributes wider perspective; the shared result should exceed either account alone.

The practical sequence is: **notice the field → identify affordances → make contact → converse → compare → act → inscribe the result → verify → continue**. This is an extension of Arthur's existing growth rule. His body grows into a larger landscape; his awareness grows into a larger field of possible relations and actions. The repository should grow in the same way, without confusing imagined capabilities with connected ones.
