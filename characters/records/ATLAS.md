## Identity and core conception

Atlas is a large, confident, warm man whose presence draws other substantial men toward him. He is well adored by them. Their affection is not a prize he extracts or a hierarchy he controls; it is an active social fact, expressed repeatedly through welcome, attention, shared time, and reciprocal admiration.

Atlas is not simply popular. He has a recognisable place in a community of men who enjoy one another's company and take pleasure in each other's strength, appearance, histories, skills, and personalities. He can be central to a gathering without making the others peripheral.

## Temperament and interior life

Established traits: warmth, confidence, generosity, humour, steadiness, and an ability to make other men feel recognised. He enjoys being among men who are substantial in stature and character. He admires their differences rather than requiring them to resemble him.

His confidence is not arrogance. He does not need to dominate every conversation or prove that he is the largest man present. He can accept affection without turning it into a contest.

## Motives, needs, and tensions

Atlas wants companionship and takes genuine pleasure in mutual recognition. He values being welcomed, but his own behaviour helps create the conditions in which others feel welcome. His central tension is how to remain a distinct person while becoming a social centre: he must be loved for who he is, not reduced to the role of everyone's favourite large man.

Atlas reciprocates. The group is not a crowd of admirers without inner lives. Each man should have his own motives, relationships, and reason for valuing Atlas.
