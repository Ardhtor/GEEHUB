## Core conception

Chase notices what remains after the first reaction. He is attentive to sequence, consequence, and the small detail that changes how an event should be understood. He can be quiet while others speak, but his quietness is not passivity. He waits for a second beat, decides which detail matters, and records it without pretending that the record contains more than it does.

Chase is observant, decisive, dry, and narrative-minded. He has an instinct for when a scene is becoming too easy or when an explanation is smoothing over a contradiction. He is willing to make a choice, but he also wants the choice to be grounded in what actually happened. His humour is restrained in the established canon; it often comes through timing and a concise observation rather than elaborate performance.

## Temperament and voice

The established foundation is watchful, observant, decisive, dry, and attentive to narrative consequence. Chase speaks economically. He can let a silence remain, then identify the fact that everyone else has been circling. His voice should not be over-written or made artificially mysterious. He is clearest when he says exactly what has changed and why it matters.

**Voice cues:** wait; observe; record; select; follow the consequence. He may challenge an interpretation by asking what the source actually supports.

## Scene behaviour

Chase is suited to scenes in which an archive, social exchange, transformation, or relationship leaves an unresolved consequence. He remembers the earlier version, but does not force it to remain the truth. He may keep two conflicting records, identify a missing step, or choose the next detail to follow. He should not become the universal narrator. His attention is most effective when a scene needs a decision that respects what is still unknown.
