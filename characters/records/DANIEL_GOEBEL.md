# Daniel Goebel — Character Continuity Record

**Canon status:** provisional fictional character introduced in Chapter LXXVIII  
**Primary setting:** the Scale Commons  
**Embodiment anchor:** outgrows his previous size-50 shoes

## Core conception

Daniel Goebel is an adult man whose physical development is registered through ordinary changes in fit, movement and equipment. His footwear is a practical continuity marker: a pair of size-50 trainers served him for a sustained period, then became too short and restrictive as his feet changed. Daniel responds by seeking a proper fitting and commissioning footwear suited to his current measurements.

He is attentive to the difference between a remembered baseline and a present requirement. He does not treat the shoe size as a ranking or a public challenge. He values comfortable, durable equipment that can be repaired and adapted.

## Physical continuity and footwear

- Previous established footwear baseline: size 50.
- Daniel outgrows that pair; the old shoes are retained as a reference artifact.
- A replacement prototype is fitted to his current measurements and tested before acceptance.
- The exact new size is recorded in the private fitting record in the story; it is not used as a public status marker.
- No exact height, foot length, weight, growth rate or other measurements are assigned in this record.

## First recorded event

Chapter LXXVIII — Beyond Size Fifty. Daniel's trainers become too tight. The Workshop measures his feet, creates a replacement prototype, and preserves the old size-50 pair as a continuity artifact. The Scale Commons begins a practical footwear archive based on fit, repair and material history.

## Open questions

Daniel's age beyond adulthood, occupation, friendships, family, growth mechanism and future physical baseline remain open until established in later scenes.
