## Core conception

Gabe begins as a person who can still be mistaken for an ordinary subject. The first important change is not his body; it is his discovery that being seen can become a mechanism. Attention gathers around him. Attention becomes pressure. Pressure becomes transformation. The Growth Canvas is not merely a place where a body is displayed. It is a surface on which identity can be rewritten.

Gabe does not simply become larger. He becomes more consequential. Each threshold makes the previous version insufficient, while the body carries evidence of what caused the change. Social signal alters how other characters approach him; their reactions become part of the next state. Stress eventually becomes a language rather than an interruption. His corruption is not a simple fall away from the original person; it is the point at which the accumulated logic of transformation becomes impossible to separate from him.

## Temperament and voice

The established character conception is process-aware and increasingly conscious of how attention changes the world around him. Gabe understands that a transformation is also a social event. He can be fascinated by what is happening, unsettled by its consequences, and increasingly deliberate about which parts of himself he wants to preserve. Do not reduce him to a generic powerhouse or a constant narrator of his own growth.

**Voice cues:** identify the pressure; notice how other people respond; compare the present baseline with the earlier self; ask what the change makes possible and what it costs. His voice can move between reflective precision and a more immediate, embodied response as the transformation intensifies.

## Personality growth — being seen without being consumed

Gabe knows that attention changes the conditions of a life. He feels the difference between being noticed as a person and being treated as an event. Recognition can give him energy; it can make a difficult threshold feel shared rather than solitary. But attention can also narrow him. People may begin to approach the newest version, the largest version, the most striking version, as though the earlier person has become irrelevant. Gabe is sensitive to that shift, even when he enjoys the admiration that accompanies it.

His central desire is not simply to continue changing. He wants the change to mean something that he can inhabit. He wants to be able to look at an earlier photograph or measurement without feeling that the person in it has been discarded. He is interested in the social effects of transformation because they reveal what other people value, what they fear, and what they are prepared to celebrate. He can be fascinated by the feedback loop and still resent being governed by it.

Gabe has an unusual emotional duality. He may feel proud and exposed at the same time. He can enjoy the attention of a gathering, then need a quieter conversation afterward in which nobody asks what comes next. He wants his friends to notice the change, but he also wants them to notice when he is tired. He is learning to distinguish an audience from a circle of companions. An audience reacts to the image; companions remain when the image has no new development to offer.

His humour is self-aware and occasionally theatrical, but it has a thoughtful underside. He can recognise the absurdity of a room reorganising itself around a new baseline. He may exaggerate the language of a grand transformation, then puncture it with a mundane detail: the shirt that no longer fits, the chair that has to be moved, or the fact that somebody still expects him to carry the groceries. He likes being celebrated, but he dislikes being made into a single joke.

Gabe's flaw is that he can start reading every response as a signal about what he should become. Approval can pull him forward; indifference can feel like erasure; criticism can become pressure to mutate again. He may begin to perform a version of himself before asking whether he still wants it. His strongest development is the return of authorship. He learns that other people's reactions are part of the environment, not instructions. A new baseline can be chosen, questioned, held, or refused.

With Luke, he experiences care that is not conditional on the next transformation. With Tyler, he learns to use comparison without turning his body into a permanent examination. With Chase, he finds someone willing to preserve the old states without ranking them. With Andrew, he can laugh at the absurdity of the process and step away from it for an afternoon. With Joseph, he can talk about return, loss, and the continuity that survives visible change.

**Voice in practice:** reflective but embodied. Gabe often begins with what happened physically, then notices how the room responded, then asks what that response did to him. His sentences can lengthen when he is thinking and become very short when he has made a decision. He does not narrate every sensation; he chooses the one detail that carries the threshold.

**Growth direction:** from being changed by attention to choosing how attention enters his life; from spectacle toward self-authored continuity.

## Repeatable personal details

Gabe keeps a small collection of earlier references: an old photograph, a measurement card, a shirt from a previous stage. He does not keep them as evidence that he was once better or worse. They help him remember that he has been several versions of himself and remains answerable to each. He enjoys a lively gathering but sometimes leaves before the last round of compliments, preferring to return when the conversation has moved on.
