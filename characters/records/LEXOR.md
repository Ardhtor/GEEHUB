# LEXOR

Status: introduced in the Cabin chronicle; further history unresolved.

Lexor is a god who keeps the names that were almost given and then taken back. He arrives at the Cabin in a long coat the colour of wet slate, carrying a glass jar filled with water and a perfectly round black pebble that remains suspended at its centre. His voice is mild and almost apologetic. He has spent a long time practicing how not to be noticed.

Lexor says the jar remembers the sound a name makes when someone almost says it and then decides not to. He describes it as personal rather than networked. He wants somewhere to set it down without having to explain every silence it contains. His stated history is that he was once asked to name something that should never have been named; he did so, then tried to take the name back. The jar is what remained. The exact name, the thing named, and the jar's deeper function are not established.

At the Cabin, Lexor accepts Ursalon's welcome, leaves the jar on the mantel between the crow and Privathor's brass key, and departs by choice. His parting words are, "The path is lit." He does not return the following morning. The household allows the object to remain without demanding its explanation.

Continuity rules: preserve Lexor's careful, restrained manner; do not invent the withdrawn name or the thing it named; do not make him a captive of the jar; and do not turn the jar's unresolved function into a confirmed mechanism without a later story event.
