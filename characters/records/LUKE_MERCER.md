# Luke Mercer — Character Record

**Record ID:** `character-luke-mercer`  
**Canon status:** active fictional character; first appearance in Chapter LXXVI  
**Role:** first baseman; member of Andrew's baseball team  
**Distinction:** this is a second character named Luke, separate from the central Luke of the House and Institute narratives. Use **Luke Mercer** or **Mercer** whenever both men are present.

## Core conception

Luke Mercer is an adult baseball player on Andrew's team. He plays first base and is known for reliable hands, a broad receiving stance, and a calm understanding of how a play develops across the infield. His work is not flashy. He gives an infielder a visible target, keeps his feet active, and makes difficult throws easier to finish. He is particularly good at reading a bad hop early enough to adjust without making the adjustment look dramatic.

Mercer is a substantial, athletic man whose body has continued to grow alongside the other men. His build is broad through the torso and strong through the legs, with forearms shaped by years of catching and controlling hard throws. His growth has begun to change the fit of his uniform and the depth of his first-base crouch. He remains coordinated and mobile; he does not confuse size with range or strength with good fielding. Exact height, weight, measurements, batting average, and throwing speed remain unestablished.

## Temperament

Mercer is composed, dryly funny, and attentive. He is not quiet because he has nothing to say; he prefers to see the play before commenting on it. He has a practical sense of humour and is particularly good at puncturing Andrew's more elaborate theories without dismissing the curiosity underneath them. He can be competitive, but he dislikes turning routine practice into a public ranking. He values a teammate who makes the next play easier.

He is comfortable with Andrew's eccentricity because he knows the difference between Andrew being distracted and Andrew noticing something useful. Mercer may initially give him a look, ask one direct question, and then help test the idea. He is not an automatic audience for every tangent. Their friendship has enough history to include disagreement without requiring either man to win.

## Baseball identity

- **Team:** Andrew's current baseball team; the team name and league are not yet established.
- **Position:** first baseman.
- **Playing style:** steady receiving, strong footwork around the bag, dependable hands, sensible positioning, and an ability to turn difficult throws into ordinary outs.
- **Batting:** left-handed at the plate is not established; leave handedness open until shown in a scene.
- **Equipment:** first-baseman's mitt, batting helmet, practice jersey, and field shoes. Exact brands, number, and colour scheme remain open.
- **Team habits:** arrives prepared, checks the infield dirt near first base, and helps make sure the next player has a clear target.

Mercer understands that a first baseman is involved in almost every infield routine even when the ball is not hit to him. His job is not merely to wait at the bag. He reads the hitter, checks the runner, adjusts his depth, gives the thrower a target, and prepares for the possibility that the ball will arrive badly. This practical attentiveness is central to his character.

## Relationships

**Andrew:** Teammate and friend. Mercer knows Andrew as a strong, inventive player whose mind frequently follows a strange detail away from the immediate drill. Mercer can tease him about the notebook, but he respects Andrew's athletic seriousness and unusual observational skill. Their shared baseball language lets them disagree quickly and get back to playing.

**Luke (central character):** Another man with the same first name, not a relative and not a duplicate identity. Their first shared scene is lightly comic because teammates and friends have to specify which Luke they mean. Mercer does not compete with the central Luke for narrative importance; he has his own place on Andrew's team and his own relationships there.

**Tyler:** Mercer appreciates Tyler's attention to mechanics and measurements, especially when it improves field safety and the consistency of a drill. He does not need every practice to become a formal test.

**The rest of Andrew's team:** Mercer is an established teammate with ordinary shared history, nicknames, routines, and responsibilities. Develop the other players individually as they enter the story rather than treating the team as a faceless crowd.

## Voice

Mercer speaks in compact, practical sentences, often with dry humour. He is capable of warmth without making a speech about it. He asks direct questions when Andrew's reasoning gets too far ahead of the group. He can be blunt, but he is not cruel. A recurring distinction in his dialogue is that he wants the play to work before he wants to explain why it worked.

**Sample authored line:** “You can listen to the fence after we finish the inning. Right now, give me a throw I can catch.”
