## Identity and continuity

Mara Venn is an adult fictional woman whose present life includes large-scale travel and the inspection of roads and structures used by people of extraordinary size. She is already a giant when she enters the Scale Commons and continues to grow during the events of Chapter LXXIX. Her exact height, age, family history, cultural background, and longer biography have not yet been established. Do not fill those gaps with plausible-sounding facts and then treat them as canon.

Her scale is part of her daily life, not her entire identity. She is observant, patient, direct, and attentive to the people responsible for the structures she uses. She waits for signals, asks for measurements, and does not assume that a bridge is safe merely because a crowd wants it to be. She is capable of accepting help without surrendering her own judgement. The western road matters to her because she needs a reliable route through the Commons; she does not arrive to become a public spectacle.

## Temperament and working method

Mara approaches a place by reading its routes and constraints. She pays attention to support walls, marked lanes, signal flags, joints, load paths, and the people who maintain them. She does not rush a crossing to prove that she can make it. When the western bridge is reported as shifted, she waits for inspection, asks for the earlier reading, and accepts the temporary closure.

Her speech is clear and unhurried. She does not require admiration, but she does expect accurate treatment. She can understand that people may feel unsettled by her size without accepting responsibility for every feeling her presence produces. She distinguishes a person's private reaction from the way he chooses to treat her.
