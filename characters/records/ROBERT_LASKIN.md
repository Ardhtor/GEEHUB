# Robert Laskin — Character Record

**Record ID:** `character-robert-laskin`  
**Canon status:** active Book II resident; personal history and temperament remain open  
**Role:** resident of the paired residential country within Kirk's anatomy  
**Residence:** Laskin Mansion

## Established direction

Robert Laskin wants to live permanently within Kirk's extraordinary anatomical country. His initial proposal was not for a temporary camp or a novelty room, but for a proper home with lighting, a library, a recreation hall, and a view of the surrounding landscape. Book II, Chapter V develops this proposal into Laskin Mansion.

The mansion has a broad entry court, a two-level library gallery, a miniature theatre, a recreation hall, sheltered gardens, a viewing room, a second staircase, and a smaller private book room. These are established residential features, not evidence of an otherwise unspecified biography or personality.

## Residence and relationships

Laskin Mansion stands within the paired residential country described in [Book II — Local Spatial Atlas](../../novel/chapters/BOOK_II_SPATIAL_ATLAS.md). It is one of four independent estates around the shared green. The exact position within the paired chambers remains unmeasured until a later survey. Robert's residence does not confer ownership over Kirk, automatic access to Kirk's private rooms, or a rank above other residents.

The mansion is established with Kirk's knowledge and offer; Robert chooses whether to accept it. Visits between residents remain by invitation, and Robert's own routines and private life should continue when the narrative follows other characters.

## Chapter VI — the viewing arch

Robert develops a screenable viewing arch at Laskin Mansion, allowing the Long Return to be watched from the estate when residents choose to gather. The screen can close, so the game does not turn the shared green into a permanent performance. Robert also proposes a miniature-theatre projection with the route map beside the image. His mansion's library, theatre, recreation hall, gardens, and viewing room continue to acquire practical uses through resident life.
