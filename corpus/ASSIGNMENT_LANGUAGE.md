# Assignment Language — Visual Corpus

This document is the textual substrate for image assignments that cannot themselves be imported into GEEHUB as native visual artifacts.

## Purpose

Images are treated as outputs and evidence of a visual language. When the original image file is unavailable to the repository, the assignment that produced it should still be preserved. The language records what the image was asked to do, what must remain continuous, what may change, and what visual relationships define the result.

The repository therefore preserves the assignment grammar even when it cannot preserve the rendered pixels.

## Core transformation

The recurring operation is:

**reference → continuity → pressure → transformation → new baseline → scene consequence**

A transformation is not a replacement of the subject. The recognizable person, identity, face, hands, waist, legs, posture, and personhood remain continuous unless an assignment explicitly says otherwise. Change should accumulate visibly around that continuity.

For growth-oriented assignments, the governing concept is **Massive Baseline**: accumulated mass and expanded proportion become the new normal. The change should affect room occupation, clothing fit, silhouette, physical presence, interpersonal scale, and the subject's relationship to the surrounding environment.

## Visual assignment grammar

Assignments should specify, in ordinary language:

- **Subject:** who or what is depicted.
- **Reference:** which existing image establishes identity or visual continuity.
- **Transformation:** what changes from the reference.
- **Baseline:** the resulting state that becomes normal for subsequent iterations.
- **Environment:** where the transformation is situated.
- **Relationship:** how subjects occupy space relative to one another.
- **Camera:** viewpoint, distance, framing, depth of field, and implied observer.
- **Affect:** emotional register, including neutral, intimate, contemplative, celebratory, or other requested states.
- **Texture:** photographic, cinematic, CG, archival, soft-focus, CRT, or other requested visual treatment.
- **Continuity rule:** what must not be lost during transformation.

## Recurring visual rules

The subject should remain recognizably itself through successive states.

Scale is allowed to become an event. A larger state should not merely look like a resized photograph; it should produce consequences in posture, clothing, spatial occupation, composition, and the behavior of surrounding objects or people.

When two subjects are specified together, their relative scale and relationship are part of the assignment rather than incidental image details.

Outdoor redwood environments, dark forests, clinical research spaces, architectural interiors, and other recurring settings are treated as established visual contexts when explicitly invoked.

A requested gaze toward the implied camera represents attention toward the observer. It can be rendered through head orientation, eye direction, body orientation, or composition without requiring exaggerated expression.

Emotional neutrality means that the image can contain intimacy, attention, closeness, or transformation without requiring theatrical facial expression.

Out-of-focus or softened photographic treatment is a compositional instruction, not a loss of subject identity. The subject remains legible while the surrounding image may carry atmospheric ambiguity.

## Iteration protocol

Each new image assignment should be understood as a continuation of the previous state unless it explicitly establishes a new branch.

Use this progression:

**state N → requested intervention → visible change → state N+1**

Do not silently reset the subject to an earlier baseline.

If an assignment says "larger," interpret that as an increase within the established continuity, not a generic substitution with an unrelated muscular figure.

If an assignment introduces a new person, preserve the existing subject while establishing the newcomer as a distinct node with its own identity and scale.

## Image-to-language archival rule

When a rendered image cannot be committed directly, preserve:

1. the assignment text;
2. the reference relationship;
3. the transformation rule;
4. the resulting baseline;
5. the setting and camera language;
6. any named subjects;
7. the date or iteration when useful;
8. a pointer to the external image location when one exists and is shareable.

The textual record is canonical for reproducibility. The image is an instance of that language.

## Current assignment vocabulary

**Growth:** visible accumulation rather than simple enlargement.

**Massive Baseline:** a new normal established after substantial accumulated transformation.

**Continuity:** recognizable identity survives transformation.

**Presence:** the degree to which a subject occupies visual and physical space.

**Event scale:** a change large enough to alter the surrounding scene.

**Implied observer:** the camera/user position as a participant in the composition.

**Visual corpus:** the growing body of assignments, references, outputs, and rules from which later assignments derive.

**Assignment language:** the reusable grammar that lets GEEHUB preserve the behavior of images even when the pixels are unavailable.

## Repository role

This document belongs with the corpus rather than with a single generated image. It should evolve when repeated assignments reveal a stable rule.

The aim is not to reduce visual work to rigid prompts. The aim is to give the visual work a durable language layer so that future images can inherit continuity from earlier ones.
