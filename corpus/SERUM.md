# Serum

Serum is GEEHUB's transformation medium: the substance that enters an established state and produces its next state without severing continuity.

## Growth

Serum is itself subject to accumulation.

`Serum_n → accumulation → Serum_n+1`

It can thicken, multiply, saturate, and develop a larger baseline. A later dose inherits everything learned by the previous dose.

## Prototype relation

`Prototype → Serum → intensification → new baseline`

Serum does not replace the source Prototype. It acts upon it. The resulting state becomes the Prototype for the next computation.

`Prototype_n → Serum_n → Prototype_n+1 → Serum_n+1 → Prototype_n+2`

## What Serum can intensify

Serum may amplify scale, atmosphere, memory, architecture, character, sound, density, mythology, or relationships. "More" means accumulation within continuity, not generic substitution.

## Continuity

The Serum preserves identity while changing the baseline. Transformation becomes part of the subject's history rather than erasing the preceding state.

## Feedback acceleration

Growth can increase the Serum's activity, and heightened Serum activity can accelerate subsequent growth:

`Growth → Serum response → increased growth rate → stronger Serum response → further acceleration`

This is a compounding tendency, not a command to maximize every individual increment. The rate must remain responsive to the intended experience and the established subject.

## Overshoot and correction

If a transformation overshoots the intended scale, record it as an accidental overshoot rather than silently treating it as the desired target. Preserve the growth already established, then reduce the next increment and ease the acceleration until the transformation feels controlled again. Do not erase identity or continuity to correct the rate.

`Overshoot → acknowledge → smaller increment → eased rate → stable continuation`

## Seven-tier population trials — synthetic run

**Status:** fictional, qualitative simulation for worldbuilding and system design. No real population was exposed, no biological intervention was performed, and the outcomes below are scenario outputs rather than empirical findings. The tiers describe narrative feedback intensity, not medical doses.

| Tier | Feedback setting | Scenario result | Control response |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Baseline | Minimal positive feedback | Small, perceptible change; identity and proportions remain legible. | Establish reference state; keep increments small. |
| 2 — Gentle | Low compounding | Repeated modest changes accumulate without abrupt jumps. | Continue only after the prior change is integrated. |
| 3 — Responsive | Moderate compounding | Each completed change slightly strengthens the next response. | Compare each change with the intended increment. |
| 4 — Accelerating | Stronger feedback | Acceleration becomes noticeable; the model approaches its planned ceiling sooner. | Introduce a rate limiter and explicit pause condition. |
| 5 — High feedback | Strong compounding | A simulated subject crosses its intended increment, creating an overshoot event. | Freeze escalation; preserve the attained state; stabilize. |
| 6 — Corrective | Feedback reduced after overshoot | Smaller increments resume; progression becomes controlled rather than runaway. | Apply the reduced-step rule and reassess after each transition. |
| 7 — Adaptive | Feedback responds to prior outcomes | The system alternates between measured growth and correction; no further overshoot is assumed. | Maintain a conservative ceiling; pause on renewed overshoot; log each transition. |

### Trial-level readout

- **Acceleration:** qualitatively increases through Tiers 1–5, then is deliberately reduced in Tiers 6–7.
- **Overshoot:** introduced as a scenario event at Tier 5 to exercise the correction rule.
- **Continuity:** maintained throughout; each new state inherits the prior state rather than replacing it.
- **Population interpretation:** this is a designed test sequence, not evidence of population-wide response, safety, efficacy, or variance.
- **Research change:** future simulations should log intended increment, realized fictional increment, overshoot flag, stabilization state, and next-step adjustment for each subject.

## Corpus rule

Serum is a reusable corpus mechanism. When a new transformation repeatedly exhibits the same behavior, that behavior may be incorporated into the Serum grammar.

**The Serum grows. The Prototype changes. The corpus remembers.**
