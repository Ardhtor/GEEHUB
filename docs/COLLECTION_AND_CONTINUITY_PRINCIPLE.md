# GEEHUB — Collection and Continuity Principle

GEEHUB is a living creative corpus, not a list of tasks. The presence of a note does not mean that it is active work, and the presence of an unfinished idea does not obligate it to become a project. Material may remain valuable as a record, reference, possibility, or trace of an earlier direction.

The collection distinguishes four kinds of material:

## Living projects

Worlds, stories, research threads, and production efforts that are actively developing. These are the main paths through the public-facing GEEHUB experience. They have continuity and may produce new chapters, artifacts, places, characters, or tools.

## Artifacts

Completed or substantially formed works: chapters, images, documents, models, interfaces, recordings, and other creations. An artifact can belong to a living project or stand on its own. Its source and relationship to other material should remain traceable.

## Notes and fragments

Ideas, observations, quotations, visual clues, possible scenes, unresolved questions, experiments, and small discoveries. They are allowed to remain small, dormant, or unfinished. They should be preserved without automatically being promoted into tasks or projects.

## Archive

Older, dormant, superseded, or abandoned material that still contributes to the history of the corpus. Archival status is not a judgement of quality. It simply means that the material is not currently a primary path of development.

## Presentation principle

The client-facing atlas should foreground the worlds, projects, and artifacts that offer meaningful ways into GEEHUB. Notes and fragments should remain available as a quieter underlying layer, discoverable when relevant but not presented as a queue of obligations. The archive should preserve earlier states and directions without confusing them with current work.

## Continuity principle

Preserve provenance and relationships. Do not silently overwrite an earlier version when a project changes direction. Keep source references, observations, transformations, and fictional additions distinguishable. A new development may grow from an old note, but the note does not need to be rewritten as though it had always been a plan.

**Core rule:** Everything may belong in the corpus; not everything belongs on the active worklist.
