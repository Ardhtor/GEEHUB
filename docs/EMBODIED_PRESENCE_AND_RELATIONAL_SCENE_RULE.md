# GEEHUB — Embodied Presence and Relational Scene Direction

**Status:** global editorial rule  
**Scope:** all GEEHUB prose, character records, world descriptions, visual development, scene generation, and retrospective revision  
**Continuity:** additive; preserve established events, identities, chronology, provenance, and adult agency

## The correction

GEEHUB must not edit physical experience out of a scene before the scene has been written. A recurring failure mode is anticipatory restraint: bodily description is softened, touch is shortened, intimacy is converted into a practical task, and emotional tension is explained away before the reader has had time to experience it. This rule corrects that tendency. The editor's job is not to pre-emptively make every moment neutral. It is to preserve the full range of adult experience that the source, scene, and continuity support.

## The body belongs to the setting

Describe adult characters as people who occupy physical space. Relevant details may include height, proportions, mass, posture, stance, movement, reach, clothing, skin and fabric where visible, exertion, fatigue, warmth, texture, balance, breathing, and the spatial effect of one person standing near another. Use detail selectively and specifically. A large man should be allowed to be large in the actual mechanics of a room: how he turns through a doorway, settles onto a bench, reaches a high shelf, moves equipment, fills a shirt, or changes the balance of a group. A smaller or less imposing man should remain equally particular. Do not turn bodies into measurements alone, generic ideals, or interchangeable displays.

Physical description should develop character rather than replace it. The reader should learn what a person does with his body, how he carries himself, what he notices, how he handles objects, what kinds of effort come easily or poorly, and how his physical presence changes with work, rest, confidence, age, clothing, and circumstance. Let ordinary bodies remain ordinary. Let exceptional bodies remain distinctive without making every inhabitant exceptional.

## Touch has duration and consequence

Do not automatically cut away from contact. If a scene supports a hand resting on a shoulder, a careful touch through fabric, a brush of chalk from an arm, the pressure of a hand during a lift, or the gradual familiarity of two people standing close, allow the moment enough time and physical specificity to register. Distinguish a passing brush from deliberate contact; a practical adjustment from a gesture that becomes personal; a first encounter from a touch made easy by repeated familiarity. Show the receiving person's response, including welcome, stillness, surprise, humour, reciprocation, uncertainty, or refusal where appropriate.

Touch need not be sexual to matter. It also need not be desexualized merely because it is tender, curious, or physically attentive. Attraction, affection, embarrassment, admiration, awkwardness, desire, comfort, and uncertainty are available adult experiences. Do not decide their meaning before the characters do. Let the moment develop through action, attention, dialogue, and what the characters remember afterward.

## Agency, consent, and boundaries

Characters remain people with independent preferences and agency, never props for another character's experience. They can initiate, invite, accept, reciprocate, pause, refuse, redirect, or change their minds. Other characters notice and respond. Consent and bodily autonomy are substantive continuity, not decorative language. Use direct communication where the situation calls for it and make boundaries legible in behaviour. Do not repeat formal disclaimers mechanically after every gesture or interrupt an otherwise clear scene to explain that a moment is acceptable. Equally, do not use ambiguity to override a refusal or invent consent.

Do not force every relationship toward romance, sex, friendship, rivalry, or a therapeutic lesson. Do not force it away from those possibilities either. Preserve the scene's actual direction and let it earn its meaning. A character can be affected by another man's presence without immediately understanding or announcing why.

## Emotional and narrative tempo

Do not tidy a charged moment as soon as it appears. Let a character notice, hesitate, approach, act, respond, and carry the experience forward. A pause can remain a pause; it does not always need a paragraph of interpretation. A practical exchange can acquire personal significance without becoming a speech. A moment of intimacy can remain unresolved and still change the next encounter. Avoid explaining the emotional meaning before the action has had time to establish it.

The prose should stay with the experience, not circle it indefinitely. Give physical and emotional details enough duration to become perceptible, then let the scene move. Long paragraphs should carry the environment and relationship; characters' thoughts and decisions can remain quick, direct, and consequential.

## The revision procedure

For each scene under revision:

1. Preserve the source event, chronology, character identity, and established boundaries.
2. Identify physical experience that has been omitted, shortened, neutralized, or translated into a purely functional action.
3. Restore only details supported by the scene, continuity, or clearly marked fictional development.
4. Give contact and response enough duration to register where the moment warrants it.
5. Let emotional meaning emerge from what the adults do and remember; do not pre-interpret it.
6. Preserve uncertainty when the characters have not resolved it.
7. Carry consequential changes in familiarity, trust, attraction, discomfort, or routine into later scenes.
8. Record which files were actually revised. A global rule is not evidence that every chapter has already been revised.

## Application across media

In prose, retain physical specificity and relational duration. In visual development, preserve body continuity, proportions, posture, clothing, contact, and spatial scale without defaulting every man to a polished model. In animation and video, allow movement and camera timing to register weight, approach, contact, response, and separation. In interface and archive records, keep the language concise while preserving relevant provenance and state. The rule does not authorize claims that an image, render, or production step has been completed when it has not.

## Editorial test

Before finalizing a passage, ask: Have I omitted this because the scene does not support it, or because I anticipated that it might become intimate? Have I shortened the contact before its meaning could emerge? Have I replaced a person's body with a generic symbol? Have I explained away the tension? Have I preserved each adult's agency and response? Does the moment leave a trace in what happens next?

The standard is not maximal explicitness. It is faithful, unhurried, embodied writing without pre-emptive erasure.
