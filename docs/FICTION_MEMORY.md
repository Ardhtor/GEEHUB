# GEEHUB Fiction Memory

## Core Memory

GEEHUB should remember the fiction as a living archive-world, not as a conventional linear story.

The computer is primarily an archive, record keeper, terminal, and inhabited memory-space. Its purpose is to preserve encounters, images, characters, places, transformations, passages, artifacts, unresolved material, and relationships so that they can be encountered again and recombined.

The world remembers everything that happens to it. Because it remembers, it can change without losing itself.

## Narrative Architecture

The fiction develops through:

mechanism → interface → transformation rule → continuity principle → setting → mythology

The current mechanism is the sensation/memory engine. Encounters become records; records alter associations; altered associations generate new material.

The interface is the Hub/terminal: current state is interactable, a face or character can act as a location anchor, and a Creations space exposes the artifacts being produced.

## Persistent growth and giantness

Growth and giantness are part of the fiction's continuous physical language. In growth-oriented scenes, keep at least one active embodiment of scale present throughout: a man in an ongoing transformation, an established giant whose body changes the dimensions of ordinary life, a scale echo across other men, or a material consequence inherited from an earlier increase. Show height, reach, breadth, proportion, movement, clothing, furniture, architecture, and shared routines responding in a coherent way. A pause in growth is not an absence of giantness; the changed baseline remains part of the room and the relationships. Do not confine the motif to a token mention or one spectacular entrance. Preserve individual variation, agency, and identity while letting physical transformations become real events in the remembered world.

Beefythiq is the grammar of transformation. Growth is not merely enlargement. It is intensification through pressure, stimulus, stress, mutation, and establishment of a new baseline.

Seed → Desire → Growth → Stress → Mutation → Corruption → New Baseline

The central continuity principle is Berit: identity is permitted to persist through transformation.

A character may change radically while remaining recognizably that character. Transformation therefore becomes an event in the archive.

## Collective scale-confusion: the everywhere error

A recurring feature of collective male hysteria is that the men confuse what is happening in their immediate presence with what is happening everywhere. One growing giant becomes, in their shared interpretation, evidence that the whole world is changing. A few scale echoes become proof that every man is transforming. A reinforced doorway becomes a prediction about every building. The claim spreads through conversation and expectation, sometimes faster than the bodies themselves change.

Preserve the distinction between world fact, direct local observation, hearsay, and collective inference. Characters may collapse these categories; the narrative should not. Their mistaken universal certainty can be funny, affectionate, socially contagious, and consequential. Expectation may contribute to the fictional scale echo, but it does not automatically make every claim true. A later discovery may confirm that the phenomenon has spread, but confirmation must arrive through new evidence.

The characteristic loop is: local event → universal claim → expectation everywhere → selective noticing → apparent confirmation → larger universal claim. Keep individual men distinct in how they participate: exaggeration, sincere belief, practical preparation, careful measurement, or scepticism. The giant's physical growth remains active throughout the scene, giving the room a real event around which its increasingly expansive theory can form.


## Relationships as primary memory

For collective adult-male growth fiction, preserve relationships as the central narrative substance. Do not archive only who grew, how much, or what the crowd believed. Record who noticed, who understood, who misunderstood, who stayed, who left, who defended whom, who became jealous, who apologised, who shared the work, and which bonds changed afterward.

Remember relationships as particular and pairwise as well as collective: shared history, private language, habits of care, attraction, trust, rivalry, conflict, repair, promises, and unresolved distance. The central giant has his own distinct bond with each man, while the other men have independent relationships with one another that remain active outside his presence.

Keep the Everywhere Error as a pressure on those bonds. A man's universal claim may be an attempt to share excitement, to win recognition, or to hide uncertainty. A friend's correction may be an act of care rather than opposition. A disagreement about what is happening everywhere should have a relational consequence, not become an extended explanatory detour.

Growth and giantness remain continuous physical realities in these memories. Preserve how a changed body affects shared routines, touch, movement, clothing, furniture, work, and the way two men occupy the same space. The archive should remember not only the new baseline but what the men did for one another as it changed.
