# GEEHUB — Global Environmental Broadcast Revision

## Canonical rule

Apply this rule across the entire hub: every prose line break, paragraph break, section transition, and scene transition should introduce or deepen non-plot information about the world. The primary material is environment: geography, architecture, weather, light, color, atmosphere, spatial depth, surfaces, scale, ambient sound, temperature, distance, machinery, and the physical presence of people within a place.

The prose must not use line breaks merely as plot beats, action steps, or dramatic emphasis. A paragraph may contain action, but its entry, texture, or transition is grounded in the surrounding world. Environmental description is not filler; it is the continuous medium through which the reader perceives change.

## Broadcast voices

The default narration is a broadcast carried by Seth Feroce and Nick Mercx as two distinct co-narrators. Their primary vantage is across the active scene, with the people and environment held in view, and their commentary travels outward toward the viewer. The audience is on the receiving side of the scene; the narrators make the viewer feel present by sharing what can be seen from that position. Use an energetic, conversational fitness-broadcast atmosphere: direct observations, reactions, comparisons of scale, and exchanges that make the environment feel present. Do not claim these are verbatim quotations or reproduce any person's exact distinctive phrasing. Preserve character dialogue as character dialogue; narrator commentary remains commentary.

Seth and Nick point into the shared view. They can address the viewer at selected moments, distinguish their different observations, disagree about a visible detail, or return to an environmental feature as its meaning changes. They are not default omniscient narrators and should not claim access to private thoughts or unseen events. Avoid making the audience a character unless a scene explicitly establishes that role. The broadcast is not merely from within the world: it comes across the world to us.

When mythic figures are present, they are participants in the scene rather than extra announcers. Neptune, Zeus, and God Daddy must retain distinct domains, temperaments, actions, and dialogue. Seth and Nick continue to carry the viewer-facing broadcast; they do not speak for the gods or turn their voices into narration. See [The Lineage Pantheon](../lore/LINEAGE_PANTHEON.md) for the role contract. God Daddy is distinct from the separate archival phenomenon called Big Daddy's Voice.

## Formatting and continuity

The hub-wide paragraph standard is long-form continuity. Write prose in substantial paragraphs that carry a complete movement of thought or experience, generally using several connected sentences before a break. Keep related action, observation, physical detail, memory, and consequence together rather than splitting them into short dramatic lines. Break when the focus or environment meaningfully changes, and use that transition to deepen context. This applies to all prose surfaces, including documentation, lore, research, production records, interface descriptions, captions, and generated narrative. Functional structures such as headings, lists, metadata, code, tables, and step-by-step procedures remain structured where needed; do not disguise prose as a list.



- Use paragraphs as environmental descriptors, not isolated plot beats.
- Let scene transitions travel through changes in light, weather, architecture, sound, terrain, distance, material, or atmosphere.
- Keep the plot and established events intact. This is a presentation-layer revision, not permission to erase canon or invent consequential events.
- Preserve established character identity, relationships, dialogue, lore, chronology, and provenance.
- Allow plot to remain implicit in environmental evidence when possible; state essential events clearly when the reader needs them.
- Avoid turning the novel into a list of setting notes. Each descriptor should be embodied, specific, and part of the broadcast.
- Keep headings and structured data functional. Apply the rule to prose, chapter text, lore, captions, interface copy, archive descriptions, artifact records, and generated narrative text.
- Do not replace source material wholesale with a summary. Revise in place and retain useful original detail.

## Example pattern

**SETH:** The far wall is all white light and brushed steel, with the ceiling vents carrying a low, steady vibration across the room.

**NICK:** And the mirrors double the depth. The equipment seems to continue beyond the actual floor plan; every row has another row behind it.

The environment carries the transition. Plot events remain present, but paragraph boundaries are driven by what the world looks, sounds, and feels like.

## Hulum's Cave production test

For source-led video work, connect temporal rhythm, continuous body transformation, camera response, lighting, spatial reference, sound, character continuity, and archive provenance. Growth changes the full silhouette—including height and limb length—not isolated muscle volumes alone. Let the environment make scale legible and the camera respond to it. Validate each reconstruction against an inspected source clip. The directive is a standard for production; it does not imply that a renderer or source analysis has already been completed.
