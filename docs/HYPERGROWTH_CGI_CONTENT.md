# Hypergrowth CGI Content

Status: active creative direction for GEEHUB  
Purpose: define the actual content the CGI medium exists to produce.

Hypergrowth is a continuous visual event, not a before-and-after gimmick. The viewer experiences a known subject crossing successive physical baselines, each stage retaining the identity and history of the one before it.

## The growth event

A subject begins at a legible baseline. Mass gathers and expands through the shoulders, chest, back, arms, and legs. Limbs lengthen and thicken in proportion to the new mass so the body remains structurally coherent. The silhouette changes; the subject's face, recognisable features, and manner remain continuous. The room and camera provide scale. Clothing, posture, balance, contact with the ground, and movement register the physical consequences.

The central content is the transition: expansion accumulating over time until the previous baseline no longer describes the subject.

## Stages are states of one subject

Each stage is a saved state in the same character lineage, not an unrelated generated replacement.

- **Baseline:** establish identity, silhouette, stance, camera, and spatial scale.
- **Initial expansion:** increase selected regions while preserving the subject's recognisable form.
- **Distributed growth:** let shoulders, torso, arms, and legs develop together; extend limb length when needed to contain the new proportions.
- **Scale pressure:** allow the environment, clothing fit, framing, and movement to reveal how much has changed.
- **Hypergrowth:** carry the transformation beyond ordinary proportions while preserving the subject's identity and the continuity of the morph.
- **New baseline:** retain the result as the source state for the next transformation.

These are not rigid presets. The user can pause, branch, revisit, or change the growth direction at any stage.

## What makes the experience work

1. **Continuity:** the same subject remains present across the whole sequence.
2. **Readable change:** each stage visibly develops from the previous one.
3. **Embodied scale:** framing, lighting, contact, posture, and surrounding architecture communicate size.
4. **Temporal presence:** intermediate states matter; the morph is content, not merely the endpoints.
5. **Materiality:** skin, cloth, hair, and environment respond visually to changing form.
6. **Accumulation:** every accepted stage can be retained, compared, replayed, and used as the next source.
7. **World response:** the room and camera can adjust to the growing subject without turning the experience into a disconnected slideshow.

## CGI construction

Build the figure from reusable components with a whole-body assembled view. Component edits update the assembled subject. A growth pass records the affected regions, proportion changes, visual source, parent state, and resulting state. The renderer can present a still, a turntable, a sequence of stages, or an animated transition from the same lineage.

The scene should provide a stable scale reference: floor, architecture, furniture, doorways, or another consistent environmental feature. Camera distance and lens behavior should be recorded so a change in framing is not mistaken for growth.

## Content outputs

A single lineage can produce:
- a sequence of cumulative growth stages;
- an animated morph between saved states;
- component close-ups alongside the whole figure;
- a turntable or walk cycle showing the new baseline in motion;
- a scene in which the environment reacts to the subject's scale;
- a narrative record describing what changed and what remains continuous.

These are different manifestations of the same growth event, not separate subjects.

## Acceptance test

The user can identify the subject immediately, observe a meaningful increase in scale and mass from one stage to the next, inspect how the components contribute to the whole, and continue growing the latest saved state. The result feels like a body physically becoming larger inside a persistent CGI world.

**Core rule: grow the subject itself; preserve the lineage; make the transition experiencable.**
