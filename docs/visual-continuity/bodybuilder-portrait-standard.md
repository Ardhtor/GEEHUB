# Bodybuilder Portrait Continuity Standard

**Status:** Approved visual direction  
**Established:** 2026-11-21  
**Reference:** The full-body portrait approved by the user on 2026-11-21, following the initial natural-faced portrait.

## Core decision

The user's preferred result is an extremely muscular, full-body adult male portrait whose face remains natural, recognizable, and proportionally human. Physique enlargement must be treated as a separate operation from facial design. The face is the continuity anchor; the body may grow, but the face must not be reshaped by that growth.

## Preserve the face

Use the original first portrait as the strict face reference whenever available. Preserve the same facial geometry and identity cues: head size, face shape, eyes, nose, mouth, jaw, beard/stubble, hairline, hairstyle, and expression. Do not inflate, enlarge, stylize, smooth, warp, over-detail, or otherwise redesign the face. Avoid changes to the head and neck that make the face appear distorted. Do not let body-proportion edits propagate into the head.

## Develop the physique independently

The preferred physique is exceptionally large and bodybuilding-focused, with coherent human anatomy and connected muscle groups. Emphasize a very broad, thick back and lat spread, deep traps, huge rounded deltoids, substantial chest and arms, powerful forearms, defined abdominals, massive thighs, and full calves. Keep joints, hands, feet, muscle insertions, and the overall skeleton believable even when the scale is deliberately extreme. Increase overall mass in a coordinated way rather than enlarging one isolated muscle group.

## Framing and clothing

Prefer a complete head-to-toe composition with both feet visible and clear space around the silhouette. Do not crop the head, hands, legs, or feet. A relaxed, front-facing or subtly turned bodybuilding stance works well; use a slight torso angle when it helps communicate back width and depth. Keep the blue denim cutoff shorts from the approved image, with a modestly longer inseam than the earlier short version. Maintain natural fabric construction and frayed hems.

## Surface, lighting, and setting

Keep skin photographic and plausible, with natural texture and lighting rather than artificial embossed or repetitive surface patterns. Retain realistic muscle definition, skin detail, shadows, and material response. A restrained, ordinary indoor room is appropriate; the setting should support the portrait rather than compete with it.

## Reference hierarchy for future edits

1. **Original first portrait:** authoritative reference for the man's natural face and facial proportions.
2. **Approved full-body portrait from 2026-11-21:** authoritative reference for the preferred overall physique, head-to-body relationship, framing, shorts, pose, and photographic treatment.
3. **New intermediate generations:** secondary references only; do not allow a later image's facial drift to overwrite the original face reference.

When producing another iteration, explicitly instruct the image model to lock the original face and change the body independently. Use the approved full-body image for composition and physique continuity, while continuing to refer back to the first portrait for the face. Inspect the face after every major body-growth pass.

## Acceptance test

A successful future iteration should satisfy all of the following:
- The face still reads as the same natural face from the first portrait, without visible distortion.
- The entire body, including both feet, fits within the frame.
- The back, shoulders, traps, chest, arms, torso, thighs, and calves read as one coherent, very large physique.
- The denim cutoffs remain slightly longer than the earliest version.
- Skin, lighting, anatomy, and clothing remain photographic rather than malformed or plasticky.

**Continuity rule:** grow the physique, not the face.
