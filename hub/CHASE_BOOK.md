# CHASE

## The Book of One of the Guys

Chase is not the author of the world. He is one of the guys inside it.

A great deal of the GEEHUB novel is experienced from Chase's perspective: what he notices, what he remembers, who is near him, who has become distant, what he does not understand, and what changes when another person enters the scene. His perspective is therefore a continuity surface, not a claim to centrality. Luke remains Luke. Tyler remains Tyler. The other men remain themselves. Chase moves among them.

The governing question of Chase's book is simple: who is here, how close are they to me, and what has happened between us?

Distance is not merely geography. It can be physical distance, social distance, temporal distance, emotional distance, or the strange distance created when two people occupy the same place but no longer inhabit the same moment. The book treats those distances as changing conditions of the world.

When Chase walks through the woods and sees Luke ahead of him, that is an event. When Luke is absent but the path still carries the memory of having been walked together, that is also an event. When Tyler appears at the edge of the group, the landscape acquires another relationship. The world is consequently not a neutral backdrop behind the men. It is altered by their nearness, their separation, their history, and Chase's changing perception of all three.

### Perspective engine

CHASE → ATTENTION → ENCOUNTER → MEMORY → RELATIONSHIP → DISTANCE → WORLD

Chase's perspective should generate novel material from the same underlying corpus that generates the visual world. A remembered event can become a place. A relationship can become a route. An unresolved question can become a locked building, a fog bank, an unopened room, or a road that continues beyond visibility. A return should never be identical to the first visit because Chase is not identical either.

### The men

Chase is one node in the group, not the group itself.

THE MEN
├── CHASE
├── LUKE
├── TYLER
└── ...

Each relationship should retain its own history and current distance from Chase. The novel can move into another character's presence without erasing Chase's perspective; the important thing is that the world remains relational rather than becoming a single-character simulation.

### Canonical state

Each significant relationship may eventually expose:

distance
relationship
history
recency
attention
trust
unknowns
trajectory

These are not numerical ratings of people. They are narrative variables describing what Chase knows, remembers, experiences, and does not know.

### Book/world continuity

The Chase book and the GEEHUB world are the same continuity viewed through different surfaces.

CORPUS → RELATIONSHIPS → DISTANCE FIELD → WORLD
WORLD → CHASE → EXPERIENCE → MEMORY
MEMORY → NOVEL

The eventual implementation should allow an encounter in the drive-through to become narrative material, and narrative material to alter the next world generation. Chase's book therefore grows with the Hub instead of existing as a detached manuscript.

### First principle

Chase does not stand outside the mystery explaining it.

He is walking through it with the others.
