# GEEHUB Workplace Charter

The workplace exists for the inhabitants who use GEEHUB. It is not an administrative layer for repository maintainers.

## How work moves

1. An inhabitant registers a name, role, department, and availability.
2. Anyone may publish a concrete work request. A useful request states the expected result, relevant context, urgency, and department.
3. A member may accept an open request. Acceptance means taking responsibility for the next step, not promising an impossible outcome.
4. Work remains visible while it is in progress. The assignee records changes, blockers, and decisions in the handoff.
5. The requester or assignee marks the request complete when the expected result is reached.
6. Help offers make skills, tools, time, and resources discoverable to the rest of the workplace.

## Department principles

Departments are working communities, not rigid castes. An inhabitant may contribute across departments and may change roles as their interests and abilities develop.

- **Construction & Maintenance** — spaces, facilities, repairs, infrastructure, and upkeep.
- **Archives & Records** — cataloguing, provenance, documentation, memory, and retrieval.
- **Production & Media** — images, video, sound, events, and finished creative artifacts.
- **Research & Discovery** — investigation, experiments, reference gathering, and new methods.
- **Provisioning & Hospitality** — supplies, kitchens, welcoming, scheduling, and shared resources.
- **Civic Office** — notices, coordination, disputes, shared standards, and cross-department work.

These are initial departments, not an exhaustive map. Inhabitants may propose new departments when a recurring body of work needs a home.

## Handoff standard

A useful handoff answers four questions: What changed? What is finished? What remains? Where can the next person find the relevant artifact?

A task is not complete merely because somebody stopped working on it. Leave enough context for another inhabitant to continue without reconstructing the entire history.

## Fairness and autonomy

Participation is voluntary. Availability is a status, not a debt. Urgency should be explained rather than used as pressure. Credit the people who contribute, preserve the origin of work, and make it possible to decline or renegotiate an assignment.

## Current technical boundary

The Common Room currently stores its board in browser-local storage and supports JSON export/import for exchanging snapshots. It does not yet provide authenticated accounts, live multi-device synchronization, private messaging, or server-side access control. Do not treat a local board as a real-time shared database. Those capabilities require a shared backend and explicit identity and permission design.
