# The Beginning of the Dimension

**Status:** Incubating story branch  
**Scope:** Cross-board seed connecting the Joseph–Kirk story, Veyrthalis, the Tractus, Beefythiq, and the mythic archive  
**Continuity:** This is a new fictional branch. It does not overwrite established Veyrthalis events or silently promote experimental material into world canon.

## The first room

The story begins before the magical dimension has a name. Joseph and Kirk are in an ordinary room. The furniture is familiar, the windows look onto an ordinary street, and nothing in the architecture announces that another order of reality is nearby. Their relationship is present before any supernatural explanation: they recognize one another, speak naturally, and share the moment without being reduced to symbols or roles.

A page appears. Its margin carries the words:

> Destiny, her O Dreadmaster.

The phrase is strange, grandiose, and faintly ridiculous. It is not yet a prophecy with a known author or an established office. It is simply the first inscription: a line that seems to address someone before that someone has appeared. Kirk reads it; Joseph looks over his shoulder. A bell sounds once beyond the window. The room has not changed, but the page has made the ordinary world feel newly permeable.

The beginning is deliberately small. There is no palace, registry, army, dimensional hall, or explanatory voice. The Dreadmaster has no assigned identity, history, appearance, or authority beyond the title on the page. The story must allow the title to acquire meaning through later events rather than filling its silence in advance.

## The reversal

A later, comic spell—the so-called Bussy Opener—becomes the accidental instrument of temporal reversal. Its silliness is retained; it is not recast as a solemn ritual. The reversal carries Joseph and Kirk back past the courtyard, the conjuration building, and the altered room, toward the first page and the conditions before the dimension was understood. The return is not a reset that erases what happened. It creates a second vantage on the beginning. They can now notice correspondences that were invisible the first time, while their memories and relationship remain continuous.

## The dimension before its name

The magical dimension is not initially a mapped country or a hidden building. It is a capacity for places, people, and possibilities to enter into relation. At first it appears only through small disturbances: a bell whose source cannot be located, a line in a book, a room that seems to have more depth than its walls permit, a path glimpsed between trees. Its geography must be discovered through actual scenes. Later halls, courtyards, forests, residences, and crossings are consequences of accumulated encounters, not preloaded scenery.

The dimension conjuror remains a function to be discovered: the principle or agent through which dimensions are brought into relation. Do not assign a personal identity, body, biography, or motive until the story establishes one. The same restraint applies to Reagan Grimes: this branch records only the supplied fact that Reagan is down. No additional role or characterization is implied.

## The first correspondences across boards

The first room is a shared origin point for several independent GEEHUB boards, not a device for collapsing their canons.

- **Joseph–Kirk / Veyrthalis:** Joseph and Kirk retain their separate identities, mutual recognition, and relationship continuity. This new branch may echo the experimental hyperspace expedition, but it does not rewrite that expedition or claim that the two events are already the same event.
- **The Tractus / World Atlas:** The Tractus can preserve the page, the room, the bell, and the later crossings as traceable artifacts. Its shared registry connects worlds without making their local histories interchangeable.
- **Beefythiq / Growth Canvas / Mass1v1ng:** The larger will unfolds through time. A change in one state alters the conditions of the next: desire, growth, comparison, mutation, and a new baseline. In this branch, that principle first appears as an alteration in what the room can contain. Physical development remains concrete when it enters the scene; it is not replaced by vague cosmic language.
- **Veyrthalis / Digitorium:** The scribe's first ledger is an ordinary record of ordinary things. Its later importance comes from preserving the sequence of events, not from knowing the future. The Foggy Dark Expanse, the city, and the Digitorium keep their own geography and history.
- **Muscle Myth / Lineage Pantheon:** Mythic correspondences may emerge later, including the older stories of strength, companionship, inheritance, and mortality. Gilgamesh belongs in the deep archive as a parallel mythic inheritance, not as an unexplained visitor to the first room.
- **Residential worlds / Institute / Dormitory:** The future residence begins as a practical possibility: shared rooms, dining, training, rest, work, and spaces where men can develop alongside one another. Its architecture grows from use and accumulated life, rather than appearing fully formed.

## Governing continuity

The branch follows the Tractus principle: what happened, what was seen, what changed, and what remains must be distinguishable. Preserve the order of events. Preserve the source of each new detail. Keep experiments marked as experiments until reviewed. A crossing can carry an image, passage, question, or artifact between boards without transferring the whole canon of one world into another.

The central transformation rule is recursive: encounter changes attention; attention changes desire; desire changes action; action changes the body, the room, or the institution; the resulting state changes what can be imagined next. The world grows by retaining consequences.

## Open threads

- Who wrote the phrase, and why does it address a figure not yet encountered?
- What is the bell, and where does it sound from?
- What precisely can the first room contain that its visible dimensions do not explain?
- How do Joseph and Kirk discover the first crossing without losing the ordinary texture of their relationship?
- What does the dimension conjuror actually do, and what are the limits of that function?
- Which later places genuinely belong to this branch, and which are only correspondences to independent settings?
- What does the Dreadmaster title come to mean, if anything beyond its first comic and ominous appearance?

The first page remains the origin artifact. Later discoveries should be written as additions to its history, not as explanations retroactively pretending to have been known from the beginning.
