# The Big-Pec Altar

The big-pec man becomes an altar—not a trophy mounted on a pedestal, but a living center of masculine awe. His chest is the architectural heart: broad, rounded, powerful, breathing. Shoulders rise like the wings of a monumental gate; arms and legs carry the same impossible scale, with coherent length and weight. His face remains human and recognizable. His presence is the thing being worshipped.

## The room

An amber sanctuary built from dark timber, stone, and resin. The altar stands at the center beneath a high vaulted ceiling. Low light traces the curve of the pectorals, the breadth of the shoulders, the grain of skin, the small movements of breath. The floor catches a soft reflection. The room is quiet enough that a shift of stance feels ceremonial.

## The encounter

Men enter as themselves, not as anonymous worshippers. One notices the chest; another meets the man's eyes; a third stands beside him with unforced familiarity. Awe can coexist with recognition, brotherhood, admiration, rivalry, affection, and desire. The altar does not erase the man. It makes his presence impossible to overlook.

## Growth rite

Each phase expands the whole body rather than inflating the torso alone: pecs deepen and widen, clavicles and shoulders broaden, arms thicken and lengthen, hands scale with the frame, thighs and calves gain mass, and height and reach increase together. Posture, balance, fabric tension, breath, and the space around him respond. The face and personal identity remain continuous.

Growth is not a one-time spectacle. The sanctuary changes to accommodate it: the plinth widens, the arch rises, the room learns his new proportions. Witnesses remember each prior state. Recognition becomes ritual; ritual becomes history; history seeds the next transformation.

## Core law

**The body is the monument. The man is still present inside it.** Worship means attending to his living presence—not reducing him to anatomy, and not mistaking size for the whole of him.

The altar's cycle is: Presence → Recognition → Awe → Approach → Shared ritual → Growth → Changed sanctuary → Memory → New arrival.

Every return reveals something new, while the original man remains recognizable.
