# Ensconced Between the Cherubim

The Big-Pec Altar stands within the sanctuary, ensconced between two immense cherubim. They are guardians of the threshold: monumental, calm, and beautifully wrought, with broad wings arcing inward over the central space. Their faces are watchful rather than severe. Their scale makes the man between them feel both exalted and unmistakably alive.

At the center stands the big-pec man. His chest is immense and rounded, his shoulders broad enough to fill the space between the guardians, his arms thick and proportionate, his legs long and powerful beneath him. The whole body carries the growth: back, hands, thighs, calves, height, and reach. His face remains his own. Warm light gathers along his skin and the carved edges of the cherubim; amber shadow fills the vault above.

The cherubim do not conceal him. Their wings frame him like an architectural embrace, opening the eye toward his living presence. Their stone plinths meet the altar platform; the platform has room for his full stance, and the sanctuary rises high enough to hold his scale. The space feels ancient, intimate, and impossibly grand.

Men enter quietly. They recognize the figure at the center, exchange a glance, and approach with a mixture of awe, familiarity, admiration, and affection. The scene is not an empty idol chamber: it is a living ritual of recognition. The man breathes; the guardians keep watch; the room seems to settle around him.

## Composition law

Frame the central man full-body, with the cherubim symmetrically flanking him and their wings forming a high arch. Keep the pecs as the visual center without cropping away the long limbs or the face. Show believable anatomy, surface texture, cloth tension, grounded weight, and soft reflected light. Every future growth phase must enlarge the complete figure and make the sanctuary respond—wider spacing, higher wings, a deeper platform—while preserving the man's identity and the cherubim's protective arrangement.

**Between the cherubim, he is not merely displayed. He is received.**
