# DIABOUND KERNEL — THE RIDING COMPACT

## The Creature at the Blackwater Gate

Diabound Kernel comes to the Tractus as a living ka: a dark, intelligent creature whose body seems assembled from shadow, muscle, scale, and old ceremonial armour. Its shape keeps the recognisable silhouette of Diabound: a powerful, partly humanoid torso; a crown of backward-sweeping horns; four feathered wings arranged in two pairs; and a long, articulated serpent-tail whose smaller head watches independently of the main body. Its hide carries a muted green cast beneath the darkness, crossed by blue markings that become visible when it moves through moonlight. The chest bears a luminous eye-like mark, not a rider's handle or a target, but a sensory organ that responds to movement, heat, and the presence of other ka.

The Kernel is not a vehicle that happens to breathe. It is a creature with preferences, memory, caution, curiosity, and the right to refuse. It can be ridden, but riding is a relationship established through repeated contact. A person first approaches on foot, learns the creature's signals, offers food and objects it has shown interest in, and waits for Diabound to lower one wing. Only then may a rider climb onto the broad, protected ridge between its shoulders. A flexible riding harness rests around the torso without binding the wings or restricting the tail. There are no reins attached to the mouth or horns. The rider communicates through a set of light pressure cues, voice, and a small set of agreed gestures. Diabound can ignore any cue it considers unsafe.

## Temperament and Interaction

Diabound is watchful before it is affectionate. It studies a new person from several angles, often allowing the serpent-head at the end of its tail to investigate first. It dislikes sudden grabbing, enclosed spaces, bright flashes, and anyone attempting to command it through fear. It is drawn to moving water, high stone ledges, old ruins, polished metal, and places where it can see the approaches from several directions. It will sometimes fold its wings around itself like a dark shelter, leaving a narrow opening through which its chest-eye remains visible.

Its interactions are legible but not domesticated into a set of tricks. A lowered wing means an invitation to approach; a slow curl of the tail means it is considering something; the serpent-head touching the ground marks a boundary; a brief pulse from the chest-eye signals recognition. When content, Diabound makes a low, resonant sound that can be felt through a rider's legs and the stone beneath its claws. It may nudge a familiar person with its shoulder, bring back a found object, or settle beside a campfire with its wings partly open. It does not perform on demand, and it does not accept being treated as a trophy.

The creature learns names and routes, recognises familiar voices, and remembers whether a person has respected its refusal. Its intelligence is demonstrated through choices: it may take a longer route to avoid a frightened crowd, lower itself so an injured traveller can climb aboard, or return to a place where it once found shelter. It can be playful, but its play is large-scale and must be understood. A sweep of one wing can scatter leaves across a courtyard; a tail-tip can lift a dropped tool without touching the person who lost it.

## Riding, Flight, and Passage

A rider mounts only after Diabound has offered the wing. The safest position is low between the shoulders, behind the neck and ahead of the wing roots. The harness includes a broad seat, two handholds, and a loose safety line that prevents an accidental fall without tethering the rider to the creature in a crash. The rider's weight is distributed across a padded frame; the wings, spine, and tail remain free. Diabound can carry one rider comfortably and a second only when it chooses and the journey is short. It can also carry a light travel pack, but not a full troop or heavy cargo.

On the ground, Diabound moves with a measured, feline quietness that seems impossible for its size. Its claws find purchase on wet stone, roots, and broken masonry. It can climb steep slopes by using its forelimbs and wings for balance, and it can cross shallow water without hesitation. In flight, the lower pair of wings stabilises its body while the upper pair supplies the main lift and manoeuvring force. It prefers broad arcs and high passages to sudden dives. A rider who leans too far forward or pulls against the harness receives a warning vibration through the saddle before Diabound changes course.

Diabound can briefly conceal itself in deep shadow, becoming difficult to distinguish from the darkness around it. This is not a reliable substitute for ordinary travel: direct light exposes its outline, and the creature may choose not to use the ability if it senses that concealment would frighten or endanger nearby people. Its signature attack, the Helical Shockwave, remains a dangerous spiral discharge. Within the Tractus it is treated as a last-resort defensive capacity, never as a riding demonstration or a spectacle for crowds. The creature's ability to grow stronger through conflict is recorded as part of its inherited nature, but the Geatish company and the Northgate handlers establish a strict compact: no one may provoke a fight or injure another creature merely to force Diabound to evolve.

## The Geatish Company and the First Bond

The first sustained relationship forms with Wiglaf, who approaches Diabound without trying to prove that he is unafraid. He sits at a respectful distance in the old quarry yard and speaks about the ship, the river, and the work waiting at the Geatish House. Diabound listens, occasionally turning its head so that one eye follows him while the serpent-tail traces slow circles in the dust. On the fourth visit it lowers a wing. Wiglaf does not climb at once. He waits until the following morning, returns with the same quiet manner, and accepts the invitation only when it is offered again.

Their first ride is a short circuit over the river road, no higher than the roofline of the workshops. Wiglaf keeps his hands on the forward grips and gives no command beyond the agreed signal to rise. Diabound chooses the route, banks over the water, and lands in the meadow beside the Geatish House. The company has gathered to watch, but nobody cheers until the creature has folded its wings and turned toward them. Beowulf's empty chair stands near the hearth inside. Wiglaf dismounts, rests a hand against Diabound's shoulder, and says that the route was well chosen. The creature answers with its low, resonant rumble.

The Geats do not claim Diabound as property or as a war-beast. It is given a sheltered roost beside the Geatish House, with a high platform, a shallow pool, a darkened sleeping chamber, and an open flight path toward the river. It may leave whenever it wishes. Its food, health, and equipment are maintained by a small group trained to recognise its needs. Northgate's animal-care scholars, the Geatish shipwrights, and the university's makers collaborate on the harness, which is inspected after every flight and altered only with Diabound's tolerance.

## The Kernel's Growth

Diabound's form is not fixed. It can alter its appearance as its powers develop, and it may inherit aspects of abilities it has encountered. The Tractus records these changes carefully without treating every change as an upgrade. A new horn, a brighter chest-eye, a denser wing, or a changed marking may bring a new capability and a new difficulty. The creature's growth is therefore documented as a living history rather than a score.

The name Kernel is used for this stage because the creature is understood to contain many possible forms without being required to become any one of them. Its evolution is neither a public contest nor a demand placed upon its rider. The rider's responsibility is to notice changes, keep the routes safe, and make space for the creature to choose how it develops. Diabound's own agency remains central: it may refuse a battle, abandon a route, decline a rider, or return to the shadowed roost for days.

## Field Notes for the Tractus

Diabound Kernel is a rideable, interactable creature, not a mount prop. It can be approached, befriended, fed, groomed where it permits, equipped with a fitted riding harness, ridden on land or in flight, and engaged through ongoing routines. Its tail-head, wings, chest-eye, shadow concealment, and resonant vocalisations should all remain available as meaningful interaction channels. The creature must respond to context and relationship rather than a single generic “mount” command.

Its central rule is simple: **the rider is invited; the creature is never owned.** The Geatish company becomes its first home in the Tractus, but Diabound remains free to form other relationships and to travel beyond the river valley. Its presence gives the world a new kind of passage: not merely a road or a ship route, but a living route that can decide where it wishes to go.
