# GEEHUB // EXPANSION ATLAS
## 2026-11-10 — The Confluence Layer

This document expands the existing GEEHUB canon without replacing it. It establishes a larger connective geography for worlds, institutions, characters, production systems, and remembered events. Existing names and histories remain authoritative where already established. New material is additive and can be revised through later encounters.

## The Confluence

Beyond the known regions lies the Confluence: not a single city, but the inhabited connective territory through which the separate worlds exchange people, objects, customs, images, stories, and techniques. It is the part of GEEHUB that makes the atlas feel inhabited. Roads do not merely connect destinations; they carry habits. A workshop can influence a university. A song heard in a dormitory can become the sound of a distant temple. A design discovered in a media archive can become the ornament of a building several worlds away.

The Confluence has no central capital. Its principal meeting point is the Exchange Hall, a vast timber-and-glass structure built around a high central court. Galleries overlook the court at several levels. Each gallery belongs to a different discipline: image-making, architecture, movement, sound, writing, material study, history, and experimental play. A visitor can arrive with a fragment and leave with a prototype, a new friendship, a changed room, or a question that continues elsewhere. The Hall keeps a record of transformations but does not dictate what a fragment must become.

The Exchange Hall is joined to the older Common Table, Lantern Market, Cedar House, Bluewater Quay, Cat Gardens, Northgate University, Founders Dormitory, and Athletic Commons. These are not disposable backdrops. Each has opening hours, weather, staff, recurring visitors, maintenance needs, seasonal changes, and remembered occasions. Their continuity is expressed through ordinary details: a repaired stair, a notice pinned crookedly to a board, a familiar mug, the smell of bread in the morning, a bench polished by repeated use.

## The regional structure

The Confluence is divided into six broad territories.

**The Hearthlands** contain homes, dining halls, gardens, markets, residential colleges, and neighbourhood streets. Their purpose is ordinary continuity: eating, resting, meeting, returning, and developing a sense of belonging. The Cedar House is a principal anchor. Its oak beams, cedar walls, deep sitting room, broad kitchen, sheltered porch, and upper reading room make it a place that can hold many different kinds of visit without becoming a ceremonial set.

**The Collegiate Belt** contains Northgate University, Founders Dormitory, the Institute of Becoming, athletic facilities, libraries, studios, lecture theatres, and workshops. Its culture is ambitious but not uniformly competitive. Some men are scholars, some makers, some athletes, some musicians, and some are still discovering what they want to become. The university calendar gives the region a rhythm: arrivals, orientation, studio reviews, fixtures, exhibitions, examinations, summer projects, and reunions.

**The Industrial Marches** contain fabrication halls, timber yards, machine shops, foundries, vehicle depots, material libraries, and large-scale construction sites. The region is focused on making. Buildings here reveal their structure rather than concealing it: bolted plates, exposed beams, lifting gantries, service bridges, heavy doors, and broad floors designed for repeated use. The Industrial Marches supply the rest of the Confluence with objects and infrastructure.

**The Observatory Range** contains the Facility, observation rings, data galleries, image theatres, archives, sensory chambers, and the quiet rooms of Discovery. It studies events without reducing them to metrics alone. A record may include measurements, a sequence of images, a spoken recollection, a diagram, and the unresolved question that remained after the event. Observation is not ownership. A subject retains agency over how their fictional record is used within the world.

**The Mythic Verge** is the threshold territory between inhabited geography and the stranger laws of Veyrthalis, the Foggy Dark Expanse, the Temple of Moon, and the Digitorium. Paths here can become unreliable, but continuity is preserved through landmarks, witnesses, repeated sounds, and objects carried from one crossing to another. The Verge does not erase the ordinary world; it allows the ordinary world to acquire another depth.

**The Scale Commons** contains adaptive buildings, monumental courtyards, giant-compatible transit, miniature districts, measurement stations, and landscapes designed to accommodate changing scale. Its architecture is modular and responsive. A familiar place can retain its identity while its clearances, furniture, circulation, and structural systems change around a character. Earlier dimensions remain recorded as part of the place's history.

## The Institute of Becoming

The Institute of Becoming is the Collegiate Belt's central institution for transformation as a discipline. It is not a single laboratory. It is a campus of studios, gyms, libraries, anatomy and movement rooms, fabrication workshops, residential houses, media suites, and outdoor training grounds. Its central proposition is that development is not a linear ladder. It is an interaction between intention, environment, practice, observation, and time.

The Institute maintains five connected schools. The School of Form studies bodies, posture, movement, clothing, proportion, and visual continuity. The School of Matter studies materials, structures, fabrication, and the physical consequences of change. The School of Presence studies voice, expression, social rhythm, attention, and the way a person occupies a room. The School of Image studies photography, animation, cinematography, modelling, and the preservation of visual identity. The School of Continuance studies memory, narrative, relationships, and the accumulated effects of repeated encounters.

Students cross between schools freely. A sculptor may study movement to understand weight distribution. A filmmaker may spend a term in the material library. An athlete may work with the voice school to develop a recognizable speaking presence. A historian may help a character reconstruct an earlier stage from photographs and written records. The Institute treats these exchanges as the ordinary mechanism of learning.

Its main building is the Long Gallery, a broad, many-storeyed structure of oak, pale stone, blue-green glass, and exposed steel. A central stair rises through the full height of the building. Bridges connect studios across the atrium. A large model room occupies the lower level, while the upper floors contain quiet writing rooms and a panoramic image theatre. The building has a visible memory wall: not a ranking display, but a changing record of completed projects, earlier forms, prototypes, and collaborations.

## The character continuity system

Characters are persistent inhabitants rather than isolated prompts. Each character has a continuity record containing identity anchors, history, voice, relationships, physical baseline, known transformations, preferences expressed in canon, unresolved questions, and a list of scenes in which meaningful changes occurred.

Luke, Tyler, Kirk, Joseph, Joey, Marcus, Dan Roman, Andrew, and the other established figures retain their distinct identities. Similarity is not sameness. A character's voice is built from sentence length, preferred vocabulary, humour, pace, confidence, hesitation, conversational habits, and the kinds of things they notice. A voice reference is a guide to continuity, not a script that prevents development.

Physical development is recorded as a sequence of states rather than a replacement description. Height, mass, proportions, reach, stance, posture, clothing fit, movement, and environmental interaction can change independently. Where measurements are unknown, the record says unknown. Visual impressions are not silently converted into exact measurements. A new state retains a link to the previous state so that the difference can be understood.

Relationships also have histories. A relationship record distinguishes established events, current interpretation, hopes, uncertainty, and unresolved communication. It does not convert imagined reciprocity into a real-world claim. Within fiction, characters can misunderstand, reconcile, become closer, drift apart, or discover new forms of companionship. Their continuity comes from remembered events and changing behaviour rather than from a frozen relationship label.

## The physical development grammar

The Beefythiq and Growth Canvas systems become a shared grammar used across the Institute, Scale Commons, and visual production spaces. The basic cycle remains baseline → pressure → adaptation → mutation → stabilization → new baseline. The Corruption Engine remains a distinct, more volatile branch: Seed → Desire → Growth → Stress → Mutation → Corruption → New Baseline.

A development pass may affect one or several channels: muscular volume, skeletal proportion, posture, reach, strength, coordination, clothing, voice, confidence, spatial footprint, or environmental affordances. These channels need not rise at the same rate. A character may gain reach before height, improve coordination while mass changes, or discover that a familiar movement must be relearned after a major transformation.

The visual production rule is to preserve identity while changing the state. Face, head shape, neck, shoulders, characteristic expression, skin detail, hair, and other stable visual anchors should remain coherent unless a story explicitly changes them. Body components remain modular where the production system supports it: torso, arms, forearms, hands, legs, feet, clothing, accessories, and environment pieces can be edited independently. Deep and wide sculpting are separate controls. A broadening pass should not automatically become a height pass.

Every major transformation receives a before-state, a change record, an after-state, and a short account of what the character can now do differently. The record should include environmental consequences: door clearance, seating, reach to controls, turning radius, floor loading, camera distance, shadow, and the way other characters respond. The world is allowed to react, but it should not reduce the character to a measurement.

## The production territories

The Confluence supports a complete production chain.

The **Reference Arcade** collects source material with provenance: original creator, title, source location, date when known, rights or access status, and the specific visual or technical feature being studied. It links to sources rather than copying restricted media. A reference can be tagged for silhouette, lighting, motion, clothing, camera position, texture, environment, sound, or editing rhythm.

The **Frame Foundry** turns references into frame studies, contact sheets, motion notes, and shot plans. It records what is visible and what remains uncertain. A frame study is a working observation, not an assertion about hidden content.

The **Sculpting Floor** contains modular body and environment components. Its workspaces support separate depth and width passes, proportion adjustments, pose studies, clothing construction, material tests, and assembly. Assets are reusable. A forearm, jacket, boot, beam, door, bench, or window can be developed as a component before being placed in a larger scene.

The **Motion Theatre** handles animation, camera movement, timing, transitions, and continuity between shots. It keeps a shot ledger so that a character's proportions, clothing, lighting, and environment do not drift arbitrarily between frames. DaVinci Resolve, Blender, and other tools can participate as production applications; GEEHUB remains the record of intent, assets, decisions, and results.

The **Sound House** develops original sound palettes: room tone, timber movement, distant machinery, footsteps, fabric, breath, weather, synthesized tones, procedural ambience, and music sketches. Sound is treated as spatial information. A room should sound different when empty, crowded, cavernous, or newly altered.

The **Assembly Hall** joins the outputs into finished scenes, interactive experiences, short films, books, games, and installations. It preserves the relationship between a finished output and the component assets that produced it.

## The living geography

Places are represented by more than coordinates. A place record contains its name, region, geometry, materials, climate, access routes, inhabitants, recurring activities, sound profile, lighting, history, connected places, and current state. A building can have a ground plan, vertical section, room list, circulation map, and construction history. A landscape can have terrain, water, vegetation, weather, paths, and points of observation.

The map is only the overview. Each place should be enterable as a lived environment. The user should be able to move from the map into a room, inspect its objects, encounter its inhabitants, and return to the atlas without losing the previous state. A location can host events, and events can alter the location. A workshop exhibition may add a permanent display. A storm may damage a footbridge. A festival may leave lanterns in the courtyard for several days. Maintenance and ordinary change make the world credible.

Travel between regions is recorded as a chain of places rather than a teleporting list. Routes can include a walk through the market, a stop at the Common Table, a crossing of the university quad, and an arrival at the Institute. Each segment can carry weather, time, companions, observations, and a memory trace.

## The social fabric

The established preference for male-only fictional environments is implemented as a worldbuilding constraint for the relevant settings, not as a claim about real-world communities. The Confluence contains all-male residential colleges, clubs, teams, workshops, houses, and social institutions where specified by canon. Their cultures differ. A university house may be studious and noisy; an athletic club may be ritualized and friendly; a makers' guild may be practical and eccentric; a residential house may be quiet and domestic.

The world should not make every man interchangeable. Characters differ in age, build, background, temperament, interests, competence, confidence, humour, and social role. Some are reserved, some theatrical, some exacting, some warm, some awkward, some mischievous. They have projects beyond their bodies and relationships beyond rivalry. Competition can be one social current among many, but collaboration, mentorship, friendship, ordinary conversation, shared meals, and private interests give the setting its breadth.

## The memory and provenance architecture

Every consequential addition should be recorded in a form that can be found again. The minimum record contains a stable identifier, title, date, type, source or origin, related entities, status, and a short account of what changed. Larger artifacts add versions, dependencies, media references, measurements, and validation notes.

Canon, experiment, and hypothesis remain distinguishable. Canon records what is established. Experiments record what is being tried. Hypotheses record what might be true but has not been established. A discarded branch remains available as an archived possibility when it contains useful history. Contradictions are not silently flattened; they are represented as competing versions with dates and context.

The Tractus is the user-facing continuity surface. It should show the living world, not expose a wall of database fields. Its deeper record layer retains the underlying structure. The user sees places, characters, scenes, artifacts, and current activity. The system preserves provenance, links, versions, and unresolved questions beneath that surface.

The memory cycle is encounter → residue → record → relation → transformation → return. A record is valuable when it changes what can happen next. The system should therefore make it easy to reopen a character, revisit a place, compare two states, continue a scene, or branch an experiment without reconstructing the entire history.

## The RUN principle

RUN means continue from the present state. It does not mean reset, generate an unrelated sample, or summarize the whole archive. A run should inspect the active world state, identify the most consequential open thread, select a useful next action, create or update an artifact, and preserve the result. When multiple branches are equally plausible, the system can keep them as parallel experiments rather than forcing a false single path.

A run has five stages: observe, choose, produce, verify, preserve. Observation checks the existing records. Choice selects a bounded next development. Production creates a tangible result. Verification checks that the result exists and is internally coherent. Preservation records what changed and where it belongs. A claim that a file was written, a test was executed, or a media asset was processed must be supported by the corresponding result.

## New persistent places

**The Exchange Hall** is the Confluence's central meeting architecture: a multi-level timber-and-glass court with studios, galleries, a public table, and a broad stair. It hosts exhibitions, demonstrations, reunions, and spontaneous collaborations.

**The Long Gallery** is the Institute of Becoming's main building. Its bridges, model room, image theatre, writing rooms, and memory wall make it a place where different disciplines encounter one another.

**The Material Library** is an industrial archive of wood, stone, metal, glass, resin, textiles, fasteners, finishes, and structural samples. Each specimen has a tactile description, origin, use history, and links to projects in which it appears.

**The Motion Yard** is an outdoor training and movement ground with adaptable surfaces, marked distances, covered stands, equipment storage, and observation platforms. It supports movement studies and athletic practice without becoming a single-purpose arena.

**The Night Archive** is a quiet building open after dark. It holds the day's unfinished observations, late-night writing, sound experiments, and material that has not yet found a category. Its blue-green lamps and long oak tables make it a place for slow work.

**The Meridian Station** is the transport interchange linking the Hearthlands, Collegiate Belt, Industrial Marches, Observatory Range, Mythic Verge, and Scale Commons. Its architecture changes by district, but its central clock and departure hall remain constant.

**The Glasshouse Quarter** is a district of botanical houses, winter gardens, small laboratories, cafés, and sheltered courtyards. Its climate-controlled rooms make it possible to preserve plant life from different regions of the Confluence.

**The Archive of Earlier Forms** is a dedicated repository for superseded designs, earlier character states, old maps, retired interfaces, and abandoned prototypes. Nothing here is presented as current merely because it is preserved. The archive exists so that the present can remember how it came to be.

## Long-form narrative seed: The First Exchange

The Exchange Hall opened on a morning when the rain had not quite decided whether to stop. Water ran in narrow lines down the glass roof, and the central court held a pale reflection of the sky. The building was already in use before the official opening. A carpenter was adjusting a handrail on the western stair. Two students were carrying a model through the lower gallery. Someone had left a thermos beside the public table, and a notice about an evening film screening had been pinned over an older notice about a lecture.

The first exhibition was not a polished collection. It was a set of working objects brought from different parts of the Confluence: a carved timber joint from the Industrial Marches, a sequence of motion frames from the Reference Arcade, a small architectural model from Northgate, a sound recording of the Bluewater Quay at dawn, and a notebook of observations from the Observatory Range. None of the objects had been made for the same purpose. Their relationship emerged because they were placed in the same room.

By midday, the carpenter had used the architectural model to propose a new bridge detail. The sound recording had prompted a lighting study for the waterfront. The motion frames had become a discussion about how a character's stance changes when the floor beneath him changes scale. A visitor from the Night Archive began a written account of the morning, then stopped because the account was becoming a story rather than a report.

The Hall did not resolve these differences. It gave them a place to remain in contact. At closing time, the objects were returned to their owners, but the connections were recorded. The bridge detail entered the Material Library. The lighting study went to the Glasshouse Quarter. The stance study entered the Sculpting Floor. The written account remained unfinished in the Night Archive.

The next morning, each fragment had changed slightly because it had encountered the others. That was the first rule of the Exchange: no object needed to lose its origin in order to acquire another use.

## Expansion policy

GEEHUB grows by adding places, people, systems, and artifacts that have relationships to what already exists. New material should create more possible activity, not simply more names. Each new place needs a reason to be visited. Each new character needs a recognizable point of view. Each new system needs a tangible operation. Each new artifact needs a provenance trail and a path back into the living world.

The map can grow indefinitely, but navigation must remain legible. The world can become stranger without becoming incoherent. The archive can become enormous without becoming a pile. The novel can become long without losing its earlier chapters. The user-facing experience should remain immediate: enter, see, encounter, make, return.

The governing principle remains continuance. GEEHUB is not a finished world waiting to be explored. It is a world that changes because it is explored.
