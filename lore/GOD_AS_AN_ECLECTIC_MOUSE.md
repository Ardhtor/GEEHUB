# God as an Eclectic Mouse

**Origin:** User's phrase, 24 November 2026: “god as an eclectic mouse”; clarified as “on a nature walk.”

**Status:** Shared thematic seed across the three principal works. Not yet a literal character, established deity, or numbered chapter.

## The image

A small, curious divine presence wanders outdoors after rain. It notices a mushroom, a beetle, a feather caught in a bramble, a snail shell, leaves of different greens, the rhythm of a woodpecker, a blue flower growing through a discarded bottle cap, and a pebble with a natural hole. It gathers without imposing a hierarchy of useful and useless, beautiful and ugly, sacred and ordinary. Its pockets fill with specimens, impressions, mysteries, and unfinished theories.

The mouse returns home with an unexpected universe assembled from the walk. It does not create everything from nothing. It notices, collects, carries, compares, and changes relations. The computer mouse is a secondary resonance: attention moves across a world and selects what may be brought into being.

## Three-book influence

- **The Road of Seven Wells:** a mode of attention to overlooked signs, small objects, natural particulars, and relations between places. Let it deepen the existing imagery of wells, paths, rooms, water, and unseen continuities.
- **The Golden Lion and the Seven Great Houses:** curiosity moving through grounds, gardens, quarry, and signal country; noticing how different households arrange, preserve, or overlook things without making the mouse an authority over them.
- **The Many Open Ends:** fragments from separate places becoming connected through objects, messages, observations, and practical clues. Preserve credible information travel; no distant character knows what has not reached them.

## Writing constraints

Preserve the phrase “God as an eclectic mouse.” Keep the humour gentle and the divinity curious rather than grandiose. The mouse's power is attention. Avoid turning it into a moral lecture, a mechanical allegory, or an explanatory narrator. Let the particular things encountered carry the meaning.
