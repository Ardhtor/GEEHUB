# The Artifact Chronicles — Second Cabinet
## Objects that returned with new work

### IX. The Mortar Sample

Neral brought the mortar sample to the north arcade in a small cloth bag. He had wrapped it carefully so that the broken edges would not grind against one another during the walk. The rain had returned overnight, but the channel was flowing more freely now that the grate had been cleared. Water moved along the stone bed and disappeared beneath the eastern threshold without rising against the wall. The mark left by the earlier leak remained visible, a darker band on the lower course.

Tyler laid the sample beside the piece he had taken from the old joint. The two were not identical. One contained a coarser aggregate and had been applied in a way that left a narrow gap at the stone edge. The other was softer and more granular, with a colour closer to the original mortar. Tyler did not announce a solution. He asked Neral where the first repair material had come from, and Neral went to find the old maintenance box.

Joseph recorded the source of each fragment. Luke asked whether the arcade could remain open while the sample was tested. Neral said yes, provided the men left the grate accessible and marked the wet section. They set a temporary notice beside the entrance and agreed to return after the next inspection. The repair had acquired a second stage, but it had not become a completed job.

The first man stood across the passage, looking at the damp wall. He did not touch the sample. "You have kept the difference between the old material and the new," he said.

Tyler nodded. "Otherwise we wouldn't know what we're repairing."

The sample was labelled and placed in a small box. Its record included the place it came from, the date it was taken, and the fact that no permanent repair had yet been made. Neral signed the note. The channel continued to run. The House had not solved the arcade's history; it had made the next repair more honest.

### X. The Seed Beside the Two Maps

The seed remained on its card for several days. The Hall of Accounts had placed it beside Mateo's map, not because it was a symbol of the western country but because it came from a particular tree in a particular place. Its dark shell held the light differently from the paper around it. The pale line along one side had become easier to see as the seed dried.

Izel wanted to ask Mateo whether the seed could be planted. Neral reminded him that Mateo had not given permission for that. The question was therefore entered in the margin rather than acted upon. A later note could record an answer if Mateo chose to provide one. Until then, the object was available to be observed and nothing more.

The two maps were still beside one another. On one, the western passage narrowed as it approached the Hall. On the other, the road from Mateo's country ran through a series of terraces and market towns. The cartographers had added the alternate place names without crossing either out. A new reader could now see where the accounts overlapped and where they remained different.

When Mateo next visited, he found the seed and maps together. He examined the card, then smiled at the note about planting.

"You remembered," he said.

"We did not want to assume."

"Good. My brother will ask about the seed. He will probably say we should plant it."

"Would you?"

Mateo looked out the window toward the western passage. "I would ask what the tree needs first."

He did not answer the question of permission. The Hall did not press him. Instead he told Izel the name his family used for the tree and described the shade it made beside the road. The name was added in Spanish, with a note that no translation had been supplied. The seed had acquired a little more context without losing the limit that protected it.

### XI. The Clearing Song at the Long Table

Sigeferth played the Northgate version of the clearing song at the next Geatish supper. He did not announce that it was a performance. He sat near the end of the long table and began quietly while Eadwine was explaining the new provisions ledger. The first notes were familiar to Wulfgar, but the ending was different. It turned lower, rested for a beat, and then returned to the opening phrase with a slight change in rhythm.

Asta recognized the point where the melody had been altered. "You kept the old part."

"I didn't want to lose it," Sigeferth said.

"And the new part?"

"That happened here."

Eadric asked him to play it again. The second time, several of the younger Geats joined the final note. They did not know the old version well enough to imitate it, so they followed the new ending. The result was neither a restoration nor a replacement. It was another event in the song's life.

The archive clerk wrote down the date and the people present. He did not attempt to transcribe the melody from memory. Sigeferth agreed to make a notation later, but he kept the instrument with him. The two written descriptions remained side by side: the Heorot account and the Northgate return. Both were needed to understand why the song had changed.

When supper ended, Eadwine moved the empty chair closer to the hearth. Nobody treated the movement as a ritual. It was simply where the next visitor would sit.

### XII. The Beam That Found Another Place

The twisted beam from the Dad Convent delivery remained beneath the covered temporary rack. It had not been used in the lumber store's main frame because its bearing surface would have pulled the structure out of square. Tyler checked it again after the rain and found that the twist was stable. The beam was not fit for the intended post, but it could serve as a low cross-member in the tool rack if its ends were cut and the load remained modest.

The Builder measured the proposed use. The Architect checked the span. The Keeper looked up the delivery identifier and found the original note. The Witness asked whether the rack would obstruct the passage. The Heir revised the drawing without deleting the first version. The beam was then marked for reuse, with the original rejection preserved.

The men cut it the following morning. The new ends were clean, and the member sat squarely in the rack. It held the hand tools used most often in the service court. The work did not make the beam perfect. It gave the beam a place where its actual qualities were useful.

The registry added one line: *Not accepted for the first structural position; accepted for secondary storage use after inspection.* The earlier decision remained. The new use did not make the first judgement wrong.

### XIII. The Frame That Changed the Bridge

At Northgate, Elliot brought the first off-centre test frame back to the engineering table. He placed it beside the new recording, which included a fixed scale marker and a second camera facing the base. The first frame showed the movement beginning before the visible deflection. The new recording made the sequence easier to measure.

Dan Roman watched both versions. Marek marked the frame where the joint began to move. Noah compared the timing with the diagram used in the athletic commons, while Adrian checked whether the model had been reset in exactly the same way between tests. They found one difference in the base support and repeated the test after correcting it.

The second test produced less movement, but not none. The group kept both recordings. They had not proved that the bridge was safe or complete. They had learned which part of the model needed another revision. The first failure remained useful because the later test could be compared against it.

The archive stored the frames under one model identifier with two test conditions. The difference between the recordings was not a competition for which image looked better. It was a record of what the team had changed and what the structure had done in response.

### XIV. The Route That Was Not Travelled

In Veyrthalis, the copied route remained folded inside the archive sleeve. Luke took it out only when the older Luke asked whether the line had been checked against the forest survey. They placed both maps on the same table and compared the ridges. The copied line crossed one contour that did not appear on the older survey, then disappeared beyond the paper.

No one went out to follow it. The unnamed man had not asked them to. The route was kept because someone had drawn it and because it avoided the two family gates. Its destination remained unknown.

Martin added a new note: *A route may be preserved without being claimed.* The older Luke read the sentence and left it as written. Luke placed the copy back in the sleeve.

The archive did not turn the road into an adventure merely because it was available. The route would remain until someone chose to travel it or new evidence changed its status. For now, the act of preserving it without crossing it was the event.

## Continuity rule for the second cabinet

An artifact becomes more useful when its record carries the difference between states: sample taken and repair pending; seed observed and planting not authorized; song remembered and song revised; beam rejected for one place and reused in another; test failed and test repeated; route copied and route not travelled. These distinctions allow future chapters to return to the same object without making earlier scenes disappear.

The cabinet now contains not only objects that connect places, but objects that preserve the boundaries of what has and has not happened.
