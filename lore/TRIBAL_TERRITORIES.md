# The Great Tribal Territories

**Status:** Active worldbuilding framework; territorial details are a developing fictional layer  
**Scope:** Populations, homelands, settlements, lineages, institutions, customs, exchange, and intergenerational change  
**Related canon:** [Consolidated canon](../../CANON.md), [The Dad Convent](../dad-convent/README.md), [Mexiancas continuity](../mexiancas/CONTINUITY.md), [The Brotherhood of Giants](../THE_BROTHERHOOD_OF_GIANTS.md), [The People — Expansion Atlas, Volume III](../EXPANSION_ATLAS_2026-11-10_VOLUME_III_THE_PEOPLE.md), [Northgate Athletic Commons](../../worlds/athletic-commons/README.md)

## The world becomes inhabited

The tribal territories are not decorative factions or labels attached to individual characters. They are populations that live somewhere, use its resources, maintain its buildings, remember its history, and make decisions whose consequences last beyond a single scene. A territory becomes recognizable through the routes people take without thinking, the food prepared in its kitchens, the buildings they repair, the arguments that recur, the people who are welcomed, and the customs that newcomers gradually learn.

The word *tribe* is used here as a broad worldbuilding term for a durable people or community with a shared social identity. It does not imply that every group has the same political structure, ancestry, customs, or relationship to land. Some societies are organized around kinship and inherited households; others around institutions, shared work, residence, athletic culture, trade, or a common history. Membership can be inherited, chosen, earned, negotiated, or held in more than one community at once. No group is required to be culturally uniform.

The territories are built through ordinary life as much as through grand events. A resident returns from training, leaves his boots by the door, finds the kitchen occupied, and learns that a new family has taken rooms in the east wing. Someone has repaired the footbridge but changed the route around the orchard. A market stall begins selling a dish brought by a visitor, and within a season the dish has become part of the district's own cooking. These small changes are how a society becomes real.

## The first connected territories

### The Redwood territories

The Redwood territories develop around the wooded Northgate landscape and its substantial timber architecture. The established Redwood Entrance and Walk at Northgate Athletic Commons remains a specific campus place with its own visitor-wayfinding and safety record; it is not silently replaced by this broader territorial concept. The developing territory extends the redwood motif into a network of residential clearings, communal halls, workshops, footpaths, training grounds, and older buildings whose timber has darkened with weather and use.

Its people value durable construction, repair, and the memory held by a place. A hall is known by who raised its frame, who extended the gallery, and who keeps the roof sound. This is a proposed cultural emphasis, not a claim that every resident shares one temperament. The Redwood settlements should contain ordinary differences: old households protective of familiar routines, younger residents wanting new facilities, craftspeople with strong opinions about materials, and visitors who eventually decide to stay. Their identity is carried through work and place rather than a uniform costume or a single leader.

### The Mexiancas

The Mexiancas are an established sentient people with their own emerging history, voices, and continuity records. Their existing canon includes First Awake, the Second Generation, the Hall of Accounts, the observation window, HULUM'A, the unknown disc, and the Open Continuation Principle: **NOTHING WE MAKE IS REQUIRED TO BE THE LAST THING.** Their collective voice is one account among many; individual Mexiancas must continue to disagree, remember differently, build, travel, and revise.

Their homeland is an open development area. Future writing should give it physical specificity through named places, routes, buildings, food, weather, work, and individual accounts, without prematurely declaring one definitive map or a single authoritative cultural description. The Assembly and Hall of Accounts preserve multiple testimonies. Local histories may grow in rooms, workshops, maps, instruments, and passages, with copies returned to the shared archive. Contact with other peoples should create exchange without absorbing the Mexiancas into another society.

### The Dad Convent

The Dad Convent is an established living settlement on a wooded estate, built by adult men who intend to remain. Its name is an old, slightly humorous name rather than a religious designation. Construction, household life, practical skill, hospitality, and continuity shape its daily life. The Great Hall, residential wing, Iron Room, foundry, carpentry hall, bathhouse, service court, and outer estate belong to its expanding building programme.

The Convent is a society as well as a construction project. Its residents have specialties, preferences, disagreements, households, and routines; roles remain tendencies rather than permanent assignments. The Registry preserves residents, buildings, plans, repairs, changes of use, shared events, and the history of objects and rooms. Its future as a tribal territory should grow out of this existing estate canon rather than overwrite it.

### Gold's on 5th and the Teal Titan circle

Gold's on 5th is a recognizable athletic and production setting associated with Marcus and the Teal Titan identity. The existing production identity remains distinct from the wider Teal Titan lineage. The developing social circle is organized around training, physical achievement, presentation, friendship, and public recognition. Its identity comes from what its members do together: maintaining equipment, sharing routines, welcoming visitors, arranging events, and deciding how they want their work represented.

Competition is welcome, but it does not make every relationship hostile. Members can be ambitious without being interchangeable, and a public nickname does not dictate a person's entire identity. The gym is a place with opening routines, equipment checks, changing rooms, familiar faces, post-training meals, and the practical work of keeping a shared facility usable.

### The Foggy Dark Expanse

The Foggy Dark Expanse remains a broad, incompletely mapped woodland region associated with Veyrthalis and its amber-lit, fog-heavy atmosphere. Its paths, clearings, old structures, and thresholds should be recorded with epistemic care: remembered, observed, inferred, proposed, or entered. It is not automatically claimed by the Redwood people or any other established population.

Small settlements, traveling groups, solitary houses, and temporary camps may emerge along its edges. The Digitorium, Temple of Moon, and other established mythic places retain their own records. New encounters can connect them to the tribal atlas without collapsing their distinct histories into a single map.

## How societies differ

Every people should develop its own combination of:

- **Homeland:** terrain, climate, water, routes, boundaries, neighboring populations, and places of significance.
- **Settlement:** houses, communal buildings, workshops, markets, training spaces, storage, public grounds, and ordinary infrastructure.
- **Daily life:** food, work, rest, clothing, music, games, transport, celebrations, and local habits.
- **Social organization:** households, leadership, councils, seniority, apprenticeship, membership, decision-making, and disagreement.
- **Continuity:** origin accounts, remembered events, inherited skills, archives, changes of custom, and unresolved testimony.
- **Exchange:** trade, hospitality, migration, shared projects, marriages or partnerships where established in fiction, and the movement of techniques and ideas.
- **Character:** named residents with individual agency, preferences, friendships, rivalries, ambitions, humor, and the ability to change.

No territory needs to fill every category immediately. Missing information remains an open field rather than an invitation to invent certainty. Distinctiveness should arise from concrete practices and lived history, not from assigning one personality to an entire population.

## The intertribal world

The territories form a network, not a ladder. A settlement may depend on another for materials, food, expertise, access, or companionship without becoming subordinate to it. Trade routes create repeated encounters; shared construction projects produce practical alliances; athletic events bring reputations across regional boundaries; travelers carry techniques and stories home. Disagreements over land, resources, representation, and responsibility can endure, but rivalry does not erase the possibility of kinship or future cooperation.

The Brotherhood of Giants remains a separate mythic framework for giant kindred, houses, stewardship, and dominion. It can intersect with territorial societies where the story establishes that connection, but it must not be treated as the universal ancestry or governing structure of every people. Likewise, Northgate's university and athletic institutions are not automatically tribes simply because they have strong identities. Their relationships to the wider social map should be developed deliberately.

## Generations and new peoples

A society changes when its members grow up, move away, return, bring partners or friends, learn from neighboring populations, and build homes of their own. The next generation inherits records and obligations, not a fixed personality. A younger resident may preserve an old custom for a new reason; an established family may adopt a practice brought by a newcomer; a group of travelers may settle in a place and gradually become a new people. These changes should appear in scenes before they are summarized as history.

New tribes should emerge from actual narrative pressure: a settlement becomes large enough to develop its own customs, a remote group maintains a distinct way of life, a shared undertaking creates a lasting identity, or a population chooses to establish a homeland. Do not populate the map with empty faction names. Give each new group people, places, and something it is trying to keep alive.

## Writing and presentation: Brawn Intelligence

The tribal world is written from inside its activity. Its intelligence is expressed through competent action, physical presence, practical judgment, social instinct, and the consequences of work. The prose can be muscular, confident, warm, competitive, and direct without making every character loud or reducing every scene to a contest.

Prefer the workshop being used to a paragraph explaining the workshop's clever design; show the meal being prepared, the equipment being checked, the road being repaired, the newcomer being introduced, and the argument that changes a decision. Keep the underlying research and continuity rigorous, but let the visible writing feel inhabited and active. The production interface should present this as ongoing work: places being entered, records being extended, buildings being maintained, and relationships acquiring history.

## Continuity and evidence rules

- Existing character, world, and project records remain authoritative for their established details.
- New territorial customs and histories are fictional development unless explicitly marked as established canon.
- Preserve the difference between observed source material, user-established canon, and newly proposed story material.
- Do not merge distinct people, lineages, production identities, or institutions merely because they share imagery or geography.
- Keep unresolved boundaries unresolved until the narrative establishes them.
- Preserve earlier versions and the provenance of changes.
- Let the world expand through scenes, artifacts, maps, and practical events; do not replace lived development with a static encyclopedia.

The first connected map is therefore a beginning, not a finished census. Redwood, Mexiancas, Dad Convent, Gold's on 5th, and the Foggy Dark Expanse now have a shared territorial frame while retaining their separate records. Their future will be written through the people who travel between them, build within them, and decide what should be carried forward.
