# DAD CONVENT — BUILD PROGRAMME

**Programme state:** Phase 0 — survey and enabling works  
**Operating rule:** There is always a real next job, and every job leaves a trace in the Registry.

## Site organization

The estate is organized as a working campus rather than a collection of isolated buildings.

- **North / arrival:** entrance road, gate, delivery turn, visitor parking, and the first view of the main house.
- **Central / communal:** main house, Great Hall site, kitchen yard, covered porch, and shared courtyard.
- **East / fabrication:** garage, service court, metal shop, carpentry hall, lumber storage, and loading access.
- **South / recreation:** Iron Room, open yard, outdoor work area, and future bathhouse.
- **West / quiet grounds:** orchard, walking paths, gardens, and future residential extensions.

This is a planning orientation, not a surveyed geographic claim. Exact dimensions and utility routes remain to be measured on site.

## Phase sequence

### Phase 0 — Survey and enabling works
- Walk and map the property; mark existing structures, trees, slopes, drainage, access, and utility connections.
- Establish a shared site plan and a numbered building / work-zone register.
- Identify safe delivery and equipment routes.
- Set up temporary covered material storage and a clean tool-return point.
- Record existing conditions with photographs and dated notes.

**Exit condition:** a coordinated site plan, an initial materials inventory, and a list of constraints before permanent construction begins.

### Phase 1 — The working core
- Repair and organize the existing garage and workshop.
- Build covered lumber and material storage.
- Establish a fabrication area with appropriate ventilation, fire protection, electrical planning, and safe clearances.
- Improve the main access road and drainage where needed.
- Create a temporary covered gathering and meal area near the work zone.

**Exit condition:** the residents can safely store materials, maintain tools, receive deliveries, and work without repeatedly dismantling the site.

### Phase 2 — The Great Hall
- Confirm the footprint, occupancy needs, structural approach, and service connections.
- Prepare foundations and drainage.
- Raise the primary timber structure.
- Enclose and weatherproof the building.
- Install electrical, plumbing, heating / cooling, and fire-safety systems as designed and inspected.
- Complete the kitchen connection, long-table room, storage, and upper gallery.
- Furnish the hall with durable pieces made in the carpentry shop.

**Exit condition:** the Great Hall is safe, weather-tight, serviced, furnished, and in daily use.

### Phase 3 — Residential expansion
- Add rooms in manageable wings rather than one oversized block.
- Provide private sleeping space, shared lounges, washrooms, laundry, storage, and accessible circulation.
- Connect wings with sheltered walkways and courtyards.
- Preserve quiet zones away from fabrication and vehicle traffic.

**Exit condition:** each household has privacy while remaining connected to shared life.

### Phase 4 — Iron Room and Bathhouse
- Build the Iron Room with floor loading, ceiling height, ventilation, equipment movement, and safe egress planned from the outset.
- Add the bathhouse only after water, drainage, heating, moisture control, and maintenance requirements are resolved.
- Connect both to the residential and communal routes without crossing heavy delivery paths.

**Exit condition:** recreation and recovery spaces are fully operational and maintainable.

### Phase 5 — Outer estate
- Extend paths, roads, bridges, retaining walls, gardens, orchard rows, and future building pads.
- Add lighting and wayfinding.
- Preserve existing mature trees where feasible.
- Keep the settlement's growth legible through courtyards, axes, and recognizable thresholds.

**Exit condition:** the estate reads as one coherent place, with room for future expansion.

## Working protocol

Every active work package receives:
- a unique identifier;
- a named purpose and location;
- a responsible lead and contributing residents;
- a materials / tools list;
- prerequisites and safety constraints;
- a status: queued, planned, active, paused, complete, or superseded;
- dated progress notes and a completion record.

No scene should imply that a building is complete merely because it has been proposed. Distinguish concept, design, active construction, weather-tight shell, serviced interior, and occupied building.
