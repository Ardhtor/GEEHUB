# Dimensional Conjuration
*The Beginning of the Dimension — world rules and discovery record*

**Status:** Incubating; rules are provisional and should be revised through scenes.  
**Primary story:** [The Beginning of the Dimension](../BEGINNING_OF_THE_DIMENSION.md)  
**Opening chapter:** [The First Room](FIRST_ROOM_CHAPTER_01.md)

Dimensional conjuration is the practice—or underlying capacity—by which distinct spaces, orders of reality, and possible worlds are brought into relation. It is not simply teleportation, portal construction, or travel to a second location. A conjuration can reveal that a room has more depth than its walls account for, allow a passage to become perceptible, preserve two incompatible descriptions of one event, or make a route available without immediately explaining where it leads. The first manifestations are small because the story begins before anyone understands the system.

## The first principles

**Relation before destination.** A crossing begins when two places or accounts become related. A destination need not be known in advance. The first passage between buildings appears before Joseph and Kirk know what lies beyond it.

**Continuity before spectacle.** A crossing does not erase the place from which someone departed. The first room remains the first room. Later scenes must carry forward what the characters remember, what they recorded, and what has physically changed.

**Observation is partial.** Different witnesses may notice different details. Their accounts can be compared, but they are not automatically merged into a single authoritative memory. The bell in the opening chapter is heard from different directions; both reports remain in the record.

**A threshold is not consent.** Seeing a passage does not oblige anyone to enter it. A door, path, or invitation may be observed without being accepted. The characters retain the right to wait, refuse, return, or ask for more information.

**Scale is relational.** A space may feel larger because the observer's sense of distance, body, attention, or possible movement has changed. When a physical transformation occurs, describe the body and its environmental consequences concretely. Do not use dimensional language to conceal the actual form of a change.

**The new state inherits the old state.** Every successful crossing retains traces of the earlier condition. A new room may have a wider doorway, but the old doorway remains part of the building's history. A changed body retains identity and the memory of its previous baseline.

## The stages of discovery

The opening sequence is not a fixed quest. It is a record of what the characters have so far encountered.

1. **Anomaly:** a detail appears that the ordinary account cannot explain: the page, the bell, the altered depth of the window.
2. **Comparison:** witnesses preserve their observations separately and establish where they agree or differ.
3. **Correspondence:** a second place or record presents a related feature: the open circle, the passage, a repeated bell, or a matching architectural mark.
4. **Threshold:** a route becomes perceptible. Its destination and risks remain uncertain until investigated.
5. **Crossing:** a character chooses to pass. The departure place, arrival place, and transition are all recorded.
6. **Aftereffect:** the crossing changes what is possible in one or both places. The effect may be architectural, bodily, social, temporal, or informational.
7. **Return and revision:** the character can return with an altered understanding. Earlier notes remain intact; new annotations are dated and attributed.

## The conjuror

“The dimension conjuror” is initially a name for the function that brings dimensions into relation, not a fully described character. It may eventually refer to a person, an office, a discipline, a distributed intelligence, or a combination of these. The story must discover which interpretation is warranted. Until then, do not assign the conjuror an appearance, biography, personal motive, or private history.

The same evidential restraint applies to Reagan Grimes. The only established note for this branch is that Reagan is down. Do not turn that statement into an invented biography or make him responsible for the first room's events without new source material.

## Temporal reversal

The Bussy Opener is a comic spell-name that accidentally becomes associated with a reversal. Its absurdity should survive the event. It is not an ancient solemn rite merely because it produces a serious consequence. The reversal carries Joseph and Kirk back toward the first room, but it does not delete the intervening journey. Their memories survive; the earlier environment is encountered again from a later vantage.

This establishes two different kinds of time movement:

- **Chronological return:** the characters revisit an earlier state while retaining later memories.
- **Historical revision:** the record gains a later annotation without changing what was written at the time.

The first is a story event; the second is an archival practice. Do not confuse them.

## Places and architecture

The first room, the street passage, the courtyard, the forest route, and the conjuration hall should each be established through physical details and use. A place becomes real through its construction, light, weather, access, maintenance, inhabitants, and the traces left by previous events. The later hall should not appear as an all-purpose fantasy lobby. It needs doors with distinct destinations, people who maintain it, rules for entry, spaces for waiting, records of failed or incomplete crossings, and ordinary reasons for visitors to be there.

A dimensional map should show known relations and mark unknown destinations as unknown. It should not draw a complete world from a single glimpse. The map changes as characters travel and return.

## The archive protocol

Every crossing record should distinguish:
- observer and date;
- departure place and arrival place;
- what was directly seen or heard;
- what was inferred;
- what remains unresolved;
- whether the crossing was chosen, accidental, or merely observed;
- what changed after return.

A later account may correct an earlier interpretation, but it must not silently replace the earlier testimony. This is the bridge to Veyrthalis's Digitorium and the Tractus's broader continuity practice.

## Open questions

The origin of the page remains unknown. The bell's source remains unknown. The open circle has not been assigned a stable meaning. The street passage has not yet been entered. The title “O Dreadmaster” has not been assigned to a person or institution. The conjuror's nature is unresolved. These are active absences, not gaps to fill with convenient answers.


## Pilling and forbidden thresholds

The third chapter introduces a provisional principle: **contact leaves a surface history**. Repeated contact may gather residue, wear a seam, polish a handle, loosen a label, or change the way a threshold is recognized. Such traces do not automatically prove a dimensional effect. Ordinary causes remain available until evidence distinguishes them. The archive therefore records the object, its location, the observer, and the circumstances before proposing a relation.

A second rule follows: **a prohibition is evidence of a claim, not proof of authority**. The word “forbidden” on a door tells the observers that someone marked a boundary. It does not establish who made the mark, whom it addresses, whether it remains in force, or what consequence follows from crossing it. Characters may treat a warning seriously without surrendering judgement to an unknown author. This extends the threshold principle: a visible door does not compel entry, and a written prohibition does not settle the ethics or mechanics of refusal.

The courtyard is now understood as potentially inhabited and maintained. A worker carrying a ladder is the first sign that its routes have practical uses beyond the protagonists' investigation. Future scenes should allow for residents, trades, deliveries, repairs, forgotten tools, and local knowledge that predates the visitors. The dimension's inhabitants must not be reduced to clues.
