# The Gravitation of Waste
*The Beginning of the Dimension — Chapter Six*

The waste district began as a route Joseph and Kirk took because it was quieter than the main walk between the Institute and the old municipal buildings. The service gate opened onto a strip of cracked paving, then a sloping yard bounded by retaining walls and a row of workshops whose doors had not all been opened in years. Beyond them lay the discarded remains of several different periods of construction: timber beams darkened by rain, metal frames, broken benches, obsolete machines, chipped tiles, torn canvas, lengths of cable, old doors, and furniture whose original rooms had disappeared. Nothing had been arranged as an exhibition. The things had simply been put aside, one after another, until the place acquired the density of a history that nobody had bothered to catalogue.

Joseph found that he liked the scale of it. The yard did not ask him to stand on a marked platform or compare himself with a number on a card. He could move among the objects at his own pace. His shoulders had broadened again since the measurements, and his chest and arms now gave his upper body a heavy, unmistakable volume. The width was visible even in an ordinary fitted training shirt; the fabric followed the rounded mass of his deltoids, crossed the deep chest, and stretched over the thickness of his back. His waist remained comparatively narrow, but his thighs had grown heavier and his calves carried more of the frame than before. He was learning to turn through narrow spaces without scraping a shoulder against a wall.

Kirk moved differently through the yard. His back had become the dominant feature of his build, a broad, dense sweep from the traps through the lats, while his shoulders and arms had thickened around it. His chest was deep, his forearms heavy, and his legs had developed into a firm base beneath the upper mass. When he lifted a bent steel frame to see whether it could be salvaged, the movement looked less like a demonstration of strength than a practical adjustment to the space. He turned the frame, tested its balance, and set it down carefully. The men had become extraordinarily muscular, but the district gave them work to do that was not arranged for an audience.

Seth and Nick watched from across the yard, their familiar viewing position looking over the activity and out toward the viewer. They did not step into Joseph and Kirk's conversation. Their commentary followed the visible changes: the way Joseph had to angle his shoulders through the gap between two stacks, the way Kirk's back altered the fall of his shirt, and the way both men's legs had continued to develop so that their growing upper bodies remained supported by a powerful lower frame.

SETH: This is a different kind of scale from the measurement room. There, the body is compared with a fixed wall and a set of instruments. Here, you see it among things that have already been used, broken, moved, and left behind. Joseph is broad enough to change how he approaches the passage. Kirk can move that frame, but he's still taking care with its balance. The size is real because it changes the work.

NICK: And neither build has stopped developing. Joseph's chest and arms carry the eye first, then the width of his back and the thickness through his legs. Kirk's back creates that enormous outline, but look at the forearms and thighs too. The whole body is answering the same escalation. They are not two versions of one physique.

A maintenance worker came out of the nearest workshop carrying a clipboard. He recognized the men from the courtyard and asked whether the keeper had sent them to inspect the yard. Joseph said they had come to look around. The worker nodded as though that were a perfectly adequate reason. He explained that the district had no single owner in the way the Institute did. Different departments had deposited things there over many years. Some materials were awaiting repair, some were awaiting collection, and some had been forgotten. The worker had been trying to establish which was which, but the records were incomplete.

"Then why not clear it?" Kirk asked.

"Because clearing it would mean knowing what it is," the worker replied. "Half the labels are gone. Some of these things came from buildings that aren't on the current plan. If we throw everything out, we lose the evidence."

The answer gave Joseph a new way of looking at the yard. The waste was not merely the remainder of useful things. It was a record of decisions made elsewhere: objects no longer wanted, objects whose purpose had changed, objects that had outlasted the rooms built around them. The men began sorting a small area beside the workshop. Joseph separated intact timber from split boards. Kirk examined the metal frames and identified two that could be repaired. They found a pair of broad handles, a damaged bench, a set of weights with mismatched collars, and several pieces of thick rubber flooring. The worker marked each item on his clipboard.

By the end of the morning, the cleared patch had become a work surface. Joseph carried the heavier timber to one side, adjusting his grip as his enlarged forearms and chest changed the distance between his hands. Kirk lifted the bench frame and placed it on blocks so the worker could inspect the joints. Their strength made the work easier, but their size also changed the logistics. They needed room to turn, wider routes for carrying long objects, and a place to set things down without obstructing one another. Their bodies had become part of the yard's practical geometry.

A bell sounded from the old municipal building. It was not the Institute bell, and it did not have the same clear tone. The note was lower, almost swallowed by the surrounding walls. Several men who had been working in the district paused and looked toward the far end of the yard. The maintenance worker checked his clipboard, then walked to a narrow passage behind the workshops. Joseph and Kirk followed at a distance.

The passage ended at a covered store with a corrugated roof. Its doors stood open. Inside were objects that seemed to have been collected from unrelated places: a cracked mirror, a child's-sized chair, a heavy wooden cabinet, a stack of picture frames, a brass lamp, and a group of old display figures wrapped in cloth. The worker said the store had been sealed for years, though he could not remember who had ordered it. Dust lay evenly across the floor except for a narrow track leading toward the rear wall.

Kirk noticed the track first. Joseph noticed the object at the end of it.

It was a male double-figure made from two male dolls joined side by side. Each had its own head and face. Their bodies were connected in one continuous construction, giving the object a shared lower silhouette while preserving the distinction between the two faces. The surface was worn, with small cracks in the finish and a darkened seam where the two figures met. It was not a toy in the ordinary sense. Its proportions and presentation suggested that it had once been made as a display object, perhaps for a collection or a room whose purpose had been lost.

The worker did not touch it. He said it had been recorded under several different descriptions, none of them consistent. In one old inventory it was listed as a pair of figures; in another, as a single joined object. A handwritten note called it the Brothers. No record explained who had made it or why it had been stored there.

Joseph and Kirk stood before it for some time. They did not agree on what they were seeing. Joseph thought the two faces seemed to look in slightly different directions. Kirk thought that was an effect of the uneven light and the object's worn surface. Neither account was accepted as the final one. They took photographs from the front and both sides, then asked the worker to note the object's condition before anyone moved it.

Seth and Nick remained outside the store, visible through the open doors from the far side of the yard.

SETH: The waste district has produced something that does not fit the categories the worker has. Two figures, one object; two faces, one joined construction. It has been moved between inventories without anyone deciding what it is.

NICK: Keep the distinction between what is visible and what is suspected. We can see the two heads and the shared form. We can see the old finish, the seam, and the different angles of the faces. We cannot yet say that it moves, or that it has any intention. The record begins with careful looking.

The worker found a temporary plinth and a clean cloth. Joseph and Kirk helped move the plinth into a better-lit part of the store, but the object itself remained where it was until its weight and construction could be checked. The worker opened a new condition sheet. He wrote: two male figures conjoined side by side; two distinct heads and faces; continuous joined body construction; surface worn; provenance uncertain; movement unverified. Beneath that he copied the informal name, The Brothers, and added the location where it had been found.

The keeper from the Institute arrived later that afternoon. He had heard that Joseph and Kirk were helping in the yard and wanted to see what they had discovered. He looked at the object without touching it, then examined the photographs and the worker's notes. He did not offer an explanation. Instead, he asked whether the store could be kept open while the district was surveyed. The worker said that would require a new access record and someone responsible for the inventory. The keeper agreed to arrange it.

By then, the waste district had begun to change around the men. The cleared patch was no longer a dumping place but a repair yard. A few residents had arrived with tools. Someone had brought food from the Institute dining room. A broad table was assembled from two reclaimed sections, and the men sat around it while the worker explained the old labels. The meal was simple and filling. Joseph and Kirk ate more than they had at breakfast; their appetites had risen with the continuing growth, and neither needed to pretend otherwise. Their shirts were still fitted, but the shoulders and sleeves had become close enough that the tailoring room would need to take new measurements.

The next change came while they were clearing a route beside the store. Joseph lifted a long timber beam with help from the worker, and a familiar pressure gathered through his shoulders and chest. His frame expanded in a slow, unmistakable progression. His deltoids rounded further, his chest gained another layer of depth, and his back thickened until the shirt lay tightly across it. His arms grew heavier through the upper and lower sections, his forearms becoming especially dense around the wrists. The change continued through his waist, thighs, and calves, preserving the strong taper of his build while increasing the mass of the whole frame. He set the beam down before the pressure became distracting and waited for the sensation to pass.

Kirk's change followed a different route. It began through his upper back, widening the line of his shoulders and deepening the muscular sweep beneath them. His chest thickened, his arms expanded, and his forearms gained a powerful density. His thighs grew broader and heavier, and his calves filled out beneath them, giving his stance an even more substantial foundation. The scale of both men had moved beyond the measurements taken in the Institute. They were not simply becoming larger in the same proportions; their individual patterns were intensifying.

The worker brought them water and asked whether they wanted the keeper. Both said they were steady. The change had left them breathing hard, but they remained alert and in control. Their clothes were now too close through the shoulders and thighs for comfortable work. The worker found two spare sleeveless tops in a supply cabinet, and they changed before continuing. The yard had no fitting room, so they used the clean store office and left their previous shirts folded on a shelf for the tailoring staff to collect.

The new size altered the repair work immediately. Joseph could lift the beam more easily, but he needed a wider turning space. Kirk could hold the bench frame steady with one hand while the worker checked the joint, but the frame itself looked smaller against his expanded chest and arms. Their reach had increased slightly, their leverage had changed, and the old work gloves no longer fitted. The keeper recorded the event as a further growth stage, with measurements to be taken at the Institute. He also noted that the waste district had become the first place outside the training floor where a growth event had been observed during ordinary work.

The men returned to the store before leaving. The Brothers stood where they had been found, now with a condition sheet and a series of photographs. The two faces remained distinct. One appeared to look toward the door; the other toward the rear wall. Joseph still thought the angles were different. Kirk still thought the light could explain them. Neither withdrew his observation. The worker locked the store and placed the new record in a folder marked UNRESOLVED OBJECTS.

On the walk back, Joseph looked over the yard. The broken bench would be repaired. The timber would be measured and cut. The rubber flooring might be used to make a better carry lane. The old handles could be cleaned and tested. The objects had not become valuable because someone had declared them rare. They had become valuable because the men had found ways to look at them again.

The keeper called the phenomenon the Gravitation of Waste. It was not a claim that discarded things physically pulled people toward them. It was the name given to a recurring pattern: men were choosing the neglected routes, finding objects whose histories had been interrupted, and making new uses without erasing the old ones. The district was drawing people through the possibility of another purpose.

Seth and Nick closed the day's account from across the yard.

SETH: The men came here because it was quiet. They found a repair yard, a store, an object with two faces, and a place where the next stage of growth happened outside the gym. The district is no longer just where things go when the rest of the world is finished with them.

NICK: And look at the scale now. Joseph and Kirk have both grown again. The bodies are bigger, but so is the world they can act in. There are more things to lift, repair, record, and understand. The waste is becoming a new kind of beginning.

The folder closed with the photographs of the Brothers, the repair list, and the new growth record. Nothing in it explained the bell, the doll, or the attraction of the yard. It did preserve what had happened. The Institute had a new route to maintain, the municipal worker had a district to inventory, and Joseph and Kirk had found a place where their growing strength could become useful. The next visit would begin from this altered baseline.
