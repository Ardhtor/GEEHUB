# The Hall of Three Powers
*The Beginning of the Dimension — Chapter Ten*

The lower works were quieter than the upper forest, but they were not still. Water travelled through the stone channels with a steady underground sound, gathering beneath grates and passing behind the walls before emerging farther down the slope. The air carried the smell of wet oak, iron, and earth that had been sealed away from sunlight for a long time. Joseph and Kirk returned with Mara, Pell, and Orlan after the crew had checked the roof supports and secured the lower terrace. They brought the new drainage plan, the copied diagram of the divided square, two lanterns, and a coil of line. Nobody had come to claim the lower works. They had come to learn whether the route could be opened without damaging the forest or endangering the people who used it.

Seth and Nick held their familiar position at the far end of the hall, beyond the worktable and the line of lanterns. The viewer's angle looked across the room toward the crew, with the old oak frame visible behind them and a narrow reflection of the water channel running along the stone floor. The narrators did not join the inspection. They spoke across the scene, sharing the view with us.

SETH: Look at the construction down here. It's not a throne room. It's a working passage with a drainage system, storage, supports, and a route that has to remain usable. You can see the forest above it in the materials, but this is the part that carries the maintenance.

NICK: And look at the water. It isn't decoration. Every channel has a fall, every grate has a purpose, and the people here have to know where it goes. Keep that in mind, because the first thing that changes is going to happen below the floor.

Mara set the plan on the worktable. Pell lowered the lantern toward the inspection channel while Orlan checked the old oak frame against the measurements taken the previous day. The frame had been made from heavy boards, carefully joined at the corners, with two crossbars set at different heights. The divided-square mark had been cut into its upper rail. Nothing about it suggested a door, though the opening was wide enough for two people to stand within it. Joseph compared the mark with the drawing. Kirk checked the base for wear, finding two polished patches where the frame had repeatedly rested against stone. The object had been moved many times, but the direction of its use remained unclear.

The water beneath the grate began to rise.

It did not flood the channel. It lifted slowly until its surface came level with the iron bars, then held there without overflowing. The current continued beneath it, visible as a faint movement of darker water under the reflection. Pell stepped back and reached for the hand pump. Mara asked him to wait. She watched the level against a small mark cut into the side of the stone and saw that it had risen without any corresponding change in the upper drainage line. The crew checked the second channel. It was dry. The lower basin was receiving water from somewhere that did not appear on the plan.

The reflection changed before the water itself did. The lanterns remained above it, but the dark surface began to show a depth that the shallow channel could not contain. A long blue distance opened beneath the stone floor, and a line of pale light moved across it like the surface of a sea seen from below. The air grew cool. A soft pressure reached the soles of their shoes, then travelled through the timber supports and into the worktable.

A figure appeared in the water's reflection. He did not rise out of the channel or displace a drop. His presence gathered in the depth beneath the surface, as though the reflection had become a place with its own distance. When he spoke, the water carried the sound through the floor.

NEPTUNE: You have opened the channel, but you have not yet learned what it remembers.

Joseph looked toward the others. Kirk kept his eyes on the water. Mara held the plan flat with one hand as the paper began to tremble in the pressure. Nobody mistook the voice for the drainage crew. Nobody claimed to understand it.

SETH: There he is. Look at the depth in that reflection. The channel is only a few inches deep, but the view beneath it is opening out much farther than the stonework can account for.

NICK: And the water hasn't broken the room. It's showing another scale underneath the one they measured. That's Neptune's territory here: depth, pressure, and memory held below the visible surface.

NEPTUNE: Water keeps the shape of every route that has carried it. The channels above have been repaired. The lower passage has been covered. The old current remains.

Mara asked whether the water was safe. Neptune's reflection turned toward her. He did not answer with a command. He asked her where the crew had measured the channel, how long they had watched it, and whether the plan showed the old outlet. Mara answered each question carefully. She described the level marks, the dry side channel, and the missing connection beneath the slope. Neptune listened, then shifted the reflection. A narrow line appeared beneath the surface, extending beyond the wall and bending under the old oak frame. The line followed the course of a buried waterway. It was not a complete map, but it gave the crew a place to test.

Pell placed a second marker on the plan. The new line corresponded with the lower diagram, though the scale differed. He checked the level again and found the water settling. The route had not been forced open. It had been made visible long enough for them to compare its direction with the records.

A low thunder travelled through the roots above them.

It was not the small vibration of the bell mechanism. The sound arrived from the upper forest, passed down through the great tree, and struck the lower hall as a single, deliberate roll. The lantern flames leaned. Dust lifted from the oak frame. At the top of the stair, the daylight changed from green to a hard silver-grey. A storm had gathered over the Crownheart, although the morning had been clear when they entered the lower terrace.

The light at the stair became brighter. A second figure stood there, framed by the opening and the distant wet trunks. The thunder did not drown his voice. It seemed to wait for it.

ZEUS: A hidden route is not a public decision. If this passage is to be opened, let the people who maintain it see what is being asked of them.

His words carried the authority of a decision already placed before a gathering. Yet he did not order the crew to proceed. He looked at Mara's plan, at the marked waterline, and at the workers who had to answer for the route after the encounter ended.

ZEUS: I can call the storm. I cannot make the work harmless by declaring it so. Show me what the structure can bear. Show everyone what remains uncertain. Then the decision can be made in the open.

SETH: That's a different kind of pressure. Neptune is showing them the waterway beneath the floor. Zeus is making the question public. This isn't one force doing everything; they're approaching the same place from different directions.

NICK: And the crew still has the plan. They're the people who know the supports, the drains, and the work that has to happen tomorrow. A storm doesn't replace a structural survey. Keep that distinction.

A gust entered the stairwell and moved across the lower hall. One of the older roof braces shifted with a dry crack. Pell called out and pointed toward the beam above the worktable. The support had held through the previous inspection, but the change in pressure had loosened a joint that was already weakened by water. The beam began to settle. Mara moved the plan away from the table. Kirk stepped toward Pell, while Joseph reached for the lanterns.

A man crossed the hall from the side passage. He did not arrive with thunder or rise from the water. He came directly toward the slipping support, saw where the weight was travelling, and placed himself beneath the beam before it could fall across the worktable. His shoulders and arms took the load. The timber came down against his hands and stopped. The floor gave a small shudder under his feet, but he remained steady.

GOD DADDY: Get everyone clear first. The room can be repaired.

He spoke without raising his voice. The crew moved toward the stair, and he kept the beam supported while Kirk helped Pell guide the lanterns out of the way. Joseph checked that Mara had the plan and the copied diagrams. Orlan counted the people leaving. Nobody was told to stay behind to prove anything. Once the others were clear, God Daddy shifted his stance and let the damaged support settle onto a temporary brace. He did not hold the building up indefinitely. He gave the crew enough time to get clear and enough room to return with the right materials.

The storm continued above the roots. God Daddy looked at Joseph and Kirk, then at the route that remained open beyond the lower hall.

GOD DADDY: You can be strong and still accept help. You can protect a place without deciding who belongs in it. No door gets to claim the person standing in front of it.

Joseph nodded. Kirk looked back at the brace. They had heard the words, but the action had come first: the beam had been held, the people had been moved, and the way out had remained open. God Daddy's strength had made room for other people rather than making them dependent on him.

NEPTUNE: The lower current has not changed its course. It is waiting to be understood.

ZEUS: And the storm will remain until the crew has a clear view of the damage. No test is worth hiding the cost.

God Daddy glanced toward the upper stair. His attention was not fixed on the gods or on the visitors. He watched the crew gather outside, making sure everyone had come out safely. Neptune's reflection remained beneath the grate. Zeus stood where the daylight entered. Their domains did not combine into one power. The water kept its depth; the storm held its public consequence; protection remained close to the people who needed room to act.

SETH: Now you can see all three positions. Neptune has shown them the route under the roots. Zeus has made the structural question answerable in public. God Daddy has protected the people in the room and kept the exit open. None of those actions is interchangeable.

NICK: And notice that the crew hasn't surrendered its judgement. Mara still has to revise the plan. Pell still has to inspect the brace. Orlan still has to enter the work into the service ledger. The gods have changed the conditions, but the people who live here still have work to do.

Outside, the storm loosened. Rain moved through the upper canopy in a brief silver sheet, then thinned as the cloud travelled east. The crew returned to the lower terrace only after the damaged support had been marked and the immediate danger had passed. God Daddy helped Pell place a second brace beneath the beam, then stepped away so the worker could check the fit. He did not insist on remaining at the centre of the repair. Once the timber was stable, he moved aside and let Mara inspect the joint.

Neptune's reflection narrowed into the channel. Before it disappeared, the blue line beneath the water became visible one final time. It ran beyond the lower hall, under the oak frame, and toward a larger basin farther beneath the sequoias. The current followed the same direction. Mara copied the course into the plan and marked it as an observed correspondence requiring a physical survey. The water level returned to its earlier mark.

Zeus remained at the stair until the crew had completed its first inspection. He watched the work and asked Mara what she would need before the route could be opened. She said a new support, a dry-weather reading, and a second survey of the hidden outlet. Zeus accepted the list. He did not replace it with a decree. When the storm had cleared the tree line, he was gone from the stair, leaving the smell of rain and a few drops of water on the stone.

God Daddy stayed until the crew had finished securing the lower passage. He asked the men whether they needed another pair of hands. Pell gave him a short length of timber to carry, and he took it without ceremony. Later, when the workers gathered their tools, he stood beside the entrance and let each person pass first. No one had to ask permission to leave. No one had to prove they deserved protection.

The day's record became three separate entries. The waterway beneath the lower hall was marked as a Neptune correspondence: depth revealed, route not yet surveyed. The storm and its demand for public inspection were recorded under Zeus: authority made accountable to consequence. The rescue of the crew and the temporary support were entered under God Daddy: chosen protection, practical help, and the preservation of free passage. The names were not added to the municipal plan as if they were contractors. They were recorded in the mythic ledger, with the material observations kept beside them.

That evening, Joseph and Kirk returned to their room. The copied diagrams lay across the table, with Mara's revised waterline beneath them. Beyond the window, the sequoias moved gently in the clearing air. The Crownheart gallery was lit again, and somewhere along the ridge a worker was closing the tool store for the night. The lower route remained closed pending a survey, but it was no longer an empty mark on a drawing. Three distinct powers had acted around it, and each had left a different consequence.

Seth and Nick watched the upper forest settle beneath the last of the rain. Their view remained across the landscape, with the great trunks filling the distance and the line of warm gallery lights leading back toward the residential ridge.

SETH: That's what I want you to remember from this one. The gods haven't replaced the people doing the work. They've made the stakes clearer, and each one has had to act according to what he stands for.

NICK: And the route is still waiting. The water has shown them a direction; the storm has exposed a structural problem; the protection has kept the crew safe. The next decision belongs to the people who will maintain the passage after everyone else has gone.

The lower works were not opened that day. The brace would need another inspection, the drainage plan would need revision, and the hidden basin would need to be surveyed before anyone could travel through it. But the hall had become more than a collection of old rooms. It had become a place where Neptune, Zeus, and God Daddy were known by what they did, not merely by the titles given to them. The water remembered. The storm answered. The protection made room. The work continued.
