# The Nightmare Doll — Research Dossier

**Branch:** The Beginning of the Dimension  
**Artifact class:** Male double-figure / conjoined doll  
**First recorded location:** The waste district beneath the old yard  
**Status:** Story artifact; research-informed design, not a medical specimen

## Canonical form

The Nightmare Doll is male and is shaped as two male dolls conjoined side by side. It must read immediately as two distinct doll figures joined along their adjacent sides, not as one ordinary doll with two faces and not as a pair merely tied together. Each side retains its own head, face, neck, shoulder line, arms, torso identity, and legs; the joining seam runs continuously through the adjacent flanks and the shared construction. Their heads may sit at slightly different heights and their expressions may differ. The combined silhouette is asymmetrical but balanced enough to stand. The material is old and tactile: porcelain-like faces, painted features, jointed hands, worn fabric, repaired seams, and a visible central join. It is a male doll in its own right, not a depiction of a living person.

The two faces are individually legible. One can be watchful and severe; the other quieter or faintly amused. Their difference should be expressed through gaze, brow, mouth, and slight head angle rather than grotesque distortion. The clothes are male miniature garments assembled from discarded gymwear, old uniform cloth, and upholstery remnants. Avoid treating the doll as a generic horror prop. Its unsettling quality comes from the precision of the double construction, its stillness, and the sense that it has been carefully kept after being discarded.

## Anatomical research: lateral conjunction

The closest anatomical comparison is parapagus, a category of laterally conjoined twins. In parapagus dicephalus, two distinct heads are joined on a shared trunk; the extent of duplication varies, and anatomical cases may have different numbers of upper and lower limbs. This is a useful vocabulary for understanding how two lateral identities can occupy one continuous construction, but it is not an exact match for the doll's deliberately legible two-doll design. The doll preserves two recognizable miniature male figures joined side by side rather than reproducing a specific clinical configuration.

A radiology review describes parapagus as lateral union, commonly involving a shared pelvis and varying degrees of trunk conjunction. It distinguishes dicephalus, with two heads and a fused trunk, from diprosopus, with one head and two faces. This distinction is important for the design: the Nightmare Doll has two heads, not one double-faced head. [Research: Conjoined twins — role of imaging and recent advances](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5769666/).

A study of a historical anatomical museum specimen curated by Jacob Henle documents a parapagus dicephalus form with duplicated upper structures merging into shared lower structures. Its digital visualization compares specimen photography with a computer model, showing how careful documentation can preserve complex surface form. For GEEHUB, the useful lesson is observational: record the doll from front, back, both sides, and above; describe the join and the changing silhouette rather than relying on a single frontal image. The doll remains an invented object, not a reconstruction of that specimen. [Research: Extended reality visualization of medical museum specimens](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9489256/).

A published male parapagus dicephalus case report provides a documented example of two male heads on a compound body and emphasizes that limb number and the degree of duplication can vary. It supports the terminology and the principle that a double form cannot be understood from the heads alone. It does not determine the doll's anatomy or imply that the doll represents a real individual. [Research: A parapagus dicephalus tripus tribrachius conjoined twin](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33008470/).

## Museum-object research and visual method

Historical anatomical collections show how unusual forms have been preserved, catalogued, photographed, and reinterpreted over time. The Nightmare Doll borrows the museum's habits of attention—object number, provenance, condition notes, measured views, repairs, and changes in interpretation—not the sensational display of human remains. Its archive should treat it as an artifact recovered from the waste district. The record can include material observations, the position in which it was found, old repairs, fabric fragments, paint loss, and each occasion on which its orientation changes.

The medical-museum visualization study is especially useful as a method reference: a specimen can be documented through multiple views and a stable three-dimensional representation. For the doll, this becomes a production protocol: establish a neutral orthographic set, preserve the two separate faces, map the continuous side join, and track any new marks or displaced objects between observations. The visual record should distinguish what the camera shows from what the witnesses infer.

## GEEHUB interpretation

The doll appears in the Gravitation of Waste, where discarded forms gather rather than vanish. It is not established as a supernatural agent. Its apparent changes of orientation remain observations made by Joseph, Kirk, Seth, and Nick; the cause is unresolved. The men leave small discarded objects nearby, turning the site into an informal archive of previous forms. The doll becomes a focal artifact because it embodies the district's central question: when two forms are joined, what remains distinct, and what becomes shared?

Its name, **Nightmare Doll**, belongs to the residents' response to it. The research label is **male conjoined double-figure doll**. The Brothers is the informal name for its two faces, not a replacement for the artifact's canonical name.

## Production checklist

- Preserve two distinct male heads and two distinct faces.
- Make the side-by-side conjoining unmistakable through the full height of the figure.
- Keep the shared seam visible without turning it into a crude wound.
- Record the front, back, left, right, and top views.
- Keep the two expressions different but restrained.
- Use worn miniature male garments and mixed recovered materials.
- Maintain a dated condition record; do not silently change the design between appearances.
- Keep anatomical research clearly separated from fictional canon.
