# Boner Alert

*Unnumbered scene draft · Joey and Kirk*

The first person to notice was Kirk.

Joey had stopped halfway through the doorway, one hand resting against the frame, apparently distracted by something happening in the adjoining room. He was wearing his usual grey tank and short posing trunks, and the afternoon light caught the breadth of his shoulders as he turned.

"What's funny?"

"Nothing," Kirk said.

"You're looking at me."

"I am."

Joey looked down, then back up. It took him a moment to understand what Kirk had noticed. His expression changed, first to amusement and then to a rather pleased smile.

"Oh. That."

Kirk leaned back in his chair. "You seem remarkably unconcerned."

"Why would I be concerned?"

There was something different about Joey's confidence lately. He had become accustomed to being noticed, but he hadn't become indifferent to it. If anything, he seemed to enjoy discovering just how much attention his presence could command.

Kirk stood and approached him.

"You're getting impossible," he said.

Joey grinned. "You've been saying that for weeks."

"And you've been getting bigger for weeks."

"Then I suppose we're both consistent."

He stepped aside to let Kirk pass, but Kirk stopped beside him and looked him over again. Joey caught the glance.

"Boner alert," Joey announced, laughing.

From the adjoining room came a voice.

"Whose?"

Joey looked at Kirk.

"Apparently we're having a meeting."

The man in the adjoining room appeared in the doorway with a mug in his hand. He took in Joey's grin, Kirk's expression, and the fact that neither of them seemed particularly interested in explaining themselves.

"I asked a question," he said.

"And we heard you," Joey replied.

"That isn't an answer."

"It's the answer you're getting."

The man gave Kirk a long look. Kirk raised his mug in a small, innocent salute. The visitor shook his head and went back into the other room, muttering something about the standards of conversation in the residence.

Joey laughed after him. "He's going to ask again."

"Probably."

"Are you going to tell him?"

"Tell him what?"

Joey turned towards Kirk, enjoying the performance now. "You were the one staring."

"I was looking at you. There's a difference."

"Is there?"

"There is when I'm trying to have a conversation."

Joey's smile softened. He shifted his weight, the movement making the doorway seem narrower than it had a moment earlier. Kirk had seen him take up more space over the months: first in the gym, then at the dining table, then in the ordinary passageways where the old proportions of the residence had begun to look like somebody else's measurements. He knew the broad outline of Joey's development. He had watched the training, heard the complaints about shirts, and helped carry the first pieces of equipment into the expanded room. But familiarity had not made Joey's presence ordinary.

"You're doing it again," Joey said.

"What?"

"Looking."

Kirk glanced at his face. "You keep interrupting me."

"Maybe I like the attention."

"That much is obvious."

Joey laughed and moved out of the doorway. "Come on. Walk with me."

They crossed the corridor together. The adjoining room was occupied now by two men sorting through a delivery of folded clothing. One of them held up a garment, checked the label, and put it on the correct pile. The other was trying to work out why the delivery sheet listed two different measurements for the same item.

Joey paused to look.

"That's mine."

"We know," one of them said. "We're trying to decide whether the supplier made a mistake or whether you've changed again."

"Could be both."

Kirk picked up the sheet. The old measurement had been copied from the previous fitting; the new one had been added in a different hand. There was no date beside it.

"Don't guess," he said. "Check the fitting book."

The man with the garment nodded and went to fetch it. Joey watched him leave, then looked at Kirk.

"You're very serious about my clothes."

"I'm serious about not ordering the wrong ones twice."

"That happened once."

"Three times."

"The first two were experiments."

"They were shirts."

"They were experimental shirts."

The men at the table laughed. Kirk folded the delivery sheet and set it beneath the mug so it would not get lost. Joey picked up the garment and held it against himself, judging the cut. It was close through the shoulders but too narrow across the chest. He handed it back without irritation.

"Keep it for the next fitting. I want to see what the tailor can do with the pattern."

The practical decision settled the matter. One of the men wrote a note on the delivery sheet; the other added the garment to the fitting pile. Joey had already moved on, asking whether the workshop had finished the new bench supports.

As they left, Kirk fell into step beside him.

"You know," he said, "you could have just said you liked being looked at."

"I did."

"You made an announcement."

"People remember announcements."

"Unfortunately."

Joey bumped his shoulder lightly against Kirk's. The contact was casual, but Kirk had to adjust his step to accommodate the difference in their size. Joey noticed and immediately slowed down.

"Sorry."

"You're fine."

"I keep forgetting."

"Then keep remembering."

Joey nodded. They walked the rest of the corridor at Kirk's pace. At the turn, Joey stopped to let a pair of residents pass with a long package between them. He held the door while they manoeuvred it through, then asked where it was going. The answer sent him looking towards the workshop, where another small problem was waiting to be solved.

Kirk watched him take an interest in it. The teasing had not distracted Joey from the rest of the day; it had simply become one more part of it. That was what Kirk liked about him. Joey could enjoy being admired without demanding that admiration become the only thing happening in a room.

Behind them, someone called, "Hey, Joey."

He turned.

"Boner alert!"

The shout came with a burst of laughter from the adjoining room. Joey closed his eyes for a second, then looked at Kirk.

"See? Announcements."

Kirk shook his head, smiling despite himself.

"You're never going to let that go."

"Not a chance."

By dinner, the phrase had appeared on the corner of the workshop noticeboard in small, neat lettering. Nobody admitted to writing it. Joey saw it, read it twice, and left it there.
