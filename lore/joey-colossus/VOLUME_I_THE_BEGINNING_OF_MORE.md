# Volume I — The Beginning of More

*The Colossus Chronicles · Book One*

## Chapter 1 — The First Record

The gym at the residence was still quiet when Joey arrived. The fluorescent lights had come on in sections, leaving the far end of the room in a soft grey half-light. A row of benches stood beneath the mirrors; the dumbbell racks were orderly, the floor recently cleaned, and the old wall clock made a small mechanical sound every second. Joey liked the place before anyone else arrived. There was no audience to perform for and no need to explain why he had come in early. He could warm up, train, write down what happened, and leave with the private satisfaction of having kept a promise to himself.

At the registration desk, Martin had left a new training book with Joey's name on the cover. Martin was a broad-shouldered, fair-haired former rugby player whose good humour tended to arrive before he did. He had ruled the first page into columns: date, bodyweight, session, working sets, recovery, observations. Beneath those he had added one final heading in block capitals: **WHAT CHANGED?**

Joey laughed when he saw it. “You make this look like a research department.”

“It is a research department,” Martin said from behind him. “You are the research.”

The joke stayed with Joey through the warm-up. He moved through his first working sets with concentration, not rushing the repetitions or trying to impress the empty room. His shoulders and chest were already well developed, but he had begun to notice how differently his body responded when he treated the whole training cycle as one connected undertaking. Back work improved his posture; leg sessions changed the way he stood; careful recovery made the next session more productive. The notebook began to collect more than numbers. It recorded the small discoveries that made the work feel cumulative.

At the end of the session, Joey stood before the mirror and turned slightly, checking the balance of his frame. His shirt sat differently across the upper back. His arms filled the sleeves more firmly, and the line of his shoulders looked rounder. Nothing about it was supernatural yet. What mattered was that the change had arrived without him having to invent it. Martin came over with two coffees and looked at the page.

“That's a good first entry,” he said.

Joey wrote one more sentence beneath the numbers: *I want to see what happens if I keep going.*

## Chapter 2 — The Men Who Stayed

The residence was not a competition house, although competition found its way into nearly everything. It was a place for adult men who liked training, liked company, and had discovered that a shared routine could become a kind of friendship. Daniel, a dark-haired mechanic with a dry sense of humour, treated every piece of gym equipment as something that ought to be repairable. Ellis, a tall blond swimmer, preferred long sessions and careful technique. Noah was a cheerful, compact powerlifter who could turn a quiet breakfast into a debate about the best post-training meal. Martin was the loudest of them, but he was also the first to notice when someone had missed a session or seemed unusually withdrawn.

Joey found his place among them gradually. He was not required to become the loudest man in the room or to turn every workout into a demonstration. The others learned his habits: the way he checked the collars twice, the way he listened before answering, the quick smile that appeared when someone made a particularly ridiculous claim about a new personal best. He learned theirs in return. Daniel hummed while repairing things. Ellis counted repetitions under his breath. Noah had a habit of announcing that he was “only having a small breakfast” before assembling a plate large enough to require two hands.

The first group photograph was taken after a Saturday session. They stood in a line in ordinary training clothes, flushed from the workout, laughing because Martin had set the camera timer too short and nearly missed the frame. Joey kept a copy in the notebook. The photograph became the first artifact in a story that none of them yet knew they were making.

## Chapter 3 — The Baseline Moves

Over the next months, Joey's training acquired a rhythm. His log showed steady progression, but the more interesting entries described how his body was changing as a whole. His chest had gained depth; his deltoids gave his shoulders a rounder outline; his back was becoming wider, and his arms looked fuller even when relaxed. His legs were no longer an afterthought. The work had become more balanced, and the result was visible in the way he moved through the residence: more grounded, more assured, and increasingly at ease with being noticed.

One evening, after a particularly good session, Joey found Martin waiting by the noticeboard. A new sheet had been pinned there: **RESIDENTS' CUP — FIRST SEASON**. The event would be a friendly series of strength, presentation, and training challenges. Nobody was being asked to become someone else. The point was to celebrate the work and give the residents a shared occasion.

Joey read the sheet twice. “Are you entering?”

Martin grinned. “Obviously. Are you?”

Joey looked down at his training book. The first page was already full. He thought of the first photograph, the early mornings, the small changes that had become a larger pattern.

“Yeah,” he said. “I think I am.”

The first volume closes with the group preparing for the Residents' Cup. Joey's development is real within the story, but the scale of what is coming remains unknown. The notebook has become a record of continuity: not just what he lifted, but who was there, what they noticed, and what they built together.
