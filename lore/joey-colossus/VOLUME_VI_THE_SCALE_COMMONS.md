# Volume VI — The Scale Commons

*The Colossus Chronicles · Book Six · Scale and play*

## Chapter 1 — The New Threshold

The first thing Marcus noticed was the doorway. It had been built for the previous version of the residence, and he had become too tall for it without quite noticing when the change had happened. At eight feet three inches, he could still pass through if he turned his shoulders and remembered to duck, but the movement had become a small daily negotiation. The new Scale Commons was being built to make that negotiation optional.

The building stood beyond the Colossus Facility, across a broad paved court where the old campus gave way to a new arrangement of terraces, reinforced lawns, high windows and long timber spans. Daniel had supervised the structural work. Adrian had designed modular fittings that could be replaced without rebuilding an entire room. Peter had developed clothing and equipment storage for several proportions at once. Ellis had mapped the movement routes, and Martin had insisted that the social rooms should be as carefully planned as the training halls. Nobody wanted a place that was impressive only when empty.

Marcus stepped onto the calibration platform while Daniel checked the reading. Eight feet three. The figure went into the ledger as a measured milestone, not a title. He was in the Elevated class: tall enough to change the ordinary proportions of a room, still close enough to human scale that much of the existing campus remained usable. The next class began at ten feet. Marcus looked at the mark on the wall and grinned as if it had personally challenged him.

Joey arrived while the others were reviewing the first floor plan. His Stage 5 Colossus baseline was already established; the Scale Commons did not pretend that he was beginning again, and nobody assigned him a new height merely to fill a blank on a form. His physical scale had outgrown the assumptions of the original facility long before this building was conceived. He moved through the high entrance with the familiar care that had become part of his character, looking first at the people and then at the architecture.

“It's bigger than the old place,” Marcus said.

“That's the idea,” Joey answered. “But it has to work when we're all here.”

The architects had given the Commons a simple rule: no resident should have to choose between being welcome and being accommodated. Some rooms were built around ordinary adult proportions; others rose through several storeys, with wide turning spaces, adjustable platforms and long sightlines. The building did not force everyone into one scale. It let different scales meet without making any one of them a mistake.

## Chapter 2 — The Tableland

The Tableland occupied the centre of the Commons. From the gallery it looked like a miniature campus laid out across a broad, waist-high landscape: small streets, a recreation hall, a gymnasium, a dormitory courtyard, a running track, a swimming pool and a line of trees that marked the edge of a model park. The buildings were sturdy demonstration pieces, designed to be handled and rearranged. Their purpose was not to imitate a real city perfectly. It was to let the residents experiment with distance, proportion and perspective from a giant's point of view.

Marcus approached first. The model gymnasium was almost small enough to fit between his hands. He leaned over the table, comparing its doors with the doorway he had just passed through. Joey stood beside him, and the difference in their scale made the miniature campus look smaller still. For a moment neither spoke. The room's high windows reflected the model streets in long strips of light.

“Move the hall,” Daniel suggested. “See what happens to the route.”

Marcus shifted one of the model buildings. A set of lights traced the new path from the dormitory to the gym. Joey moved a small bridge, then turned the model courtyard so that its entrances faced the other way. The Tableland registered each change and updated its little map. What had begun as a model became a shared puzzle: where should the buildings go, how much open space did the residents need, and what would make the campus enjoyable at several scales?

Joey found that he liked the carefulness of it. A giant hand could move a building in a second, but the better question was where it belonged afterward. He could have treated the model as a toy to be rearranged without thought. Instead, he listened to Marcus's suggestions and let the smaller man take the lead on the street plan. The play came from the contrast itself: the size of the hands, the miniature architecture, the enormous room around them, and the fact that both men were absorbed in the same small decision.

The Tableland acquired its first permanent feature that afternoon. Marcus placed the model recreation hall beside the park, leaving a broad green square between them. Joey added a path that curved around the edge rather than cutting through the middle. Daniel saved the arrangement as the Commons' first shared layout. It was not the final plan. It was the first one that had been made by the residents rather than imposed on them.

## Chapter 3 — The Classes of Scale

The scale board was installed in the central gallery a week later. It divided height into five working classes: Elevated, from seven feet to under ten; Colossal, from ten feet to under twenty-five; Monumental, from twenty-five feet to under one hundred; Titan, from one hundred feet to under five hundred; and Continental, from five hundred feet upward. The categories were descriptive rather than competitive. They helped the builders decide how much clearance a room needed, helped the camera crew choose a useful distance, and gave the residents a common language for describing what had changed.

The board carried a second note beneath the measurements: growth stage and scale class were not the same thing. Joey's Stage 5 was a chapter in his personal transformation history, not a height category. A resident could advance through growth stages without crossing a scale boundary, or cross a scale boundary through a change that required a new environmental plan. The Commons would record what had actually been measured and leave the rest open.

Marcus returned to the platform several times. He liked seeing his first entry beside the new threshold. The ten-foot mark remained ahead of him, close enough to be interesting and distant enough to require another chapter. Martin made a game of guessing which part of the campus Marcus would outgrow next. Peter quietly began a new clothing pattern. Ellis set up a movement course with several different clearances. Nobody treated the next milestone as an obligation. It was an invitation.

The first Commons night brought the whole group together. The high hall was lit from above, the Tableland glowed beneath its glass canopy, and the outer court had been marked with a series of scale gates. Residents could walk the route, compare their reach against the markers, test the adjustable seating, or simply watch from the gallery. The atmosphere was playful without becoming a contest. A man could be enormous in one room and an observer in another. He could take part, step back, or change the activity.

Joey spent most of the evening with the others rather than at the centre of the room. He tried the new seating, inspected the high doors, and returned to the Tableland to see whether anyone had changed the model. Marcus had added a small covered walkway between the gym and the dining hall. Joey approved of it, then placed a second path on the opposite side so that the model campus had two ways through.

By the time the lights dimmed, the Commons had already begun to acquire a history. There were measurements on the board, a saved arrangement in the Tableland, new clothing patterns in Peter's room, and a list of architectural adjustments for Daniel and Adrian. The residents had not simply entered a building designed for giants. They had started to make it theirs.

The final inscription was placed beside the entrance the following morning:

**NO FINAL SCALE. NO LOST BASELINE. MAKE ROOM, THEN KEEP BECOMING.**

The Scale Commons became a permanent part of the Colossus campus. Joey continued from his established baseline. Marcus kept the ten-foot threshold in view. The others pursued their own forms of development. The world had grown large enough to hold them, and it remained open to whatever scale came next.
