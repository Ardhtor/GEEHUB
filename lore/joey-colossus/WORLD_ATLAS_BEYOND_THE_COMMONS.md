# World Atlas — Beyond the Scale Commons

*The Colossus Chronicles · regional atlas · living geography*

## The world opens outward

The Colossus campus was never meant to be the whole world. It is one landmark in a lived-in region, with a city, parks, workshops, rail lines, towns, forests, coast, and distant high country beyond its gates. The Scale Commons made it possible for residents of different proportions to share a place. The wider region now has to do the same thing at the scale of a society.

This atlas establishes the geography around the campus. Names and places introduced here are fictional canon for The Colossus Chronicles. They do not merge Joey's story with the other independent worlds in The Tractus.

The larger region is called the **Meridian Basin**. Its principal city is **Crownmere**, built along the River Aven beneath a chain of wooded ridges. The Colossus campus occupies a high terrace north of the city. On a clear day, the residents can see the river bends, the pale roof of the Central Station, and the green line of the Long Park. Beyond those familiar landmarks, roads and railways continue toward the cedar country, the eastern tablelands, and the coast.

The region is not an empty landscape waiting for the characters to arrive. People live and work there. Workshops have histories, neighbourhoods have loyalties, parks have regular visitors, and institutions disagree about how the future should be built. The residents of the Colossus campus are part of that life, not its sole purpose.

## The campus and its approaches

The **Colossus Campus** contains the original Colossus Facility, the residence, the Scale Commons, the Tableland, the Mirror Gallery, the modular Workshop, and the Outer Court. Its northern edge follows the ridge. A broad promenade leads down from the main gate to the city, while the east service road connects the Workshop to the Foundry Ward. The southern path crosses a wooded slope and joins the Long Park.

The old approach remains narrow enough to preserve the memory of the first residence. It is now a quiet walking route, not the main access for large equipment. The **High Approach** is the newer route: a long, gently graded road with reinforced paving, rest terraces, wide turning areas, and covered places to meet. Daniel and Adrian designed it to serve people, deliveries, and public events without making the route feel like a service corridor.

Three routes connect the campus to Crownmere:

- **The Park Walk** descends through the trees and enters the Long Park. It is the slow, social route, favoured for Sunday walks and unhurried conversation.
- **The Foundry Road** runs east to the workshops and the Meridian rail depot. It carries equipment, materials, and the sound of metalwork.
- **The River Way** follows a lower terrace toward the river quarter. It is the easiest route for an afternoon in the city and the first choice for visitors who want to see Crownmere.

The routes meet at the **North Gate**, where a public map shows accessible paths, current construction, transport connections, and places where different scale classes can gather. The map is updated by the people who use the routes; it is not treated as a final plan.

## Crownmere

Crownmere is a large, handsome working city, neither a theme park nor a city built solely for giants. Its older streets retain human proportions. Newer civic buildings make room for broader circulation, taller entrances, adjustable public furniture, and several kinds of gathering space. The city has learned to adapt without flattening its differences.

### The Long Park

The Long Park is a green corridor running from the campus ridge toward the river. It contains formal lawns, old trees, a lake, stone bridges, a running circuit, open exercise grounds, and several pavilions. The paths are wide and gradual. Smaller loops pass through gardens and shaded courtyards; the central promenade continues for several kilometres without forcing walkers to leave the park.

The park is where the campus first becomes part of ordinary city life. Residents walk with friends, meet local athletes, watch rowing practice, and sit at the water. The large scale gates near the northern entrance are informational landmarks rather than competitive equipment. People use them to understand clearances and distances, then continue with their day.

The **Glass Pavilion** houses a public café and reading room. Its long windows face the lake, and its furniture is modular rather than monumental. A room can accommodate a small group at ordinary proportions or be rearranged for a larger gathering. The point is hospitality, not spectacle.

### The River Quarter

The River Quarter follows the old Aven embankment. Boat sheds, rowing clubs, open-air markets, cafés, and broad stone steps line the water. The **Aven Steps** are a favourite place to sit in the evening. The river makes the city feel larger than its streets: it carries reflections from one district into another and gives walkers a sense of distance without requiring a journey.

The rowing houses have their own traditions and rivalries. Their adult male crews train early, keep detailed logs, and argue about technique over breakfast. They are curious about the Colossus residents, but they have their own lives and sporting ambitions. Ellis will eventually find that he has more in common with the river crews than he expected.

### Glassmarket

Glassmarket is the city's clothing, design, and small-manufacturing district. Peter's contacts work here: cloth merchants, pattern cutters, leatherworkers, bootmakers, photographers, and designers of adaptable garments. The arcades preserve old shopfronts, while upper floors contain modern fitting studios and open workshops.

The **Guild of Cloth and Measure** has its principal hall in Glassmarket. It maintains pattern archives, fabric tests, fitting records, and a shared library of techniques. It is not a guild that dictates how people should look. Its stated purpose is to make clothing that allows the wearer to move, work, train, relax, and present himself as he chooses. Peter will be welcomed as a contributor, but he will also encounter methods that challenge his own.

### Foundry Ward

Foundry Ward sits beside the eastern rail depot. It contains metal shops, engineering studios, timber yards, equipment makers, and the city's largest materials library. Daniel and Adrian make regular visits. They know the district by sound: hammers in the morning, loading cranes at midday, and the low mechanical rhythm of the late shift.

The **Institute for Scale, Movement and Architecture** occupies a converted railway works at the centre of the ward. Its teams study spatial access, structural systems, movement, material behaviour, and public design across a range of fictional body scales. It is a practical institution with a public workshop and a lively disagreement culture. Residents can bring a problem, test a prototype, and take part in the redesign.

### Old Crown

Old Crown is the older civic centre, built around a covered market, a library, a concert hall, and a sequence of narrow streets. The streets remain narrow because they are part of the city's history; wider routes run around them, and public buildings provide accessible entrances from the adjoining squares. Old Crown is full of ordinary things that give a city its depth: bookshops, repair counters, late cafés, barbers, small galleries, and men who seem to know everyone.

The **Civic Archive** keeps maps, oral histories, photographs, planning documents, and records of how Crownmere has changed. The Colossus campus has begun depositing its own records there. Peter's first oversized pattern, the original Scale Commons plan, and the Tableland's earliest saved layout will all become part of the public history.

### Observatory Rise

A steep, wooded road climbs from Old Crown to Observatory Rise. The hill is quiet at dawn and busy at sunset. At its top stands the **Aven Observatory**, a public building with a wide viewing terrace and a large mechanical map of the Meridian Basin. Visitors can see the coast on unusually clear days. The observatory is also where the regional transport lines are displayed as a single system.

The map has a small blank area beyond the eastern edge. It is not an error. The survey there is incomplete, and the residents have already begun arguing about what deserves to be built first.

## Beyond the city

Crownmere is the centre of the Meridian Basin, not its boundary. Four outer regions are known and connected by public routes.

**Cedar Reach** lies northwest of the city. It is a broad woodland of cedar groves, dark streams, old forestry roads, and timber settlements. The trees have shaped the region's architecture. Long beams, covered walkways, and open-sided halls are common. Adrian is interested in the local construction methods, while Joey likes the quiet scale of the forest and the way the paths open into clearings without warning.

**The Eastern Tablelands** rise beyond Foundry Ward. Their open ground, low ridges, and long horizons make them suitable for movement courses and large outdoor gatherings. A route called the **Switchback Road** climbs through several terraces before reaching the high plain. The residents plan to establish an outdoor campus there, with a modular training field, a public meeting hall, and a landscape route that can be altered as the community grows.

**Saltline Coast** is a chain of harbour towns, beaches, seawalls, and wind-cut headlands south of the river mouth. The principal town, **Merehaven**, has a working port and a public promenade. The coast is not a resort built for the Colossus residents. It is a place with fishermen, boat builders, swimmers, cafés, and its own seasonal calendar. The wide beach at Merehaven will become a setting for a future gathering where the scale gates are replaced by natural distances: shoreline, tide, horizon, and the time it takes to walk together.

**The Northern Ranges** rise beyond Cedar Reach. Their lower slopes contain villages and mountain workshops; higher up are lakes, passes, and weather stations. The range has not yet been fully mapped. Travel there requires preparation, and the residents have no reason to pretend that every place is already accessible. It is a genuine frontier for later volumes.

## A region that accommodates difference

The Meridian Basin uses shared design principles rather than a single standard body size. Public buildings retain ordinary entrances and intimate rooms while providing alternative routes and larger spaces where needed. Transit vehicles have modular seating and clear circulation. Public furniture is replaceable and adjustable. Parks include quiet paths, open grounds, and places to watch without being asked to participate.

Scale classes help with planning, but they do not determine a person's status. The five classes in the Scale Commons remain useful as a local planning language; the city does not rank its residents by height. A person's place in the community comes from his relationships, work, interests, and choices.

This principle produces real design disagreements. Some residents want more monumental public architecture; others argue that scale should not overwhelm intimacy. The Institute tests prototypes. The Guild develops clothing. The city council negotiates public use. The campus residents contribute, but they do not get to decide everything. The wider world has its own agency.

## The shared calendar

Crownmere's civic year is organized around events that bring neighbourhoods into contact:

- **First Light Walk:** a spring walk from the campus ridge through the Long Park to the river.
- **Foundry Open Week:** public workshops, demonstrations, equipment trials, and evening meals in Foundry Ward.
- **The Glassmarket Fitting Days:** a clothing and design festival where makers show new patterns and visitors can commission or adapt garments.
- **Aven River Regatta:** the rowing clubs' principal event, with the campus residents invited as guests and occasional collaborators.
- **Open Grounds:** an autumn gathering on the Eastern Tablelands. Residents design temporary spaces, movement routes, outdoor training stations, and communal kitchens.
- **The Long Table:** a winter supper that moves between districts and invites groups who would not normally share a room.

These events allow supporting characters to develop interests beyond Joey. Ellis can become involved with the rowing clubs; Peter can take part in the Guild's pattern archive; Daniel and Adrian can build relationships with the Institute; Martin can become one of the organizers of Open Grounds; and Marcus can use the Tableland to develop his miniature-district experiments.

## The map is not finished

The Meridian Basin is established geography, but it is not a closed map. Cedar Reach has roads that have not been surveyed. The Northern Ranges have passes that appear only in certain seasons. Saltline has smaller harbour communities beyond Merehaven. The Eastern Tablelands contain sites that have not yet been assigned a use.

New places should arrive through travel, correspondence, work, friendship, or discovery. They should have residents, histories, and practical reasons to exist. A place becomes canon when a scene gives it life and the atlas records what the scene actually establishes.

The Colossus campus remains home. Crownmere becomes the nearby city. The Meridian Basin becomes the wider region. The rest of the world remains open.

**The residence was a beginning, not a border.**
