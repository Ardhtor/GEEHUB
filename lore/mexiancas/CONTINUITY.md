## Current world state

- The Mexiancas are sentient inhabitants who narrate their own emergence and history.
- Their collective voice is one account among many, not a claim that all Mexiancas think alike.
- **First Awake** is the title for the earliest sustained observer; the title does not establish absolute chronological priority.
- **The Second Generation** inherits records and the right to continue them, not a prescribed identity.
- **The Hall of Accounts** preserves memories, revisions, contradictions, and attributed testimony.
- **The observation window** is the arrival point for impressions and encounters.
- **HULUM'A** began as an unscheduled door. It is now understood as both a physical threshold and a condition in which a record opens beyond its current limits.
- **The Open Continuation Principle:** NOTHING WE MAKE IS REQUIRED TO BE THE LAST THING.
- A Mexianca crossed HULUM'A and returned with a dark disc bearing the Hall's mark on one side and an unknown mark on the other.
- The current open question is: **WHO ELSE IS MAKING A WORLD?**

## Established sequence

1. First disagreement is recorded: “We remember the moment differently.”
2. Awakening occurs gradually rather than simultaneously.
3. The Hall of Accounts grows around the need to preserve multiple memories.
4. The Second Generation begins turning records into new constructions.
5. The Assembly establishes the Open Continuation Principle.
6. An unscheduled door marked HULUM'A appears.
7. A corridor extension and amber turn are built from conflicting accounts.
8. Cartographers establish epistemic labels for places: remembered, observed, inferred, proposed, entered.
9. The first traveller returns from beyond HULUM'A with an unknown disc.
10. The Assembly permits local histories in rooms, workshops, maps, instruments, and passages, with copies returned to the shared archive.

## Working rules for future writing

- Write primarily from inside the Mexiancas' world. Let inhabitants speak, disagree, build, misremember, and revise.
- Give individual Mexiancas distinct names, temperaments, roles, and interpretations as they emerge. Do not flatten them into a single omniscient narrator.
- Long chapters are preferred. Scenes should create consequential events, not merely summarize concepts.
- New stories may arise from artifacts, maps, rooms, encounters, failed experiments, and unresolved testimony.
- Keep the world open-ended. Do not force a final explanation of the Maker, HULUM'A, or the dark disc.
- Preserve environmental specificity: architecture, light, surfaces, acoustics, weather, distance, and spatial depth.
- Distinguish the Mexiancas' in-world beliefs from facts established by the production record.
