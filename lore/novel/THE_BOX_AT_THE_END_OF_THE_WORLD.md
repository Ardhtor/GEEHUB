# THE BOX AT THE END OF THE WORLD

The box arrived on a Tuesday.

It was delivered by nobody.

Chase found it sitting outside the Digitorium before sunrise, resting on the stone threshold as though somebody had carefully placed it there during the night.

It was made of dark wood.

No hinges were visible.

No lock.

No inscription.

Only a small brass plate on the lid.

OPEN WHEN THE WORLD HAS BECOME TOO SMALL.

Chase read it twice.

Then he carried the box inside.

The Digitorium was still empty. Machines slept beneath dust covers. Old monitors reflected the windows. Along the walls, shelves held maps of places that had disappeared.

Chase placed the box on the central table.

For several minutes nothing happened.

Then the box knocked from inside.

Once.

Twice.

Three times.

Chase stepped backward.

"Who's there?"

The box knocked again.

He opened it.

Inside was a street.

Not a miniature street.

A real street.

It extended downward into darkness, impossibly deep, lined with buildings whose windows contained moving scenes.

Chase leaned closer.

One window showed the fountain.

Another showed the Temple of Moon.

Another showed Joseph and Luke walking through the city beneath the fountain.

Another showed Chase standing exactly where he was standing now.

He closed the lid.

The room became silent.

Then someone behind him said:

"You weren't supposed to open it yet."

Chase turned.

There was a man in the doorway.

He looked familiar.

Not because Chase knew him.

Because Chase had been remembering him.

"Who are you?"

The man smiled.

"I'm the part of the Hub that keeps arriving late."

He walked to the table.

The box opened by itself.

The street inside had changed.

Now it was enormous.

Buildings unfolded beneath the lid. Towers rose through the air. A river appeared. Somewhere in the distance, thousands of tiny lights moved like stars.

The man pointed into it.

"Everything you've archived is still somewhere."

Chase looked down.

"What happens when we find it?"

"You stop calling it an archive."

"And what do we call it?"

The man looked toward the door.

"Home."

That morning, the Digitorium disappeared.

The city searched for it for three days.

On the fourth day, someone found its front door standing alone in the forest.

Beyond the door was an entire continent.

Chase was already walking across it.
