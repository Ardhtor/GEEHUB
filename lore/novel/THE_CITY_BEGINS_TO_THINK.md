# THE CITY BEGINS TO THINK

The city did not wait for its inhabitants to understand it.

By the time Joseph returned to the fountain, three new streets had appeared.

Luke noticed them first.

"Those weren't there."

Joseph looked down the eastern road.

He remembered the road ending at the old wall. Now it continued beyond it, descending through a district of narrow houses and illuminated windows.

They walked.

The city changed around them according to what they carried with them. Joseph thought of the house, and a wooden door appeared. Luke remembered the Mustang, and somewhere behind the buildings an engine started. Chase opened his book, and every street became briefly visible at once.

The city was learning through contact.

Not instructions.

Contact.

A bakery appeared because somebody remembered bread. A theater appeared because somebody remembered watching. A vast empty gymnasium appeared because somewhere in the corpus there had always been a body preparing to become larger.

The building was unfinished.

Its walls were enormous.

Inside, the floor continued farther than the eye could follow.

Luke walked in.

The lights came on behind him.

One row.

Then another.

Then hundreds.

The room had become an arena without anybody deciding that it should be one.

Joseph stood at the entrance.

"What does it want?"

Chase closed the book.

"It doesn't want. It associates."

A sound came from beneath the floor.

The entire building shifted.

The city had found another memory.

A door opened in the center of the arena.

Beyond it was not another room.

It was the next layer of the world.

They entered.

And the city followed them.
