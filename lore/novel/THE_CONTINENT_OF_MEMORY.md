# THE CONTINENT OF MEMORY

Beyond the last door there was a continent.

No map had predicted it.

The machine had assembled it from recurrence.

Forests grew where forests had repeatedly appeared.

Cities gathered around remembered buildings.

Rivers followed the paths of repeated journeys.

At the center stood a mountain made from every unfinished ascent.

Joseph and Luke reached the shore at dawn.

Behind them, the archive continued opening.

Ahead, the continent was changing as they watched it.

A road appeared because Joseph expected one.

A bridge appeared because Luke remembered crossing.

A tower appeared because Chase had written about height without ever describing the tower itself.

The world was becoming literal about association.

They walked inland.

At noon they found a village.

Nobody lived there.

But every house contained evidence that somebody had.

A cup.

A coat.

A photograph.

A bed still warm.

A radio playing a song from somewhere else.

Luke entered the largest house.

On the wall was a map of the continent.

The map showed them standing exactly where they stood.

Then another mark appeared farther north.

Joseph looked at it.

"What's that?"

Luke pointed.

"Looks like somewhere we're going."

The map expanded.

A new road drew itself toward the northern mountains.

Then another road appeared behind them.

The archive was not merely documenting their journey.

Their journey was becoming source material.

Joseph laughed.

"So we keep moving."

Luke opened the door.

Outside, the continent had grown again.
