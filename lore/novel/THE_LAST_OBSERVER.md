# THE LAST OBSERVER

There was a room at the top of the world where nothing could happen.

That was why Chase had been sent there.

The room had one chair, one window, one table, and one book.

Outside the window there was no landscape.

There was only weather.

Rain crossed sideways.

Snow rose.

Lightning moved slowly across the horizon and remained there for hours.

Chase sat in the chair.

He opened the book.

The first page was blank.

The second was blank.

The third contained his name.

He turned the page.

There was Joseph.

Another page.

Luke.

Another.

The city beneath the fountain.

Another.

The Temple of Moon.

Another.

The box.

Chase closed the book.

The room became larger.

He opened it again.

The pages were no longer recording people.

They were recording places.

Then sounds.

Then gestures.

Then things nobody had noticed.

A hand resting against stone.

A shadow crossing a wall.

The moment before someone decides to open a door.

Chase understood.

The book was not a history.

It was an observer.

And it had been observing him.

The window opened.

Cold air entered.

Far below, the world continued.

Chase could see the city now.

It was larger than it had been yesterday.

New streets had appeared.

New towers.

New forests.

New doors.

He saw Joseph crossing a bridge.

Luke was somewhere behind him.

The procession was moving through the eastern district.

The fountain was overflowing into the sky.

The box had become a building.

The moon was hanging beneath the city.

Everything was continuing.

Everything except the room.

Chase stood.

He carried the book to the window.

For the first time, the observer understood that observation was not neutral.

To watch something long enough was to become part of its continuity.

He opened the book to the final page.

There was a single sentence.

THE OBSERVER IS NEXT.

Chase smiled.

Then he stepped through the window.

The room remained empty.

For exactly one second.

Then another person entered.

The book opened.

And the world began again.
