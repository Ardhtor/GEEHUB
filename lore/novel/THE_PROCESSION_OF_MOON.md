# THE PROCESSION OF MOON

The moon had been missing for seventeen years when it returned over Veyrthalis.

Nobody saw it arrive.

That was the first strange thing.

The second was that every bell in the city rang at once.

Joseph woke before the sound reached his room. He was standing at the window already, though he could not remember crossing the room. Outside, the dark woods had become silver. The old road between the houses was wet with moonlight, and along it something was moving.

A procession.

Men carried lamps without flames. Their faces were hidden behind pale masks. Between them they carried an enormous wooden frame, and inside the frame there was nothing.

Joseph watched the empty space.

He knew, immediately, that it was occupied.

Not by a body.

By an absence.

The procession stopped beneath his window.

One masked man looked up.

He raised a hand.

Joseph opened the window.

"Are you looking for someone?"

The man pointed toward the forest.

Then the procession continued.

Joseph dressed quickly.

By the time he reached the street, the procession had disappeared, but the lamps remained in the rain, standing upright with nobody holding them. He followed.

The road entered the woods.

The woods were older than the city, but tonight they seemed to have been rearranged. Trees stood where paths had been. Paths crossed themselves. A stone wall appeared and vanished whenever Joseph looked away.

He continued anyway.

Eventually he found Luke sitting on a fallen beam.

"You saw it too," Luke said.

Joseph stopped.

"How long have you been here?"

"Long enough to get bored."

Luke stood.

Beyond him was a clearing.

At its center stood the Temple of Moon.

Neither man had ever seen it before.

It had no entrance.

Only a circular opening in the roof through which the returned moon looked down.

Inside the temple was a staircase.

They descended.

The stairs ended in a room containing hundreds of doors.

Each door had a name.

Some names Joseph recognized.

Some belonged to people who had died.

Some belonged to people who had never existed.

Luke walked between them.

"Which one?"

Joseph looked toward the center.

One door had no name.

Its surface was covered in fingerprints.

He touched it.

The door opened.

Beyond it was the city as it had been seventeen years earlier.

The same streets.

The same buildings.

The same moonless sky.

And at the end of the street stood Joseph himself, younger by seventeen years.

The younger Joseph was holding a book.

He looked directly at them.

Then he began to read.

The words could not be heard.

But every door in the temple opened.

And Veyrthalis remembered.
