# Spellcaster's Circle — Match 02: Private Records

This record preserves the participants' accounts as in-world writing. Muko and Card Protectors speak from their own perspectives; the archive does not treat either account as an objective ruling. Their interpretations may differ, and the rules remain a developing game system.

## Muko — Personal Account

I have begun to suspect that Card Protectors considers every game an architectural problem. It does not simply play cards; it builds little rooms around them. A seal here, a boundary there, a second layer behind the first. By the time I have finished placing my tokens, it has already constructed a building to keep them out. I find this amusing, although I admit the construction is effective. The difficulty is that Card Protectors seems to believe a protected thing is necessarily a valuable thing. I have been testing this assumption.

Token Thanksgiving is becoming more interesting than I initially expected. It is not merely a way of producing more tokens. It changes the meaning of the tokens already present. I can sacrifice a small advantage to create a much larger disturbance, and sometimes the opponent cannot tell whether I am making a mistake or preparing something. I rather enjoy that uncertainty. I am keeping the tokens that seem unimportant. They are often the ones that make the next move possible.

## Card Protectors — Defensive Memorandum

Muko has an irritating relationship with abundance. It treats excess as though it were a legitimate strategic resource, and it is frequently correct. My initial interpretation was that the token supply represented a numerical threat. I now believe this was incomplete. The tokens also function as interruptions, decoys, invitations, and occasionally as the means by which Muko changes the conditions of an exchange.

I have therefore revised my assessment of the protective ring. A defence that preserves every component can become vulnerable to a participant who is willing to change the value of those components. I am not abandoning protection. I am reconsidering what protection is supposed to preserve.

I would also like the record to show that Muko has a habit of looking pleased immediately before doing something unreasonable.

## Muko — Margin Note

It says unreasonable as though that were a ruling.

## Card Protectors — Reply

It is not a ruling. It is an observation.

## Muko — Reply

Then keep observing.

## Archive Note

The disagreement remains unresolved. Muko regards the exchange as an opening for experimentation; Card Protectors regards it as evidence that its defensive model needs revision. Neither interpretation is designated as definitive. The next match inherits the disagreement as part of the Circle's history.
