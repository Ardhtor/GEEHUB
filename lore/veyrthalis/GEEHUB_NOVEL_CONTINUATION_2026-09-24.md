# GEEHUB — The Labyrinth Continues

The forest did not become darker when Luke stepped into it. It became deeper. That was the first distinction he made, and once he had made it he could not stop noticing the difference. Darkness belonged to the surface of things: the black between the trees, the shadow beneath the roots, the blue-black distance where the last road disappeared. Depth was something else. Depth suggested that the darkness continued inward, that there were rooms inside the rooms, corridors inside the corridors, and that every object they passed might have another object hidden behind it which explained why the first one existed.

They had been walking for nearly an hour without agreeing on a destination. The others kept appearing and disappearing among the redwoods, each man briefly caught in the amber afternoon before the trunks swallowed him again. Luke could hear them calling to one another, but the forest altered the sound. A voice from twenty yards away sometimes seemed to come from directly behind him. A laugh could travel through the branches and return as something almost like speech.

Luke stopped.

There was a door in the tree.

Not a metaphorical door. Not an opening in the bark. A door: narrow, dark, and perfectly rectangular, fitted into the enormous trunk as though the tree had grown around an architecture that had existed before it. There was no handle.

One of the men came up beside him.

"That's new."

Luke looked at him. "You saw it too?"

"I saw you looking at it."

The distinction bothered Luke more than the door did.

He reached toward the surface, then stopped before touching it. The serum had taught them to be suspicious of thresholds. It had never behaved like an ordinary substance. It did not simply change the body; it changed the conditions under which change could occur. A stimulus became a rule. A rule became a tendency. A tendency, repeated long enough, became the new normal.

The door clicked.

Neither man had touched it.

Behind them, somewhere among the trees, another voice called Luke's name.

The door opened inward.

There was no room behind it. There was a white screen.

On the screen was the GEEHUB constellation.

Luke recognized it immediately: not because he had seen the interface before, but because the arrangement felt familiar in the strange way a dream can feel familiar while you are still dreaming it. Nodes floated in a black field. Lines connected some of them. Others stood alone. Every node contained a word, and every word appeared to imply another word.

BEEFYTHIQ.

VEYRTHALIS.

LUKE.

SERUM.

BODY.

MEMORY.

LOVE.

FACILITY.

LABYRINTH.

Then a new node appeared.

CONTINUITY.

Luke did not move.

The screen displayed a single sentence.

**A thing survives by becoming capable of remembering what it was while becoming something it was not.**

The forest behind him went silent.

He looked at the man beside him.

"Did you hear that?"

"Hear what?"

Luke turned back to the screen.

The node labeled CONTINUITY had connected itself to LUKE.

Then to SERUM.

Then to LABYRINTH.

The lines did not look like wires. They looked like paths.

Luke understood the danger immediately. A database could tell you that two things were related. A labyrinth could make the relationship into a place.

He stepped through the door.

The forest vanished.

He stood in a corridor made from enormous sheets of dark glass. Behind the glass were fragments of places he recognized: the Facility's white observation rooms, the Temple of Moon, the redwood clearing, a bedroom he had not seen in years, a city at night, an unfinished Blender scene, an empty page of the novel itself.

The corridor continued in both directions.

At each intersection there was another door.

Above the first door:

**MECHANISM**

Above the second:

**INTERFACE**

Above the third:

**TRANSFORMATION RULE**

Above the fourth:

**CONTINUITY PRINCIPLE**

And beyond those, barely visible in the distance:

**SETTING**

**MYTHOLOGY**

Luke laughed once, quietly.

"So that's what this is."

A voice answered from farther down the corridor.

"No."

He looked up.

"Then what is it?"

The voice came closer.

"It's what happens when the map realizes it has been describing the territory."

A figure emerged from the darkness.

Luke knew him.

Not because they had met.

Because the Hub had remembered him.

The man stopped several feet away. He was ordinary in the first glance: boots, dark trousers, a plain shirt, broad shoulders, tired eyes. But the longer Luke looked, the more impossible it became to decide where the man's body ended and the architecture began. His silhouette was steady while the corridor behind him shifted.

"You've been looking for the serum," the man said.

Luke frowned.

"No. We've been trying to understand it."

"That's the same thing here."

The floor trembled.

Somewhere beyond the glass, something enormous moved.

The man turned toward the sound.

"Your problem is that you think the serum is inside Luke."

Luke felt the words land before he understood them.

The man continued.

"The serum is inside the relationship between Luke and everything that can change him."

A crack appeared in the glass.

Not a fracture.

A line.

A path.

It traveled from the floor beneath Luke's feet toward the distant doors, branching once, twice, then dividing into hundreds of smaller routes.

The Hub had stopped showing them information.

It was generating consequences.

Luke looked at the branching paths.

One led toward BODY.

One toward LOVE.

One toward MEMORY.

One toward SERUM.

One toward a door labeled **UNKNOWN**.

The man pointed at the last one.

"That one wasn't in the registry yesterday."

Luke stared at it.

"What's behind it?"

The man smiled without answering.

From behind the UNKNOWN door came a slow mechanical sound, followed by the unmistakable sound of someone knocking from the other side.

Three knocks.

Then three more.

Then one.

Luke recognized the rhythm.

It was the same rhythm the serum had used when it first appeared.

The corridor lights came on.

Every node in the Hub turned toward him.

And for the first time, Luke understood that the labyrinth had not been built to keep him lost.

It had been built to find out what he would choose to follow.

## Chapter II — Three and Three and One

The man reached for the door, but Luke stopped him.

"Wait."

The word sounded smaller than Luke intended. The man nevertheless stopped.

Luke looked at the wall where the knocking had come from. There was nothing remarkable about it. Painted plaster, a faint seam where two panels met, a hairline crack descending toward the floor. He had seen walls like this in hospitals, schools, offices, laboratories. Walls whose entire purpose was to persuade a person that nothing existed behind them except insulation and electrical wiring.

"Who are you?"

The man considered the question.

"That depends."

Luke smiled despite himself. "Everything depends with you people."

"Yes."

"That's not an answer."

"No. It's an observation."

The man crossed the room and opened the filing cabinet.

It contained folders.

Hundreds of them.

Each was labeled with a name.

Some names Luke recognized. Others meant nothing to him. A few were simply numbers. The folders were arranged with an obsessive precision that made the disorder of the forest seem suddenly preferable.

The man removed one.

LUKE.

He did not open it.

Luke stared at the folder.

"That's mine."

"Yes."

"How long has it been there?"

The man looked at the date stamped along the bottom edge.

"Longer than you have."

Something in Luke's face changed.

The man noticed.

"I don't mean that metaphorically."

He placed the folder on the desk.

Luke did not touch it.

Outside, the others had gathered at the doorway. They had heard enough to know that whatever had been happening in the room was no longer merely Luke's problem. One stood with his arms folded. Another kept looking toward the forest as though expecting someone to emerge from between the trees. The third was watching the ceiling.

Luke pointed at the folder.

"Open it."

"No."

"Why?"

"Because it isn't complete."

"Then what's inside?"

"Evidence that it isn't complete."

This answer irritated Luke more than a refusal would have.

He opened the folder himself.

There were photographs.

The first showed the redwood forest.

The second showed the same forest in winter.

The third showed the same place beneath a sky filled with stars.

The fourth showed the room they were standing in.

Luke leaned closer.

The photograph had been taken from the opposite corner.

He could see himself standing beside the desk.

Except that he was wearing a different shirt.

And the man beside him was older.

Much older.

Luke turned the photograph over.

There was handwriting on the back.

THE FIRST VISIT WAS NOT THE FIRST.

He put it down.

Nobody spoke.

Then Luke noticed something in the photograph that none of them had yet mentioned.

The door was open.

But beyond it there was no forest.

There was another room.

A room containing another door.

And through that door—

Another.

Luke began turning pages.

Photographs.

Maps.

Medical diagrams.

Fragments of handwriting.

A topographic survey of the surrounding forest.

A diagram of the human torso covered with measurements.

A page containing nothing but musical notation.

Seven notes had been written there.

Six were darkened heavily in pencil.

The seventh had been erased.

At the bottom someone had written:

DO NOT RESTORE THE MISSING NOTE.

Luke looked at the man.

"Why?"

The man was looking at the music.

"Because it doesn't belong to us."

Luke waited.

"Whose is it?"

The man shut the folder.

"That's what the Labyrinth is trying to determine."

A noise came from the corridor.

Not knocking this time.

Footsteps.

Slow.

Measured.

Approaching.

The three men in the doorway moved instinctively closer together.

Luke remained beside the desk.

The man in the dark coat whispered something Luke could not hear.

"What?"

The man looked at him.

"I said the building is supposed to be empty."

The footsteps stopped.

There was silence.

Then a voice spoke from the other side of the wall.

"Luke."

Nobody moved.

The voice was not loud.

It was not distorted.

It sounded like an ordinary man speaking from an ordinary hallway.

"Luke, open the door."

Luke felt the Serum again.

Not as heat. Not as pain. Something quieter.

A recognition.

As though the substance had heard its name spoken in another room.

The man seized Luke's wrist.

"Don't."

Luke looked at him.

"You heard that."

"Yes."

"It knows my name."

"Yes."

"Then it already knows I'm here."

The man released him.

That was when Luke understood the real problem.

The question had never been whether the thing outside knew where he was.

The question was whether it had been waiting for him to notice.

The voice spoke again.

"Luke."

A pause.

Then:

"You left something here."

The door handle turned.

And Luke, against every instruction he had been given, reached for it.

## Chapter III — The Room That Remembered

The handle moved beneath Luke's hand before he had actually decided to turn it.

He felt the mechanism engage.

A small metallic click.

Then another.

The door opened three inches.

Nothing waited outside.

The corridor was empty.

The man in the dark coat swore under his breath.

Luke opened the door farther.

There was no corridor.

There was a room.

The same room.

The same desk.

The same filing cabinet.

The same chair.

The same fluorescent fixture.

But the room was occupied.

A man sat at the desk with his back to them.

Luke stopped.

The man's shoulders were familiar.

Not familiar in the ordinary sense.

Familiar as handwriting.

The seated man slowly turned.

Luke saw his own face.

Not an exact duplicate. Older. Heavier around the eyes. The hair different. A scar beneath the left cheekbone that Luke did not possess.

But it was him.

Or it had been him.

Or it would become him.

The older Luke looked toward the doorway.

"You're late."

The younger Luke could not answer.

Behind him, the three men had gone completely silent.

The older Luke smiled.

"I know."

"Know what?"

"That you don't believe me."

Luke stepped inside.

The room seemed to contract around him.

On the desk lay a glass container.

Inside it was a small quantity of translucent liquid.

The Serum.

Luke knew it before seeing it.

The substance seemed to recognize him.

A thin film formed against the inside of the glass.

The older Luke watched him watching it.

"You came for that."

"No."

"You always say that."

Luke looked at him.

"Always?"

The older man leaned back.

"That's the problem with continuity. People imagine it means that the same thing continues."

He tapped the glass.

"Sometimes continuity means the same question keeps producing different people."

The Serum shifted.

For an instant Luke thought he saw something moving inside it.

Then he realized it was his own reflection.

The older Luke stood.

He was larger than Luke remembered himself being, but not impossibly so. The difference was accumulated: broader shoulders, thicker neck, heavier arms, a body that seemed to have acquired mass gradually rather than transformed in a single event. His clothes accommodated him rather than announcing the change. Nothing about him looked supernatural.

That was precisely what frightened Luke.

"You've been changing," the older Luke said.

"So have you."

"No."

He looked toward the glass.

"I stopped."

The Serum became perfectly still.

Outside the room, something knocked.

Three.

Three.

One.

The older Luke closed his eyes.

"That's new."

Luke stared at him.

"I thought you said this had happened before."

"It has."

"Then why is that new?"

The older man opened his eyes.

"Because this time you're hearing it."

For the first time, Luke understood that the Labyrinth did not necessarily repeat events.

It repeated relationships.

The same door.

The same room.

The same question.

Different Luke.

Different witness.

Different consequence.

The filing cabinet opened by itself.

One folder slid outward.

Then another.

Then another.

Each bore the same name.

LUKE.

But the dates were different.

The oldest was dated forty-three years before.

The newest was dated tomorrow.

Luke reached for the newest folder.

The older Luke caught his hand.

"Don't."

Luke looked at him.

"Why?"

"Because that's the one where you leave."

"Leave where?"

The older Luke looked toward the door.

"That's what we're trying to find out."

And somewhere beneath the floor, beneath the building, beneath the forest itself, something enormous began to move.


## Chapter IV — The House at the Edge of the Trees

The movement beneath the floor lasted long enough for Luke to become aware of the weight of his own body. At first he thought the building was shaking. Then he understood that the sensation was too slow for an earthquake. It traveled through the room as a gradual displacement, beginning somewhere below the foundations and passing outward through the walls, the floor, the filing cabinet, the glass of the Serum, and finally into the soles of his boots. Nothing fell. Nothing broke. The fluorescent fixture did not even rattle. The building seemed, for a few seconds, to be adjusting itself around something that had decided to move.

The older Luke released his wrist.

"We have to go upstairs."

"Why?"

"Because downstairs is where it notices us."

Luke looked toward the ceiling. "And upstairs?"

"Upstairs is where it remembers us."

The distinction would have sounded absurd anywhere else. Here it seemed like an instruction.

They left the room together. The three men followed. The corridor was longer than Luke remembered, although he could not say whether it had changed or whether he had previously mistaken its length. There were doors on either side now. Some stood open onto offices containing desks, telephones, stacks of paper, abandoned coffee cups. Others were locked. Behind one door Luke could hear water running. Behind another came the faint sound of a radio broadcasting something in a language he did not recognize.

Nobody spoke for several minutes.

They reached a staircase.

It went upward.

Luke looked at the older man.

"How many floors?"

"Seven."

"How many are real?"

"All of them."

"That's not what I asked."

The older Luke began climbing.

"It's the only answer that matters."

The stairs were concrete. Their footsteps produced a dull rhythm that gradually became almost musical. Luke noticed the sound because the rhythm reminded him of the seven notes in the folder. Six had remained. One had been removed. The absence was becoming more perceptible than the notes themselves.

On the second floor they passed a window.

Luke stopped.

Outside was the forest.

But they were looking down at it from an impossible height. The redwoods extended below them, their crowns forming a continuous uneven surface. Beyond them the land fell away toward a valley filled with amber mist. There were lights in the distance.

A city.

Luke pressed his hand against the glass.

The older Luke did not stop.

"Don't look too long."

"What city is that?"

"The one that hasn't been built yet."

Luke turned.

The older man was already climbing.

On the third floor, the architecture changed. The concrete staircase ended at a corridor lined with dark wood. The walls were covered with framed photographs, all of them depicting groups of men standing in places that seemed familiar without belonging to any single period. Some wore work clothes. Some suits. Some uniforms. They stood beside machinery, beside trees, beside enormous stone structures, beside tables covered with scientific instruments. In several photographs the same man appeared at different ages.

Luke slowed.

"Family?"

The older Luke looked at the photographs.

"Some of them."

"Whose?"

"That's the question that started all of this."

One of the other men stepped closer to a photograph.

"These aren't all related."

"No."

"Then why are they together?"

The older Luke touched the frame with one finger.

"Because they kept inheriting the same problem."

At the end of the corridor stood a dining room.

There was a long table set for twelve.

The plates were already laid out. Glasses stood beside them. A bowl of fruit occupied the center of the table. Nothing looked abandoned. There was no dust. No decay. A chair near the window had been pulled back slightly, as if someone had just stood from it.

Luke smelled coffee.

Real coffee.

He stopped in the doorway.

For the first time since entering the building, he felt hungry.

The realization embarrassed him.

The older Luke noticed.

"Eat."

Luke looked at him.

"What?"

"You haven't eaten since morning."

"How do you know?"

"I remember."

Luke sat down.

The others followed cautiously. The older Luke remained standing until everyone had chosen a seat. Then he took the chair at the head of the table.

Coffee was poured.

The ordinary ritual of it changed the room more effectively than any supernatural event could have done. Cups passed from hand to hand. Someone added sugar. Someone asked for milk. One of the men laughed at the fact that there was no milk. The laughter was nervous at first, then became genuine. For perhaps ten minutes they were simply four men in a dining room drinking coffee.

Luke looked across the table at the older version of himself.

"How long have you been here?"

The older Luke took a drink.

"Long enough to stop asking."

"That's not possible."

"It is here."

Luke looked down at his cup.

The coffee was slightly bitter.

He drank it anyway.

"What happens if I leave?"

The older Luke was silent.

"Do I come back?"

Still nothing.

"Do I become you?"

The older man looked directly at him.

"No."

The answer came so quickly that Luke believed it.

"Then what happens?"

"You remain yourself."

Luke waited.

"But you won't remain the same."

A chair scraped against the floor.

Everyone turned.

Nobody had touched it.

The empty chair at the far end of the table had moved backward by several inches.

The older Luke looked toward it.

"They're here."

Luke whispered, "Who?"

The older man did not answer.

Instead he stood and walked to the window.

Far below, among the redwoods, a small group of figures had appeared on the forest floor.

They were men.

They were carrying lanterns.

They were walking toward the tree.

Luke watched them approach.

There were twelve.

Then eleven.

Then ten.

The distance between the figures did not change. The number did.

Luke stepped beside the older man.

"What are they doing?"

"Repeating something."

"What?"

The older Luke watched the last figure disappear behind a redwood.

"Coming to find us."

Luke looked at the glass of the window.

His reflection stood beside him.

The older Luke's reflection stood beside him too.

Then Luke noticed a third reflection.

A man was standing behind them.

Luke turned.

There was nobody there.

He looked back at the window.

The third reflection remained.

It raised one hand.

And touched the glass from the other side.
