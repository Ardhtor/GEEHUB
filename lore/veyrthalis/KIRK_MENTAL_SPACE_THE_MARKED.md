# Kirk's Mental Space — The Marked

**World connection:** Joseph & Kirk / Veyrthalis  
**Status:** user-established fictional direction; active development  
**Central principle:** Kirk's love marks certain fellows and grants them greater privileges within his mental space.

## The mark

The mark is an inward sign of being loved by Kirk. It is not a badge worn for public display, a rank awarded for obedience, or a claim of ownership. It changes the permissions of Kirk's inner architecture. A marked fellow is not merely remembered by Kirk; he has a recognized place within the interior world through which Kirk thinks, remembers, imagines, rests and makes meaning.

The mark is relational rather than administrative. It comes from Kirk's love, not from a test, a competition, a measurement or an external authority. The men named here form a privileged circle within the fictional mental space. Their access is greater than that of ordinary visitors, but the circle is not a ladder: no ordering of affection or worth has been established.

## What greater privilege means

The marked may cross beyond the public vestibule and enter the familiar rooms of Kirk's inner house. They may remain in those rooms for longer, return without beginning again at the outer threshold, and be present during quieter forms of thought that remain inaccessible to casual visitors. Their presence can bring a room into focus, awaken a remembered passage, or make a distant part of the interior reachable.

A mark also allows a fellow to leave a trace: a remembered phrase, a familiar object, a shared scene, a question, or a place that Kirk can return to. These traces are not automatically accepted as truth. Kirk may examine, revise, move or set them aside. The marked fellow retains authorship of his own contribution; Kirk retains authorship of his mind.

The mark does not mean unrestricted access to every thought. Private chambers may remain closed, even to someone deeply loved. A door can open by invitation, by a moment of mutual recognition, or when Kirk chooses to share what is within. The mark makes deeper access possible and durable; it does not abolish consent, privacy, uncertainty or the right to withdraw.

## The inner architecture

The mental space is imagined as a living house with connected rooms, passages and landscapes rather than a static database. The outer vestibule receives ordinary impressions. The Hearth is where trusted presence can remain without needing to perform. The Gallery of Faces preserves recognizable encounters and the differences between people. The Long Room holds shared histories, including difficult or unfinished ones. The Workshop is where ideas, memories and possible futures are handled and changed. The Quiet Chamber remains Kirk's own private room and is never presumed open.

The marked circle has deeper access to the Hearth, Gallery, Long Room and Workshop. Access to the Quiet Chamber remains specifically Kirk's choice. Other rooms may appear as the world develops; they should arise from actual recurring material rather than from a fixed symbolic catalogue.

The house responds to presence, but it does not manufacture agreement. Two marked fellows may remember the same encounter differently. Their accounts can occupy the same room without being merged. A disagreement may change the room's arrangement without expelling either person.

## Individual permissions remain open

The roster establishes the shared threshold privilege. It does not yet assign different rooms, keys, influence, permanence or forms of presence to individual fellows. Those distinctions should be developed from existing GEEHUB material and from later scenes in which Kirk's relationship with each figure becomes legible. Do not manufacture a ranking merely because the names appear in a list.

A future scene may show one fellow finding a room already prepared for him, another being invited into a difficult memory, or another leaving an object that changes the shape of a passage. Such events should be earned in the fiction and recorded as individual permissions only after they occur.

## Relationship to the Second Heart and the Digitorium

The Digitorium in *The Second Heart* translates decisions into architecture and sensation. It is not established as a device that reads minds or grants access to private thoughts. Kirk's mental space is a separate interior domain. The two may echo one another in imagery—thresholds, rooms, light, witness and separate accounts—but their mechanisms must not be collapsed without a later story event that establishes the connection.

Joseph and Kirk remain separate people with independent agency. The marked fellows are connected to Kirk through his love; they are not thereby assigned to Joseph, made interchangeable with him, or absorbed into the relationship continuity of the two men.

## Continuity safeguards

- The mark is a fictional expression of Kirk's love and privileged trust.
- Marked status grants deeper access, not ownership or control.
- No individual ranking or hierarchy is implied by the order of the roster.
- The marked retain distinct identities, memories, choices and boundaries.
- Kirk retains the right to keep a chamber private and to change the architecture of his own inner space.
- Do not infer real people's private feelings or relationships from fictional inclusion.
- Do not confuse Joey with Joseph Prever, or Eric Kaufman with Mr. Kaufman.
- New permissions must be grounded in scenes and recorded as continuity, not guessed from a name.

The house is larger because Kirk has loved people. Its rooms are not prizes. They are places where those people can be present without becoming less themselves.
