# The Low Ledger — The First Bell
*Veyrthalis observation record; a parallel motif, not a confirmed shared event*

**Recorder:** Merrow Vale, Soft Recorder  
**Location:** Western cornice and lower market streets  
**Status:** Unresolved observation

Merrow heard the bell at the end of the afternoon count. It sounded once, without the usual unevenness of the market bells and without the long tail that came from the Temple of Moon's upper bronze. The sound appeared to come from the west, though the crow on the Digitorium cornice turned toward the lower streets. A porter unloading sacks beside the public kitchen stopped and looked toward the northern avenue. A child at the fountain continued filling a cup. The market keeper recorded no interruption in trade.

Merrow did not write that the bell had come from the Digitorium. He wrote that he had heard one note and that the crow had turned west. These were separate observations. The porter later said the note had seemed to come from behind him. The market keeper had heard nothing. The accounts were entered on separate lines.

On the following morning, Merrow found a thin circle drawn in the margin of the page. It was open at the bottom. He checked the nib of his pen, the binding, and the page beneath it. The mark had not transferred from another sheet. He placed a tracing in the archive envelope and left the original untouched.

The circle resembles no official mark used by the Digitorium. It is not the closed eye of the western foundations, nor the seal used by the municipal water office. Merrow has therefore entered it as an unclassified mark. He has not assigned it to the Procession, the Binding, the Temple of Moon, or the investigation beneath the western quarter.

A second note concerns the old passage between two market buildings. The municipal plan shows a continuous wall. A porter claims that he saw a narrow lane there at dusk, lit by a warm lamp beyond the first turn. The architectural salvage keeper says the buildings once had a service passage, closed during the drainage works forty-three years ago. The plan in the municipal office does not show it. The keeper's memory is not enough to establish that the passage is open now.

The crow returned to the western cornice at the same hour. It looked toward the wall, then toward the Digitorium, then flew to the roof of the public kitchen. No one followed it. The passage has not been entered. The bell has not repeated.

Merrow's marginal record:

*One note. Three directions named. No common source established.*

*An open circle, not the city's closed eye.*

*A passage remembered by one man, denied by the plan, not yet tested.*

*The record must keep what was seen apart from what was supposed.*

This entry is preserved as a Veyrthalis parallel to the first-room motif in the Tractus story branch. It does not establish that Joseph and Kirk's page, bell, or passage physically occurred in Veyrthalis. A later crossing may connect the records if a scene supplies evidence. Until then, the correspondence remains an archival comparison only.
