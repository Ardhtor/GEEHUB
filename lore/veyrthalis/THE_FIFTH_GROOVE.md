# Chapter IV — The Fifth Groove

**World:** Veyrthalis. Experimental fiction. This chapter concerns fictional characters and makes no claims about any real person's thoughts, actions, awareness, or participation.

## I. The place left open

The Procession had not changed while they were below. Four entrances opened onto the bridge. The account table stood beneath the high window, its four plates still occupied. The blank centre remained pale.

Joseph went to the first plate. Kirk stopped at the second. The two other travelers took opposite ends of the table.

“There should be a fifth plate,” said the third traveler.

“Should there?” Kirk asked.

“I expected one.”

Joseph looked at the central blank. “The fifth groove was below. This table may not be a copy.”

The fourth traveler traced the air above the blank without touching it. “It may be the same record from another side.”

“Or a different kind of record,” Joseph said.

They had returned with four new observations, but the Procession offered no place to put them. Joseph took a narrow strip of paper from his coat and laid it beside the table. He wrote four lines: the empty bowl; the light inside the column; the panel that did not open; the fifth groove. Beneath them he left a space.

Kirk read the note. “A record of the empty place?”

“Only of the fact that we found one.”

“And that we don't know who it belongs to.”

Joseph nodded. He added one more line: **Author not established.**

He folded the paper once and put it away. It had not become a fifth account. It was a note about the absence of one.

## II. Four approaches

They chose to return to the lower chamber, but not together. The third traveler took the lower passage, wanting to inspect the groove from the side where it had first appeared. The fourth chose the upper passage and the stone disc that had turned in its recess. Kirk took the direct stair, intending to compare the lower table with the four plates above. Joseph remained in the Procession to examine his note before following.

He read the four observations again. Their order made them look like a sequence. He crossed out the commas and placed each phrase on its own line. The words no longer resembled a path. They resembled four separate entries.

At the landing, Kirk found the dust line unchanged. From one position it appeared continuous; from another it seemed broken. He moved one pace to the left. It became continuous again. He recorded the change without deciding which view was correct.

The third traveler found the fifth groove apparently deeper than the others. When he changed his angle, it looked level with them. The fourth checked the stone discs. The turned disc remained with its rim-groove facing downward. Beneath it he noticed a short vertical mark, though he could not remember whether it had been there before. He recorded that uncertainty rather than calling it new.

They met at the table. The fifth groove was still empty.

## III. An account without an author

Kirk placed his palm on the stone beside the groove. It was cool. He felt no vibration.

The third traveler crouched. “It doesn't begin at an edge.”

The fourth stood opposite him. “It doesn't end at one either.”

Joseph arrived and listened before looking. He placed his folded note beside—not inside—the four channels.

“Could it be for a record that hasn't been made?” Kirk asked.

“Possibly,” Joseph said.

“Or one made somewhere else.”

“Possibly.”

The third traveler suggested that the groove might be kept ready for someone who had not arrived. The fourth pointed out that this assumed someone was coming.

The fifth groove required neither a future witness nor a past one. It might be a place for an omitted account, a channel for another kind of observation, or simply a feature of the table. Its presence did not establish its purpose.

Joseph unfolded his note and added: **The fifth groove was present when we returned. No one saw it form. No account has been assigned to it.**

He placed the note beside the groove. The stone darkened beneath the paper, but did not copy the words.

“Does that count as putting something in it?” Kirk asked.

“No. It's next to it.”

“Then keep that distinction.”

The third traveler looked at the blank channel. “What would count?”

“An account made by someone who can say what he saw,” Joseph replied. “Not one we invent for him.”

They left the groove empty. They did not make waiting into an obligation. They could continue and leave the space as it was.

## IV. The mark

The second heartbeat sounded beneath the floor. Kirk heard it through the table. Joseph felt it in his boots. The third traveler reported pressure in his jaw. The fourth heard nothing.

They did not try to synchronize their bodies. Each described what he noticed, and the descriptions were added to the four occupied channels. The fifth groove remained untouched.

When the last account was spoken, a pale seam appeared at the far end of the fifth groove. It extended no more than the width of a fingernail. It did not resemble writing. It did not form a letter or a symbol.

The third traveler stepped back. “That's new.”

“New to us,” the fourth said.

Kirk examined it from the side. “It might have been under the dust.”

Joseph did not approach. “We should check from another position.”

They moved around the table one at a time. The seam remained visible, though its apparent length changed. It ended inside the groove. It did not point toward the empty bowl or the passage.

The stone had made a mark. It had not supplied an author.

“Could the table be recording our refusal to fill it?” Kirk asked.

“That's a possible story about it,” Joseph said. “But the mark doesn't tell us that.”

They recorded the sequence and the uncertainty. Joseph chose to return to the Procession. Kirk decided to stay until the light changed. The third traveler stayed with him. The fourth chose to accompany Joseph. The city did not turn their separate decisions into one.

## V. What each man carries

Joseph and the fourth traveler reached the Procession first. Joseph took out his note and added that a pale seam had appeared at one end of the fifth groove after the four travelers recorded their different sensations of the heartbeat. He noted that Kirk and the third traveler had remained below. He did not write what he thought the seam meant.

“You're keeping your own account,” the fourth traveler said.

“Yes.”

“Even though the others saw the same thing?”

“They saw it from their positions. We can compare later.”

The fourth traveler looked toward the four routes. He chose the glass causeway, wanting to see whether the small light still moved beneath it. Joseph chose the root passage. He wanted to return toward the Digitorium and see whether the routes remained open from the other side.

They parted at the first division in the bridge. Neither asked the other to change his choice.

Below, Kirk and the third traveler watched the pale seam until the light at the arch shifted. It did not lengthen. The heartbeat came once more. Kirk heard it in the table and then, after a pause, in the floor.

“Did it move?” the third traveler asked.

“I don't know.”

They left the groove untouched. Kirk drew the table in his notebook, marking the four occupied channels, the fifth groove, and the seam. He added the positions from which the mark had been visible. The drawing was not a copy of the table; it was a record of how the table had appeared to him.

When they finally returned to the Procession, Joseph's note rested beside the four plates. Kirk recognized the handwriting and did not open it. He placed it in the shallow recess beside the records, visible but not mistaken for a fifth account.

The Procession held four records and one note about an unassigned place. The lower table held four channels and a fifth groove. Neither table supplied a witness.

Kirk stood beneath the high window. The second heartbeat was quiet, or too far away to hear. He could not tell whether the city had paused or whether he had reached the end of what it was willing to show him.

He chose to leave.

## VI. The long attention

The chamber had a way of making short distances feel generous. The walk from the table to the low arch took only a few seconds, but the light changed several times along the floor, and each change revealed another texture in the stone. Near the table, the surface was polished and cool. At the arch it became granular, with small dark inclusions that caught against the soles of their boots. The men began to walk more slowly, not because the passage was difficult, but because there was more to notice when they stopped treating it as empty space between events.

Kirk returned to the table once more before leaving. He leaned over the fifth groove and saw that the dust was not a uniform layer. It had gathered more thickly at one end, where the stone dipped almost imperceptibly. A tiny pale fibre lay across the channel. It could have come from clothing, a plant, or some material used in the chamber's construction. He did not lift it. He drew the groove as he saw it, including the fibre and the uneven dust, then wrote the date of the observation in the margin of his notebook. The drawing was not the groove; it was the trace of his attention to it.

Joseph waited at the arch, turning the folded note in his hand. The paper had softened along its creases. He unfolded it and read the lines again, noticing that the phrase “author not established” occupied more space than the description of the groove itself. The imbalance pleased him. It meant the record was giving its greatest emphasis to the limit of what they knew, rather than dressing the unknown in a more attractive name. He added the pale seam as a separate observation and left the meaning blank.

The third traveler came to stand beside Kirk. They looked at the four channels, then at the fifth. The table seemed less like a device now than a piece of furniture in a room that had been used for generations. Its edges bore small dents. One corner had been repaired with a darker stone. There was a faint circular stain near the far plate, perhaps left by a vessel. The table had a history that did not begin with their arrival, and it would continue to have one after they left. That thought made the empty groove feel less like a riddle waiting for them and more like a feature whose place in the larger structure had not yet been discovered.

They stepped back together. The chamber did not brighten, close, or offer a final sign. Its ordinary details remained: the seam in the wall, the shelf, the dry bowl, the dust in the groove, the pale thread of light inside the column. The heartbeat was absent for several minutes, then returned so softly that only Joseph noticed it. He did not call the others back. He made a note and let the moment remain his own.

At the upper passage, Kirk paused and looked behind him. The table was still visible through the arch, but its proportions had changed with the distance. The fifth groove was now a fine line among other lines. He could no longer see the fibre. He could still remember where it lay. He understood that returning would mean more than finding the same room again. It would mean bringing back a changed observer, with a different arrangement of attention, and checking what had altered in the room rather than assuming that everything had.

## Sensory deepening — What the empty channel holds

The next morning, the four travelers came back while the valley was still blue with early light. A thin mist had gathered in the upper arcade, and the stone held the night's coolness beneath their palms. The fifth groove looked smaller than it had the evening before. Its edges were no less precise, but daylight revealed how ordinary its construction might be: a shallow channel cut with a narrow chisel, then smoothed by use or by the patient work of someone who had understood stone. The pale fibre remained where Kirk had drawn it. A bead of moisture had settled beside it, bright enough to resemble a tiny glass pin. Nobody touched either one.

They stood together without arranging themselves around the groove. Joseph noticed that the room smelled faintly of cedar, although there was no visible wood in the lower chamber. The third traveler heard a cart crossing the upper bridge; the fourth saw a brief reflection move across the column and vanish. These observations did not combine into a message. They made the room more particular. It was no longer only the place where a fifth channel had been found. It was a place with a morning, a smell, a distant working city, and a small bright drop of water that would eventually dry. Kirk wrote that down too. He wanted the record to remember that the unknown had been encountered in a living world, not in a sealed puzzle designed for their benefit.
