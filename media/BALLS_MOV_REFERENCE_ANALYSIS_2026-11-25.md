## Visual grammar

**Environment.** A minimal teal / blue-green studio replaces a detailed location. The wooden slatted platform is the main physical prop and grounding plane. It gives the figure a stable horizontal reference and allows close foreground interaction. Keep the background consistent and let changes in camera scale and body size carry the drama.

**Catalyst objects.** The luminous spheres are simple, saturated, self-illuminated forms. Their different colours, sizes, positions, and changing proximity to the subject create a readable progression. They should be authored as persistent scene objects with independent transforms, emission strength, and interaction events. Do not bake them into the character mesh or use them as a substitute for an explicit growth state.

**Character presentation.** The performance alternates between an initial reserved stance, arms-open motion, double-biceps flex, held frontal pose, hands-at-waist presentation, bending/reaching interaction, and the final double-biceps hold. The poses are deliberate and readable. Holds matter: the audience needs time to register each new silhouette.

**Camera.** The dominant language is front-facing and centred. The clip alternates full-body or medium-wide views with close anatomical crops and one clear facial insert. The wide view establishes total scale; close views reveal local volume and surface continuity. Preserve consistent landmarks between crops. Do not treat a crop change as evidence of physical growth.

**Rendering.** The subject is smooth stylized 3D rather than photoreal live-action. Skin uses warm brown shading with soft, broad highlights and rounded muscle transitions. The final form is highly exaggerated, but the major masses remain readable. Keep material detail subordinate to silhouette and anatomical volume. The teal field and simple platform prevent competing visual noise.

## Character and deformation model

Build a reusable adult character with independent channels:

- Identity: head shape, face, skin, persistent character ID, and revision history.
- Baseline proportions: head-to-body ratio, shoulder span, chest depth, waist, pelvis, arm length, thigh and calf proportions.
- Growth: separately controlled regional values for deltoids, pectorals, upper back, biceps/triceps, forearms, abdominal wall, gluteal mass, thighs, and calves.
- Pose: arm and torso articulation, flex states, hand placement, and held presentation.
- Wardrobe: garment choice, fit, visibility, collision, and any deliberate state transition.
- Props: each sphere's colour, scale, position, emission, and interaction timing.
- Shot: camera position, lens, crop, orientation, focus, and cut timing.
- Surface and lighting: skin material, highlight response, studio fill, contact shadows, and background.
- Provenance: source hash, timestamp range, reference frame IDs, annotation version, and generated output lineage.

Growth must be a controlled shape change, not uniform scaling of every bone. Preserve joint centres and limb lengths by default; expand regional cross-sections and use sculpted corrective targets to maintain shoulder, armpit, elbow, hip, knee, and thigh transitions. Pose changes must not advance the growth driver. A held growth state must remain fixed until a new catalyst event explicitly changes it.

Use staged shape targets rather than a single guessed linear curve: baseline; first enlarged bodybuilder state; stronger torso and limb state; extreme final state. Intermediate targets and their timecodes remain provisional until measured against aligned frames. The final reference is a silhouette and presentation target, not a measured anatomical truth.

## Reusable shot grammar for GEEHUB

1. **Establish** the adult character, studio, platform, and catalyst objects in one uncluttered wide composition.
2. **Introduce** the catalyst with a separate insert and a readable hand/object interaction.
3. **Present** the baseline body through a small number of clean, held flex poses.
4. **Bridge** into the first growth state with close-up inserts that reveal local changes, then return to a comparable frontal view.
5. **Reveal** the new full-body silhouette and preserve the catalyst object as a visible continuity anchor.
6. **Inspect** regional anatomy through deliberate crops; preserve identity and distinguish body change from camera change.
7. **Escalate** with a second catalyst beat and a wider pose hold.
8. **Anchor identity** with a face insert before the final scale demonstration.
9. **Conclude** with a stable low frontal wide shot, a clear final flex, and enough held time for the final silhouette to register.

This grammar is reusable across Joey/Swolebeast and other adult GEEHUB characters. Character-specific facial identity, baseline proportions, wardrobe, and history must not be copied from one character to another.

## Iterative production loop

This is an iterative build, not a one-shot generation. Each pass must use the previous pass as its working state and correct it toward the BALLS.mov target. Do not discard a promising result and restart from an unrelated interpretation unless the current branch is demonstrably unusable. Preserve each accepted state and its source settings so later passes can improve it without losing continuity.

Use this loop for both stills and sequences:

1. **Establish a comparable checkpoint.** Fix the character identity, selected growth stage, pose, camera view, wardrobe, and lighting. For body comparison, use the same camera and pose across passes so perspective or articulation cannot disguise a proportion error.
2. **Generate the current pass.** Build the broad silhouette and scene first. For a sequence, include the complete progression and a readable final hold rather than polishing only an isolated frame.
3. **Compare against the reference.** Review the output beside the relevant BALLS.mov time range and reference stills. Assess silhouette and mass first, then regional anatomy, pose, face/identity, wardrobe, catalyst props, camera, materials, lighting, and timing. Record concrete mismatches rather than the vague judgement that it needs to be bigger or better.
4. **Choose the next correction.** Select the highest-impact mismatch and identify the responsible control: regional growth target, corrective shape, articulation, camera, prop, material, light, or edit timing. Change that control deliberately. Keep unrelated controls fixed when testing a body or pose correction.
5. **Render and compare again.** Repeat the same checkpoint comparison. Keep the new pass only when it improves the target without breaking identity, anatomy, continuity, or another already accepted property. Otherwise revert that change and try a different correction.
6. **Lock accepted dimensions.** Once a dimension is working, preserve it while iterating on the next one. Do not repeatedly redesign the face, wardrobe, environment, or camera when the remaining problem is muscle volume or progression timing.
7. **Validate the whole result.** After local passes, review the complete sequence from establishing shot through final hold. Check that the growth is visibly progressive, each stage is distinct, all cuts reconcile to the same character and body state, and the final scale matches the intended level of exaggeration.

Iteration priorities: first establish identity and overall silhouette; then match the scale and regional distribution of muscular growth; then correct anatomical transitions and poses; then align the studio, platform, catalyst interactions, and shot coverage; finally refine surface, lighting, motion, and editorial timing. This order is a practical priority, not a reason to leave later dimensions unfinished.

For a still-generation workflow, iterate on the selected stage until it reaches the reference's intended level of development; then carry the accepted character and visual settings into the next stage. For video, maintain a sequence-level state across all stages, not a collection of unrelated generated frames. Compare adjacent stages for continuity and compare matching poses across stages for actual growth.

Keep a compact pass record for each iteration: pass ID, parent pass, reference timecode, fixed variables, changed variables, observed mismatch, result, decision (keep/revise/revert), and next correction. Preserve failed passes when they contain useful information; do not promote them to canon. Distinguish generated proposals from accepted canon.

## Acceptance tests

1. The same character ID and face remain recognizable at every growth stage.
2. A front and three-quarter comparison shows that growth changes regional volume rather than merely changing camera distance.
3. The shoulder, chest, armpit, elbow, waist, hip, thigh, and knee transitions remain coherent.
4. A pose change at fixed growth leaves the underlying body volume unchanged.
5. A growth change at fixed pose is visibly measurable and does not change limb length or joint placement unintentionally.
6. The spheres remain independently animatable and keep consistent colour / material identity.
7. Wide shots and detail inserts reconcile to the same character and growth state.
8. Wardrobe changes are deliberate, separately authored, and never inferred from a growth parameter.
9. The final wide hold preserves the platform, framing, teal environment, and requested body scale.
10. Every reference frame and derived render links to the exact source hash and timestamp.
