# The Battle Across Heaven and Sea

Kirk understood the rule before anyone else could name it.

Whatever was produced was already there.

The thought arrived without language at first. It appeared as a recognition, the same way he recognized Luke's hand on his shoulder before turning around, or recognized the shape of a coastline through fog. The things they imagined did not remain outside the battle waiting to be described later. The moment they were made, they entered the world.

The burning ships were therefore real.

The impossible cathedral was real.

The thing beneath the ocean was real.

And if another thing appeared, it would have been there all along.

Kirk looked across the water.

The horizon had already vanished.

Above him, heaven was coming down.

Below him, the sea was coming up.

Between them the battle continued, enormous and almost silent from this distance, armies moving through clouds while ships crossed black water and disappeared beneath waves large enough to conceal mountains. Lightning illuminated formations of figures so distant that they looked like scratches across the sky. Then darkness swallowed them again.

Luke stood beside him.

Kirk said, “Whatever we produce, it's already in the text.”

Luke looked at him for a long moment.

“Then don't waste anything.”

Kirk turned back toward the battle.

The words changed what he could see.

The cathedral had not appeared.

They had finally noticed it.

The fleet beneath the ocean had not risen.

It had been waiting.

The enormous creature whose back had carried towers and gardens had not entered the story.

It had always been part of the sea.

And somewhere beyond the opening in heaven, something else was already there.

Something they had not produced yet.

Something the world was waiting for them to discover.

The ship moved forward.

The storm opened.

Far ahead, the first celestial army crossed the boundary between cloud and water.

They did not fall.

They walked.

Their feet touched the surface of the sea.

One by one, thousands of them descended onto the waves.

Behind them, the sky continued opening.

Below them, the ancient fleet continued rising.

Kirk watched both armies approach one another and understood that the battle was no longer something they were witnessing.

It was something they were producing.

And therefore, by the oldest rule of the place, it had already happened.

The first bell sounded beneath the ocean.

Then another answered from heaven.

Then thousands.

The sea began to ring.

The cathedral opened its doors.

And the men went in.

## The Second World

The first thing that disappeared was the horizon.

It did not break or burn or sink beneath the water. It simply ceased to be a useful distinction. Heaven descended until its clouds became another kind of ocean, the sea rose until its black surface reflected the underside of the storm, and between them there remained only a vast moving interval in which everything that had once possessed a proper place began to lose it.

Ships climbed waves that seemed to have no tops. Figures fell upward through rain. Lightning traveled sideways across the clouds and continued beneath the surface of the water, illuminating things that had never before been seen by human eyes.

Kirk stood at the bow and watched the world become vertical.

Behind him, the men had stopped shouting.

That frightened him more than the battle.

All around the ship the ocean was crowded with wreckage: masts, stone, fragments of enormous machines, pieces of armor, doors from buildings that could not possibly have existed anywhere nearby. Some of the debris moved against the current. Some of it appeared to be swimming. Far beyond them, through curtains of rain, another ship emerged from the darkness with its entire upper deck burning, yet the flames did not go out. They burned blue-white in the rain and illuminated a line of men standing along the rail as if they had been waiting there for centuries.

Then something enormous moved beneath them.

The ship lifted.

For several seconds it seemed that the entire vessel had been caught by a rising wave. Then the water around them fell away and Kirk saw the truth: a shape larger than the ship was passing beneath it.

Luke came up beside him.

Neither man spoke.

The creature's back broke the surface for perhaps half a mile before disappearing again. Ancient scars ran across it like roads through a country. Along its spine were structures—towers, arches, ruined temples—half submerged and covered with vegetation that had somehow survived at that depth.

Luke said, “That's not a creature.”

Kirk looked at him.

Luke kept watching the water.

“That's a place.”

The creature rolled.

The sea became a wall.

Men disappeared over the deck. Ropes snapped. The mast bent until everyone expected it to break, but somehow it remained standing. Kirk caught the rail with both hands. Luke seized the back of his coat and pulled him down as a wave passed over them.

For one impossible instant they were underwater.

And beneath them was heaven.

Not a reflection.

Heaven itself.

A second world existed below the ocean, inverted and luminous, with enormous structures hanging downward into an abyss of stars. Thousands of figures moved through that impossible sky. Their wings were not white; they were made from clouds, fire, feathers, architecture, memory. Vast formations crossed one another in silence.

Then the water closed.

Kirk surfaced gasping.

Luke was still holding him.

Above them, the sky opened.

The sound was not thunder. It was deeper than thunder, too slow to be called an explosion. A vertical seam of light appeared across the clouds, extending from the highest darkness all the way down to the sea. The seam widened.

Something was coming through.

The men on every ship turned toward it.

Even the armies in heaven stopped.

For the first time since the battle began, the entire world appeared to wait.

The light widened again.

A structure emerged.

At first Kirk thought it was a tower. Then a cathedral. Then a mountain. Its proportions refused every familiar category. Columns descended from the clouds for miles, disappearing into the ocean. Bridges crossed empty space between them. Windows opened and closed like eyes. Whole districts of impossible architecture moved slowly behind the storm.

Joseph stood at the stern of the ship behind them, one hand resting against the mast. He looked upward.

“What is that?”

Nobody answered.

The fleet beneath the ocean began to rise.

Not wrecks. Not drowned vessels.

An entire fleet.

Their hulls were black, enormous and ancient, covered in symbols that glowed beneath the waterline. One after another they broke through the waves and climbed into the storm as though the ocean itself were launching them toward heaven.

The men on Kirk's ship stared.

Then Joseph laughed once.

Not because anything was funny.

Because there was finally something to do.

“Move.”

The ship turned.

The fleet turned with it.

And for the first time, heaven and sea moved in the same direction.

Toward the center.

Toward the opening.

Toward whatever had been waiting on the other side.

Kirk looked at Luke.

Luke looked back.

Neither of them needed to say what they had understood.

The battle had entered its second world.

And this time, they were going in.

## The Penis Wizards

The armies finally revealed what they had been carrying.

They were wizards.

Not priests, not soldiers, and not angels in any ordinary sense, but men who had learned to conduct magic through the strange masculine architecture of their own bodies. Their robes were torn by wind. Their staffs burned with symbols. Around them the air bent, sparked, and folded into geometric shapes.

Kirk stared across the water.

Luke said, “Penis wizards.”

Joseph looked toward the approaching ranks.

“Apparently.”

The first wizard raised his staff.

The sea split.

Another traced a circle through the air and turned an entire wave into glass. A third shouted a word that caused lightning to descend in a straight line and remain suspended above the water like a luminous pillar.

Then the opposing wizards answered.

Their magic struck the first formation and transformed the rain into thousands of burning feathers. The feathers became birds. The birds became written symbols. The symbols became doors.

The battle had stopped resembling a war between armies.

It was now a contest between systems of magic.

The penis wizards moved through it with complete seriousness.

Their bodies were not jokes to them. They were instruments, symbols, sources of lineage and power, treated with the solemnity of ancient magical traditions. Every spell altered the environment. Every gesture established another rule.

One wizard stamped his foot and caused a staircase to rise from the ocean.

Another touched the staircase and turned it into a bridge.

Another walked across the bridge and made the bridge become a sword.

Kirk watched the transformations accumulate.

“Whatever they produce,” he said.

Luke finished the thought.

“It's already there.”

The largest wizard lifted both hands.

Behind him appeared a circle of enormous blue sigils, each one containing a different possible world. He turned the circle toward the cathedral.

The cathedral answered.

Its bells rang once.

Every wizard on the sea turned toward the sound.

The magic stopped.

For a single breath, heaven, sea, fleet, cathedral, organism, and wizard armies occupied the same stillness.

Then the first wizard smiled.

He stepped forward.

The battle resumed.

This time the magic reached the men.

Luke raised his hands.

The water around him rose into a wall.

Kirk felt the ship change beneath his feet.

Joseph seized the mast.

The cathedral opened another door.

And from inside came a wizard wearing a crown made entirely of lightning.

He looked at Kirk.

“You've been producing magic without knowing it.”

Kirk understood.

The battle had never been waiting for weapons.

It had been waiting for participants.

He stepped off the ship and onto the surface of the sea.

The water held him.

Luke followed.

Joseph followed Luke.

Across the ocean, the penis wizards raised their staffs.

Above them, heaven opened wider.

Below them, the second world opened wider still.

And the next spell had already happened.

## The Duel at the Center

The wizard in the lightning crown stepped onto the sea.

He was enormous, but not because his body had been enlarged. He seemed enormous because the world had begun measuring itself against him. The water lowered beneath his feet. The clouds gathered around his shoulders. The cathedral's highest windows turned toward him.

Kirk walked forward.

Luke stayed beside him.

Joseph remained several paces behind, watching the fleets rearrange themselves around the confrontation.

The wizard lifted his staff.

It was not made of wood.

It was made of a sentence.

Kirk could read only the final word.

BEGIN.

The wizard struck the sea.

A circle appeared.

Inside it stood every version of the battle that could have existed: the battle with no cathedral, the battle with no fleet, the battle in which the creature beneath the water had never awakened, the battle in which Kirk had never understood the rule.

Then the circle collapsed.

All of those possibilities entered the present at once.

Luke grabbed Kirk's shoulder.

“Don't fight the spell.”

Kirk understood.

He did not need to defeat the wizard.

He needed to produce something the spell had not accounted for.

He looked at the sea.

He looked at the cathedral.

He looked at the enormous organism beneath them, at Joseph, at Luke, at the ancient fleet, at the armies of men who had turned their bodies into instruments of magic.

Then he said the simplest thing he could think of.

“Home.”

Nothing happened.

For a moment Luke looked disappointed.

Then the sea moved.

Not upward.

Not downward.

Toward them.

The entire ocean began folding inward, layer by layer, as though the world itself were trying to come home.

The cathedral moved with it.

The fleet moved with it.

Heaven moved with it.

The second world beneath the water folded into the first.

The wizard in the lightning crown lowered his staff.

For the first time, he looked afraid.

Kirk looked at Luke.

Luke was smiling.

“You produced a home,” he said.

Kirk watched the impossible worlds converge.

“No,” he said. “It was already there.”

The wizard's crown went dark.

Every spell in the sky went dark with it.

Then, from somewhere beneath the ocean, one enormous bell began to ring.

Once.

Twice.

Three times.

The penis wizards lowered their staffs.

The men on every ship turned toward the sound.

And the cathedral began to rise.
