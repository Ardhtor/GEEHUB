# Book II Atlas — The Kirk Expedition

**Scope:** The local geography and inhabited settlement introduced in Book II of the Kirk expedition.  
**Status:** Active working atlas; new districts remain subject to revision as the expedition discovers them.  
**Geographic rule:** Kirk's body is living geography, not inert scenery. Changes in his posture, movement, comfort, or condition can alter terrain and access. Kirk remains an adult person with agency, privacy, and the right to pause or revise the expedition's arrangements.

## Canon boundary

This atlas governs the distinct Book II expedition opened in [Chapter I — The Lost Country](chapters/BOOK_II_CHAPTER_I_THE_LOST_COUNTRY.md). It records the local country explored by the expedition and the residences built there. Shared names and motifs do not by themselves merge this book with the Joseph–Kirk story in *The Beginning of the Dimension*, Veyrthalis's experimental *Second Heart* account, or the Colossus Chronicles and Scale Commons. Those worlds retain their own histories. Correspondences may be recorded, but any actual crossing or canon merger must be established deliberately.

The Tractus atlas rule applies: a place needs a route of arrival, a physical character, daily use, people who maintain it, and a reason to return. The map records what is known, what remains open, what is experimental, and what has been retired. It does not pretend the country has been fully surveyed.

## Local geography

### 1. The Ridge

The Ridge is the first long route followed by the expedition. Its broad upper surface carries the remains of an observation platform, a sequence of cedar markers, and the first measurements of Kirk's changing landscape. The Ridge shifts when Kirk moves. Its lines are therefore recorded as dated states rather than treated as permanent coordinates.

**Known:** the first observation platform; cedar marker; route to the concealed Old Trail; the initial survey line.  
**Open:** the western end beyond the first survey; the source of the low travelling pressure; older routes beneath the ridge.  
**Use:** survey, orientation, movement tests, and return checks.

### 2. The Old Trail

The Old Trail descends behind a fold in the Ridge. Its cleared surface, boundary stones, carved posts, and resting places show that other travellers used it before the current expedition. The trail is not yet attributed to a known builder.

**Known:** the carved markers, the sheltered descent, the route toward the First Shelter.  
**Open:** who made it, how many people used it, and where its lower branches lead.  
**Rule:** do not treat a symbol as an instruction until its use is understood.

### 3. The First Shelter

The First Shelter is the expedition's first stable camp and the place where the receiver and primary map were installed. It contains a cedar post, temporary sleeping places, a worktable, a route board, and a small sheltered area for equipment. The receiver carries spoken messages between Kirk and the expedition; it does not transmit thoughts or grant access to anything Kirk has not chosen to communicate.

**Known:** entrance from the Old Trail; receiver; first map wall; camp and equipment storage.  
**Open:** the dark opening beyond the chamber and the changing narrow passage on its far side.  
**Daily rhythm:** morning measurements, meals, map revision, equipment checks, rest, and scheduled contact with Kirk.

### 4. The Long Fold

The Long Fold is the lower route beyond the First Shelter. It appears as a broad, gradually turning passage whose floor and walls do not preserve a single shape between surveys. The ball tests performed by Andrew create an observation layer alongside the surveyors' measurements and Kirk's own description of what he feels.

**Known:** a passage that changes angle; the first rolling-ball observations; the Warm Table at its lower approach.  
**Open:** the full length of the passage, the cause of its changing curvature, and the route beyond the Warm Table.  
**Evidence rule:** instrument readings, visible terrain, and Kirk's reported sensations remain separate records until they can be compared.

### 5. The Warm Table

The Warm Table is a wide, gently sloped shelf at the lower end of the Long Fold. Its surface is warmer than the surrounding stone-like tissue and carries the quiet recurring movement of the Twin Grounds. It is the arrival terrace for the settlement and the point at which the expedition first understands that the two adjoining grounds can support a residential district.

**Known:** broad approach, sheltered surface, two rising grounds, and a valley between them.  
**Open:** the far edges of both grounds and whether the valley contains older construction.  
**Use:** arrival, gathering, route planning, and the shared approach to the settlement.

### 6. The Twin Grounds

The Twin Grounds are two adjoining, living territories within Kirk's extraordinary anatomy, separated by a sheltered valley and joined by the Cedar Bridge. The western and eastern grounds have distinct slopes, surfaces, and movement patterns. Residents use the neutral name **Twin Grounds** on public maps; the expedition's first informal description remains in the historical record.

The district is not treated as a fixed model. A change in Kirk's posture or condition may alter a terrace, road, view, or threshold. Residents must be able to report a change and request a pause. The settlement's architecture is designed to accommodate gradual movement without concealing the fact that the ground has changed.

**Known:** western terrace; northern rise; eastern garden terrace; bridge valley; Cedar Bridge; shared hall and four residential mansions.  
**Open:** outer boundary, southern route, lower access, older traces beneath the eastern terrace.  
**Daily rhythm:** meals in the shared hall, work on houses and paths, private time, walks between residences, recreational use of the bridge valley, and direct contact with Kirk when mutually convenient.

## Residences

All four residences are independent homes, not administrative offices. No one receives authority over the settlement merely by arriving first, owning the largest house, or speaking most often with Kirk.

### Robert Laskin — Western Mansion

**Site:** upper western terrace.  
**Character:** cedar-built mansion with deep porch, observatory, library wing, guest gallery, and a lawn facing the Cedar Bridge.  
**Use:** hosting, reading, looking outward, and receiving visitors.  
**Distinctive provision:** a secondary bridge stair so visitors can reach the shared route without crossing Robert's private rooms.  
**Continuity:** Robert's residence was first imagined as the Laskin Residency; this atlas records it as the first completed mansion in the Twin Grounds.

### Chase — Northern Archive House

**Site:** northern rise, overlooking the bridge without dominating it.  
**Character:** a mansion built around an archive court with a cedar tree, four covered walks, a map room, a high library, a large kitchen, reading rooms, and a private terrace.  
**Use:** keeping records, comparing accounts, quiet hospitality, and preserving the changing history of the district.  
**Distinctive provision:** the archive keeps original maps beside later revisions. Conflicting observations are attributed rather than forced into one account. The house is a home, not a command post.

### Gabe — Eastern Garden Mansion

**Site:** eastern terrace beside a sheltered garden.  
**Character:** a staged timber-and-glass house with a great central hall, kitchen, workshop, training room, plant rooms, balconies, portrait gallery, and an unfinished upper room.  
**Use:** making, repair, training, gardening, and gathering without requiring anyone to perform or display a transformation.  
**Distinctive provision:** portraits and measurement cards preserve earlier baselines as part of a person's history rather than ranking them. The upper room remains unfinished until its purpose is chosen.

### Joseph — East Bridge Mansion

**Site:** quiet eastern end of the Cedar Bridge.  
**Character:** a broad veranda, two residential wings joined by a bright central room, paired garden courts, a domestic kitchen, library, and a narrow rear tower overlooking the bridge.  
**Use:** reading, cooking, private conversation, and a quiet view across the settlement.  
**Distinctive provision:** openable windows in every main room; the house is designed to hear the district rather than withdraw from it. The rear tower is for occasional observation, not permanent watch.

## Shared infrastructure

### Cedar Bridge

The Cedar Bridge crosses the valley between the two grounds. It is a shared route and a social place in its own right, with broad timber decking, sheltered benches, low lanterns, and two independent approach stairs. It is checked after any reported movement. The bridge is not a private extension of any mansion.

### Common Hall

The Common Hall stands beside the bridge on a neutral shelf. Its dining room, covered recreation court, assembly room, washrooms, and receiver alcove serve all residents. The receiver alcove is a practical communication point, not a surveillance station. Attendance is voluntary.

### Paths and maintenance

The paths between the mansions are pale, lightly textured paving with small timber markers. They are inspected after changes in the Twin Grounds. Drainage, lighting, handrails, and resting places are maintained by a rotating resident crew. No one is expected to cross the valley alone at night or during a movement alert.

## Social geography and relationship continuity

Andrew Sim is not a resident of one of the four mansions at the time of the first settlement plan. His attention tends to gather near the receiver, the Cedar Bridge, and whichever work gives him a reason to speak with Kirk. His friendship and chemistry with Kirk remain an established, renewable relationship. As Andrew's need for contact intensifies, his repeated calls begin to change the social geography: Joseph starts choosing the quieter parts of the East Bridge Mansion rather than competing for a place at the receiver. This is a developing tension, not a final decision or a declaration that Joseph has left.

The relationship canon remains active: attraction to the same man does not automatically make the others enemies; every character keeps agency; attention is not an entitlement; and established bonds are not silently erased. Kirk may set limits on the frequency and purpose of calls. Andrew may want more attention, but must learn to ask directly and accept an answer. Joseph's established history with Kirk remains intact and must be developed through his own actions, not reduced to a rival role.

Chase's records preserve the difference between what Andrew asks, what Kirk answers, and what Joseph chooses. Gabe notices the social effects of attention and can distinguish a lively gathering from a man being made into an audience. Neither becomes a narrator who claims to know another person's private thoughts.

## Atlas state register

### Known

- Ridge, Old Trail, First Shelter, Long Fold, Warm Table, and the Twin Grounds.
- Cedar Bridge and Common Hall.
- Robert's Western Mansion, Chase's Northern Archive House, Gabe's Eastern Garden Mansion, and Joseph's East Bridge Mansion.
- The receiver as a spoken communication device with Kirk.
- The initial social tension caused by Andrew seeking more of Kirk's attention and Joseph taking a step back.

### Open

- The outer limits and full dimensions of both grounds.
- Older routes and settlement traces below the Warm Table.
- Whether the Cedar Bridge needs a flexible joint or a second crossing.
- The long-term use of Gabe's unfinished upper room.
- How Andrew and Joseph will negotiate their changed access to Kirk without turning the relationship into a contest.

### Experimental

- Any proposal for a third ground, underground district, or further mansion.
- A permanent schedule for Kirk's calls.
- Any cross-reference that would merge this expedition with the Beginning of the Dimension, Veyrthalis, or the Colossus Chronicles.

### Retired

- None at this atlas revision. Superseded measurements remain in Chase's archive rather than being deleted.

## Mapping protocol

Every significant movement produces a dated map state with: the route observed, the source of the observation, Kirk's own account where available, changes to access, affected buildings, and any safety or maintenance action. Previous versions remain available. Do not assign exact heights, anatomical measurements, distances, or geographic coordinates without an established measurement in the story. The map must show uncertainty honestly.

**Governing line:** The ground may move; the record keeps its history, and the residents keep their right to choose.
