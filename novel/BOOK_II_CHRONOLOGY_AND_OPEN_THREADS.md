# Book II — Chronology and Open-Thread Map

**Work:** *The Kirk Expedition*  
**Status:** working editorial map, 24 November 2026  
**Scope:** canonical Chapters I–IX and the supporting Book II spatial, relationship, and Evermeasure records.  
**Purpose:** preserve the order of events, distinguish established facts from unresolved questions, and guide the whole-book revision without inventing dates, measurements, or outcomes.

This map is a navigation aid for revision, not a replacement for the chapters. The scenes remain the source of narrative truth. Where the chapters establish sequence but not elapsed time, the map records the sequence and leaves the calendar open. Where a place has been named but not surveyed, it remains named and unmeasured. Where a character has made a promise, the promise remains a choice that must be lived rather than a guarantee that future conflict has disappeared.

## Chronology of the present manuscript

### Movement 1 — The ridge and the First Shelter

**Source:** [Chapter I — The Lost Country](chapters/BOOK_II_CHAPTER_I_THE_LOST_COUNTRY.md)

The expedition begins on the upper ridge, believing it can establish a stable observation point and use repeated measurements to orient itself. Marcus challenges the surveyor's reliance on step-counting when the ridge appears to curve around the party. The expedition leader preserves both accounts rather than deciding prematurely whether the disagreement comes from movement, perspective, or instrument alignment.

A movement passes through the country while the men are present. The observation platform shifts, its supports no longer meet the ground, and the cedar marker is displaced. The men recover their instruments and establish a second marker to preserve the difference between the first and second states. The movement is not treated as an ordinary earthquake. The expedition understands that Kirk has moved and that the geography belongs to a living adult man.

The party discovers the maintained Old Trail, including carved posts, handholds, repaired stones, and traces of earlier travellers. The route descends into an amber-lit corridor and reaches the First Shelter, where the dark opening remains unentered. Kirk communicates through the receiver, confirms that he felt the movement, asks whether the men are safe, and tells them not to proceed farther that night. The expedition makes a layered map and establishes a temporary camp.

**State carried forward:** upper ridge; shifted observation platform; cedar marker and its displaced reference; Old Trail; amber corridor; First Shelter; dark opening; first two-state map. The identity of the earlier trail-makers and the destination of the dark opening remain unknown.

### Movement 2 — The Measure House and its keepers

**Source:** [Chapter II — The Keepers of the Old Trail](chapters/BOOK_II_CHAPTER_II_THE_KEEPERS_OF_THE_OLD_TRAIL.md)

After recording the altered First Shelter twice, the second expedition follows the Old Trail under an agreed signal protocol. Marcus carries the cedar marker; the surveyor carries the instruments; the leader keeps the receiver; three men remain at the shelter. The party chooses the upper branch at a fork and discovers a gallery of niches, tokens, and a preserved map table.

Oren, Vale, and Senn meet the newcomers. They belong to an established settlement of twenty-three residents at the Measure House, with additional people travelling along the routes. Their archive contains maps revised over months and years. The settlement knows Kirk and has communicated with him. Its records treat the country as living anatomy and the man as a person whose experience cannot be replaced by expedition measurements. The fork's paired lines and circle remain ambiguous until Senn compares the newcomers' copies with the register; the sign indicates an unresolved route rather than a destination. The settlement's daily life includes a workshop, kitchen, repaired furniture, spare markers, and the ongoing work of maintaining the archive. Incomplete pages and conflicting accounts are preserved rather than silently completed by guesswork.

Oren shows the newcomers a newer map in which the upper ridge, sheltered corridor, and lower route have changed. The party sends a report to Kirk and leaves the lower route unexplored. The Measure House becomes a partner in the expedition's work rather than a mysterious ruin or a storehouse of answers.

**State carried forward:** Measure House; Oren, Vale, and Senn; twenty-three residents at the time of the visit; trail-marking conventions; dated map archive; signal exchange between the First Shelter and the settlement. The lower route is known to have changed and remains closed to the newcomers.

### Movement 3 — The Long Fold and the Warm Table

**Source:** [Chapter III — The Place That Moved](chapters/BOOK_II_CHAPTER_III_THE_PLACE_THAT_MOVED.md)

Oren reads the newcomers' report twice and questions both what it says and what it leaves out. He explains that the Measure House maps routes from places the residents can return to, not only from the point where a traveller entered. He selects an older map showing the Long Fold and Warm Table, where a formerly continuous lower route has been altered over time.

Oren and Vale accompany the party. At the Long Fold, the old chamber has divided: a raised band crosses the floor, the lower resting area has narrowed, and an upper gallery has opened. The former route remains legible in its traces but no longer functions in the same way. The Warm Table has tilted, and a narrow dark opening has appeared behind it. The expedition measures the changed state and sends the findings to Kirk. Kirk reports a tightening followed by release and asks the men to remain clear of the opening until he understands the change. The old marker is partly buried in the raised band, and a former lamp recess remains visible but is no longer treated as a usable shelter. The upper gallery is recorded as a possible passage without a confirmed connection. The survey party withdraws after measuring the Warm Table's tilt, the air current, and the repeated vibration; the dark opening remains unentered.

The party withdraws. Senn prepares a new map that preserves the former chamber, raised band, new gallery, and two states of the Warm Table. The opening remains a question, not a route. The Measure House begins placing successive maps beside one another as a dated archive.

**State carried forward:** Long Fold; raised band; upper gallery; Warm Table in former and current positions; dark opening behind the Warm Table; the rule that familiarity does not establish present safety. No one has entered the opening.

### Movement 4 — Andrew and Kirk renew their promises

**Source:** [Chapter IV — The Promises That Keep](chapters/BOOK_II_CHAPTER_IV_THE_PROMISES_THAT_KEEP.md)

Andrew Sim arrives at the Measure House after the lower-route survey. His familiar humour and athletic attention give the expedition another way to observe the country, but he does not replace the surveyor's measurements or Kirk's account. His relationship with Kirk is established before this book's events. Their chemistry returns through teasing, careful listening, shared experiments, and an honest conversation about what each can promise.

Andrew uses a baseball to observe a small movement near the lower passage. The ball's path becomes a separate observation layer rather than proof of what the movement means. Kirk describes his sensations and the limits of what he can infer. They renew their promises as commitments that can be chosen again: Andrew will listen before inventing explanations, return when he says he will, and leave Kirk room to decide; Kirk will communicate changes and not turn Andrew's return into a test. Their closeness remains affectionate without becoming ownership.

**State carried forward:** Andrew's established bond with Kirk; the receiver as a means of direct communication; rolling-ball observations kept separate from measurements and bodily reports; renewable promises. The lower opening remains unentered.

### Movement 5 — The four mansions and the residential district

**Source:** [Chapter V — Four Mansions](chapters/BOOK_II_CHAPTER_V_FOUR_MANSIONS.md)

Kirk offers the expedition members the possibility of separate homes in a broad, sheltered region within the paired chambers. The offer is not an instruction to settle. Robert, Chase, Gabe, and Joseph each choose a different kind of home: Laskin Mansion, the Record House, the Canvas House, and the House of Returns. Their houses are arranged around a shared green with a long table, fountain, trees, playing field, pavilion, common kitchen, and recreation hall. The positions are provisional; the exact chamber placement and distances are not surveyed.

Andrew's wish for Kirk's attention begins to crowd Joseph out of shared time. Joseph tells Kirk that he wants their existing promises to remain real without having to defend his place every time Andrew arrives. Kirk acknowledges the commitment and honours the visit already promised to Joseph. Andrew recognises that his desire for closeness does not entitle him to every interval. The conflict is not resolved, but its terms become explicit.

**State carried forward:** four separate homes and their distinct purposes; shared green and common facilities; the paired residential country; Andrew's attention pressure; Joseph's request for reliable time; Kirk's commitment to honour promises already made.

### Movement 6 — The Long Return and the first repair in the social field

**Source:** [Chapter VI — The Game Between the Houses](chapters/BOOK_II_CHAPTER_VI_THE_GAME_BETWEEN_THE_HOUSES.md)

The residents develop the Long Return, a cooperative rolling-ball route across the Twin Grounds, using the Cedar Span, eastern hollow, markers, and a return arch. Gabe insists on warm-up, recovery, and alternate routes. Chase separates measurements, Kirk's reported sensations, and Andrew's hypotheses. Robert develops a viewing arch and miniature-theatre projection. Joseph participates by choice and suggests a lower return line.

Andrew again allows his desire for Kirk's attention to dominate a shared exchange. Joseph leaves rather than compete for the receiver. Andrew notices the consequence, visits Joseph, and acknowledges that he made it harder for Joseph to remain in the conversation. Joseph tells him not to make every room a place where he must move aside. Andrew agrees to ask for time rather than take it, and to let Kirk finish the time already promised to Joseph. Kirk's later visit with Joseph proceeds as arranged; Andrew keeps the time he has been offered for the following morning.

**State carried forward:** Long Return as a cooperative game; Cedar Span and eastern hollow; recovery points and alternate routes; Andrew's first explicit conduct change after Joseph names the harm. The Long Return is not a contest over Kirk's body or attention.

### Movement 7 — The Evermeasure becomes an enduring culture

**Source:** [Chapter VII — The Contest That Never Ends](chapters/BOOK_II_CHAPTER_VII_THE_CONTEST_THAT_NEVER_ENDS.md)

The residents turn their tendency to show off and answer one another into the Evermeasure, a continuing exhibition and challenge practice. Andrew invents games; Chase insists on defensible records; Gabe demonstrates technique and physical development; Robert designs presentation and facilities; Joseph contributes through quiet craft; Michael Brock arrives as a very large, heavily muscled adult visitor and participates in the public exhibition.

The Evermeasure Board preserves dated achievements, methods, previous marks, and next challenges. It is not a permanent ranking of men's worth. Michael and Kirk share a secret, gentle relationship that remains unknown to the other residents. Their private exchange is kept outside the public record. Kirk may enjoy the residents' inventiveness but is not required to perform bodily movements on demand or serve as a prize in their rivalry.

**State carried forward:** Evermeasure and its Board; recursive one-upmanship; Michael as visitor; Michael and Kirk's secret connection; the rule that affection and bodily autonomy are not scored.

### Movement 8 — Michael's day in the pavilion

**Source:** [Chapter VIII — A Day in the Open Pavilion](chapters/BOOK_II_CHAPTER_VIII_A_DAY_IN_THE_OPEN_PAVILION.md)

Michael returns as a visitor for a day of practical work. Gabe gives him a warm-up and recovery sequence before he helps carry the removable Evermeasure display frame. The first route passes through the Cedar Span; a second route uses the western opening and a resting bay. Andrew initially wants speed, but Michael asks him to listen and stop when needed. The paired carry becomes cleaner when they match their pace.

Joseph's Cedar Span bench receives a practical adjustment. Michael's day includes shared lunch and ordinary conversation, and his conspicuous physical size becomes one aspect of his participation rather than the entire event. His private evening exchange with Kirk remains gentle and secret. The public record describes the carry and coordination, not the relationship.

**State carried forward:** removable display frame; completed first carry; Cedar Span bench adjustment; improved coordination through matched pace; Michael remains a visitor and has no assigned mansion. The next proposed challenge is a longer passage with a narrower turn, subject to survey.

### Movement 9 — The Narrow Turn and the visual study

**Source:** [Chapter IX — The Narrow Turn](chapters/BOOK_II_CHAPTER_IX_THE_NARROW_TURN.md)

Andrew's proposed longer carry becomes a temporary adjustable mock-up inside the common pavilion. The mock-up is not a new geographic feature and does not alter the Cedar Span, eastern hollow, or Kirk's body. Robert designs the apparatus; Chase records clearances and distinguishes first success from repeatability; Gabe maintains voluntary warm-up and recovery; Joseph makes a removable oak corner cradle; Andrew and Gabe complete the first cradle-assisted passage, while Robert and Andrew complete a second-pair passage after instruction.

Robert develops a miniature-theatre reconstruction with separate views of the carriers' hands and the far corner. The reconstruction explains the geometry but is not footage of the actual passage. The Evermeasure gains two possible records: cleanest controlled passage and fastest clean passage. A stop or contact makes a timed attempt ineligible, but the failed attempt remains useful evidence.

Andrew asks Joseph for technical judgement and acknowledges that he wants to be noticed without making Joseph compete for a place beside him. Kirk independently confirms an already arranged conversation with Andrew. No bodily movement is requested or performed for the exhibition. Michael is absent as an ordinary visitor; the secrecy of his relationship with Kirk is preserved.

**State carried forward:** temporary Narrow Turn mock-up; revised oak cradle; first two carrier-pair successes; miniature-theatre reconstruction; clean-control and speed categories proposed; fresh-day repeatability test still open.

## Geographic and object continuity

**Established places:** upper ridge; First Shelter; Old Trail; Measure House; Long Fold; upper gallery; Warm Table; paired chambers; four mansions; shared green; common pavilion; Twin Grounds; Cedar Span; eastern hollow; viewing arch; Long Return route.

**Unentered or incompletely surveyed places:** dark opening behind the Warm Table; lower opening beyond the Warm Table; exact route between the Warm Table and paired residential country; relative positions and measured separations of the four mansions; exact Cedar Span alignment and Long Return distances; any stable route after a future bodily movement.

**Objects and records that carry history:** cedar marker and displaced reference; layered maps; Measure House trail symbols; receiver; baseball used for rolling-ball observations; Long Return ball and markers; Evermeasure Board; removable display frame; Cedar Span bench; original and revised oak corner cradle; temporary Narrow Turn mock-up; miniature-theatre reconstruction. The first cradle version remains in Joseph's workshop drawer; the revised one stays with the display frame.

No coordinates, distances, or permanent new landforms are introduced by Chapter IX. The Narrow Turn is an adjustable pavilion apparatus, not a route across Kirk's anatomy.

## Open threads and their present status

1. **The dark opening behind the Warm Table.** Unentered. No safe route, destination, or purpose has been established. A fresh survey and Kirk's knowledge are required before anyone proceeds.
2. **The Old Trail's origin.** The Measure House knows the trail and maintains its marks, but the first builders and earliest history remain unknown.
3. **The Measure House's wider network.** Twenty-three residents were present at the visit, with additional people travelling. Their wider routes and relations remain only partly known.
4. **The changing Long Fold and Warm Table.** Their previous and current states are preserved. Future movements may change them again; old maps remain historical evidence.
5. **The paired residential country.** Four houses are established, but exact chamber positions and distances remain unmeasured.
6. **Kirk's movement signals.** The expedition has a receiver and reports, but no complete predictive method. Kirk may describe what he feels without knowing the map's geometry.
7. **The Long Return.** Its first cooperative route is established. Its later variations must retain alternate routes, recovery points, and the right to stop.
8. **The Evermeasure.** Its recursive culture is established. No final champion or single ranking is intended.
9. **The Narrow Turn.** Two pairs have passed the mock-up. A fresh-day repeatability test remains open; the timed record has not been earned.
10. **Andrew's attention and conduct.** He has recognised the cost to Joseph and begun asking for time. The change must be demonstrated in later shared scenes and in his next conversation with Kirk.
11. **Joseph and Kirk's promises.** Their relationship remains active and chosen. The next scenes must show the promises as lived time, not merely as words preserved in a record.
12. **Michael's privacy.** His secret relationship with Kirk remains unknown to the residents. No disclosure or residence assignment has been established.
13. **The exhibition's visual language.** Robert's miniature-theatre study is a reconstruction, not source footage. A finished exhibition sequence has not yet been produced or reviewed.

## Revision order and editorial gates

The nine chapters are at different stages. Chapter I has completed its first in-place expansion pass at 4,609 narrative words, within the publishing guide's standard 4,000–7,000-word range; its whole-book continuity review remains pending. Chapters IV–VIII remain below that range and need development where the material supports it. Chapter IX is a new working draft at 4,162 words, also within the range. These figures are editorial guides, not quotas. Expansion must come from scenes that deepen character, place, evidence, action, and consequence, not from padding or repeated explanation.

After each revision, fetch the committed source again, recalculate its narrative word count excluding the continuity record, update the source hash, and review its influence on the atlas, relationship records, indexes, and later chapters. The book is not complete until its ending has been earned and the entire manuscript has passed a final continuity and copyedit review.
