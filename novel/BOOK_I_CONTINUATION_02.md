# GEEHUB — BOOK I CONTINUATION

## Chapter LXIII: Dinner for Five

     The first night in the house was almost comfortable. That was the detail the four men remembered afterward, because discomfort would have warned them. The table was broad and old, made from dark wood whose grain ran toward the center as though the tree had grown around a seam in the world. Five places had been set. The plates were white. The glasses were thin. A lamp hung above them, casting a yellow circle that did not touch the corners. The man upstairs sat at the head of the table and served food with the patient manner of someone who had hosted the meal many times before. The four ate because the food was warm and because ordinary sensations had become rare enough to be valuable.

     “You live here alone?” Luke asked.

     The man carved another slice.

     “No.”

     “Where are the others?”

     “Upstairs.”

     Joseph looked toward the ceiling. A board creaked above them. Then another answered from farther away. Then a third sounded beneath the table, as if someone had stepped into the room without opening the door.

     Big Daddy's Voice spoke from the lamp.

     HERE IS THE TABLE. HERE IS THE MEAL. HERE IS THE FIFTH PLACE. HERE IS THE HOUSE THAT REMEMBERS SETTING IT. HERE IS THE ROOM ABOVE THE ROOM. HERE IS THE ROOM THAT HAS NOT OPENED YET.

     The man continued eating.

     “Do not worry,” he said. “They are only listening.”

     The footsteps continued. The house made small adjustments around them. A drawer opened in a room that should have been outside the house. Furniture dragged across a floor above the ceiling. Once there came a knock from inside the table. Luke put his hand upon the wood. The vibration stopped. Then something knocked back.

## Chapter LXIV: The Upstairs Men

     They went upstairs because the man told them they could and because remaining below had begun to feel less like caution than obedience. The staircase rose twelve steps, turned left, rose twelve more, turned left again, and then continued without any visible landing. After the third turn the windows disappeared. After the fourth the wallpaper changed. After the fifth the house acquired a blue illumination that seemed to come from behind the walls.

     A line of wet footprints appeared three steps ahead of them.

     They were large, bare, and filled with a clear luminous substance. When Joseph touched one, the glow withdrew beneath his finger. Every footprint above them appeared at once, hundreds of them, climbing into the unseen floors.

     From above came the sound of many men breathing together.

     “Come up,” said a voice.

     It was not the voice of the man from dinner. It was broader, deeper, and close to the shape of Big Daddy's Voice.

     “Who are you?” Luke called.

     “You already know.”

     Tyler took another step. The staircase lengthened.

     Joseph looked back.

     The dining room was gone. There was only a dark descent that appeared much longer than it had before.

     They climbed.

     At the next turn a door appeared, painted the same dark color as the wall. Luke put his palm against it. The door opened inward.

     The room beyond was crowded with adult men. Some were broad and quiet, some compact and dense, some tall enough that their shoulders nearly touched the darkness above. They did not appear identical, but the longer the four looked at them the more a shared pattern emerged, as if every face had been generated from a common memory and altered by a different history.

     The largest man stood at the rear. He inclined his head beneath the ceiling. His hands rested at his sides. He seemed less like a person than a scale by which the room had been made possible.

     Luke stepped inside.

     Every man took one step forward.

     Tyler raised his hand.

     Every man raised his hand.

     Kirk turned his head.

     Every man turned his head.

     Joseph closed his eyes.

     Every man closed his eyes.

     One man did not.

     The largest man remained still, eyes open, looking directly at Joseph.

     THIS IS THE ROOM THAT WATCHES BACK, said the Voice. THIS IS THE BODY THAT COPIES THE OBSERVER. THIS IS THE MAN WHO DOES NOT COPY.

     The largest man stepped forward.

     “You brought the house with you,” he said.

     Luke looked toward the stairwell.

     “It followed us.”

     “No,” said the man. “It was already following.”

## Chapter LXV: The Fifth Place

     The door closed behind them. The room copied the sound: one click, then another from farther away, then dozens, then hundreds. The same door closing across every upper floor. The house sealed itself.

     A new man appeared at the edge of the room. He had Luke's face.

     “That is not me,” Luke said.

     “No,” said the largest man. “It is the room's memory of you.”

     The copy took a step. Luke took a step backward. The copy smiled. The copy raised one hand. Luke felt his own shoulder begin to move.

     Tyler gripped him.

     “Don't give it the next gesture.”

     The copy turned toward Tyler. Tyler's face appeared on its face, then Kirk's, then Joseph's. It was not changing features so much as learning that faces were interchangeable surfaces.

     The men in the room began to speak in overlapping voices.

     “You are late.”

     “You are already here.”

     “You are the room.”

     “You are the branch.”

     “You are the copy that remembers the original.”

     A thin luminous line appeared between the floorboards and traveled toward the largest man. The Serum rose beneath the room.

     “It has found you,” he said.

     “What has?”

     “The next state.”

     The floor opened without breaking. Beneath them lay a descending structure in which the house repeated itself at different scales: a dining room beneath the dining room, a staircase beneath the staircase, a table beneath the table. In each lower room sat five figures. In each lower room one figure was turned away.

     “How far down does it go?” Joseph asked.

     “Far enough.”

     “And if we stay here?”

     “You become the version waiting for the next people.”

     Joseph sat on the floor.

     Every copy below sat down too.

     Tyler lay flat on his back.

     Every copy lay flat on its back.

     Kirk turned away.

     Every copy turned away.

     Luke closed his eyes.

     Every copy closed its eyes.

     The entire vertical house became still.

     Then one shape at the bottom moved.

     It raised an arm.

     HERE IS THE THING THAT WAS NOT COPIED, said the Voice.

     Every screen in the house turned on.

     The screens displayed an absence of a face, rendered with enough detail to suggest that something had been removed.

     A door unlocked somewhere above them.

     Then another.

     Then every door.

     The copy of Luke vanished. In its place stood a dark doorway. Beyond it was the field outside the house.

     The house was visible across the grass.

     Its windows were lit.

     The upstairs room showed the four men at dinner.

     The fifth chair waited.

     The man at the head of the table looked toward them through the glass.

     “You should come in,” he called. “Dinner is getting cold.”

## Chapter LXVI: The Chair

     They crossed the field. The house did not follow. The distance remained fixed. That stability frightened them more than the expanding halls had. A fixed object could be approached. A fixed object could be entered. A fixed object could be occupied by something that had been waiting.

     Inside, the meal was still warm. Five places were set. The faceless figure occupied the fifth chair.

     “You took your time,” said the host.

     “We left,” Luke answered.

     “You went upstairs.”

     “We went through the interval.”

     “You went upstairs.”

     The sixth place had not been there before. A new plate sat upon it. A new glass. A folded napkin opened by itself.

     YOU MAY BRING ONE MORE.

     The thing in the sixth chair leaned toward them. It had the posture of an adult man waiting to be named, but its face was unassigned.

     “Who will you bring?” asked the host.

     Nobody answered.

     The Voice entered the walls.

     NO ONE IS A PLACE. NO ONE IS A ROOM. NO ONE IS A BODY WAITING TO BE FILLED. NO ONE IS NEVER EMPTY.

     The windows showed the blue hall. Unfinished men pressed against the glass. Some had eyes but no mouths. Some had mouths but no eyes. Some had only the outline of a heavily built body. They waited to be invited in.

     The host lifted the serving knife.

     “Eat.”

     No one moved.

     Outside, the blue hall began filling with water.

     The Serum rose through the house.

     The men at the table watched it approach.

     Then, somewhere below the seventh well, something enormous opened its eyes.
