# GEEHUB — BOOK I MISSING CHAPTERS / CONTINUITY PASS

## Chapter XXIV: The House of the Seventh Light

     The city did not sleep after the battle at the lake. It changed its arrangement each night, as though sleep were a form of cartography and every dream moved a building. The men discovered this on the first morning when they woke to find the street outside their residence ending at the wall of a room that had not existed when they went to bed. Luke opened the door. Beyond it stood a field. Tyler opened a window. Beyond it stood the same field from a different season. Kirk climbed onto the roof and found that the roof overlooked the lake, although the house stood miles inland. Joseph remained at the table, studying the blank paper that had followed him from the northern road.

     “It is drawing itself,” Joseph said.

     Luke came back inside.

     “The city?”

     “The next place.”

     The paper had acquired a line. It was not a map in the ordinary sense. It was a relation between four points: the house, the lake, the seventh well, and a fifth place represented only by a circle. Whenever Joseph moved the paper, the line changed. Whenever he stopped touching it, the line returned to the same position.

     The men understood that the city had become too large to navigate by distance. It could only be navigated by continuity. They began walking.

     At dusk they reached the house of the seventh light. It stood alone beyond the last district, built from dark timber and pale stone. Seven lamps burned above the entrance. Six were blue. The seventh was amber.

     Inside they found no furniture except a long table and seven chairs.

     Six men sat.

     The seventh chair was empty.

     A voice came from upstairs.

     “You took long enough.”

     Luke looked toward the ceiling.

     “We've heard that before.”

     Something laughed above them.

     The house became still.

     They waited.

     Then a man descended the stairs carrying a lantern.

     He was not the seventh man.

     He was the first keeper.

     “The city has remembered you,” he said.

     Joseph looked at the empty chair.

     “And what happens now?”

     The keeper placed the lantern on the table.

     “Now it remembers what you do next.”

## Chapter XXV: The Keeper's Map

     The keeper's map was made from cloth.

     It covered the entire table and seemed at first to contain only a blank field. When the keeper poured water onto it, roads appeared. When he removed the water, the roads remained for several minutes before fading.

     “The map cannot show a place until somebody has carried something there,” he explained.

     Kirk examined the northern road.

     “Then this isn't a map of geography.”

     “No.”

     Tyler pointed to a thin line extending beyond the city.

     “What is that?”

     “A decision.”

     Luke looked at the keeper.

     “Whose?”

     “Yours.”

     Nobody touched the line.

     The keeper smiled.

     “You keep expecting the world to tell you where to go.”

     Joseph folded the blank paper.

     “It keeps giving us places.”

     “Places are easy. Consequences are harder.”

     The keeper led them into the back room. Seven objects rested on shelves: the brass key, the black stone, the folded map, the blank paper, a piece of red glass, a wooden handle, and a small metal stop from the Sacred Fountain.

     Each object belonged to a different history.

     The keeper placed them together.

     Nothing happened.

     Then Luke reached toward the key.

     The other six objects moved.

     Not physically. Their relationships changed. The key became the beginning of the map. The stone became the weight of the road. The paper became the possibility of return. The wooden handle became the memory of a door.

     The keeper nodded.

     “Now you understand.”

     “Understand what?”

     “An artifact is never only itself.”

     Outside, the seventh amber lamp went dark.

     The house had finished its lesson.

## Chapter XXVI: The Battle Beneath the City

     The first attack came from below.

     The pavement opened beneath the eastern district and swallowed an entire row of residences. Men ran through the streets carrying screens, books, tools, photographs, and pieces of machinery. The city did not collapse. It rearranged itself around the missing buildings.

     Luke reached the square first.

     Something was rising from the opening.

     It had no fixed shape. At one moment it resembled a machine. At another, a man. At another, a staircase descending into itself.

     “That's from the reservoir,” Tyler said.

     “No,” Kirk answered. “It's from the part of the reservoir nobody remembered.”

     The creature struck the square.

     The impact produced no explosion. Instead, every person nearby remembered a different version of the same moment. Some remembered the creature arriving. Others remembered it already standing there. One man remembered defeating it before the battle began.

     Joseph understood the danger.

     “Don't fight its versions.”

     Luke looked at him.

     “Then what do we fight?”

     “The thing connecting them.”

     They stopped attacking the body.

     They began following the relation.

     Wherever one version moved, another appeared. Wherever one door closed, another opened. The men moved from street to street until they found the central point: a small black mark beneath the pavement.

     Kirk placed his hand over it.

     “This is the artifact.”

     Tyler understood.

     “The creature is what happens when the artifact has no history.”

     Joseph placed the blank paper beside the mark.

     Luke placed the key on top.

     The black shape stopped moving.

     The city breathed.

     The creature collapsed into a single shadow.

     Then it became a road.

## Chapter XXVII: The Road Made of Men

     The road began at the eastern gate and continued into the forest. It was not made from stone. It was made from memory.

     Every few yards another footprint appeared, and each footprint belonged to somebody who had once crossed the city. Soldiers. Children. Physicians. Device Men. The sister from the lake. The keeper. The first man. People whose names had been lost.

     The men walked among them.

     Luke recognized one footprint.

     “That's mine.”

     Tyler pointed farther ahead.

     “That's yours too.”

     Luke laughed.

     “I haven't been there.”

     “Not yet.”

     They continued.

     The road became crowded with possible histories. A man would appear beside them for a few steps and then disappear. A woman carrying a lantern walked beside Joseph. A child followed Kirk. Tyler heard someone calling his name from a bridge that did not exist.

     None of them turned.

     The road had taught them something the city had not: not every appearance required an answer.

     At midnight they reached a clearing.

     In the center stood a door.

     There was no wall around it.

     The door opened onto darkness.

     Joseph approached.

     “Is this the next room?”

     Luke shook his head.

     “No.”

     “How do you know?”

     Luke looked at the footprints.

     “Because somebody already came through.”

     On the other side of the door was a road leading away.

     They followed it.

## Chapter XXVIII: The Country Between

     The country beyond the door had no name because names changed too quickly there.

     Mountains appeared for an hour and became walls. Rivers became streets. Forests became houses. A field became an ocean when Tyler remembered the lake.

     The men learned to travel by objects.

     The brass key always pointed toward the nearest door.

     The black stone always pointed toward the deepest place.

     The blank paper always pointed toward what had not happened.

     The folded map always pointed toward where they had been.

     The wooden handle pointed backward.

     The metal stop pointed toward water.

     Together they formed a compass.

     “This is absurd,” Kirk said.

     “It's working,” Luke replied.

     They reached a settlement at dusk.

     The inhabitants had no faces.

     Not because their faces were hidden, but because the country had not decided which memories to use for them.

     One faceless woman offered Joseph bread.

     One offered Tyler a coat.

     One offered Kirk a room.

     Luke asked for nothing.

     A faceless child approached him.

     “You forgot something.”

     Luke crouched.

     “What?”

     The child held out a photograph.

     It showed the five men standing beside the Sacred Fountain.

     There were only four men in the photograph.

     The child pointed at the empty space.

     “You forgot him.”

     Luke looked at Joseph.

     Joseph looked at the photograph.

     “Who?”

     The child smiled without a face.

     “You'll know.”

## Chapter XXIX: The Missing Man

     They left the settlement before dawn.

     The photograph remained in Luke's pocket.

     The missing place in it became heavier as they walked.

     By noon Luke could no longer carry it.

     He placed it on the ground.

     The photograph unfolded.

     A fifth figure appeared.

     He stood behind them.

     He was not the returned man.

     He was not the seventh man.

     He was not Bill.

     He was someone whose absence had become so complete that the world had begun constructing him from the shape left behind.

     “What is your name?” Joseph asked.

     The man looked at him.

     “I don't have one.”

     “Then what should we call you?”

     The man looked at the road.

     “Call me when you need me.”

     He walked away.

     Kirk stared after him.

     “That's all?”

     “For now,” Joseph said.

     The man disappeared into the forest.

     The photograph became ordinary again.

     But the road had changed.

     There were now five sets of footprints.

## Chapter XXX: The Seventh Light Opens

     They returned to the house of the seventh light after what felt like years.

     The keeper was still sitting at the table.

     The seven chairs were occupied.

     The sixth man was the one from the photograph.

     The keeper looked at him and nodded.

     “You took long enough.”

     The man sat down.

     The amber lamp above the door came on.

     The city appeared beyond the windows.

     The lake appeared beyond the city.

     The forest appeared beyond the lake.

     The northern road appeared beyond the forest.

     Every place they had crossed became visible at once.

     The keeper opened the cloth map.

     All the roads were now connected.

     “The world is ready,” he said.

     Luke looked at the map.

     “Ready for what?”

     The keeper folded it.

     “For the part you haven't written.”

     The house shook.

     The seventh light went white.

     Far away, beneath the earth, the Sacred Fountain began to run.

     Something had pulled the first stop.

     Then the second.

     Then the third.

     One by one, the men around the table felt the water moving through the world.

     The keeper stood.

     “Now we go to war.”

     Nobody asked against whom.

     They knew.

     The war was against isolation.

     And the battlefield was everywhere.

## Chapter LVI: Play Again

     The command appeared on every screen simultaneously.

     PLAY AGAIN.

     The men had spent twenty years in hyperspace learning what the Hub did when nobody told it what to do. It generated rooms. It generated games. It generated weather, voices, bodies, maps, songs, arguments, meals, roads, and increasingly elaborate ways of bringing one event into contact with another.

     The first twenty-year session had begun as an experiment.

     It had become a culture.

     The men did not remember all of it.

     They remembered the important parts: the first game, the first house, the first battle, the first time someone laughed after a world had collapsed, the first time a player returned carrying an artifact from a place that had supposedly never existed.

     Luke stood before the largest screen.

     “Again?”

     Tyler smiled.

     “Again.”

     Kirk reached for the button.

     Joseph stopped him.

     “Don't press it.”

     Kirk looked back.

     “Why?”

     “Because we already know what happens when we press it.”

     The screen changed.

     A new button appeared.

     It was smaller.

     It was labeled:

     MAKE.

     Joseph smiled.

     “Press that.”

     Kirk did.

     The Hub did not restart.

     It continued.

     New worlds unfolded inside old ones. The previous twenty years became the foundation of the next twenty. Characters returned altered by what they had learned. The game stopped being repetition.

     It became inheritance.

     The men played.

     The worlds played.

     The stories played.

     Somewhere outside hyperspace, the first morning of the next age began.

## Chapter LXVI: The Keeper of the First Stop

     The stranger entered the unfinished novel and found that it had no floor.

     Pages extended beneath him, but every time he stepped onto one, the sentence changed into another surface. A road became a table. The table became a bridge. The bridge became a hallway. The hallway became the interior of a house.

     Luke was already there.

     “Where are we?”

     “Inside what we wrote.”

     Tyler looked upward.

     “Then where is the reader?”

     Joseph answered.

     “Behind us.”

     Kirk looked back.

     There was no one.

     The stranger held the wooden handle.

     “We need to find the first stop.”

     They walked through chapters rather than rooms. The northern road passed beneath their feet. The lake rose around them. The Blue Hall opened like a cathedral. The city appeared, disappeared, and appeared again.

     At last they reached a small chamber containing a single metal stop.

     It was older than the Sacred Fountain.

     Bill stood beside it.

     “You came.”

     Joseph looked at the stop.

     “What does it control?”

     Bill shook his head.

     “Nothing.”

     Luke frowned.

     “Then why is it here?”

     Bill smiled.

     “Because you keep assuming every artifact has to control something.”

     He touched the stop.

     The chamber opened.

     Beyond it was the world.

     Not a world in the sense of a map or kingdom, but the entire accumulated field of everything they had made: roads, houses, bodies, games, memories, wars, cities, screens, fountains, stories, and rooms.

     Bill removed his hand.

     “The first stop doesn't start the machine.”

     “What does it do?”

     “It proves the machine was started.”

     The men stood silently.

     Bill stepped aside.

     “Go.”

     They passed him.

     Behind them, the chamber remained open.

     The first stop remained where it was.

     The story did not erase its beginning.

     It carried it forward.

     And the unfinished novel finally had a road leading all the way from its first sentence to its last.
