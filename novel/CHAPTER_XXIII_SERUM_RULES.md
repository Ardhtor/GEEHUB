## Chapter XXIII: The Seven Rules

By morning they had stopped arguing about whether the Serum was alive.

The argument had become irrelevant.

What mattered was that it behaved consistently.

The seven men gathered around the vessel and began writing observations on the walls.

The first rule was written by the seventh man.

**RULE ONE: THE SERUM AMPLIFIES WHAT IS ALREADY PRESENT.**

It did not invent a new person. It did not replace one state with another. It found an existing tendency and increased it.

Strength became greater strength.

A room became more room.

A memory became more persistent.

A connection became more connected.

A desire became a direction.

The second rule was written beneath it.

**RULE TWO: NO ESTABLISHED CHANGE IS ERASED.**

The old state remained part of the new state.

A person who had crossed a threshold still contained the person who had stood before it.

A house that had expanded still remembered its original walls.

A road that had divided still contained the first road.

The Serum did not overwrite.

It accumulated.

The third rule required a diagram.

**RULE THREE: EVERY CHANGE CREATES A NEW BASELINE.**

They drew seven circles.

The first circle represented the beginning.

The second represented the first established change.

The third represented the next change measured from the second.

Then another.

Then another.

The distance between the circles did not necessarily remain constant.

That was the fourth rule.

**RULE FOUR: THE SERUM COMPOUNDS.**

Each completed transformation altered the conditions for the next one.

The larger body required a larger room.

The larger room permitted larger movement.

The larger movement created new thresholds.

The new thresholds gave the Serum something else to amplify.

This was why acceleration appeared.

The Serum was not becoming impatient.

The system was becoming capable of more.

The fifth rule concerned thresholds.

**RULE FIVE: CHANGE OCCURS WHEN A THRESHOLD IS CROSSED.**

The Serum did not respond continuously to every passing second.

It responded when something became meaningfully different.

A door became too small.

A corridor became too narrow.

A body crossed a measured proportion.

A memory became stable enough to survive independently.

A relationship became strong enough to alter the behavior of two people at once.

At that point the Serum recognized the new condition.

Then it established it.

The sixth rule was the one that frightened them.

**RULE SIX: THE ENVIRONMENT MUST ACCOMMODATE A STABLE CHANGE.**

They tested it with a chair.

The largest man sat down.

The chair widened.

They tested it with a doorway.

He approached.

The frame rose.

They tested it with the room itself.

He stood.

The walls moved.

Nobody touched them.

The building simply incorporated the new measurement.

The seventh rule took the longest to write.

The seventh man finally took the chalk.

**RULE SEVEN: THE SERUM SEEKS THE NEXT STABLE STATE.**

Not the largest possible state.

Not the fastest state.

The next state that could hold.

That distinction changed everything.

Growth without stability collapsed.

Growth that could be supported became permanent.

The Serum therefore pushed toward thresholds that could become architecture.

Body into room.

Room into house.

House into city.

City into world.

World into branch.

Branch into history.

The men stared at the seven rules.

Then the largest man pointed toward the vessel.

“There is one more.”

Nobody had written it.

The Serum was already moving.

He watched the liquid climb the glass.

“The rules describe what it does,” he said. “They don't describe what it wants.”

The room became quiet.

The seventh man looked at the vessel.

“No,” he said. “They describe exactly what it wants.”

He touched the first rule.

“More of what exists.”

The second.

“Nothing lost.”

The third.

“New baseline.”

The fourth.

“Compounding.”

The fifth.

“Threshold.”

The sixth.

“Accommodation.”

The seventh.

“Stability.”

He turned toward the others.

“It doesn't need a desire.”

The Serum brightened.

“It has a method.”

For several seconds nothing happened.

Then the vessel became too large for the table.

The table widened.

The room widened.

The building widened.

Far below them, the streets of the city shifted by a fraction to accommodate a structure that had not existed when they woke that morning.

Nobody panicked.

They had finally learned how to read the change.

The Serum had not begun a new event.

It had completed a rule.

The largest man looked toward the opening doorway.

Beyond it lay another road.

The road had not been there yesterday.

“Where does that go?” someone asked.

The seventh man smiled.

“Next baseline.”

And they followed it.
