# CHAPTER XXIX — LUKE INSIDE

That evening Luke disappeared for eleven minutes.

There was no alarm. No flash of light. No dramatic change in the woods. He simply walked into the trees after dusk and did not come back when everyone expected him to.

I found him standing beside a pool of dark water.

The surface was perfectly still.

Luke was staring at his reflection.

“I'm inside it,” he said.

I stopped behind him.

“The Serum?”

He nodded.

“Not around me. Not on me. Inside.”

The phrase should have sounded impossible, but by then impossibility had become a poor measure of certainty.

Luke put one hand against his chest.

“It's not changing me from the outside.”

He pressed his palm flat and waited.

“It keeps finding the place where the new version of me can already exist.”

The water reflected him a fraction of a second late.

Luke noticed.

“So did you.”

I looked at the surface.

My reflection moved normally.

His moved after.

He stepped closer to the pool.

The second image stepped closer too.

Luke smiled without amusement.

“That's what I mean.”

A ripple crossed the water.

The reflection expanded slightly.

Not grotesquely. Not suddenly. It was more like watching a camera choose a wider lens. His shoulders became a little broader. His chest seemed deeper. The proportions remained his. The changes looked like the same person being rendered at a new scale.

Luke watched it happen.

Then he stepped back.

The reflection did not.

For a long moment neither of us moved.

“Luke.”

“I know.”

The image in the water turned its head toward us.

It was still him.

That was the frightening part.

The Serum was not producing an imitation. It was preserving continuity so carefully that even the impossible version remained recognizable.

Luke crouched beside the pool.

“I think everybody else is studying the Serum from outside.”

“And you?”

He touched the water.

“It keeps teaching me what outside means.”

The surface closed over his fingertips.

For an instant the reflected woods seemed to continue downward beneath the pool, as though the entire forest had another forest underneath it.

Luke stood.

“Tomorrow,” he said, “they'll all want to know what happened.”

“What are you going to tell them?”

He looked at me.

“The truth.”

“And that is?”

Luke glanced once more at the water.

“I don't think the Serum is growing toward a final body.”

He turned toward the path.

“I think it's growing toward a world where that body makes sense.”
