# CHAPTER XXVIII — THE RULE OF PROXIMITY

The first measurable change after the meeting began when the men stood closer together.

It was not dramatic enough for anyone to call it growth. At first it looked like crowding. Shoulders touched where they had not touched before. The space between bodies seemed to disappear. A man would step backward to make room, only to discover another man behind him. Someone would move to the side and find that the edge of the table had arrived sooner than expected.

Luke noticed it before the others did.

“Back up,” he said.

Everyone looked at him.

“Why?”

“Because we are changing the measurement by standing like this.”

The men separated.

The clearing appeared larger immediately.

Luke exhaled.

“There.”

One of the fathers frowned. “There what?”

“The Serum isn't only responding to us. It's responding to the distance between us.”

He walked from one side of the clearing to the other. The others watched him calculate silently, taking in the width of the path, the turning radius around the table, the clearance between two redwoods, the amount of space his shoulders required when he turned.

“I think it uses proximity as pressure.”

The idea passed through the group slowly.

A younger man from the second family said, “Then rivalry is a stimulus.”

“Yes.”

“And comparison is a stimulus.”

“Yes.”

“And being around people with similar changes—”

“Yes.”

Nobody finished the sentence.

Luke did.

“It makes the next baseline easier to reach.”

The Serum had already taught them that every established change became a foundation. Now they had found something stranger: the foundation could be social.

A man's body did not exist in isolation. It existed beside other bodies. Every doorway measured him against itself. Every chair remembered his hips. Every coat remembered his shoulders. Every handshake had a distance. Every photograph had a frame.

The families began moving through the woods in pairs.

They tested spaces without talking. Two men walking shoulder to shoulder between trees. Three men sitting on a fallen trunk. A group turning at once in a narrow passage. The exercise became oddly intimate, not because anyone said anything sentimental, but because each man became a reference point for the others.

Luke stood beside me at the edge of the path.

“You know what this means?”

“I think so.”

He shook his head.

“It means the Serum has found the one thing none of these measurements can isolate.”

“What?”

“Relationship.”

Then the forest changed.

Not visibly.

The sound changed.

Footsteps that should have taken a second to arrive seemed to arrive twice. Leaves fell from high branches and landed in different places than the men expected. The distance between the table and the road felt longer.

One of the fathers stepped forward.

The ground beneath him gave a soft, wooden creak.

He stopped.

Luke stared at the dirt.

“That's new.”

The man looked down.

A hairline crack had opened in the soil.

Nothing broke.

Nothing collapsed.

But the clearing had acquired another measurement.

The Serum had found a way to count space itself.
