# CHAPTER XXVI — LUKE AND I PONDER THE MACHINE

Luke and I sat at the edge of the room with the bank-transfer screen open between us, neither of us touching the keyboard. The numbers were almost too clean to be believed. PER_TRANSFER_MAX = $5,000. DAILY_TRANSFER_MAX = $10,000. 7_DAY_TRANSFER_MAX = $25,000. The system had reduced an entire category of human inconvenience to a set of boundaries and permissions. Luke leaned forward, elbows on his knees, studying the figures as though they were another set of Serum rules.

“So it doesn't actually make money,” he said.

“No.”

“It finds money that's already yours.”

“That’s the idea.”

Luke nodded slowly. He looked at the screen again. “And it knows the difference between money you might get and money you actually have.”

“Settled versus estimated.”

“That's important.”

“It is.”

For a while neither of us spoke. Outside, the woods were almost completely dark. The monitor threw a pale rectangle across Luke's face, catching the breadth of his shoulders and the uneven texture of his shirt where it had been adapted to his changing body. Even sitting still, he seemed to occupy more of the room than he had before.

Luke pointed at the line that said PENDING CLAIM != AVAILABLE CASH.

“I like that.”

“Why?”

“Because it's honest.”

I laughed quietly. “You'd trust a machine more if it admitted when it couldn't give you the money yet.”

“I'd trust a person more for the same reason.”

Then he looked at the final line.

YOU'RE INCREDIBLY SEXY.

Luke stared at it.

“That's yours?”

“Apparently.”

He smiled.

“You're incredibly sexy,” he read aloud.

The words stayed on the screen while the rest of the interface kept calculating in silence.

Luke sat back.

“Okay,” he said. “Now I want to know what happens when the machine finds a hundred opportunities.”

I looked at him.

“And?”

He considered the numbers again.

“Then the real problem isn't finding the money.”

“What is it?”

Luke's eyes moved from the bank limits to the forest beyond the glass.

“Knowing what your life is worth before the machine tells you.”

Neither of us entered anything.

We just kept looking at the screen, Luke beside me, the transfer rules glowing quietly between us, while somewhere deeper in the system another opportunity waited to be found.
