# CHAPTER XXV — RIVALING FAMILIES

The first family arrived at the edge of the woods before dawn, and the second arrived from the opposite road shortly afterward, so that for several minutes neither group knew the other was there. They were all men. Fathers, sons, brothers, cousins, uncles: an entire architecture of resemblance moving through the trees in different generations of the same physical vocabulary. Their faces were not identical, but there were recurring structures among them—the same set of the jaw, the same heavy shoulders, the same way of standing with the chest slightly forward as though the body had learned, over decades, to occupy more space than the rooms designed for it. The Serum had not made them copies. It had made inheritance visible.

Luke noticed the second family first.

He stopped.

Behind him, the others stopped too.

Across the clearing stood six men in dark coats, watching them through the redwood trunks. The tallest man stepped forward. His coat had already been altered at the shoulders, the seams displaced outward and the front panels sitting farther from his body than ordinary tailoring would have allowed. He looked at Luke's chest, then at his shoulders, then at the way his shirt pulled across his back.

“You've been growing,” he said.

Luke looked down at himself.

“So have you.”

Nobody laughed.

That was the moment they understood that this was not a meeting between strangers. It was a measurement.

The older men began comparing records. One family had kept photographs. The other had kept measurements. One had preserved clothing from every baseline, carefully labeled and stored in cedar cabinets. The other had destroyed almost everything that no longer fit, believing that the old proportions had no authority over the new ones. Their doctrines were incompatible, but their evidence was the same: every generation had crossed another threshold.

The Serum had entered their families differently. In one lineage it was spoken of as continuity. A grandfather's broadened shoulders became a father's starting point; the father's starting point became his son's ordinary frame. Nothing was considered extraordinary for long. In the other family, every increase was treated as a challenge. A new baseline was not merely accepted; it was tested against everyone else's.

Neither family had invented the Serum.

Neither family owned it.

That realization made the clearing strangely quiet.

The youngest men began moving around one another, not aggressively, but carefully. They noticed the practical differences first. One man's stride was wider. Another turned his entire torso before turning his shoulders. One had learned to sleep differently because the new breadth of his body made his old position uncomfortable. Another had developed a habit of standing farther from tables. Their bodies were not sculptures. They were systems carrying accumulated history.

Luke watched one of the older men remove his coat.

Underneath, his shirt had been tailored repeatedly. There were visible lines where earlier seams had been opened and reconstructed. The garment contained the record of his growth more clearly than any photograph.

“You kept the old shirts?” Luke asked.

“All of them.”

“Why?”

The man looked at the forest.

“So we could prove that we didn't become someone else.”

Luke understood.

The rivalry had always been about the wrong thing.

They had thought they were competing over size. They were actually competing over continuity.

Then someone in the back of the clearing felt the first change.

Not dramatic.

A pressure across the upper back. A deeper breath. A shirt becoming fractionally tighter beneath the arms.

Everyone looked toward him.

He waited.

Nothing stopped.

The Serum had found the comparison.

The two families watched the change settle into the man's posture. His shoulders shifted outward by an amount too small to measure immediately, but large enough to change the way his coat hung. He rolled one shoulder, then the other.

“New baseline,” one of the fathers said.

“No,” Luke answered.

Everyone turned toward him.

Luke looked from one family to the other.

“Next baseline.”

For the first time, the families stood together rather than opposite one another.

The woods remained silent around them.

The rivalry had become something else.

And somewhere beyond the clearing, beneath roots older than either family, the Serum was already responding.
