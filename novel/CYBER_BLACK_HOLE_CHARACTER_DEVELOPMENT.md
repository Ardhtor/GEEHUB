# The Unplanned Convergence — Character Development Register

**Status:** active development map; not a declaration that the characters or manuscript are complete.  
**Started:** 24 November 2026  
**Scope:** the six original guests in *The Unplanned Convergence*. This is an opening-ensemble register, not a complete GEEHUB cast list; see the [Wider Character and Ensemble Register](../characters/ENSEMBLE_REGISTER.md) for the wider population and separate story branches.  
**Authority:** established character records and scenes remain authoritative. This register identifies what the novel has not yet dramatized; it does not invent missing biographies.

## Scope correction — the six are not the whole cast

This novel's opening six are not the only men in the Confluence, and the Confluence is not the whole GEEHUB story-world. Marcus, Dan Roman, Mateo and the Mexiancas, Brograntimax and the Dad Convent crew, Luke Mercer, Tyler Boeskin, and other men already have records or appearances that must be treated as more than supporting functions. Their scenes should develop their own points of view and relationships, and should be allowed to redirect events. Separate branches—including Kirk's Book II residential world, Grand Meridian, the Scale Commons, the Cabin, the Fingerlands, and The Men at the Terminal—must retain their own continuity rather than being folded into this novel. The broader cast map is maintained in `characters/ENSEMBLE_REGISTER.md`.

## The central diagnosis

The six characters have recognizable foundations, but the opening of *The Unplanned Convergence* often presents those foundations as functions: Luke is presence, Tyler is measurement, Chase is the record, Gabe is transformation, Andrew is play, and Joseph is memory. Those are useful entry points, not complete human beings. A role can explain why a character is useful to a scene; it cannot substitute for what he wants when nobody needs his particular skill.

The opening currently moves from six separate invitations to a group meal, a set of rules, and the discovery of a city in a short sequence. The transitions are clear, but the six have not yet accumulated enough ordinary history together for their collective decisions to feel fully theirs. The city-name exchange is especially premature: Andrew proposes a name, Joseph supplies the restraint, Luke endorses it, and Chase records it before the men have developed a shared life in the place. This is now treated as a provisional piece of dialogue, not as a settled character-defining scene. The sign may remain blank; the men do not need to discuss its name yet.

The work ahead is not to add more personality adjectives. It is to let each man have a day, a private concern, a preference that inconveniences someone else, a relationship that surprises him, a mistake he must repair, and an interest that has nothing to do with the mechanism that brought him into the story. Each person must be able to change the direction of a scene for reasons that belong to him.

## Character-by-character development

### Luke — presence that must learn to receive

**Established foundation:** direct, protective, body-aware, socially attentive, and physically present. He makes belonging tangible by moving toward people, noticing their circumstances, and changing a room so it can be shared. His blind spot is treating distance as a problem to solve and helpfulness as permission.

**Already visible:** he enters the Convergence through the unfamiliar room, recognises a gym from its altered viewpoint, and is inclined to make the room usable. His character record also establishes practical care, warm teasing, and a difficulty asking to be cared for in return.

**Not yet sufficiently dramatized:** what Luke does when nobody needs his help; what he wants for himself; when his protectiveness is unwelcome; what makes him feel embarrassed or lonely; who knows how to care for him; and how he behaves when he has to wait rather than intervene.

**Development task:** give Luke an ordinary responsibility that matters to him and a moment in which his attempt to help gets ahead of another person's choice. Let him notice the effect, apologise without turning it into a speech, and accept a form of care he did not arrange. He should have scenes in which physical presence is not the answer.

### Tyler — precision that must have an emotional life

**Established foundation:** curious, skeptical, precise, quietly competitive, and alert to mismatches between a claim and its evidence. He values repeatable tests and can be blunt. His blind spot is demanding precision when another person is trying to be heard.

**Already visible:** he follows a door in a gym mirror, checks the disc without touching it, and asks whether the archive distinguishes invention from fact. His record gives him a dry sense of humour and an inward competitiveness.

**Not yet sufficiently dramatized:** why a particular result matters to him personally; what he enjoys when he is not checking anything; what he does when a test cannot answer the important question; how he handles a public mistake; and who can get him to stop checking and enjoy the day.

**Development task:** let Tyler care about an outcome that cannot be settled by measurement. Give him a mistake that affects another person, not merely a technical result. Let him use his precision to repair the consequence rather than escape into another test. Show his humour and friendships when the evidence is not the subject.

### Chase — the witness who must take part

**Established foundation:** observant, decisive, restrained, dry, and attentive to sequence and consequence. He preserves uncertainty rather than making an account look complete. His blind spot is remaining an observer in his own relationships and letting an old record outweigh a changed person.

**Already visible:** he follows a location pin without an address, arrives with a folded map, notices an artifact already in the archive, and records the difference between what is present and what is inferred.

**Not yet sufficiently dramatized:** what Chase wants to be included in; how he behaves when his own account is challenged; which people he seeks out without a record-making reason; what he finds funny; and what he is willing to do without first documenting it.

**Development task:** give Chase a choice between preserving a moment and participating in it. Let him close the notebook while the work is still unfinished. Someone should notice his absence from the activity, not merely his absence from the record. He must experience the risk of being part of the story he is preserving.

### Gabe — recognition that must not become instruction

**Established foundation:** reflective, self-aware, socially sensitive, attentive to transformation and the pressure of being seen. He can enjoy admiration and still feel consumed by it. He has an established avoidant tendency alongside kindness and attentiveness; he may withdraw when emotional exposure asks something direct of him.

**Already visible:** he follows an image of an empty chair with part of a shoulder at the edge, carries coffee into the first gathering, and asks whether the archive can invent things. His established history includes the Growth Canvas, changing baselines, and relationships in which attention can become pressure.

**Not yet sufficiently dramatized:** what Gabe wants that does not involve his body, attention, or another person's response; how his kindness appears in practical ordinary life; the difference between solitude he chooses and avoidance he uses as shelter; and how he returns after withdrawing.

**Development task:** give Gabe a scene in which he is useful, funny, or absorbed in something without being watched for transformation. Let him avoid a direct conversation, then return to it on his own terms. His desire to be seen and his desire to be left alone must both remain credible.

### Andrew — eccentricity beyond the role of catalyst

**Established foundation:** a large, muscular Italian baseball player with blue eyes; athletic, coordinated, socially warm, playful, improvisational, and genuinely eccentric. His associations have a private logic. His blind spot is moving from invitation to assumption before others have chosen.

**Already visible:** he arrives with a baseball glove, teases Luke about decorating the room, and proposes practical action when the group hesitates. The character record establishes his sporting discipline, unusual collections and private names for things, and his sensitivity to being treated as entertainment rather than taken seriously.

**Not yet sufficiently dramatized:** the actual thought-route behind one of his eccentric associations; his serious baseball practice and team obligations; what he does when a joke fails; what he wants from friendship when no game is available; and how he experiences rejection without immediately producing another activity.

**Development task:** give Andrew a specific, sustained interest that the others initially misunderstand. Show the first observation, the associations he makes, and why the idea matters to him. Let another character decline an invitation. Andrew should learn to remain present without immediately filling the silence with a new game.

### Joseph — memory that must become a present-tense choice

**Established foundation:** reflective, intimate, associative, and memory-driven. He notices what an event carries forward and wants to understand a return without forcing the past to resolve. He can interpret a memory before the other person has given their account, and may wait rather than risk asking directly.

**Already visible:** he follows a song he has not played in months, recognises the pulse beneath it, proposes separating what happened from what is imagined, and notices that two names can remain beside one another. His long-range relationship direction with Kirk is established, but the outcome must be earned through mutual choices.

**New continuity work:** Joseph now has a standalone character record at [Joseph — Character Record](../characters/records/JOSEPH.md). It gathers his established material and marks his appearance, biography, occupation, home before the Convergence, musical history, and precise voice as unresolved rather than borrowing them from Joey or another character.

**Not yet sufficiently dramatized:** which ordinary parts of life he misses; what music means to him beyond its symbolic use; what he does when no one is returning; how he asks for closeness; and what he enjoys that is not connected to remembering or explaining.

**Development task:** let Joseph want something immediate and uncomplicated. Give him an ordinary scene where he chooses a song, makes a meal, finishes a task, or spends time with someone without turning the experience into a metaphor. Later, put him in a situation where remembering correctly requires him to ask the other person what happened.

## Ensemble development

The six should not become a committee of six virtues. Their relationships need histories and friction specific to each pair, and the group should change as those pairings cross. The current reaction-state file contains useful reciprocal tendencies, but those descriptions must be proved in scenes, challenged, and revised when the men surprise one another.

Before writing intimacy or shared history into a scene, establish what each man actually knows about the other. Recognition through a public reference, an image, or a shared archive is not the same as friendship. The present opening's phrase “they already knew one another in fragments” is a beginning, not a substitute for showing what those fragments were and what each man got wrong.

The first group meal can carry this development. Who chooses a seat, who reaches for food first, who offers something, who dislikes being asked a question, who knows when to stop teasing, who leaves and returns, and who notices a person being left out are not decorative gestures. They can reveal preference, class, habit, confidence, embarrassment, appetite, humour, and the early negotiation of trust. Do not assign all six a single reaction to the same event.

## Rules for future revision

1. **A role is not a personality.** Do not let the scene use a character only to deliver the function named in his profile.
2. **Give each man an ordinary life beyond the central mechanism.** Meals, work, obligations, preferences, sport, music, domestic routines, friendships, private interests, and rest are part of character.
3. **Make desires specific.** “Wants continuity” or “wants recognition” is a foundation, not a scene objective. What does he want today, from whom, and what makes asking difficult?
4. **Let contradictions stay alive.** A caring person can be intrusive; a precise person can be evasive; an observer can want attention; an eccentric can be disciplined; a reflective person can act impulsively.
5. **Show choice and consequence.** A trait becomes believable when a decision changes a relationship, a routine, or a place.
6. **Differentiate voices by more than sentence length.** Use distinct humour, references, conversational tactics, vocabulary, timing, and ways of handling silence.
7. **Do not invent a shared past.** Establish the actual source of each connection before characters behave as though a long history exists.
8. **Keep real-person references separate from fictional biography.** Public accounts can inform a clearly bounded creative voice reference; they do not supply private histories or unobserved traits.
9. **Do not use the world-name as a shortcut to group identity.** The men should develop enough of a shared life that any collective name would emerge from their own experience.
10. **Revise the source scenes, not just the character sheets.** The reader should encounter the person through what he does, says, wants, avoids, enjoys, and repairs.

## Development sequence

- **First:** give each of the six a fuller individual scene before or immediately after the Convergence, grounded in their established records.
- **Second:** develop the first meeting and meal as a social encounter, not merely a gathering of role functions.
- **Third:** let the first rules arise from a real disagreement or a personal boundary each man has reason to care about.
- **Fourth:** build distinct two-person interactions before asking the group to make collective decisions.
- **Fifth:** let the men inhabit the city through recurring routines, local relationships, ordinary pleasures, and practical responsibilities.
- **Only then:** return to unresolved collective questions, including what they might call the wider place.

This register is a working map. It does not claim that the six characters are now fully developed. Its purpose is to stop the manuscript from mistaking a clear character function for a complete character.

## Second scene pass — 25 November 2026

A new scene has been inserted after Chapter Six in `lore/CYBER_BLACK_HOLE_NOVEL.md`, under the transition **“Later That Morning — The Observatory of Images.”** It gives the six an ordinary morning before the narrative returns to the observatory and the larger mechanism.

The scene develops three pairings without inventing a shared past:

- **Luke and Andrew:** Andrew invites Luke to the baseball field as company, not as someone to fix a problem. Luke accepts without converting the invitation into a practical task. Their exchange lets Andrew's curiosity about sound and Luke's tendency toward usefulness meet in a relaxed setting.
- **Tyler and Chase:** they disagree about whether to test a route or simply walk it. A stop at a bakery interrupts the investigation. Tyler recognises that he has been attending to the map more than the street; Chase admits that accurate records can still omit whether he enjoyed an experience. Neither is reduced to a lesson or a victory.
- **Gabe and Joseph:** Joseph asks before playing music. Gabe first declines, then invites him to play quietly and requests a short replay because he likes the sound itself. Joseph does not turn the moment into an interpretation of Gabe's feelings.

The six reunite with small traces of the morning rather than a new collective revelation. The scene is deliberately modest: it establishes that shared time can matter without producing a rule, a discovery, or a new piece of world machinery.

This is a scene-level development pass, not a completed relationship arc. The next revisions should let these pairings acquire consequences and differences over time, and should give the other pair combinations equally specific scenes.
