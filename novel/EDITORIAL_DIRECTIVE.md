# GEEHUB Novel Editorial Directive

Revise the entire manuscript as a continuous environmental broadcast. Seth Feroce and Nick Mercx are the default co-narrators, speaking from a clear vantage across the scene toward the viewer. The audience receives the scene from the other side of the room or landscape; the narrators share the view, point out details, and occasionally address the viewer directly.

The global rule is documented in [../docs/GLOBAL_ENVIRONMENTAL_BROADCAST_REVISION.md](../docs/GLOBAL_ENVIRONMENTAL_BROADCAST_REVISION.md).

## Paragraphs and breaks

Use longer, continuous paragraphs as the default throughout the manuscript and connected GEEHUB prose. Each paragraph should sustain a complete stretch of attention, allowing atmosphere, movement, physical sensation, memory, character presence, thought, and consequence to develop together across several sentences. Avoid frequent one-sentence paragraphs, isolated fragments, and line breaks used only for dramatic emphasis. Break when the scene's focus, spatial position, speaker, or environmental condition genuinely changes, and let the new paragraph establish that change. Preserve dialogue formatting where clarity requires it, but do not turn narration into screenplay beats or a stack of decorative fragments.



Every paragraph break and scene transition should be driven by non-plot information: environment, geography, architecture, weather, light, color, spatial depth, surfaces, sound, temperature, distance, machinery, atmosphere, and the physical presence of people within a place. Line breaks are environmental descriptors, not isolated plot beats.

Use substantial paragraphs. Keep atmosphere, movement, physical sensation, memory, character presence, thought, and consequence together when they belong to the same stretch of experience. Let the setting carry transitions. Avoid object inventories, screenplay beats, ornamental fragments, and explanatory padding.

## Broadcast voice

Seth and Nick speak across the scene to the viewer. Let their commentary establish a receiving position: what can be seen from the far side of the gym, across the courtyard, from the opposite bank, or through a stable camera view. They notice scale, compare details, react to sound and texture, and return to environmental features as their meaning changes. They may address the viewer with a natural “look at this” or “you can see it from here,” but do not make every paragraph a direct appeal. Not every paragraph needs dialogue, but their announcing presence should remain audible across the prose. Use a broad editorial direction rather than copying anyone's exact phrasing.

## Character and canon

Character continuity is carried by habits, speech, movement, memory, and relationships. Do not re-explain a character every time he appears. Bill's history should emerge through what he notices, remembers, and tells. Luke remains Luke while his circumstances and physical baseline change. Tyler, Kirk, Joseph, Berit, and the other recurring figures retain their established identities and relationships.

Preserve the Sacred Fountain, the wells, the house, Big Daddy's Voice, the first man, existing artifacts, chronology, and established relationships. Serum remains established but secondary lore: retain its existing consequences without making it the master explanation for growth, continuity, or the world. Do not silently promote experiments or speculation into canon.

## Revision rule

Revise in place and preserve factual events and important lines wherever possible. Rebuild the prose around them so each paragraph inhabits the environment and each break deepens the reader's sense of place. Do not replace the manuscript with a summary, erase canon, or invent consequential events merely to make a passage more dramatic.

The plot remains intact; the environment becomes the continuous carrier of the reader's experience.

## Retrospective revision of earlier chapters

The paragraph standard is retroactive. Apply it to opening chapters and every earlier chapter, draft, and connected book written before this directive. Do not leave early material in a thinner, skeletal style while later chapters receive full environmental treatment. Revisit original text in sequence, preserve its events and established facts, and expand the scene itself: give the reader a fuller sense of the room, the route into it, the people present, the physical details they notice, the wider life continuing beyond the immediate action, and the consequences that carry forward. Merge isolated one-sentence narration into the paragraph it belongs to unless the scene genuinely changes focus or location. Do not merely add explanatory paragraphs after the scene.

Work in bounded chapter batches and record exactly which chapters have been revised. The First Room sequence is foundational and deserves particular care: its domestic setting, municipal geography, courtyard, doors, maintenance activity, and gradual dimensional discoveries should feel like a continuous inhabited history rather than a chain of plot beats. Longer prose should make evidence, atmosphere, and choices more tangible, not solve questions prematurely or inflate every event into a revelation.

## Publishing-length revision

The manuscript now follows [PUBLISHING_STANDARD.md](PUBLISHING_STANDARD.md). Revise the books themselves in sequence, not merely their instructions. Standard chapters should generally reach 4,000–7,000 words through fully developed scenes, character interaction, environmental continuity, and consequential turns. Major chapters may run longer when the material earns it. Do not pad or repeat. Preserve canon, and keep research notes and production commentary outside reader-facing prose.

The Bill chapter is the first expanded chapter under this standard. Apply the same standard to the remaining chapters and connected books after reading their full continuity context. Maintain a revision ledger so the books become coherent manuscripts rather than a set of individually polished but disconnected passages.

## Cognitive tempo: quick minds, consequential action

Characters must not think at the speed of a committee meeting. They notice, infer, test, decide, and respond with human immediacy. When evidence is clear enough, let a character make the connection in the same moment and move. Do not make several characters restate the same observation, repeatedly qualify an obvious inference, or hold a discovery in suspension while the narration explains every possible interpretation. Give each person a distinct intelligence: one may recognize a pattern instantly, another may read a structure by touch, another may anticipate the social consequence, and another may improvise a solution. Let these abilities complement one another without requiring a slow round-robin of exposition.

Dialogue should have snap and direction. People interrupt, finish one another's thoughts, challenge a premise, make a joke, change their minds, and get on with it. Keep pauses when emotion, danger, uncertainty, or deliberate attention genuinely requires them—not as the default rhythm of every exchange. A character can be mistaken; let the mistake produce a quick correction, a new attempt, or a consequence rather than pages of hesitation. Prefer active decisions and visible follow-through over narrated deliberation.

This is not a demand for short prose. The environmental broadcast remains generous and materially specific, but the people inside it are mentally alive. Keep the long paragraphs for the world, the movement, the relationships, and the consequences; let thought itself be agile. A useful test: if a character has enough information to act, do they act? If two sentences express the same inference, keep the sharper one. If a scene can advance through a decisive exchange, do not bury it under procedural discussion. Maintain credible safety and technical care where needed, but let competent people work efficiently.
