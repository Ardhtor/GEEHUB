# GEEHUB Publishing Standard

**Applies to:** the main novel, connected books, and approved spinoffs  
**Editorial aim:** finished literary manuscripts, not expanded chat transcripts, outlines, lore dumps, or collections of short scenes.

## Chapter scale

- Standard full chapter: 4,000–7,000 words.
- Major turning-point chapter: 6,000–9,000 words when the material sustains it.
- Short interludes are permitted only when structurally necessary and should be identified as interludes, not used as substitutes for chapters.
- A chapter must contain a complete dramatic movement: an arrival or disturbance, sustained development, a consequential change, and an ending that opens the next movement.
- Do not inflate chapters with repetition. Length comes from developed scenes, character interaction, setting, action, discovery, memory, and consequence.

## Prose and narration

- Write finished, continuous prose with deliberate syntax, paragraph rhythm, scene transitions, and a consistent narrative perspective.
- The environment remains present throughout: geography, architecture, weather, light, sound, surfaces, distance, temperature, machinery, and the physical presence of people.
- Dialogue must reveal personality, history, disagreement, affection, status, humor, and changing understanding. Avoid dialogue that merely explains the plot to the reader.
- Give each recurring narrator and character an identifiable vocabulary, rhythm, attention, and judgment. Regional speech is conveyed through cadence and idiom, not phonetic caricature.
- Bill's Southern voice is mature, measured, observant, dryly funny, practical, and emotionally restrained. He can be wrong, surprised, amused, protective, or moved. He must not sound like a generic folksy narrator.
- Keep the prose embodied. A character's size and physicality should affect movement, clothing, furniture, architecture, camera perspective, and how others respond.
- Prefer implication and lived detail to repeated explanation. Trust the reader to connect motifs.

## Scene construction

A full chapter should normally include several developed scenes or one sustained scene with meaningful internal turns. Each scene should have:
1. A specific place, time, atmosphere, and physical arrangement.
2. Characters who want something, notice different things, and act on those perceptions.
3. An obstacle, uncertainty, disagreement, or discovery.
4. A change in knowledge, relationship, material conditions, or intention.
5. A transition that arises from the environment or consequence rather than an arbitrary cut.

Do not turn every beat into a separate paragraph. Use substantial paragraphs where thought, movement, atmosphere, and consequence belong together. Vary paragraph length for pace and emphasis.

## Character and continuity

- Preserve established canon, chronology, relationships, names, artifacts, and important lines unless an intentional revision is recorded.
- Characters remain recognizable through habits, speech, memory, physical behavior, and relationships as circumstances change.
- Give secondary characters their own motives and attention. They are not an audience assembled to admire the protagonist.
- Lineage is lived through inherited habits, rivalries, work, objects, stories, and obligations—not merely announced.
- Do not claim that a character remembers an event unless the manuscript or continuity archive supports it.
- Maintain a canon ledger for new facts, relationship changes, material changes, motifs, open threads, and the next scene.

## Research and internet narratives

- Treat web pages, posts, photographs, videos, comments, reblogs, and online communities as source material with provenance.
- Distinguish the original source, captions, later interpretations, fictional responses, and GEEHUB canon.
- Do not invent a real person's private motives, consent, identity, or biography from public media.
- Characters may encounter, interpret, discuss, respond to, and create work inspired by internet narratives. Their encounters must generate genuine dramatic and emotional consequences.
- Keep source links and research notes with the relevant manuscript or episode record.

## Structure and publishing finish

- Each book needs a stable title, subtitle where useful, contents, chapter sequence, consistent heading hierarchy, and a coherent beginning, middle, and ending.
- Chapters should be copyedited for grammar, punctuation, tense, point of view, continuity, repetition, and sentence-level clarity.
- Remove drafting notes, duplicate passages, placeholder language, process commentary, and unintentional meta-explanation from reader-facing prose.
- Preserve valuable research and production notes in separate editorial files rather than leaving them in the narrative.
- Use consistent typography and formatting conventions suitable for later EPUB, print, and PDF production.
- Do not claim a manuscript is publication-ready merely because it has been revised. A final proofread, fact check, rights review, and format-specific proof remain necessary.

## Revision workflow

1. Read the whole relevant book and its continuity records before revising a chapter.
2. Build a chapter map and identify gaps, repeated material, unresolved threads, and pacing problems.
3. Revise in sequence, preserving canon and improving the book as a whole.
4. Expand scenes where they need room; do not pad.
5. Copyedit each revised chapter and check continuity against adjacent chapters.
6. Record the revision, chapter status, and unresolved editorial issues.
7. Only promote spinoff discoveries into main canon through an explicit continuity decision.

**Default instruction:** revise the books themselves, in place, toward this standard. Do not merely produce advice about how they could be revised.
