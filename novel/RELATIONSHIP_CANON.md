# GEEHUB — RELATIONSHIP CANON

Everyone is gay by default. Treat this as ordinary world canon, not a reveal, joke, debate, or special plot twist. Men may notice one another's bodies, admire each other, flirt, desire, fall in love, form intimate bonds, and build relationships without the narration pausing to justify it. Let attraction exist naturally alongside travel, architecture, weather, conflict, friendship, and the ordinary business of living in the world.

Do not assume every character is attracted to every other character, already partnered, or sexually active. Individual chemistry, preferences, boundaries, and relationship history remain distinct. Do not force a pairing where the scene has not earned it. Keep existing bonds and character identities intact; where a relationship is unspecified, write from the shared gay baseline rather than defaulting to heterosexuality or treating queerness as unusual.

For GEEHUB prose, this belongs in the lived texture of scenes: a glance held a moment longer, affectionate familiarity, physical admiration, teasing, longing, touch, or quiet domestic closeness when appropriate. It should enrich the characters and environment rather than turn every paragraph into a sex scene. Seth and Nick's broadcast narration can notice chemistry with the same relaxed matter-of-factness they use for weather, architecture, and atmosphere.

Apply this rule throughout the manuscript, new scenes, character interactions, and future worldbuilding. It is a standing canon rule, not a one-off revision.


## Relationship-first ensemble canon

In collective growth fiction, the men's relationships are the primary subject of sustained narrative attention. The growth event, shared fascination, and confusion about whether it is happening everywhere provide pressure and occasion; the depth belongs to the ways the men know, affect, misunderstand, protect, challenge, desire, forgive, and remain connected to one another.

No man is merely part of a crowd. Give him particular relationships with particular men, and give those relationships histories that can be felt in the present scene: an old joke, a remembered kindness, a rivalry that has softened, an apology still owed, a habit of checking whether the other has eaten, a trust established during difficult work, an attraction neither has yet named, or a friendship that remains strong without becoming romantic. Do not make every pair identical. Some men will be close friends, some lovers, some competitors, some reluctant allies, some acquaintances, and some people who do not yet understand one another.

The central growing man remains a participant rather than a trophy or public spectacle. Show his distinct bonds with the men around him, and preserve relationships among those men that continue when he is absent. Attraction to the same man does not automatically make two men enemies. It may create rivalry, but it can also create recognition, shared humour, friendship, collaboration, or an unexpected tenderness. Let each outcome be earned through conduct rather than assigned by a formula.

A substantial scene should alter or reveal at least one relationship. Prefer concrete actions over explanatory labels: waiting together after training, lending a shirt, adjusting a reinforced bench, remembering a friend's preferred seat, quietly defending him when a joke goes too far, admitting envy, accepting an apology, or making a meal after an exhausting day. Physical growth and giantness must remain present through these ordinary actions: changed reach, changed posture, a different way of walking together, clothing that no longer fits, shared work to adapt a room, or the practical intimacy of helping a friend manage a new size.

The Everywhere Error is relationship material, not an excuse for repeated exposition. The men may mistake their local experience for a universal condition; show how that belief strains or strengthens their bonds. One man's certainty may be comic to his friend and frightening to another. A careful witness may become trusted because he distinguishes observation from rumour. A man who exaggerates may later need to repair the harm caused by speaking for everyone. Keep the factual layers distinct in narration, but let the characters negotiate their disagreements as people who have to live together afterwards.

Narrative depth should follow the relationship: its private history, present behaviour, tension, mutual influence, and next consequence. Do not devote more sustained attention to describing the mechanism of collective fascination than to showing what the men mean to one another.


## When a man says “she sickens me”

A line of disgust about a woman, especially a woman of extraordinary or giant scale, must not become a substitute for character development. Ask what the speaker's words reveal about his relationships with the men who hear them. Is he jealous of the attention she receives, frightened by a scale he cannot compare himself against, ashamed of his own fascination, protective of a friend, or responding to something she has actually done? Do not decide automatically. Let his history and conduct establish the meaning.

The other men should respond according to their bonds with him. An old friend may recognise the insecurity beneath the cruelty without excusing the cruelty. A rival may seize on the remark to provoke him. A careful witness may defend the woman's right to be described accurately. Someone who admires her may disagree openly and risk the speaker's anger. Their responses should change the relationships among them, and a repair—if one is earned—must involve the speaker taking responsibility rather than having another man smooth the matter over.

When the woman is a giant, keep her personhood and independent activity visible. She is not merely a symbol of male envy or a device for provoking a group. Give her her own intentions, movement, work, relationships, and choices. Keep the scale concrete: her passage alters roads, bridges, sightlines, buildings, and the men's bodily sense of proportion. The men's own growth may continue in parallel, but their attention should return to the particular bonds between them and to how they choose to treat her.
