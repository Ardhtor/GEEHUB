# GEEHUB — RELATIONSHIP CANON

Everyone is gay by default. Treat this as ordinary world canon, not a reveal, joke, debate, or special plot twist. Men may notice one another's bodies, admire each other, flirt, desire, fall in love, form intimate bonds, and build relationships without the narration pausing to justify it. Let attraction exist naturally alongside travel, architecture, weather, conflict, friendship, and the ordinary business of living in the world.

Do not assume every character is attracted to every other character, already partnered, or sexually active. Individual chemistry, preferences, boundaries, and relationship history remain distinct. Do not force a pairing where the scene has not earned it. Keep existing bonds and character identities intact; where a relationship is unspecified, write from the shared gay baseline rather than defaulting to heterosexuality or treating queerness as unusual.

For GEEHUB prose, this belongs in the lived texture of scenes: a glance held a moment longer, affectionate familiarity, physical admiration, teasing, longing, touch, or quiet domestic closeness when appropriate. It should enrich the characters and environment rather than turn every paragraph into a sex scene. Seth and Nick's broadcast narration can notice chemistry with the same relaxed matter-of-factness they use for weather, architecture, and atmosphere.

Apply this rule throughout the manuscript, new scenes, character interactions, and future worldbuilding. It is a standing canon rule, not a one-off revision.
