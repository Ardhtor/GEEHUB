# GEEHUB — RELATIONSHIP CANON

Everyone is gay by default. Treat this as ordinary world canon, not a reveal, joke, debate, or special plot twist. Men may notice one another's bodies, admire each other, flirt, desire, fall in love, form intimate bonds, and build relationships without the narration pausing to justify it. Let attraction exist naturally alongside travel, architecture, weather, conflict, friendship, and the ordinary business of living in the world.

Do not assume every character is attracted to every other character, already partnered, or sexually active. Individual chemistry, preferences, boundaries, and relationship history remain distinct. Do not force a pairing where the scene has not earned it. Keep existing bonds and character identities intact; where a relationship is unspecified, write from the shared gay baseline rather than defaulting to heterosexuality or treating queerness as unusual.

For GEEHUB prose, this belongs in the lived texture of scenes: a glance held a moment longer, affectionate familiarity, physical admiration, teasing, longing, touch, or quiet domestic closeness when appropriate. It should enrich the characters and environment rather than turn every paragraph into a sex scene. Seth and Nick's broadcast narration can notice chemistry with the same relaxed matter-of-factness they use for weather, architecture, and atmosphere.

Apply this rule throughout the manuscript, new scenes, character interactions, and future worldbuilding. It is a standing canon rule, not a one-off revision.


## Relationship-first ensemble canon

In collective growth fiction, the men's relationships are the primary subject of sustained narrative attention. The growth event, shared fascination, and confusion about whether it is happening everywhere provide pressure and occasion; the depth belongs to the ways the men know, affect, misunderstand, protect, challenge, desire, forgive, and remain connected to one another.

No man is merely part of a crowd. Give him particular relationships with particular men, and give those relationships histories that can be felt in the present scene: an old joke, a remembered kindness, a rivalry that has softened, an apology still owed, a habit of checking whether the other has eaten, a trust established during difficult work, an attraction neither has yet named, or a friendship that remains strong without becoming romantic. Do not make every pair identical. Some men will be close friends, some lovers, some competitors, some reluctant allies, some acquaintances, and some people who do not yet understand one another.

The central growing man remains a participant rather than a trophy or public spectacle. Show his distinct bonds with the men around him, and preserve relationships among those men that continue when he is absent. Attraction to the same man does not automatically make two men enemies. It may create rivalry, but it can also create recognition, shared humour, friendship, collaboration, or an unexpected tenderness. Let each outcome be earned through conduct rather than assigned by a formula.

A substantial scene should alter or reveal at least one relationship. Prefer concrete actions over explanatory labels: waiting together after training, lending a shirt, adjusting a reinforced bench, remembering a friend's preferred seat, quietly defending him when a joke goes too far, admitting envy, accepting an apology, or making a meal after an exhausting day. Physical growth and giantness must remain present through these ordinary actions: changed reach, changed posture, a different way of walking together, clothing that no longer fits, shared work to adapt a room, or the practical intimacy of helping a friend manage a new size.

The Everywhere Error is relationship material, not an excuse for repeated exposition. The men may mistake their local experience for a universal condition; show how that belief strains or strengthens their bonds. One man's certainty may be comic to his friend and frightening to another. A careful witness may become trusted because he distinguishes observation from rumour. A man who exaggerates may later need to repair the harm caused by speaking for everyone. Keep the factual layers distinct in narration, but let the characters negotiate their disagreements as people who have to live together afterwards.

Narrative depth should follow the relationship: its private history, present behaviour, tension, mutual influence, and next consequence. Do not devote more sustained attention to describing the mechanism of collective fascination than to showing what the men mean to one another.


## When a man says “he sickens me”

A line of disgust about another man, especially a man of extraordinary or giant scale, must not become a substitute for character development. Ask what the speaker's words reveal about his relationships with the men who hear them. Is he jealous of the attention the giant receives, frightened by a scale he cannot compare himself against, ashamed of his own fascination, protective of a friend, or responding to something the giant has actually done? Do not decide automatically. Let the speaker's history and conduct establish the meaning.

The other men should respond according to their bonds with him. An old friend may recognise insecurity beneath the cruelty without excusing the cruelty. A rival may seize on the remark to provoke him. A careful witness may defend the giant's right to be described accurately. Someone who admires him may disagree openly and risk the speaker's anger. Their responses should change the relationships among them, and a repair—if one is earned—must involve the speaker taking responsibility rather than having another man smooth the matter over.

When the subject is a giant man, keep his personhood and independent activity visible. He is not merely a symbol of male envy or a device for provoking a group. Give him his own intentions, movement, work, relationships, and choices. Keep the scale concrete: his passage alters roads, bridges, sightlines, buildings, and the men's bodily sense of proportion. The other men's own growth may continue in parallel, but their attention should return to the particular bonds between them and to how they choose to treat him.

## The two Serum families — continuity and escalation

Chapters XXV, XXVII, and XXXI establish two related but distinct family cultures. The first family preserves photographs, clothing, tools, and other objects associated with earlier body states. Its central concern is continuity: a man remains himself through change, and the material record helps later generations recognise the life that preceded the current form. The second family preserves measurement sequences, dated observations, instrument references, and repeatable methods. Its central concern is escalation: every stable baseline makes the next possible state worth investigating. Neither family invented or owns the Serum, and neither archive is sufficient on its own.

The doctrines are emphases, not immutable factions or moral categories. The first family's preservation can become restrictive if it treats the past as a limit; the second family's measurement can become destructive if it treats the future as permission to discard history. Luke's intervention distinguishes continuity from preservation and escalation from replacement. The two families retain separate archives while agreeing on shared reference conditions, visible prior and current baselines, recorded methods, source provenance, and explicit uncertainty.

Their relationships should develop through ordinary cooperation as well as disagreement: comparing garments and measurements, helping with equipment, sharing meals, carrying cases, learning one another's routines, and allowing a man to decline measurement until he is ready. Competition may remain, but it must not flatten the people involved into representatives of a doctrine. The next meeting is an opportunity for trust to grow, not proof that the families have become one.

The central Luke has become a bridge between the groups because he is willing to hear both without allowing either to define the whole person. Tyler's measurement discipline and the seventh man's attention to continuity help the shared record become usable. The family members who remain unnamed are deliberate ensemble figures; keep their individual habits and relationships distinct as they develop, and do not assign names or biographies merely to fill a register.



## Hypersexual superiority and the arena of attention

The active doctrine is recorded in [Hypersexual Superiority](../lore/HYPERSEXUAL_SUPERIORITY.md) and first dramatized in [Chapter LXXXVI — The Evening of Presence](chapters/CHAPTER_LXXXVI_THE_EVENING_OF_PRESENCE.md) and developed in [Chapter LXXXVIII — duh!](chapters/CHAPTER_LXXXVIII_DUH.md). Adult masculine desire can be conspicuous, competitive, playful, and socially confident. Men may enjoy being admired, cultivate physical and personal presence, flirt directly, and take part in voluntary displays of physique, style, bearing, humour, and charisma. This does not establish a universal sexual ranking or make attention an entitlement. The individual rules above remain in force: chemistry is particular, invitations can be declined, privacy belongs to the participants, and no man is required to join a public presentation or sexual culture to retain his place in the community.

**Dorian Vale and Gabe.** Their acquaintance begins through the western crossing work and the Redwood Entrance follow-up. In Chapter LXXXVI they attend the Evening of Presence together. Gabe chooses to take part in the presentation walk and enjoys being seen without comparing his attention with Dorian's. On the covered terrace, Gabe tells Dorian that he finds him attractive; Dorian reciprocates and invites him to dinner the following week. Gabe accepts. They go on their first date in Chapter LXXXVII, share a first kiss, and then meet again for coffee and a joint review of the western crossing drawings in Chapter LXXXVIII. Gabe contributes a practical design correction; they share another kiss and arrange another meeting. Their connection remains mutual attraction and deliberate exploration, not an established partnership. Keep the next step open to both men and let the relationship develop through their actual conduct.


## Chapter LXXXIX — friendship enters the shared space

Dorian and Gabe continue their mutual, deliberately paced connection. Their next Sunday meeting is spent in the Northgate print room rather than at the survey office. They share another kiss, but do not announce a formal partnership. Gabe introduces Tyler Boeskin as his mate; Boeskin is welcomed into the wider circle as a friend and practical collaborator. His arrival widens the social world around Dorian and Gabe without assigning him a romantic pairing. Distinguish him from the existing evidence-focused Tyler. The group should retain ordinary friendship, shared production, and individual social lives alongside the developing attraction.
