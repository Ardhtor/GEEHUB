# Research and Influence: Growth, Morphs, and the Living World

This is an influence map for original GEEHUB writing and visual development. It draws from the visual transformation tradition associated with Linkin's Giant Morphs, TheBodylounger as named by the project owner, Hulum's Cave, and the collaborative muscle-growth fiction culture represented by MuscleGrowth.net and its archived Evolution Forum. These are references for craft, visual grammar, community conventions, and emotional effect—not sources to copy passages, designs, characters, or distinctive sequences from.

## The four influence channels

**Linkin's Giant Morphs — the changed image as an event.** Treat a morph as more than a before-and-after joke. Preserve the recognizable person while making the scale difference immediately legible: shoulder span against a doorway, torso against a chair back, thighs against trouser seams, head height against a room, hands around an ordinary object. The image should read in a glance, but hold details when watched longer. Confidence and playfulness can be part of the pose; the enormous man need not become a generic scowling conqueror. A familiar face and an ordinary gesture can make an extreme body change feel continuous.

**TheBodylounger — a named visual reference.** The requested source is retained as a research target. Public search did not reliably identify a canonical page, so this document does not pretend to have inspected or verified specific works under that name. Use it as a user-designated reference, and add concrete works or links when available rather than inventing characteristics.

**MuscleGrowth.net / the Evolution Forum — serial transformation and audience memory.** The archive shows a community interested in detailed change, different kinds of growth, the character's response to becoming larger, social reactions, and continuity across stages. Its threads discuss transformation fiction as a space for many variants: muscle gain, height and giant scale, altered confidence, weight and body-hair changes, and changes that unfold over time. GEEHUB should borrow the broad craft lesson: growth is an experience with stages, consequences, reactions, and remembered baselines—not merely a final measurement. Distinct characters should want different things and respond differently to the same event. A recurring audience should be able to recognize what changed since the last stage.

**Hulum's Cave — CG embodiment and motion.** Hulum's Cave describes its work as muscle-growth videos and has published progress material such as *Growth Serum X* and *Fantasy/Transformation Growth*. For GEEHUB, the useful visual principles are temporal: a body changes in a sequence, not as a static label; silhouette, pose, muscle volume, clothing tension, and the space around the body all change together. Give each stage a readable silhouette, and let transitions have duration, rhythm, anticipation, and a settling moment. The environment is a measuring instrument: a doorway, gym bench, screen frame, ceiling, or companion makes scale visible without a narrator needing to announce numbers.

## GEEHUB's original synthesis

The body is not a detachable effect. Growth changes how a man occupies space, reaches for things, sits, stands, walks, dresses, trains, and shares a room with other men. Preserve his identity through the change: face, habitual expressions, voice, mannerisms, favorite clothes where possible, and familiar gestures. Then show what has become newly difficult, newly easy, or newly satisfying. Shoulders expand; the shirt's seam shifts; his arms hang farther from his sides; his legs alter the line of his stance; a chair sounds different under his weight; a friend notices the new distance between their faces. Use specific, cumulative physical evidence rather than repeating generic words like huge, massive, or gigantic.

Growth should have a felt rhythm: anticipation, first evidence, resistance or welcome, successive waves, a pause that lets the new shape register, then movement through the world at the new baseline. The sensation can be grand, strange, pleasurable, funny, intimate, awe-inspiring, or unsettling according to the character. Avoid making every transformation identical. Some men study themselves in glass; some test their strength; some laugh; some turn to see whether a friend is still looking; some are too busy finding a shirt that fits to make a speech about destiny.

Seth Feroce and Nick Mercx announce the world in a podcast-like voice. They are not omniscient lore lecturers. They can disagree, tease, notice a handsome man, linger over the shape of a shoulder, comment on a room's bad lighting, or spend a paragraph describing a vending machine while the plot waits. Their commentary should sound present and observant. Gayness is ordinary social reality in this world; it does not require explanatory framing. Attraction, admiration, teasing, affection, rivalry, and intimacy should vary naturally between people.

Environmental description remains independent of plot. Describe humidity in a gym, the blue cast of an old monitor, rubber flooring, the echo in a locker room, sun on the parking lot, the grain of a bench, the stretch of a shirt across a back, or the quiet traffic outside. Some details may later matter; many should never become clues. They belong because the world is inhabited. Paragraph breaks should follow changes in attention—room, body, weather, sound, distance, movement—not a rigid sequence of plot beats. Prefer long, connected paragraphs and dialogue integrated into the scene; avoid chopping the narration into isolated one-sentence lines.

## Production rules

- Make original prose and original visual assets. Do not reproduce or closely imitate any specific creator's distinctive image, character, composition, wording, or transformation scene.
- Use the references for broad craft principles, then apply them through GEEHUB's own cast, environments, continuity, and language.
- Track each major growth stage with a recognizable baseline and a visible next stage. The face and personhood remain continuous unless a story explicitly establishes otherwise.
- Make scale visible through objects, architecture, clothing, movement, and the reactions of nearby people—not only through numbers.
- Keep Serum secondary. Growth can be experienced without explaining the whole world through a single substance.
- Let the physical scene carry the experience. Lore should emerge from what the characters and narrators see, hear, touch, and remember.
- Continue the existing manuscript in sequence, revising chapter text rather than treating this guide as a substitute for the rewrite.
