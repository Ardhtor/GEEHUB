# The Three Books Moving at Once

This is the working map for the three principal long-form books presently being developed in GEEHUB. Their titles and internal histories remain distinct. They share a world of roads, houses, halls, ships, workshops, libraries, forests, signals, remembered objects, and men whose actions may reach farther than they expect.

They are not silently promoted into further numbered books. ## How the books move

Each book must allow more than one place to continue during the same hour. The point of view can leave a room without stopping the men in it. When the reader returns, a fire may have sunk, a letter may have arrived, a signal may still be missing, a tool may have changed hands, or a man may have made a decision without knowing that another man elsewhere has already made a different one.

The works may touch through real events and objects: a road, a drawing, a shipment, a weather front, a signal, a repaired chair, a remembered name, or a traveller. Do not manufacture a meeting simply to prove that the books are related. A man in one country does not know what is happening in another unless something carries the news. A shared sound or image can be mysterious, but the men must still distinguish what they saw from what they believe it means.

## The language inside the books

In the stories, use the names and descriptions the men themselves have chosen. The Sacred Fountain is the Sacred Fountain; the Blue Hall is the Blue Hall; the lower gate is the lower gate. The men speak of the road, the water, the old house, the upper tower, the Stone Table, the quay, the Convent, and the page they are trying to read. They do not speak in the editor's vocabulary. Terms such as subplot, thread, workflow, pipeline, asset, branch, continuity pass, and canon belong only in repository notes when unavoidable.

Write in full, exact English. Let the material be named: cedar, wet stone, counterweight cord, smoked glass, hinge leaf, oak grain, chalk line, binding cloth, river silt. Make a gesture and its consequence clear. Keep sentences musical but not inflated; let a precise short sentence end a long observation. Complexity should come from the number of lives and choices in motion, not from obscurity.

## The voices heard across the places

Seth and Nick remain the principal voices who look across the scene with the reader. Mang0 and Arin appear occasionally on a separate commentary line, especially to react to incidental noises: a chair scraping, a press clacking, a pipe knocking, a microphone brushing a sleeve, or a distant thump. Their remarks are original GEEHUB dialogue, not copied from existing broadcasts. They may be funny, distracted, skeptical, or briefly absorbed by a sound that has nothing to do with the central event. They should not take over the story or explain what the men have not yet discovered.

## Present working order

- Preserve each book's own latest point and unresolved events.
- Continue the three books in rotation, but keep their clocks and travel distances coherent.
- Use at least two genuinely simultaneous places within a substantial chapter.
- Return to an earlier place before its state becomes stale; show what changed while the reader was elsewhere.
- Carry consequences into later scenes, even when they arrive as a small inconvenience or correction.
- Update the relevant character and place records after a lasting change.
- Keep notes about source paths and manuscript state outside the fiction. Never let the men sound as though they are reading repository instructions.
