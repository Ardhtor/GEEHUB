## THE PLACE WHERE DESIRE BECAME GEOGRAPHY

The room did not announce itself as a room.

It had begun as a threshold: a place the world-engine reached whenever an unfinished question concerned another person. There were no signs explaining what happened there. The architecture simply became more attentive. Corridors narrowed. Doors remained open a little longer. Windows began reflecting people who were not standing in front of them.

Luke noticed it first.

He was standing beside the Mustang with the keys turning once around his finger, looking at a building that had not existed the last time he had passed through the Complex. Its exterior was almost embarrassingly ordinary. Concrete. Dark glass. A single light above the entrance. But the entrance seemed to know that he was looking at it.

He said, “Yo.”

Nothing answered.

That was when he understood that the silence was not emptiness. It was attention.

Inside, the world had stopped arranging itself according to rooms and started arranging itself according to proximity. A chair appeared where someone had recently wanted to sit beside someone else. A second cup existed on a table although nobody had ordered it. A hallway turned toward the sound of another person breathing.

Luke walked farther in.

The engine had asked its question again: Who is being drawn toward whom?

The question was not displayed. It had become physical.

Every person who entered changed the shape of the place by entering it. Distance became measurable. So did hesitation. So did recognition. A glance could become a doorway. A conversation could leave a permanent corridor behind it. An encounter could make two rooms become one room without either room disappearing.

Nothing here required an explanation of desire. Desire was already doing the explaining.

Farther inside, another figure appeared at the end of the corridor.

Luke stopped.

The building stopped with him.

For several seconds neither moved. Then the distance between them became the most important object in the world.

The Complex recorded it.

Not as surveillance. Not as judgment.

As memory.

And somewhere beneath the floor, another world began to grow from the fact that two people had chosen not to leave.

The next question was already waiting:

What remains after the encounter?

The answer would not be written first.

The answer would be built.
