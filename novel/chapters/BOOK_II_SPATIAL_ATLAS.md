# Book II — Local Spatial Atlas: Kirk's Country

**Scope:** The local geography of Book II's Kirk expedition. This map is subordinate to the GEEHUB Project Atlas and the Tractus World Atlas; it is not a new independent world and does not merge Book II with Veyrthalis, the Colossus Chronicles, or the Tractus itself.

## Mapping rule

The global GEEHUB map uses normalized three-dimensional coordinates and separates elevation, terrain, water, passages, settlements, characters, and memory into layers. Its present landmarks do not yet include Kirk's anatomical country. This local atlas therefore records named places, known connections, and successive states without inventing coordinates. A position becomes numeric only after a scene supplies a survey, a repeatable reference, and a date. Earlier map states remain preserved beside later ones.

The governing cartographic principle is taken from the Measure House: map the place that was, the place that is, and the change between them. A route is not considered safe merely because it was traversable before a bodily movement. Kirk's account of what he feels and the expedition's measurements remain separate evidence streams that can be compared but not substituted for one another.

## The paired residential country

The residential quarter lies beyond a surveyed threshold from the lower route, within the paired chambers of Kirk's anatomy. It is a settled, adult residential environment established with Kirk's knowledge and agreement. The chambers are treated as living geography, not inert scenery. Structural plans must allow for movement, preserve routes of return, and remain subject to revision when Kirk reports a change. The four mansions are separate homes with their own grounds and internal lives; none is a rank, prize, or claim upon Kirk.

The local names are working names that may be refined as the residents inhabit their houses.

A shared green and covered walk connect the four estates without making them one household. The green contains a long table, fountain, mature trees, playing field, and pavilion. A common kitchen and recreation hall stand apart from the private gardens. Each resident controls his own rooms and routines. Visiting is by invitation; a shared meal or gathering does not suspend anyone's right to leave or be alone. The exact placement of each mansion within the paired chambers remains unmeasured; relative positions must be added only when a later scene establishes them.

The paired region is now called the **Twin Grounds**: two related but non-identical reaches of Kirk's anatomy, with a central fold between them. The **Cedar Span** is the covered timber passage joining the grounds. Its resting bays and viewing slots make the difference between the two sides visible during the crossing. The **eastern hollow** is a broad sheltered depression on the eastern ground; its lower edge can change after Kirk moves and must be checked before use. The **viewing arch** frames the shared recreation area from Robert's estate and can be screened when residents want privacy. None of these places has a numeric coordinate or a settled distance yet.

## Recreational geography — the Long Return

The residents have established the **Long Return**, a cooperative route game across the Twin Grounds. It begins at the western marker, passes through the Cedar Span, curves around the eastern hollow, and returns by the broad sheltered passage. A shallow arch at the eastern side returns a rolling ball toward the shared field. The game has alternate routes and recovery points; a player may stop, change routes, or join midway. It is not an endurance ranking and does not treat Kirk's movements as a performance for spectators.

## Competitive culture — the Evermeasure

The residents' instinct to compete and show off is a permanent feature of Book II, not a temporary period of rivalry. A demonstration invites a response; the response establishes a new standard; the new standard becomes the next man's starting point. The cycle passes between physique, sport, technical skill, architecture, timing, precision, hospitality, humour, and personal style. No single ranking captures the whole culture, and no final champion ends it.

The first formal expression is the **Evermeasure**, the continuing exhibition and challenge practice established in Chapter VII. The **Evermeasure Board** is fixed to the common pavilion wall. It preserves dated achievements, methods, previous records, and next challenges. It does not reduce the men to a permanent hierarchy or erase a result when it is surpassed. The Long Return remains cooperative in its original form; later competitive variations must retain voluntary participation, alternate routes, and recovery points. Chapter IX introduces the **Narrow Turn**, a temporary, adjustable mock-up assembled inside the pavilion to test the removable display frame. It is an apparatus, not a new geographic landmark: it does not alter the Cedar Span or the Twin Grounds. Its two records distinguish the cleanest controlled passage from the fastest clean passage, and the method remains provisional until repeated on another day. Robert also builds a miniature-theatre reconstruction with separate views of the carriers' hands and the far corner; it explains the geometry but is not source footage or a substitute for the recorded attempts.

The phrase **forever** refers to the continuing and inheritable nature of the practice: each result becomes material for another attempt. It does not require every gathering to be competitive. Private rooms, quiet days, meals, care, and relationships remain necessary parts of the residents' lives.

## Residents and ongoing life

- **Robert Laskin** has the earlier Laskin Residency proposal. The mansion develops it into a durable home rather than treating his presence as a temporary expedition arrangement.
- **Chase** maintains records and follows consequences. His archive keeps earlier and revised maps side by side; observation does not make him a permanent spectator in his own home.
- **Gabe** maintains a Growth Canvas practice. His house preserves earlier baselines without ranking them and gives him a lively shared court as well as private rooms where no one needs to react to his latest change.
- **Joseph** has a home of his own in Kirk's bodily geography. His residence is not a claim of ownership, a prize, or an automatic entitlement to Kirk's private rooms. It is a place from which he can choose closeness, quiet, and return.
- **Andrew Sim** is a frequent visitor whose wish for Kirk's attention becomes increasingly urgent. He has a guest room at the residential approach when invited, but no fifth mansion has been established. His need for attention is a relationship pressure, not authority over the other residents.
- **Michael Brock** is a very heavily muscled adult visitor who participates in the Evermeasure culture. Chapter VIII depicts his work at the common pavilion and Cedar Span; he remains unassigned to any of the four mansions. He shares a secret, gentle relationship with Kirk, and the privacy of that connection must not be exposed or treated as a public contest.

The houses remain inhabited off-page. Each man has work, meals, guests, preferences, maintenance, and stretches of time that do not revolve around Kirk or the current expedition.

## Relationship field

Joseph and Kirk's relationship continuity remains governed by [Joseph + Kirk — Relationship Continuity](../JOSEPH_KIRK_CONTINUITY.md): their bond is accumulated through choices, returns, disagreements, and repair; neither person is reduced to the other's reward or instrument. Andrew's growing need for Kirk's attention begins to crowd Joseph out of shared time. Chapter VI makes the pattern visible during the Long Return: Andrew keeps a conversation going after Joseph has contributed, and Joseph leaves the group rather than compete for the receiver. Andrew notices the consequence and apologizes, but the need itself remains. He begins to ask for a defined time with Kirk instead of occupying every interval. Joseph may step back, name the hurt, or request time directly. Kirk decides how to respond and is not treated as a prize to be won.

The four mansions do not create an affection ranking. Their residents may disagree about time, visits, and shared space while retaining their own agency. The wider Evermeasure culture does not extend into the scoring of intimacy: Kirk's attention is not a prize, and a private relationship is not a public achievement.

Michael Brock and Kirk share a secret, gentle relationship. At this stage it is private to them; do not assign knowledge of it to Joseph, Andrew, Robert, Chase, Gabe, or the other residents without a scene establishing that knowledge. The reason for the secrecy, its duration, their exact agreements, and Michael's residence remain open. His very large muscles are part of his visible presence, but they do not confer entitlement to Kirk or status over another man.

## Boundary with other GEEHUB atlases

Kirk's physical country is not the same place as Kirk's mental house in [Kirk's Mental Space — The Marked](../../lore/veyrthalis/KIRK_MENTAL_SPACE_THE_MARKED.md). The mental house has the Hearth, Gallery of Faces, Long Room, Workshop, and a private Quiet Chamber; a physical mansion in Book II does not automatically grant access to those rooms or confer Marked status. Joseph is Joseph, not Joey. The Digitorium and the Second Heart remain their own Veyrthalis continuities.

The Tractus World Atlas states that worlds and canons remain distinct. A method or artifact may cross a threshold with its provenance; local history does not silently merge. Accordingly, this atlas records Book II's own geography and does not add the Measure House or paired residential country as landmarks in the existing Veyrthalis/Complex map until a deliberate, sourced cartographic integration is made.
