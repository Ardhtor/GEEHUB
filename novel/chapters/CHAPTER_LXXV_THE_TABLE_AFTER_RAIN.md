# Chapter LXXV — The Table After Rain

The rain had stopped during the night, but the common room still smelled of wet timber. A narrow line of water had found its way beneath the eastern window and darkened the boards along the wall. Someone had set a folded towel against the sill. The large table stood in the middle of the room, pulled a little away from its usual place so that the floor could dry.

Luke arrived first with two more towels and a mug balanced in one hand. He stopped when he saw the table.

“Who's been using it?”

“Everybody,” Andrew called from the doorway. He was carrying a small tool case and looked pleased with himself. “Which is why it has decided to become a moving object.”

The table shifted a fraction when Andrew touched its corner. One leg had loosened where the lower brace met the frame. The movement was slight, but the sound travelled through the room: a dry click, then a faint complaint from the wood.

Luke set the mug down. He put his palm against the table and tested it without leaning his weight into it. “Nobody sit here until we know what's happening.”

Tyler came in behind Andrew, already looking at the joint. “It's not the leg. The brace is walking out of the socket.”

“You saw that from the door?”

“I heard it.”

Andrew crouched beside him. “I heard a table asking for attention.”

“It asked for a repair.”

“Same thing, in this room.”

Chase entered without interrupting them. He looked at the towel, the dark floorboards, the table's new position, and the small gap between the brace and the frame. His eyes moved once around the room as if checking the order in which the night had happened. He took the folded note from the sill and read the short message someone had left: *Water came in. Floor is drying. Table moved. Don't put weight on the east corner.*

“Who wrote this?” he asked.

“I did,” Luke said. “I didn't know how far the water had travelled.”

Chase folded the note along its existing crease. “Good. It says what happened. It doesn't pretend the floor is dry.”

Tyler had already taken a ruler from the tool case. He measured the distance from the brace to the socket and wrote the number on the back of an old envelope. Andrew put one finger against the loose joint.

“Don't push it yet,” Tyler said.

“I wasn't pushing.”

“You were about to.”

“I was considering pushing.”

“That is what you call it when you don't want to admit you've started.”

Luke smiled. The laugh was small, but it settled the room. Andrew lifted both hands and backed away from the joint with an exaggerated expression of innocence.

Gabe arrived while Tyler was checking the underside. He had changed out of his outdoor clothes and brought a clean shirt folded over one arm. He paused at the threshold when he saw the gathering around the table.

“Did it happen again?”

“The table?” Andrew asked. “Or me?”

“The table.”

“Then yes.”

Gabe crossed the room and looked at the joint. He had learned to notice how a gathering changed when something broke. The first few seconds were often more revealing than the repair itself. Luke had moved close enough to steady the frame. Tyler had narrowed the problem to one connection. Andrew was waiting for a chance to try something. Chase was preserving the account. The table had become the centre of their attention, but none of them was treating the others as an audience.

“It's interesting,” Gabe said, “how quickly everybody gets a different job when something moves.”

“It's not interesting,” Tyler replied. “It's loose.”

“That's the material fact. I'm talking about the room.”

“I know what you're talking about.”

Gabe looked at him. “Do you?”

Tyler paused, then glanced at the others. “Yes. I do. I just don't think the room needs to be repaired before the table.”

Andrew laughed. “That's the most Tyler thing you've said all week.”

Joseph came in carrying a paper bag from the kitchen. He set it on the clear end of the table, then noticed the note and moved it to the windowsill.

“I brought bread,” he said. “I thought we might have breakfast here.”

“Not yet,” Luke said. “The east corner isn't safe.”

Joseph looked at the table. “It was here when the room was still being furnished. There was a split in this same brace. We thought we'd replaced the whole piece.”

Tyler checked the underside again. “We replaced the fastener. The brace is original.”

“Then the table remembers more than we do,” Andrew said.

Chase looked up. “The table remembers the repair. We have the note.”

Joseph smiled. “That's what I meant.”

The distinction mattered to Chase. He did not correct him. He turned the envelope Tyler had used so that the measurement could be read, then placed the note beside it. One record described the present gap. The other recorded the earlier repair. They were related, but not interchangeable.

Gabe watched the two pieces of paper settle beside each other. “That's the part I keep thinking about. The old state doesn't disappear because something changes. But it doesn't get to dictate what the new state has to be.”

Tyler held the brace in place while Luke checked the frame. “Agreed. But we still have to know what we're dealing with.”

“Of course.”

Andrew opened the tool case. “And we need to know whether we're allowed to have breakfast before or after the table becomes a table again.”

“After,” Luke said.

“Unreasonable.”

“Safe.”

Andrew leaned back against the cabinet. He was ready to start, but Luke's answer had made the pause real. He looked at the others and waited. It was a small adjustment, and Chase noticed it. Andrew did not fill the space with another joke. He let Tyler finish measuring.

The repair took longer than any of them expected. The socket had widened slightly, and the old fastener had pulled against the grain. Tyler proposed a small wooden shim, cut to the measured gap. Luke held the frame without forcing it. Andrew trimmed the shim, then offered it to Tyler for inspection. Chase wrote down the order of the work. Gabe collected the fragments and placed them in a shallow tray, keeping the old fastener rather than throwing it away.

Joseph remained near the kitchen end of the room. He did not hover. He opened the paper bag and set out the bread on a board, then found the jam and a small dish of butter. When Luke looked over, Joseph raised the knife.

“I'm not putting anything on the table until you say it's ready.”

“Good,” Luke said. “Keep that side clear.”

“I remember.”

The phrase made Luke turn. For a moment he looked as though he might answer with a practical instruction. Then he understood that Joseph was not asking him to explain the table. He was recalling the earlier room, the first repair, the breakfast they had eaten after moving in. Luke nodded.

“I remember too.”

Tyler fitted the shim. Andrew held the light. Luke steadied the frame. Chase watched the socket as the pieces came together. Gabe stood back far enough to see the whole table rather than only the joint. Joseph kept the food covered.

When the fastener was seated, Tyler asked everyone to move away. He measured the gap again, then checked the table diagonally. Andrew wanted to test it immediately. Luke told him to wait until the frame had settled. Andrew rolled his eyes, but he waited.

The first test was gentle. Tyler pressed down at the corner while Luke watched the opposite leg. The table held. They repeated the test from the side, then from the middle. Nothing shifted.

“Now,” Tyler said, “we can put weight on it.”

Andrew placed both hands on the tabletop and leaned forward.

“Not all your weight,” Tyler said.

“I am being scientific.”

“You are being theatrical.”

“Both can happen.”

Luke shook his head, smiling. “Just sit down.”

Andrew drew out a chair and sat. He looked around at the others as if he had been granted an honour. Gabe took the chair beside him. Joseph brought the bread. Chase moved the note and envelope to the shelf rather than leaving them among the plates. Tyler remained standing until he had checked the brace one last time.

“You can sit too,” Luke told him.

“I know.”

“Then sit.”

Tyler sat.

For a while they ate. The conversation moved away from the table. Andrew told them about a shortcut he had tried on the walk back the night before and how it had turned out to be longer than the ordinary route. Tyler asked why he had expected it to be shorter. Andrew said that the route had looked shorter on the map, which prompted Tyler to ask whether he had measured the map or merely looked at it.

“Here we go,” Chase said.

Andrew pointed at him with the bread. “You were waiting for that.”

“I was waiting for you to finish.”

Gabe laughed. Tyler tried not to, then gave in. Joseph asked Andrew to draw the route on the back of the envelope. Andrew did, and Tyler corrected the scale. The correction made the drawing worse before it made it better. They argued about the bend by the old fence, then decided to walk it together later and settle the matter in person.

Luke listened, eating slowly. He noticed that Gabe had chosen a seat where he could see the whole group. He noticed that Tyler had stopped checking the table. He noticed that Chase had put the folded note on the shelf rather than in his notebook. He did not ask about any of it. He let the room be ordinary.

After breakfast, Andrew began clearing the plates. Luke reached for the same stack.

“I've got these,” Andrew said.

“I know.”

“Then let me.”

Luke hesitated, then passed him the plates. It was not a significant sacrifice, but it was a different kind of trust. Andrew took them to the kitchen. Joseph followed with the breadboard. Tyler collected the tools. Chase put the old fastener in the labelled tray. Gabe stood for a moment beside the table and rested his hand on the repaired corner.

The table did not move.

He looked toward the others. “I like that it can be used again without pretending it was never damaged.”

“That's the point of a repair,” Tyler said from the doorway.

“Sometimes,” Chase added, “the repair is the part worth remembering.”

Andrew called from the kitchen. “And sometimes the point is that we can have breakfast.”

Luke laughed and went to help him. Joseph was already washing the board. Tyler returned to put the ruler away. Chase left the note on the shelf, where it could be found if the floor darkened again. Gabe pulled out a chair and sat for another minute, not waiting for anyone to comment on the table or on him.

The rain had left a mark beside the window. The joint had been repaired. The old fastener remained in its tray. The room had not returned to precisely the same arrangement, and nobody tried to make it.

By afternoon, the table had become ordinary again. That was the result they had wanted. Its history remained in the note, the measurement, the small piece of wood fitted into the socket, and the way six men had learned a little more about one another while making it usable.
