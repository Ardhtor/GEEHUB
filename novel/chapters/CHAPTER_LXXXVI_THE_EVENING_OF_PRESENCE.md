# Chapter LXXXVI — The Evening of Presence

The invitation had been posted above the late café for nearly a fortnight. It was printed on a heavy card, dark blue with a narrow copper border, and it carried only a few lines: **THE EVENING OF PRESENCE — ADULT RESIDENTS, ATHLETIC COMMONS, FRIDAY AFTER TRAINING.** There was no list of winners, no measurement table, and no demand that anyone demonstrate a particular lift or disclose anything private. The evening had begun as a suggestion from the men who stayed late after training, but it had grown into something larger as the news travelled through the dormitories. They wanted a night devoted to masculine presentation: physique, clothing, confidence, style, humour, conversation, and the pleasure of seeing other men take pride in themselves. The café would stay open late, the upper gallery would be cleared for a short presentation walk, and the rooms above the arcade would remain available for private gatherings afterward. Attendance was voluntary. The invitation was unmistakably adult, and its atmosphere was unmistakably erotic, but nobody was being asked to turn himself into a public exhibit.

The room had been changed before the first guests arrived. The usual café tables were arranged in groups rather than rows, leaving a wide central passage beneath the old timber beams. The amber lamps along the gallery rail had been lowered, and the tall windows reflected the warm interior against the dark campus outside. A narrow platform stood at the far end, wide enough for several men to stand comfortably without making it look like a competition stage. Along one wall, the gym staff had placed a series of mirrors and a rack of clean jackets, shirts and training tops that residents had chosen to bring. The food table was substantial: roast beef, grilled fish, potatoes, peppers, pickled vegetables, thick slices of bread, olives, and a tray of small pastries from the bakery. The coffee counter was open, and someone had put a bottle of sparkling water beside each group of glasses. Nothing was arranged as a test of endurance. It was a social evening with a deliberately heightened sense of display.

Dorian arrived with Gabe. They had come from the western road earlier that afternoon after Tyler sent the revised restraint drawing to the engineering office. The bridge remained closed; the next bracket had not yet been fabricated, and the drainage survey was still open. Dorian had left his field jacket in the car and changed into a fitted black shirt with a low, open collar and dark trousers that had been tailored to give him room through the thighs. The shirt was not tight in the way of a garment chosen to exaggerate every muscle. It followed the width of his chest and shoulders, then fell cleanly along his torso. His forearms were bare, and the cuffs sat just below the largest part of his arms. His frame had continued to broaden, and the door seemed smaller as he entered, but his posture was easy. He did not stoop to make himself less noticeable. He simply moved with the care he had learned from the bridge inspections, allowing enough space for men to pass.

Gabe had chosen a deep green shirt, a fitted jacket and clean shoes. The line of his shoulders had changed again since the last measurement, and the jacket's upper seam sat more confidently than the older one he had worn to the Commons the previous week. He liked the reflection in the dark glass beside the entrance. He had taken care with his hair and the line of his beard, and the choice of clothes had made him feel deliberate rather than disguised. He looked at Dorian once before they crossed into the room.

“You look good,” he said.

Dorian turned toward him. “So do you.”

Gabe smiled. He noticed that the compliment had landed without either man trying to turn it into a joke. They entered together, and the first few heads turned. Dorian was the larger man by a clear margin, and his physical presence carried across the room. There were men who had heard about him from the western road and the bridge inspection; there were others who knew him only from the Redwood Entrance and the Commons. The attention was immediate, but it was not hostile. A few men nodded. One raised his glass. Someone called across the room that Dorian had finally found a shirt with enough fabric. Dorian laughed and answered that the shirt was still negotiating its position.

Seth and Nick had taken a place near the upper rail, looking down over the room as though it were a great hall prepared for a feast.

**SETH:**

Now this is a different kind of measurement. Look at the shoulders coming through the door, the collars, the posture, the way each man has decided to arrive. Some of them have spent more time choosing a shirt than they spent choosing their last training cycle.

**NICK:**

And that does not make the evening less serious. They are presenting something they have worked on. The body is part of it, but so are style, confidence, timing, and the ability to be in a room without requiring everyone else to disappear.

**SETH:**

There is a lot of pride in here.

**NICK:**

And a lot of desire. Those are allowed to be visible together.

At the late table, Emil and Tomas were already seated with their drinks. Tomas had brought a dark knit shirt that fitted close through the chest, while Emil wore a light jacket with the sleeves pushed back. They were comfortable together in the way established couples often are: each knew where the other would put his glass, and neither needed to keep checking whether the other was still enjoying the evening. Marek and Adrian arrived a little later, arguing about whether Adrian's new jacket had been tailored too narrowly through the back. Marek said it was exactly right. Adrian said that Marek would praise anything that made him look broad. Marek replied that he was allowed to admire his partner. They kissed briefly beside the table and joined the group.

Dan Roman came in with Noah Bell. Their conversation had already begun before they reached the room, and they continued it while taking off their coats. Dan's easy confidence drew attention, but he did not behave as though he had arrived to collect it. Noah was more reserved, though he had a precise and dry sense of humour that emerged whenever Dan became too pleased with himself. They sat near the windows and began discussing the menu. A few other men came in from the training wing, each carrying his own version of the evening: a new haircut, a fitted shirt, a polished pair of boots, a recently grown frame, or simply the confidence to be seen without trying to impress anyone in particular.

The first part of the evening was informal. Men moved between the food table and the café groups, greeting friends and making introductions. Compliments were direct. A man might say that another's shoulders had become impressive, that his clothes suited him, that his smile had improved, or that he had a way of making a room feel less ordinary. The compliments were not all physical. Some were about humour or conversation, and a few were deliberately flirtatious. A man could accept, return, or decline the attention without needing to explain himself. Nobody was keeping a public list of who had been approached. The point was to make desire easier to acknowledge, not to turn every interaction into a transaction.

A man from the rowing group looked at Dorian's shoulders and asked whether he had always been that broad. Dorian said no, and the man asked how much he expected to grow. Dorian smiled. “I don't know yet.” The man nodded, then said that the uncertainty suited him. Dorian laughed, but he did not mistake the remark for an invitation to anything more. He thanked him and asked whether he was still rowing. The conversation moved to training, the river, and a recent equipment repair. That was one of the evening's small successes: admiration could begin an exchange without determining where it had to end.

Gabe found himself talking with two men from the print room who had noticed his jacket. One asked where he had bought it. Gabe named the shop and explained that the sleeves had been altered to accommodate his shoulders. The other said that it looked as though it had been made for him. Gabe felt a flash of pleasure at the compliment. A month earlier, he might have compared the attention with whatever Dorian was receiving across the room. Tonight he could enjoy being noticed without checking who was receiving more. He had become more comfortable with the fact that attraction was not a fixed quantity. Dorian could draw attention and Gabe could draw attention too. Their differences made the room more interesting, not less.

The presentation walk began after the first meal service. It was not a contest and had no formal scoring. The men who had volunteered could cross the central passage, pause near the mirrors, and return along the gallery. Some wore training clothes, some wore evening clothes, and a few wore jackets they had altered themselves. The audience was encouraged to respond, but the host reminded them that a presentation was an invitation to look, not permission to touch or demand conversation. The first man crossed with a deliberately theatrical stride and received a round of laughter when he turned too sharply at the end. The next was a quiet heavyweight who barely smiled, but whose posture made the space around him seem carefully arranged. A third man wore a brightly coloured training top and treated the walk like a joke, then surprised everyone by giving a precise account of the work that had gone into his conditioning.

When Dorian's name was called, the room changed. He stepped onto the platform without rushing. The black shirt followed the broad line of his upper body, and the light caught the shape of his shoulders as he turned. His hands rested naturally at his sides. He did not flex for the audience or try to make his size more dramatic than it already was. He walked to the far end, turned, and came back at the same pace. The applause was immediate and sustained. A few men called out compliments; someone near the back said that the shirt had found its limit. Dorian looked toward the voice and smiled.

“It's holding for now,” he said.

The room laughed. The applause continued, but Dorian stepped down and returned to the group rather than remaining on the platform to collect more. He seemed pleased by the attention, and he did not hide it. He had learned that accepting admiration did not mean surrendering his judgement or becoming responsible for everyone's expectations. He could enjoy the room's response and then return to the conversation.

Gabe volunteered next. He had not planned to, but the walk had become less intimidating as the evening continued. He crossed the passage in his green shirt and jacket, pausing at the mirrors long enough to see his own reflection without turning it into an inspection. His shoulders were broad, but his bearing had changed more than his measurements. He no longer entered the room as if he were asking whether anyone had noticed him. He assumed he was allowed to be there. At the far end he turned, gave a quick smile toward the table, and walked back. The applause was warmer than he expected. Luke clapped from near the side wall. Dorian stood and applauded too, not as a gesture of comparison but because he was pleased to see Gabe enjoy the moment.

When Gabe stepped down, Dorian met him near the edge of the passage. “That looked natural.”

“It didn't feel natural at first.”

“But it did by the end.”

Gabe nodded. “I think I was trying to decide what I was supposed to prove.”

“And now?”

“Now I think I wanted to be seen. That's all.”

Dorian considered him, then smiled. “That's a good reason.”

The walk continued with other men. Andrew made a deliberately extravagant entrance and nearly lost the cuff of his jacket when he turned. Tyler told him that the jacket had passed its first test but required a fastening review. Andrew claimed that the garment was designed for movement and that movement included drama. Chase photographed the empty platform between turns and asked each participant before taking an individual portrait. Nobody was required to pose. The images would be offered to the men privately, and only those who chose to share them would appear in the Commons archive. The event had a public atmosphere, but the personal record remained under the control of the person depicted.

As the evening deepened, the conversation became more direct. Two men who had been exchanging glances near the coffee counter finally introduced themselves. One asked whether the other was seeing anyone. The answer was no. He asked whether he might buy him a drink and continue the conversation away from the crowd. The invitation was accepted. Another man politely declined a similar invitation and stayed with his friends. Both exchanges were accepted without a joke at anyone's expense. The room did not become less charged when someone said no. It became more trustworthy.

Marek and Adrian had settled into a corner near the windows. Adrian was leaning back in his chair with one arm stretched along the seat behind Marek. Their conversation moved between the evening's presentations and their next training week. Marek occasionally touched Adrian's forearm as he spoke. When the music changed, Adrian stood and offered his hand. Marek accepted. They moved into the open space near the gallery, where the two of them danced without much concern for whether anyone thought they were good at it. Their confidence was partly physical and partly relational: each knew the other had chosen to be there. A few men watched and smiled. Nobody tried to turn the moment into a public account of their private life.

Dorian and Gabe found themselves outside on the covered terrace. The air had cooled, and the windows behind them held the golden reflection of the room. Through the glass they could see the upper gallery, the long table, and the changing movement of men returning from the platform. The cedar boards beneath their shoes were still faintly warm from the day. Dorian rested one hand on the rail. Gabe stood beside him, close enough to share the view without having to lean across.

“You enjoyed that,” Gabe said.

“I did.”

“Being looked at.”

“Yes.”

Gabe smiled. “You don't seem embarrassed to admit it.”

“Why would I be?”

“I don't know. Some men behave as though wanting attention makes them weak.”

Dorian turned his head. “I think pretending not to want anything can become its own performance.”

Gabe looked through the glass at the room. “I spent too long trying to work out whether I was the man people were looking at. It made me miserable.”

“You don't need to stop wanting to be noticed,” Dorian said. “You just need to stop making it the only way you know you're there.”

Gabe considered the sentence. It did not sound like advice prepared in advance. Dorian had said it because he had noticed something, and because he was willing to speak plainly. Gabe looked at him, then at the line of his open collar and the broad shape of his shoulders against the dark window.

“I find you attractive,” Gabe said.

Dorian did not answer immediately, but the pause was brief. “I find you attractive too.”

Gabe's expression changed. The admission had been simple, and the answer had been equally simple. Neither man tried to make it more dramatic than it was. Dorian asked whether Gabe would like to have dinner with him another evening, just the two of them. Gabe said yes, then added that he did not want the invitation to become a test of who was more impressive. Dorian told him that was not what he wanted. He wanted to spend time with him. Gabe accepted that answer.

They returned to the room separately, not because they were hiding the conversation but because they were not obliged to turn it into a public announcement. Luke noticed them come back within a few minutes of one another. He looked at Gabe and raised an eyebrow. Gabe smiled and shook his head, but he did not deny that something had happened. Luke returned to his drink. He understood enough not to ask for details at the table.

The final hour was the most relaxed. The platform was no longer in use, the food had been replenished, and the men had moved into smaller conversations. Emil and Tomas left together after saying goodbye to their friends. Marek and Adrian stayed longer, then walked upstairs hand in hand. Dan and Noah were deep in a discussion about a book they were reading, but they had begun to lean toward one another in the way that suggested they would leave together. Other men made plans for dates, gym sessions, or breakfast. A few exchanged numbers. One man said he had enjoyed being admired more than he expected. Another admitted that he had come intending to compete and had ended up making two new friends. Someone else said that he preferred the food.

Seth and Nick watched the room from the gallery as the lights softened and the last drinks were gathered.

**SETH:**

That is what the evening was for. Not a champion, not a permanent order of men, but a room where they could enjoy the fact that they were attractive and wanted to be seen.

**NICK:**

And look at the differences. Some men were there to display their physiques. Others came to flirt, to meet someone, to enjoy their partners, or simply to have dinner with friends. The same room held all of it without requiring every man to want the same thing.

**SETH:**

Dorian and Gabe?

**NICK:**

They have said something plainly that they had not said before. That is a meaningful change. It does not decide the whole relationship for them.

**SETH:**

The biggest man in the room did not have to win the room.

**NICK:**

And the man who once feared being overlooked found that he could be seen without competing for every glance.

The café closed after midnight. The staff stacked the chairs and wiped the tables, while the remaining residents drifted toward the dormitories in small groups. The mirrors were covered, the platform was cleared, and the dark blue invitation card remained pinned above the noticeboard. It would stay there until the next evening of presence was arranged. The event had not created a new hierarchy, and it had not made every man desire every other man. It had given the campus a regular way to celebrate adult masculine display, desire, confidence and social ease without pretending that attention was ownership.

Dorian and Gabe walked as far as the cedar landing together. The western bridge was still closed, the new restraint bracket still awaited fabrication, and the drainage survey remained unfinished. Those facts had not changed because the evening had gone well. But another possibility had opened between the two men. Gabe had been able to say what he wanted without turning it into a contest; Dorian had been able to receive the admission without taking it as a claim. They arranged to meet for dinner the following week. They did not name the relationship. They had time.

The campus windows gradually went dark behind them. The night air carried the smell of cedar, damp stone, and the last of the café's coffee. The men had left with different impressions of the evening, but the same knowledge that the place was becoming more open about what its adult residents valued: strength, beauty, sexual confidence, friendship, pleasure, and the right to decide what any of those things would mean in their own lives.
