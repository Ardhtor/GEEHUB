# SHOT DESIGN — Laboratory Serum / 60 seconds

The camera enters a clean observation room at the pace of a clinical study. The adult subject is already present, fully identifiable, and dressed in clothing that fits the athletic baseline. The opening six seconds are not dead time: they establish the face, the ordinary proportions, the room's dimensions, and the visual references that will make every later change measurable. The camera moves forward slowly enough that the room remains legible. At three seconds, extract the first identity reference.

Between six and ten seconds, the serum activation provides a readable cause. The camera comes closer without breaking the spatial relationship established in the opening. A vial, injector, or activation apparatus is shown clearly; a small skin response follows. The sound should be precise and restrained: handling, a small activation cue, and the unchanged room tone. The trigger should feel like the beginning of one event, not an insert sequence disconnected from the subject.

From ten to eighteen seconds, the first deformation is almost easy to miss. Deltoids round, shoulders widen, and the chest begins to move outward. The camera starts a slow orbit or controlled push-in, keeping the face and upper torso within a coherent frame. The fabric registers the first increase in volume. At twelve seconds, extract a continuity frame and compare it against the baseline without allowing a change in face, skin response, or camera perspective to masquerade as growth.

Between eighteen and thirty-two seconds, the transformation becomes sustained. Pectoral width, projection, and thickness develop together; arms thicken in proportion; traps rise gradually. The camera uses lingering holds and a slow lateral move rather than a succession of fast edits. The subject remains the same subject at every moment. At twenty-five seconds, extract a second continuity frame. It should be possible to trace the same shoulder, chest, arm, and torso surfaces from the previous reference, not merely compare two unrelated silhouettes.

The main passage, thirty-two to forty-eight seconds, carries the largest share of the transformation. The chest becomes dominant without losing bilateral balance, the lats flare, the back broadens, and the thighs and overall scale increase. The room begins to look smaller because the subject has changed in relation to it; the camera should not fake this effect with an arbitrary lens jump. Slow orbiting and occasional low-angle emphasis are permitted, but each move must preserve a continuous growth state. At forty-two seconds, extract the late-growth reference. Clothing stress and environmental response now become clearly readable, while the subject remains covered.

From forty-eight to fifty-five seconds, the camera widens enough to show the scale relationship. Wardrobe distortion and environmental reaction become part of the same event: material tension, room resonance, and equipment settling should occur in response to visible changes. Secondary motion gives the final mass a sense of weight, but it must remain subordinate to the primary deformation. The camera may briefly emphasize a detail only if the move is matched and does not interrupt continuity.

The final five seconds are a terminal hold. The body reaches a monumental but balanced new baseline; the camera pulls back slowly or settles into a locked low angle. Breathing and residual settling remain visible, but the principal growth curve has resolved. Hold the pose through the final frame. The ending should feel like the room has permanently acquired a new scale reference.

**Continuity rule:** the seven timeline stages are descriptive intervals, not seven separate model states. Use one continuous morph or a carefully matched sequence with persistent deformation state. The 3, 12, 25, and 42-second frames are validation checkpoints, not replacement identities. Any camera reframing must preserve subject scale and the current deformation state.

**Sound rule:** keep a continuous underlying laboratory bed. Place serum handling and activation at the trigger; add fabric tension as volume changes become visible; let room resonance and equipment settling follow the later scale changes. Keep source audio separate from designed effects, and record every cue against the same 24 fps timeline.

**Delivery:** 60 seconds, 24 fps, 1,440 frames, 16:9. The project is a design specification only until source media, identity references, rig passes, and final render have actually been produced and reviewed.
