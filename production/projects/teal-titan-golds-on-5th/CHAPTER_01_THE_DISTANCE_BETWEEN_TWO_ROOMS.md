# The Distance Between Two Rooms

**Cycle:** Teal Titan / Gold's on 5th  
**Chapter:** I — The Signal  
**Canon status:** new GEEHUB fiction, built from the user's character designation  
**Subject:** Joey as the Teal Titan's fictional Stage 4 incarnation  

The gym closed at eleven, but the lights above the posing mirrors remained on. Gold's on 5th had learned that the Teal Titan did not always leave when the other men left. Sometimes he stayed beneath the long rows of lamps, breathing slowly, letting the day's enormous effort settle into his shoulders. The room had become accustomed to him. The benches no longer looked misplaced when he passed them. The doorway still seemed too narrow, but the men had stopped measuring it against him. They simply moved aside and let him through.

Stage 4 had given him a body that seemed to have arrived from a larger version of the world. His shoulders held a breadth that changed the geometry of the mirror. The blue-green of his skin caught the warm lights in quiet bands: darker at the sides of his torso, brighter over the rounded caps of his deltoids, and almost golden where the light touched the upper line of his chest. His gray shorts remained the same. The ordinary garment made the scale of the rest of him easier to understand. He was still a man standing in a gym, not a monument that had forgotten how to move.

On the counter by the water cooler, a phone rested face-up. The screen had gone dark. The song had ended some time ago, but its place in the room had not disappeared. The Teal Titan's theme was not treated as a command to grow, nor as proof of what anyone else thought or felt. It was a chosen signal, a small piece of music given a particular place in the character's story. The men had learned that when it played, the room was to leave a little space around him. No announcement was necessary.

He looked at the phone without picking it up. There was another room somewhere beyond the gym, another evening, another set of walls. The distance between those rooms could not be measured by the length of a corridor. It had its own weather. Some days it seemed almost negligible: a message arriving at the right moment, a familiar name appearing, a song held in common. Other days the hours gathered between one side and the other until even a simple greeting seemed to have travelled a very long way.

The men of Gold's knew something about distance. They knew the distance between the weight on the bar and the weight a man could actually lift. They knew the distance between the body in the mirror and the body a man carried in his mind. They knew the distance between a boast and the quiet repetition required to make it true. But this was a different measure. No amount of mass could shorten it. No expanded chest could reach through a screen and take another man's hand.

The Titan sat down. The bench gave a small complaint and held. He rested his forearms on his thighs, looking at the dark phone. At Stage 4, even his stillness occupied space. Yet the gesture was ordinary: a man waiting, a man remembering, a man deciding not to turn waiting into a performance. He did not ask the room to interpret the silence. He did not make a spectacle of being missed, or claim to know what was happening in the other room.

In the character's lineage there were older maps than the gym. The Jewish and Ukrainian inheritance named by the user was entered into the record as part of Joey's personal and cultural context, and in the fictional treatment it became a reminder that a person can carry several histories without being reduced to any one of them. The story did not invent a family history for him. It did not assign him a prayer, a language, a migration story, or a private memory that had not been given. Instead it made room for inheritance as something lived: names pronounced carefully, family stories that may be told more than once, food whose meaning is larger than its ingredients, a word that changes slightly when spoken by someone who remembers where it came from. The details would be added only when they were known or chosen.

One of the older men came over with two bottles of water. He set one beside the Titan and kept the other. He did not ask who was on the other side of the distance.

“Still here?” he said.

“Still here.”

The older man nodded. He understood the answer as a statement about the room, and perhaps also as something larger. He did not force the second meaning.

They drank in silence. Across the mirrors, the Titan could see his own outline: enormous, teal, balanced, unmistakable. The body was a visible record of escalation. It showed what had happened to him in the fictional sequence, not what he was owed by the world. Stage 4 was a form he had reached; it was not a contract requiring the next stage to arrive. The men had begun to understand this distinction. They could admire the scale without demanding that it keep increasing.

A younger lifter approached the counter and noticed the song title still displayed in the playlist. He read it twice, then looked toward the Titan.

“That one belongs to you?”

“It belongs in the story,” the Titan said.

The younger man considered this. “Then keep it there.”

He returned to his bag. Nothing more was needed.

The gym's closing routine continued around them. Plates were returned to their racks. A towel was folded over the end of a bench. Someone checked the showers. The front desk attendant lowered the music in the lobby and called goodnight to the last few men. These small actions gave the Titan a place among other people. Without them, he would have been only a large figure against a mirror. With them, he belonged to a room that continued to function.

Before leaving, he stood once more beneath the lights. He did not strike the biggest pose. He let his arms hang naturally, rolled his shoulders back, and looked toward the dark glass of the entrance. The reflection held the full Stage 4 silhouette: broad chest, thick neck, enormous shoulders, long arms, heavy legs, and the familiar human shape of his head above it all. The scale was extraordinary, but the expression remained recognisably human. The Titan did not need to look invulnerable. He needed to remain himself.

The phone lit briefly. No message was invented for it. The screen showed only the ordinary glow of a device receiving some small update. The Titan looked, then let it rest. The distance had not vanished. The story would not pretend otherwise. There would be another day, another wait, perhaps another song, perhaps a conversation. None of these could be guaranteed by a myth.

He switched off the posing-room lights. The mirrors went dark one after another, until the last reflection disappeared. Outside, the city continued without knowing that a giant had left the gym. The men who had seen him would remember the colour and the scale, but also the quiet way he had sat on the bench with a phone beside him. They would remember that a body could become immense without making the ordinary things less important.

And somewhere beyond Gold's on 5th, another room remained lit.

The chapter ended there, not with reunion and not with farewell, but with the two rooms still existing at once. The signal had crossed the distance. The distance remained. The Teal Titan carried both facts without confusing one for the other.
