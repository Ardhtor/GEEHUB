# The Colour Key

**Cycle:** Teal Titan / Gold's on 5th  
**Chapter:** III — What the Light Can and Cannot Say  
**Canon status:** new GEEHUB fiction  

The question came from a new lifter who had only seen the Titan once. He had arrived early, before the mirrors were fully lit, and had found the great blue-green figure standing beside the dumbbell rack. The Titan had been quiet. The room had been quiet. The young man had noticed the colour first, because it seemed to change as he moved from one end of the gym to the other.

“Is it always that colour?” he asked the older man at the desk.

The older man looked toward the mirrors. “Which part?”

“The teal.”

The older man did not answer at once. He had learned that the quickest answer was often the least useful. Under the warm ceiling lamps, the Titan's shoulders looked almost green at their edges. Under the cooler strip lights near the free weights, the same skin shifted toward blue. Where the gold light reached the upper chest, it appeared brighter and more saturated. In the shadows beneath the arms, it deepened.

“Let's find out what we're actually seeing,” he said.

They brought the Room Record to the central table and opened a fresh page. The younger man wanted a single colour name. The older man wanted a method. They compromised by writing the name at the top and leaving the rest of the page open.

**TEAL TITAN — COLOUR KEY, WORKING VERSION**

They divided the observation into three parts. First, the base colour of the character's skin in the established visual reference. Second, the light falling across that skin. Third, the transformation state represented by the scene. These were not interchangeable. A blue cast from a cool lamp did not mean the Titan had changed stage. A golden reflection did not mean his skin had become gold. A dark region beneath the pectorals did not mean the anatomy had changed shape. Light could alter the appearance without altering the underlying design.

The younger man fetched a sheet of white card from the office. They held it near the mirror, then near the Titan's shoulder, without touching him. The card looked warm beneath the ceiling lamp and cool beneath the strip light. The Titan's skin shifted with it, but the base blue-green remained legible. The older man wrote: *Reference colour must be judged under a known light; do not sample a highlight or shadow as the base.*

The Titan watched them from the edge of the room. “Are you making a chart of me?”

“Of the picture,” said the older man.

“Good distinction.”

The younger man turned the page around. “We don't know whether the teal means anything beyond the transformation.”

The Titan considered the question. “Then don't decide it for me.”

So they did not. The colour could be a visual signature, a production continuity lock, a sign that the transformation had crossed into its extraordinary phase. It could carry associations that the story would discover later. But the men would not assign it a fixed religious, national, or psychological meaning simply because they wanted a neat explanation. The user's account of Joey's Jewish Ukrainian background belonged to the character's personal context; it was not a pigment formula, a costume, or a symbolic shortcut. Heritage would have its own life in the story, through details the user chose to provide, not through a colour imposed upon it.

They returned to the visual record. The blue-green skin needed to remain skin. It had subtle tonal changes, soft transitions over the deltoids, and natural falloff across the chest and abdomen. It should not become polished metal, painted rubber, stone, or a flat digital fill. The warm gym lighting should remain warm. The cool lights should remain cool. The final image needed both: the body unmistakably teal, the room unmistakably a gym.

A second page described the transformation states. At the beginning, the colour appeared as a small shift in the skin. It spread until the blue-green identity became unmistakable. As the body enlarged, the same colour continued across the new mass. It did not break into separate patches or change hue at random. Stage 4 was the current designation: the monumental form under the warm lights, with the colour stable enough to recognise at a glance. The next stage, if and when it was written, would have to inherit this one rather than replace it without explanation.

The older man added a practical note: *When comparing frames, match exposure and white balance before judging colour continuity. Record any deliberate lighting change. Never correct the skin by neutralising the room.*

The younger man read it aloud. “So we don't make him grey just to make the teal consistent.”

“Exactly.”

The Titan crossed to the mirror. He stood beneath the warm lamps, then stepped toward the cooler lights. The blue-green moved across his shoulders as the illumination changed. He turned slightly, and the reflected gold travelled over the upper line of his chest. Nothing about the character had changed. The room had simply shown another part of him.

The men watched without speaking. For once, the notebook remained untouched. They had enough observations for the page.

Before closing it, the older man added a final line: *The colour is a continuity anchor, not an explanation of the whole person.*

The younger man asked whether that meant the colour key was finished.

“No,” said the older man. “It means we know what the first version is for.”

The Titan gave a small nod. He returned to the bench and sat beneath the lights. His shoulders filled the mirror; the colour held its place. The men resumed their routines. One put the card away, one wiped the table, and one checked the playlist at the counter to make sure the designated theme remained attached to the correct entry.

The colour key went into the drawer beside the Room Record. It would be revised when better references arrived. Until then, it was enough to keep the skin consistent, the light honest, and the meaning open.

## Continuity and production notes

- Keep base skin colour distinct from illumination, exposure, white balance, and transformation stage.
- Preserve saturated blue-green skin with natural skin response; avoid flat fills, metallic gloss, rubber, stone, or random hue shifts.
- Warm gold rim light and cool gym lighting may coexist without changing the character's base colour.
- The colour is a visual continuity anchor; do not use it as shorthand for Joey's Jewish Ukrainian identity or private psychology.
- Stage 4 remains the current visual state. Later stages must inherit its colour and anatomy rather than overwrite continuity.
