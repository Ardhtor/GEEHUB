# The Teal Titan — Chapter IV
## The Exhibition of One Man

**Cycle:** Teal Titan / Gold's on 5th  
**Chapter:** IV — The Exhibition  
**Canon status:** new GEEHUB fiction  
**Character state:** Stage 4 / Muscle Bwomph Growth  
**Setting:** Gold's on 5th, late afternoon into evening

The exhibition began with a disagreement about the wall.

The older man at the front desk wanted to use the broad stretch beside the posing mirrors, where the warm lamps reached the upper half of the room and the floor remained clear of equipment. Tyler, who had been asked to help with the display because he was good at noticing when something was out of scale, said that the wall was too narrow. The display was meant to show the Teal Titan's progression, and the final panel would require enough room for the full silhouette. Luke suggested moving the reception desk. Kirk objected that the desk was not the problem. It was the people who kept putting promotional stands in front of the wall.

The argument continued until Marcus came through the entrance.

He did not need to announce himself. The front desk attendant looked up, two men near the cable machines turned, and the conversation about the wall stopped without anyone deciding to stop it. Marcus wore his familiar gray shorts. His skin held the saturated blue-green colour that had become unmistakable in the room, with the warm ceiling lights catching the upper curve of his shoulders and the broad plane of his chest. His neck and traps formed a strong transition beneath the head; the shoulders were immense without becoming detached from the arms; the pectorals carried their weight evenly on either side of the sternum. His torso remained deep, his waist and pelvis connected to the whole frame, and his legs were substantial enough to make the stance look grounded. He was monumental, but not assembled from unrelated pieces. The same man was present from head to feet.

At the doorway, he had to angle his shoulders slightly. He did it without stopping, as though the building's dimensions were a familiar inconvenience rather than an obstacle. The men had noticed the gesture before. This time Tyler wrote it down.

"Don't turn that into a measurement," Marcus said.

Tyler lowered the pencil. "I was recording the movement."

"Then record the movement."

He crossed to the central mirror. The low three-quarter view made his proportions read as a single figure rather than a collection of enlarged parts. The reflection showed the familiar outline and the colour key the men had spent the previous week refining. Under the warmer lights, teal shifted toward green at the edges; under the cooler strips near the free weights, it moved toward blue. The base colour remained stable. The room had not changed him.

The older man brought over a folder. Inside were six sheets, each one a production study rather than a finished frame. The first showed the baseline figure and the beginning of the colour shift. The second showed the shoulders and deltoids beginning to broaden. The third held the double-biceps pose, with the arms and chest developing together. The fourth showed the great expansion through the chest, back, thighs, and calves. The fifth described the full-body flex and the gym's response. The sixth held the final Stage 4 image as the character's current visual reference: the monumental blue-green figure in gray shorts, standing beneath the warm gym lights.

Marcus looked through the sheets without speaking. The men watched his expression, but nobody tried to guess what it meant.

"The last one," he said eventually.

The older man laid it on the table.

"That's the one you recognize?" Luke asked.

"That's the one that feels like the room."

Tyler looked again at the sheet. "The proportions?"

"All of it. The head, the shoulders, the chest, the way the arms hang, the legs under the whole frame. But also the light. You keep trying to make the body the only thing in the picture."

"Isn't it the subject?"

"It's the subject. It's not the whole picture."

That settled the wall question. The six sheets would be displayed as a continuous sequence along the broad side of the room, but the final study would be placed where the mirrors began. The display would not pretend that a sixth panel was a completed render or that every intermediate image had already been generated. The men would show the production sequence as a sequence of intentions and continuity requirements, with the Stage 4 reference clearly identified as the current visual anchor. The distinction mattered. A plan was not an image. A source was not a reconstruction. A reconstruction was not the same thing as a finished video.

Seth and Nick watched the men arrange the papers from the far end of the gym, where the light fell across the benches and the polished floor reflected the roof trusses.

SETH: There he is. That's what they keep missing when they only talk about size. Look at the whole outline. The neck carries into the shoulders, the shoulders carry into the arms, the chest sits inside the frame, and the legs are there to support all of it. Nothing has been left behind.

NICK: And the room matters. He's not standing in a blank studio. There's the rack, the mirror, the ceiling, the floor, the other men. You can see how large he is because the world has not disappeared around him.

Marcus had heard enough commentary to know when they were talking about him, but he did not turn around. He was looking at the display's first panel.

"That one looks like somebody before the story begins," he said.

Luke came beside him. "It is the baseline."

"Good. Keep it. Don't make it disappear because the later version is more impressive."

"We won't."

The older man added a small label beneath the first sheet: **BASELINE — RETAINED**. Beneath the final sheet he wrote: **STAGE 4 — CURRENT DESIGNATION**. The two labels were placed at opposite ends of the display. Between them, the five intermediate steps remained visible, each described in terms of what changed and what needed to stay continuous.

A younger adult lifter arrived while the men were finishing the display. His name was Noah, and he had only seen Marcus from across the room. He stood near the entrance, unsure whether the exhibition was meant to be private. The older man invited him closer. Noah read the first three labels, then paused at the final panel.

"Is he still growing?" he asked.

Marcus turned from the mirror.

"Not because you asked."

Noah looked embarrassed. "I didn't mean—"

"I know what you meant."

Marcus said it without anger. He walked toward the display and stood beside the final panel. The difference between the drawing and the man was not subtle. The drawing could describe width, depth, posture, and silhouette, but it could not hold the full presence of a person standing in the same room. Noah looked from the sheet to Marcus and back again.

"Stage 4 is the one we have," Marcus said. "The next one isn't owed to anybody."

Noah nodded. "Then Stage 4 is enough to study."

"It's a lot to study."

That made him laugh. The room relaxed.

They spent the next hour moving through the gym rather than standing in front of the display. Marcus demonstrated how the same pose could look different from a low camera and a higher mirror angle. He did not perform a new transformation. He changed his stance, shifted his feet, turned his shoulders, and raised his arms into the double-biceps position. The men could see how camera distance altered the apparent width of the chest and how a slight turn changed the depth of the torso. When he lowered his arms, the silhouette returned to its resting form. His body had not grown or shrunk. The view had changed.

Tyler recorded the camera positions on a small floor plan. He marked the mirror line, the low three-quarter position, and a second angle from the side. The plan made clear that the same person could look more imposing or less imposing depending on where the camera stood. Any future comparison would need to match the frame before declaring a change in mass. The men were learning to distinguish transformation from perspective.

A trainer near the free weights asked Marcus whether he wanted to try the new bench. It had been reinforced after the previous one began to creak under ordinary use, and the staff wanted to know whether the new support arrangement was comfortable. Marcus approached, tested the seat with one hand, and lowered himself carefully. The bench held without complaint. He adjusted his feet, settled his posture, and looked at the trainer.

"Better."

The trainer wrote down the result. Nobody treated the test as a spectacle. It was a piece of furniture being checked for a person who used it.

The exhibition attracted a few more men as the evening progressed. Some came to look at the six panels. Others came because the gym was quieter and the lights made the display easy to read. A man from the evening shift asked why the fifth panel mentioned the rack settling. The older attendant explained that the production brief included a restrained environmental response at the peak of the transformation, but that the actual source footage and any generated sequence would have to be reviewed separately. The rack was a scale cue, not evidence that a real gym had been damaged.

Another man asked about the colour. Marcus pointed to the second panel, where the teal shift began.

"That is a visual signature," he said. "The light changes it, but the colour stays mine."

The man nodded and returned to the page.

Later, the room's playlist reached the title the user had designated for the character. The men did not announce it as an official start or a signal that Marcus must pose. It played in the background, and the Titan heard it. He looked briefly toward the counter where the phone rested, then toward the mirrors. He did not need to reproduce the exact pose from the reference. He raised his arms, held the double-biceps position for a moment, and let the expression settle into a calm, unmistakable confidence.

The room watched.

The final study had been placed beneath the warmest lamps. The real figure stood a little to one side of it, so the men could compare the visual anchor with the living character without confusing the two. The display showed the Stage 4 design. Marcus supplied the scale, movement, and presence that paper could not contain. For a moment the six sheets looked less like a progression and more like a record of a person who had passed through several states without losing his identity.

Noah stood near Tyler.

"Would you ever make a seventh panel?" he asked.

Tyler looked toward Marcus, then at the open space beyond the display.

"Only if there's something real to put there."

Marcus lowered his arms.

"That's the right answer."

The exhibition closed without a speech. The men took down the first five sheets and placed them in a flat folder. The Stage 4 study remained on the wall for the next week. Beneath it, the label made clear that it was a current reference, not a final limit. The gym would keep the previous states, and it would not invent a future state merely to fill the wall.

Before leaving, Marcus stopped at the reinforced bench and placed one hand on its back. It was an ordinary piece of equipment, but the room had changed around the fact of his presence. The staff had checked the support. The camera positions had been recorded. The light had been studied. The display now had labels that separated source, observation, and interpretation. And the men had seen that the Titan could move among them without every movement becoming a demand for another transformation.

He stood beneath the lights once more, face visible, shoulders relaxed, chest balanced, arms hanging naturally, his weight settled through both legs. The blue-green skin held against the warm gold and the cooler blue reflections from the far windows. He was not a symbol that had replaced a man. He was a man whose fictional form had become extraordinary, still capable of an ordinary evening at the gym.

The older attendant switched off the display lights. The mirrors kept the final reflection for a few seconds longer.

"Tomorrow?" Luke asked.

"Tomorrow," Marcus said.

He walked toward the entrance. The doorway was still narrow. He turned his shoulders and passed through it without stopping.

The Teal Titan left Gold's on 5th at the end of the evening. Stage 4 remained on the wall. The earlier states remained in the folder. The next stage remained unwritten.

For the first time, the men understood that preserving the Titan did not mean keeping him still. It meant allowing him to move without losing the person they already recognized.
