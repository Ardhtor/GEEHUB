# Embodied Scale Research

## Purpose

This research layer expands GEEHUB's embodied-scale system: extreme bodily scale is not represented only by a larger figure, but by changes in the relationship between body, distance, architecture, movement, and perception.

The working translation is:

BODY → SCALE → AFFORDANCE → PROXIMITY → ENVIRONMENTAL RESPONSE → FELT MAGNITUDE

This is a design framework, not a claim that every fictional cue is a direct measurement of human sensation.

## Research findings

### 1. Body size can rescale the perceived environment

Experimental work has found that changing the apparent or embodied size of the observer can alter judgments of external size and distance. In one study, widening the body changed judgments of aperture width: spaces could appear smaller relative to the changed body. Other body-ownership experiments similarly found that the perceived size of the external world can shift with the size of the owned body.

Design consequence: do not show scale only by enlarging the character. Change the apparent ratio between character and world.

Useful cues:
- doorways become comparatively narrow
- furniture occupies less visual territory
- walking distances feel compressed
- objects require less apparent reach
- the camera's relationship to the body changes

### 2. Affordances are body-relative

Ecological-psychology research treats an environment in terms of what it affords a particular body: passage, climbing, reaching, sitting, turning, resting, and so on. These possibilities are relational rather than properties of architecture alone.

Design consequence: every scale change should create new or altered actions.

Instead of a generic size multiplier, use relational state:
BODY_SIZE → PASSAGE → TURN_RADIUS → SEAT → REACH → CLEARANCE

### 3. Peripersonal space matters

Research on peripersonal space shows that perception and multisensory processing behave differently for objects close to the body than for objects farther away.

Design consequence: proximity should be a first-class variable.

A scene can move through:
DISTANT → APPROACHING → NEAR → WITHIN_BODY_SPACE

without adding explicit contact. The changing distance itself becomes the event.

### 4. Architecture can become part of bodily experience

Neuro-architecture and embodied/enactive approaches emphasize the reciprocal relationship between active bodily exploration and built space. The environment is not merely scenery: movement through it changes what is perceived and what actions seem possible.

Design consequence: architecture should answer the body.

Examples:
- a floor visually reads as stronger under greater apparent load
- a doorway acquires a new threshold
- a corridor becomes a negotiation of turning radius
- clothing becomes an environmental indicator of changed proportions
- rooms are redesigned after repeated encounters with a new baseline

### 5. The body/environment relationship is dynamic

Recent neuroergonomic frameworks describe built-space experience as continuous brain-body-environment coupling. A useful GEEHUB interpretation is that scale should be tracked as a changing state rather than a static attribute.

Design consequence: preserve history.

BASELINE_0 → GROWTH_EVENT → BASELINE_1

After the transition, the old environment should not automatically return to normal. The world remembers.

### 6. Altered body-world scaling is a long-standing artistic device

Research on altered body-environment scaling in fiction describes literary traditions that deliberately change the relationship between body and surrounding architecture or landscape.

Design consequence: GEEHUB can treat scale distortion as narrative grammar, not merely visual spectacle.

A giant body can function as:
- a moving architectural reference
- a weather system
- a city-scale landmark
- a new unit of measurement
- a continuity test for relationships
- a reason for the world to rebuild itself

## GEEHUB translation grammar

### Scale variables

Use relational variables rather than one generic size number:

- BODY_WIDTH
- BODY_HEIGHT
- BODY_DEPTH
- REACH
- TURN_RADIUS
- FOOTPRINT
- CLEARANCE
- PROXIMITY
- ROOM_OCCUPANCY
- DISTANCE_COMPRESSION
- ENVIRONMENT_RESPONSE

### Sensory channels

Keep the system non-explicit and environmental:

- visual proportion
- spatial compression
- fabric tension
- footstep timing
- floor vibration
- material creak
- acoustic pressure
- shadow occupation
- airflow displacement
- camera proximity
- architectural adaptation

### Transformation rule

When a body's scale increases, at least three environmental relationships should change.

Example:

BODY_SCALE + 1
→ CLEARANCE - 1
→ ROOM_OCCUPANCY + 1
→ PROXIMITY + 1

A larger event should therefore propagate rather than merely resize.

## Pleasure-bit generation rule

A pleasure bit is strongest when it contains:

BODY CHANGE + RELATIONAL CHANGE + WORLD RESPONSE + MEMORY

Example:

The corridor had not changed. The body had. What had once been empty walking space now had a boundary, and the building remembered the difference.

This produces the desired loop:

INFORMATION → HABIT → MOTIF → PLEASURE → ARTIFACT → NEW INFORMATION

## New scene primitives

1. Threshold Test — a familiar doorway is used as a recurring scale marker.
2. Furniture Drift — objects are progressively replaced as the baseline changes.
3. Floor Reply — the environment communicates magnitude through material response.
4. Camera Compression — proximity and framing change as the subject occupies more of the visual field.
5. Old Baseline Room — an unchanged room becomes evidence of a previous body-state.
6. Adaptive Architecture — the world quietly rebuilds around the new baseline.
7. Scale Witness — a second character or object remains unchanged, providing continuity.
8. Return Visit — a previously visited place reveals accumulated adaptation.
9. Shadow Measure — apparent scale is communicated through occupied light and shadow rather than numerical labels.
10. Distance Collapse — familiar spatial intervals feel shorter because the body has become the dominant scale reference.

## Canon boundary

The research supports the design principle that body and environment are perceptually and behaviorally coupled. The fictional world may extend this into mythic or exaggerated territory, but those extensions should remain marked as creative mechanics rather than scientific findings.
