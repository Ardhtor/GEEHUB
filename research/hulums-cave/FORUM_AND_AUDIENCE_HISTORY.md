# Hulum's Cave — Forum and Audience History

This is a source-led record of community discussion around Hulum's Cave. It is part of the central Hulum's Cave lineage, not a detached sentiment summary. Forum posts are evidence of what particular users said at particular times; they do not establish universal audience opinion or the creator's intent.

## Primary surviving discussion: Evolution Forum, “a little video”

Source: https://archive.musclegrowth.net/threads/11751-p7.html  
Archive context: the Evolution Forum archive describes itself as a direct download of the forum at closure and covers 2004–2014. The relevant page is page 7 of a nine-page thread in Morphs & Artwork. The archive is a contemporary discussion record, not a recovered copy of the linked media.

### Reception and recurring observations, September–November 2009

- 29 September: participants discuss the balance between wanting frequent releases and respecting the time required to make them. One post says viewers should not pressure Hulumulu to produce one video per day; another acknowledges the time and effort involved. This establishes a visible audience expectation-management conversation.
- 1 October: a viewer praises a specific “phase 3” and the character's positioning near the end of a video. This is concrete visual feedback: viewers attended to staged progression and final pose, not merely the broad premise.
- 9 October: Hulumulu announces the first chapter of *Ultrasize*. A reader calls it a great comic and anticipates the next chapter. Another says the comic's story caught their attention. The serial narrative generated return interest independently of the videos.
- 23–26 October: new blog videos receive enthusiastic responses. One viewer calls the bathroom muscle morph cinematic despite its straightforward premise. Another says the Quickie video was especially good. A viewer says they are becoming addicted to the blog and are waiting for the next *Ultrasize* chapter. These comments distinguish visual staging, short-form animation, and serial comic storytelling as separate sources of engagement.
- 26 October: one viewer asks whether future videos might be longer, while acknowledging the skill and time involved. Treat this as a request, not evidence that existing works were considered inadequate.
- 31 October: another animator posts a derivative video using Hulumulu's *Quickie* animation. Hulumulu approves the use with credit for the animation and offers to create additional animation elements for the collaborator. Hulumulu also asks permission to repost the derivative on the blog. This is evidence of a specific collaborative exchange and attribution condition, not blanket reuse permission.
- 31 October–1 November: a viewer proposes more varied character backgrounds, a continuation of *The Bully*, simultaneous growth, and interactive character customization. Other participants discuss the *The Bully* request.
- 1 November: the audience debates *The Bully*. Some want the smaller character to get revenge; another argues that the work's refusal of the conventional “revenge of the nerd” ending is precisely what makes it distinctive. This is a reception split: viewers can share interest in a work while disagreeing about what should happen next.
- 2 November: another viewer calls *The Bully* their favorite and imagines further escalation. The title had become a reference point in the thread.
- 2–3 November: *Ultrasize* Chapter 3 is announced and praised; a reader says they want to live in a world designed by Hulumulu's fantasies. This is a response to the setting/story world, not simply the transformation image.
- 3–4 November: a short new video prompts comments about facial expression and teeth. Viewers praise the result while identifying specific face/appearance issues. Preserve these as period-specific feedback, not universal aesthetic rules.

## Reception patterns worth testing against the media

1. **Progression is legible to viewers.** The reference to “phase 3” and the final pose suggests viewers notice intermediate stages and final positioning. Inspect source videos for how stages are distinguished through pose, camera, clothing, and environment.
2. **Cinematic quality can emerge in a simple premise.** The bathroom-video comment suggests location, staging, and camera treatment may make a straightforward morph feel like a scene. This remains an audience impression until the video is inspected.
3. **Serial narrative creates anticipation.** Readers returned for *Ultrasize* chapters and discussed the story between releases. Record cliffhangers, chapter transitions, recurring characters, and unresolved plot threads when pages are recovered.
4. **Character dynamics can matter more than a conventional payoff.** The *The Bully* debate shows that a familiar reversal was not universally desired. Do not impose audience-requested sequels or moral resolutions on the original.
5. **Audience requests form a design history.** Suggestions for longer works, simultaneous growth, character variety, and interactive customization can be compared with later projects to see whether ideas recur or are realized.
6. **Collaboration is part of the production ecosystem.** The *Quickie* exchange documents reusable animation elements and explicit credit. Track source animation, derivative work, creator approval, and repost separately.

## Method and caution

- Keep forum usernames attached only to their posts in this thread; do not infer real identities or merge same-name accounts from unrelated services.
- Separate creator statements, audience reception, speculation, jokes, requests, and later interpretation.
- Preserve disagreements rather than flattening them into a single consensus.
- Do not treat a request as proof that a feature was made.
- Do not infer the content of a missing video from comments alone.
- The forum archive preserves discussion text, but linked blog pages and video payloads still require independent recovery and inspection.
