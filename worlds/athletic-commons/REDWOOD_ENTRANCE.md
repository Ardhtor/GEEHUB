# Redwood Entrance and Walk

**World:** Northgate Athletic Commons  
**Status:** Established place; visitor-wayfinding improvements in progress  
**First developed in:** [Chapter LXXXIII — The Boy at the Redwood Entrance](../../novel/chapters/CHAPTER_LXXXIII_THE_BOY_AT_THE_REDWOOD_ENTRANCE.md)

The Redwood Entrance is the threshold between the Athletic Commons' pool and café side, the playing fields, and the quieter path that runs beneath the mature redwoods toward the residential grounds. A timber-framed passage leads from the main entrance to a sign at the path junction. The walk is attractive and useful, but its first junction can be difficult to read for a visitor who has not used the campus before. The reception desk is visible from the glass doors and remains the principal return point for visitors who need assistance.

The entrance is not a remote wilderness trail. It is part of a staffed campus and must remain legible as such. Wayfinding should show the pool, café, Redwood Walk, playing fields, and reception desk together. Directional information is needed before the path divides, not only at the point where a visitor has already chosen a direction. A continuous floor marking may help, but it must be checked for durability, cleaning compatibility, and visibility in both directions. Seating near the glass doors should remain clear enough for a visitor and an accompanying adult to wait without obstructing circulation.

## Visitor-separation response

The event recorded in Chapter LXXXIII establishes the following operational pattern for a visitor who becomes separated from his responsible adult:

- Keep the visitor in a visible, public, staffed area; do not take him elsewhere alone.
- Notify reception and the duty coordinator promptly. Campus staff and security coordinate the search.
- Use the last-known location and a concise description only with staff who need the information. Do not broadcast personal identifying details over public speakers.
- Keep the visitor accompanied and informed while the responsible adult is located.
- Reunite the family through reception, confirm the handover, and keep the public maintenance record separate from any restricted incident details.

This is an institutional procedure, not an expectation that a passer-by should conduct a search independently. A bystander can notice, remain nearby, alert staff, and make the handover clear. Staff retain responsibility for the search and the official incident record.

## Follow-up established in Chapter LXXXIV

A two-sided paper mock-up has been tested from the pool side and the Redwood path. The initial alignment was adjusted so that the return arrow points toward the actual glass entrance rather than vaguely toward the building. The permanent panel is to use correctly oriented artwork on both faces; a single image should not simply be repeated without mirroring the directional information. The father and son from the earlier incident returned voluntarily with the father present and helped confirm the first-visit perspective. Their personal details remain outside the shared place record.

The bench clearance has been measured, and the display stand that narrowed the waiting space has been moved. This is an initial correction, not a guarantee that the entrance will remain clear during every peak period. The team should recheck the route during normal use.

## Open maintenance work

The temporary directional arrow remains in place. The two-direction paper-mock-up test is complete, but the permanent sign drawing, wording approval, manufacture, installation, and final sightline check remain open. The floor-marking durability test also remains open. The seating clearance has been measured and the display stand moved; recheck the clear route during ordinary peak use. The incident's shared record omits the boy's name and identifying details; any necessary personal information remains in the restricted safety report, not the public place history.
