# BLUEWATER QUAY

Bluewater Quay follows the calm edge of the reservoir where the city opens into a broad promenade. The water is deep blue in the morning, silver beneath cloud, and almost black when the lamps come on. A low stone wall runs beside the path, interrupted by steps down to floating platforms and small wooden landings. Reeds move at the sheltered edge. Beyond them, the opposite bank rises into trees and the distant lights of the Complex.

The quay is made for unhurried visits. There is a boathouse with simple rowboats, a tea room with wide windows, a covered outdoor table, and a long bench facing the water. People come to walk, talk, read, sketch, or sit without having to fill the silence. A narrow path follows the shoreline toward a small observatory deck, where the water and the city can be seen together. At certain hours the reflections make the buildings appear to float, but the place remains grounded in the ordinary details of ropes, wet stone, wooden boards, and the smell of rain.

Bluewater Quay is linked to Lantern Market by a tree-lined avenue and to the Cat Gardens by a quieter path behind the wall. It offers the city an open horizon: somewhere to go when its rooms become too close, and somewhere to return from with a little more space in mind.
