# CAT GARDENS

The Cat Gardens are a sequence of sheltered courtyards between Cedar House and the old stone wall. Cats live here as ordinary, welcome residents of the city. They sleep on warm steps, inspect parcels, occupy sunny window ledges, and appear in the paths just when someone has decided to walk more slowly. Some are sociable; others prefer the high walls and the quiet undergrowth. Their presence is never staged. The gardens are designed around their comfort as well as the people who visit: shaded retreats, dry sleeping boxes, fresh water, low planting, safe crossings, and small sheltered passages connect the courtyards.

The gardens are regarded as a sign of good luck. When a cat chooses to sit beside a doorway, people say the house has been noticed kindly. A cat crossing the path is not treated as an omen that needs decoding; it is simply a small, fortunate interruption in the day. The old custom is to leave a little space, greet the animal gently if it welcomes attention, and carry on with a lighter step. Children, older residents, visitors, and the most serious workers all seem to find themselves lingering here.

At the centre is a shallow fountain surrounded by catmint, rosemary, thyme, and low flowering plants. A tiled bench stays warm after sunset. Narrow paths lead to a quiet reading court and a small pavilion where the garden steward keeps food, water, brushes, and veterinary supplies. The cats have their own routes through the walls and planted terraces, so the courtyards never feel like a display enclosure. They are living neighbourhood gardens, and the city is considered luckier for sharing its space with them.
