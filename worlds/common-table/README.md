# THE COMMON TABLE

The Common Table is a public dining hall at the centre of the inhabited district, built around one long oak table and several smaller tables that can be joined when the room fills. The kitchen serves generous, uncomplicated food: roast vegetables, fresh bread, soups, rice, fruit, slow-cooked dishes, and whatever is in season. The menu changes with the market and the gardens. There is always a vegetarian option, plenty of water, and room for someone who has arrived late.

The hall is warm but not formal. Its high windows open toward a planted courtyard, and the ceiling beams carry the marks of years of use. People bring news from the quay, flowers from the market, books from Cedar House, and the occasional cat hair on a coat from the gardens. Nobody is required to perform sociability. A person can eat with the group, sit at the end of the table, or take a tray outside beneath the covered veranda.

The Common Table gives the district a dependable centre. It is where routes cross, where new residents learn the names of places, and where a conversation can continue days after it began. The house rules are simple: make room, pass the bread, leave the table ready for the next person, and let a newcomer find their own pace.
