# The Scale Commons

The Scale Commons is a permanent adult social, recreational and residential world within the Colossus campus. It is designed for adult men whose bodies may grow far beyond ordinary human proportions and for the communities that form around those changes. Its defining feature is not a single giant building but a network of environments built to let different scales coexist: ordinary rooms, elevated halls, monumental courts, miniature model districts, adaptable recreation spaces and open ground where the relationship between body and landscape can be experienced directly.

The Commons treats scale as a lived relationship. Height matters, but so do reach, shoulder width, depth, turning radius, footprint, clearance, movement, camera distance, furniture proportion, sound and the amount of the environment occupied by a body. A doorway that once felt generous may become a threshold. A familiar chair may become a model of an earlier baseline. A courtyard can feel small from one position and expansive from another. The world records these changes instead of restoring the old proportions automatically.

## Classes of scale

The working height classes are descriptive environmental bands, not rankings or growth stages.

| Class | Height | Environmental reading |
|---|---:|---|
| Elevated | 7 ft to under 10 ft | Ordinary architecture begins to feel undersized; doorways, seating and circulation require attention. |
| Colossal | 10 ft to under 25 ft | Rooms and furniture become visibly small; high-clearance interiors and custom equipment are needed. |
| Monumental | 25 ft to under 100 ft | Buildings become close-range surroundings; courtyards and dedicated structures become primary spaces. |
| Titan | 100 ft to under 500 ft | District-scale environments and landscape planning become necessary. |
| Continental | 500 ft and above | Geography, terrain and very large open environments become the relevant scale of design. |

Growth stages and scale classes are separate systems. A stage records a character's narrative development; a scale class records a measured height band used for environmental planning. Joey's Stage 5 Colossus baseline remains intact. His exact height in this continuity is not assigned by inference, and no new class is entered until a measurement is established in the story ledger.

## The places

The High Hall is the Commons' main gathering space. Its tall timber roof, reinforced floor, broad windows and adjustable seating allow residents of different proportions to meet without turning the room into a single-person display. The Scale Gates form a route of marked thresholds where residents can compare reach, clearance and movement against familiar architectural references. The Tableland is a large interactive model campus with miniature buildings, streets, parks, recreation spaces and transport routes. Residents can rearrange it, test new layouts and preserve each arrangement as a record of how they imagine a shared environment should work.

The Switchback is a movement course with ramps, platforms, wide turns and changing clearances. The Mirror Gallery is used for presentation, visual continuity and scale comparison. The Workshop contains modular furniture, custom clothing, posing equipment and adjustable training apparatus. The Outer Court gives the residents a place to meet beneath open sky, where shadows, distance and the surrounding buildings make scale visible without a measurement board.

## Play and social rules

The Commons is a place for imaginative, consensual play among adult fictional characters. Its activities can include scale-gate challenges, giant's-eye exploration, miniature architecture, model-city rearrangement, posing and presentation, comparative movement, friendly rivalry and collaborative design. The contrast between a giant body and a small environment is a source of play, but the environment is not treated as disposable and other residents are not reduced to props. Participants choose their roles and pace; no one is required to perform, compete or accept contact.

The Commons is not organized around a single champion. Its culture depends on curiosity, reciprocal attention and the pleasure of discovering what a different scale makes possible. A resident may enjoy being the largest man in a room, learning to move carefully through a small-scale environment, building a new hall for someone else, or watching a friend reach a milestone. These are different forms of participation, all capable of producing lasting story events.

## Continuity

The Commons preserves previous baselines. When a resident grows, the old measurements, clothing patterns, room plans and photographs remain as evidence of the earlier state. The building adapts, but it does not erase its history. New architecture is added where possible; old rooms may be retained as historical reference spaces. A character's face, voice, temperament and relationships remain continuous through physical change.

The Joey Prever in the Colossus Chronicles is the adult fictionalized protagonist of that separate story cycle. Marcus in Volume VI is a new adult fictional resident of the Scale Commons; he is not the working production subject named Marcus in the Teal Titan of Gold's on 5th project. The Scale Commons does not merge those continuities.

The governing inscription is: **NO FINAL SCALE. NO LOST BASELINE. MAKE ROOM, THEN KEEP BECOMING.**
