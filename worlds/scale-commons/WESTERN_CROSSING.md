# The Western Crossing

**World:** Scale Commons / western road  
**Status:** Closed pending engineering review and permanent repair  
**First developed in:** [Chapter LXXIX — The Giant on the Western Road](../../novel/chapters/CHAPTER_LXXIX_THE_GIANT_ON_THE_WESTERN_ROAD.md)  
**Latest development:** [Chapter LXXXV — The Three Measures of the Crossing](../../novel/chapters/CHAPTER_LXXXV_THE_THREE_MEASURES_OF_THE_CROSSING.md)

The Western Crossing carries the marked western road over a narrow gorge between two wooded slopes. Its old stone supports and repaired timber-and-steel deck are practical infrastructure, not a ceremonial monument. The bridge has a middle joint that shifted during rain, a temporary brace used for controlled inspection only, and a western approach whose settlement requires separate investigation. The full crossing remains closed. No test on a yard assembly authorizes a person or vehicle to cross the actual span.

## Three separate measures

The engineering review distinguishes three questions:

1. **Static bearing response.** A short test assembly uses hydraulic jacks and displacement gauges to check load sharing between two bearing shoes and a removable central member. The initial static test remained within its expected range for the test frame and applied loads only.
2. **Changing-load response.** A second test moved load between the bearings. It revealed a small lateral rotation at the outer restraint, below the test limit but contrary to the expected direction. The bracket geometry must be revised and retested without losing inspection access to the replaceable central member.
3. **Approach ground and drainage.** A shallow channel behind the western retaining wall was partly blocked by fine roots and compacted silt. A camera probe showed a drainage joint out of alignment near the old culvert. Water may be travelling behind the retaining wall and contributing to the observed approach settlement. The full channel run and the condition of the fill still require survey; this is a working explanation, not a final diagnosis.

The test assembly is not the bridge. Its passing static result cannot approve the span, and the sound recording made during the changing-load test is supplementary observation rather than a substitute for displacement measurements.

## Character and route continuity

Dorian Vale approaches the crossing as a traveller whose size is a design condition, not as the cause of every defect revealed during inspection. The survey marks and drainage evidence predate his arrival. His approach made the remaining structural reserve important to measure, but the origin of the joint movement and the contribution of the drainage problem must be established from site evidence.

Gabe has accompanied Dorian during the later review. Their familiarity is developing through shared work and direct conversation; they are not yet established as close friends. Tyler checks drawings and measurements, Andrew preserves separately labelled sound observations, and the engineering team retains responsibility for structural assessment.

## Open work

- Revise the lateral-restraint bracket and repeat the changing-load test.
- Preserve access to the removable central member during the redesign.
- Survey the full drainage run and the displaced joint.
- Check the condition of the fill behind the western retaining wall.
- Reassess approach settlement after the drainage condition is understood.
- Complete independent engineering review before any proposal to reopen the crossing.

Keep each result tied to its test, instrument, location, and source. Do not infer approval of the real bridge from the test-yard assembly.
